### 2B. Hypothesis Testing

**AP Alignment:** LO 1.4.C · EK 1.4.C.1, 1.4.C.3 · LO 1.4.B · EK 1.4.B.1, 1.4.B.2

Your product idea is an assumption. Now **test** it.

**Step 1: Write a hypothesis.**

> **"If _______________, then _______________, because _______________."**
>
> *(Example: "If students at our school are offered homemade energy bars at lunch for $2 each, then at least 20% of students in the cafeteria will buy one on the first day, because they currently have no healthy, affordable snack option.")*

**Step 2: Design and run a test.**

Your test must involve **actual customer behavior**, not just opinions (EK 1.4.C.3). Choose one:
- A survey with specific pricing options (not "would you buy?" but "would you pay $2? $3? $5?")
- A small-scale trial (make a few units and see if anyone buys)
- A pre-order sign-up (ask people to commit before you produce)
- A prototype/MVP feedback session (show a sketch or sample and get reactions)

> **Test method:**
>
> **Sample size:** (How many people?)
>
> **Success criteria:** (What result confirms the hypothesis? What result rejects it?)

**Step 3: Report results.**

> **What happened:**
>
> **Confirmed or rejected?**
>
> **Evidence:** (numbers, quotes, observations)

> **The risks for my product are:**

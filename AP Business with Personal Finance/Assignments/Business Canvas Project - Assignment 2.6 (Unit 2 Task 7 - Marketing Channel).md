## The framework — five rules

1. **The marketing channel is the last mile of the supply chain** (1.8). Supply chain = raw materials → production → distribution → customer; the **channel** is only the final stage, finished product → customer.
2. **Direct vs indirect turns on intermediaries — nothing else.** No middleman = **direct** (your own stall, your own site, your own chat group). One or more intermediaries = **indirect** (a shop, a platform, a consignment corner). Selling online does *not* make a channel indirect.
3. **Know the middlemen:** **wholesaler** (buys in bulk, sells to businesses — B2B) · **retailer** (sells to final consumers — B2C) · **distributor** (buys finished goods, sells onward to retailers). A **legally mandated channel** (prescription medicine, licensed dealers) removes the choice entirely — the law decides the door.
4. **Selection weighs three criteria:** cost & potential profitability (what it costs to run, what margin survives) · customer experience (control over how the product is presented and sold) · ability to reach your target customers. The trade-off grid: **direct** = control, margin, consistency — but higher cost and limited reach; **indirect** = reach and partners' expertise — but less control and less margin per unit.
5. **Two things teams forget:** indirect channels are **not always available** — shelf space is finite, rivals may already hold the distributors, gatekeepers charge listing fees (CED 2.6.B.3); and running several channels at once (**channel mix**) can create **channel conflict** when your own channels compete for the same customer.

## Important notes

1. **Same business, same team, same customer** — the channel must reach the customer you profiled in 2.1, not the biggest crowd. If the best-reach door is closed to you, say so and pick the best one you can actually open.

2. **This task is not asterisked.** No market test is required; the grade is on reasoning and numbers. (The asterisked campaign test arrives in 2.7.)

3. **Your 2.5 price is the input.** Margin per unit = committed price − unit cost − the channel's cut. If a channel's cut flips your margin negative, that is a finding — document it, don't hide it.

4. **Direct ≠ 直销 ≠ 传销.** A direct channel is a *form* (producer → customer). Legality is decided by where the money comes from — real product sales versus recruitment fees — never by whether a middleman exists.

5. **Exam connection:** LO 2.6.B asks you to *select and evaluate* channels on cost, customer experience, and reach. The two most commonly missed halves are the **trade-off grid** (you must say what you gave up) and the **access barrier** (CED 2.6.B.3: the best-reach channel may already be dominated by rivals).

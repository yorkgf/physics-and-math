## The framework — six rules

1. **Raw material ≠ product** (EK 2.4.A.1). A raw material is the input you start with; the product is **what the customer pays for**. If a customer could get your "product" by buying the raw material herself, you are reselling an input.
2. **You are joining a six-stage sequence in the middle.** Ideation → Validation → **Design → Messaging** → Production → Launch. 2.4 does Design, Messaging and the start of Branding. Your 2.3 findings are the research the value proposition must be built **from** — not decorated with.
3. **MVP = the simplest sellable version that lets real customers vote with real dollars.** Not a prototype shown to friends — an offer with a price and a way to commit. **Product-market fit = demand sufficient to generate profit.**
4. **Design is three dimensions, priced from cost data** (EK 2.4.A.1): **functionality** (how it works) · **user experience** (how the customer encounters it) · **attributes** (measurable features). The design sets the cost: parts, delivery time, hours — **and your own hours go in the cost line.**
5. **Value proposition = who + what problem + why us**, built from market research. **Positioning** = how the customer perceives you **relative to rivals** — both of them, not your favourite one.
6. **A brand is a name, term, design, symbol or combination that identifies and differentiates** your product; branding has **three jobs** — distinguish, raise awareness, generate loyalty (EK 2.4.B.1–B.3). **Trademarks** give legal protection against imitation; **generic products** are the honest alternative for buyers who prioritise cost over brand. **Brand equity is earned through consistency across every touchpoint** — it is also what a copycat cannot photocopy.

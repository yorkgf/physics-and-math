## Before You Start: The Framework

**The design-thinking loop from 1.4 (EK 1.4.C.1–1.4.C.3) — this assignment *is* stages 2–5:**

| Stage | What happens | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Observe** | You spotted a problem | ✅ Done in Assignment 1A |
| **2. Validate** | Interviews give evidence the problem exists, is clearly defined, and is experienced by multiple potential customers | Section 2 |
| **3. Ideate** | Brainstorm, sketch, prototype a solution | Section 3 |
| **4. Test** | Gather feedback on an **MVP** — "the simplest version of the product idea, with only core features… a sketch, a description, or a model" | Section 4 |
| **5. Define** | Values, competencies, vision, mission, organization type | Section 5 |

**Three rules from the 1.4 lesson you will need:**

1. **A complaint is not a gap.** "Someone needs it" ≠ "no one meets it." 共享雨伞 failed the fake-gap test — a ¥20 umbrella already met the need better than a ¥19 deposit plus rental. Before you interview, know how people solve this problem **today** and why that is unsatisfactory.
2. **Demand ≠ viability.** 共享自习室 was full every night and still barely broke even; 密室逃脱 had queues and still collapsed because nobody came twice. Crowds prove desirability, not a business — which is why your test (Section 4) must measure **behavior, not opinions**.
3. **Opinions don't count; behavior does.** "Would you buy this?" is an opinion. A pre-order, a real purchase, a sign-up, a second use — that is evidence. The CED's own example: don't survey whether students want healthier snacks — **offer the snacks and count who buys**.

**And three from 1.5:**

1. **Values are chosen; competencies are built; ethics are owed.** A value is what the business **believes**; a competency is what it is **good at**. The exam loves to swap this pair — keep the discipline in Section 5.
2. **Test a stated value by what it cost.** A value the business abandons the first time money appears was a **decoration** — Seesaw's "craft" died in the 9.9-yuan price war; Patagonia's transparency, "Don't Buy This Jacket," and free lifetime repairs all **cost real money and held**.
3. **Vision = a future worth wanting (no operations). Mission = what it does and how (the work).** Classify by **content**, not by the label on the document — Alibaba's official "mission" reads like a vision.

> **Strong-answer method (use it all through this worksheet):**
> **State the evidence → name the concept (LO/EK) → tie it to YOUR business.**

*AP Topic 1.4 · LO 1.4.A · EK 1.4.A.1, 1.4.A.2*

| Source (from class) | Part of your idea's origin? | Your evidence — the specific fact, observation, or change |
|---|---|---|
| **Gap** — a specific need not currently being met (competitors may exist — "none sell sugar-free" is a gap inside a crowded market) | Y / N | |
| **Opportunity** — a change (technology, trend, rule, resource) that makes a new or better way possible | Y / N | |
| **Existing business / replication** — a model you are copying, adapting, or improving (塔斯汀 copied 华莱士 before it innovated) | Y / N | |

> Most real ideas combine more than one source — the exam rewards you for naming **all** of them.

### 1A. The fake-gap check (共享雨伞 test)

> **How do my target customers solve this problem today?**
>
> **Why is the current solution unsatisfactory** (too expensive, too slow, unavailable, wrong features)?
>
> **Could a customer make, buy, or own something that meets the need about as well — for less trouble than my product?** (If yes, you may have a complaint, not a gap.)

### 1B. The "why now" test

> **If your idea depends on a recent change** (new technology, new rule, new trend — like mobile payment enabling deposit-free rental), **name the change and roughly when it happened:**
>
> **Is that change the source of your idea, or an enabler** — the reason the idea is possible *now*? (In class: mobile payment was the **enabler**; the source was the gap — "phone dying with nowhere to charge." Enablers answer "why now," not "where from.")

### 1C. Strengths match (the two offers)

Remember the snack box's two offers: the mega cart ran on **someone else's money**; the far-field table ran on **his** logistics and reputation. Pursue the opportunities your strengths can win (EK 1.5.A.4 previews this).

> **What strengths does your team actually bring?** (skills, time, resources, connections)
>
> **Which parts of this business run on those strengths — and which run on things you would have to buy or borrow?**

## Important Notes

1. **Same business idea** as Assignments 1A and 1.3 — same customer, same market, same competitors. If your interviews reveal the problem isn't real, **document and adjust** — iteration is the design-thinking process, and the exam rewards the documentation, not a "clean" story.

2. **Hypothesis testing is not optional.** Customer and Product are asterisked canvas elements — the CED **requires** evidence of testing. Sections 2 and 4 are that evidence. "We tested it and people liked it" is the weak version; "the number said X, so we changed Y" is the strong version.

3. **Teams:** same team as 1A; one submission; all members contribute. Interviews may be split among members — but every member must be able to explain all findings, because the exam-day FRQ is written **individually**, about the project you all know.

4. **Evidence beats assertion, still.** "People would definitely buy this" is weak; "3 of 4 interviewees said they skip the far vending machine, so I set my success threshold at 8 of 20 pre-orders" is strong.

5. **AP Exam connection:** the Business Canvas Project Exam-Day Validation FRQ (15% of your score) asks about **your own project** — your validated problem, how you tested a hypothesis and what changed as a result, and a challenge to your business's viability. Sections 2, 4, and 5 are ready-made FRQ material: your interview evidence, your test numbers, your risk–return judgment, and your two-offer answer.

6. **What's next:** Topics 1.6 (ethics) and 1.7 (roles) are AP content but not canvas tasks. Then Topic 1.8 brings **Assignment 1C** (production process + supply chain plan) — where the core competencies you listed in 5A start doing real work — and the **Unit 1 Final Canvas** asks you to commit to your idea and integrate everything.

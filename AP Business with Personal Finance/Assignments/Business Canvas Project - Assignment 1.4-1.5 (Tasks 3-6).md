## The framework — six rules

1. **The design-thinking loop** (EK 1.4.C.1–C.3): Observe (done in 1A) → **Validate** (Section 2) → **Ideate** (Section 3) → **Test** an MVP (Section 4) → **Define** the organization (Section 5).
2. **A complaint is not a gap.** 共享雨伞 failed the fake-gap test — a ¥20 umbrella already met the need better than a ¥19 deposit plus rental. Before you interview, know how people solve this problem **today** and why that is unsatisfactory.
3. **Demand ≠ viability.** 共享自习室 was full every night and still barely broke even; 密室逃脱 had queues and still collapsed because nobody came twice. Crowds prove desirability, not a business — your test must measure **behavior, not opinions.**
4. **Opinions don't count; behavior does.** "Would you buy this?" is an opinion. A pre-order, a real purchase, a sign-up, a second use — that is evidence.
5. **Values are chosen; competencies are built.** A value is what the business **believes**; a competency is what it is **good at**. The exam loves to swap this pair. Test a stated value by what it **cost** — a value abandoned the first time money appears was a **decoration.**
6. **Vision = a future worth wanting (no operations). Mission = what it does and how (the work).** Classify by **content**, not by the label on the document — Alibaba's official "mission" reads like a vision.

## Section 4 — Task 5: hypothesis test + the entrepreneur's decision

*LO 1.4.B–C · EK 1.4.B.1–B.2, 1.4.C.3 · Skill 2.B · \*asterisked*

### Step 1 — Write the hypothesis (specific and measurable)

> "**If** ______ (target customer, Section 2) **are offered** ______ (MVP — core features only), **then** at least ______ (number or %) **will** ______ (the behavior you will count: buy / pre-order / sign up / return for a second use), **because** ______ (reason drawn from your interview evidence)."

*Weak:* "If students like my product, they will buy it." *Strong:* "If 20 players are offered a pre-order form for a $3 snack box at Friday's practice, at least 8 will pre-order by Thursday, because 3 of 4 interviewed players said they skip the far vending machine."

### Step 2 — Build the MVP

The simplest version, with only core features — "a sketch, a description, or a model" (EK 1.4.C.3). Attach or describe it here.

### Step 3 — Run a BEHAVIOR test

A survey that only gathers opinions does **not count.** Choose one or more: **pre-order sign-up** (commitment at a stated price, before you produce) · **small trial sale** (make a few units, count who buys at the real price) · **observational pilot** (offer it, count actual and repeat use).

> **Test method and sample:** ______________
> **Success criteria — written BEFORE the test:** confirmed if ______; rejected if ______

### Step 4 — Results and decision

> **What happened (numbers, not adjectives):** ______________
> **Confirmed / partially confirmed / rejected:** ______________
> **How the test shaped a decision** (required documentation): I decided to ______ because ______

### Step 5 — The entrepreneur's decision (risk and return)

Bringing a product to market "requires financial, physical, and human resources, which have costs, and there is no guarantee that a new product will generate enough revenue to cover the costs or earn profits" (EK 1.4.B.1).

> **Estimated investment to start:** $______

> **Where my venture sits on the risk–return table** (candy → restaurant → tech startup): ______ risk × ______ potential return, because…
> **Why I accept the risk** (EK 1.4.B.2 — check and explain): potential future profits / the satisfaction of solving a problem / the ability to pursue a passion

## The framework — six rules

1. **Marketing ≠ advertising.** Marketing is *all* the activities that **identify** customers' problems, needs and wants and **promote, sell and deliver** products that meet them (EK 2.1.A.1). If your answer to "what is your marketing?" is "we'll post on 小红书," you have named one activity out of four.
2. **Customer data feeds three decisions: who / what / how** (EK 2.1.A.2). Every data point should trace to one of them.
3. **Traits of the person = demographic. Contents of the mind = psychographic** (EK 2.1.A.3). The **census test** settles it: *could a census form record it?* Age, income, location — yes. Interests, values, lifestyle — no.
4. **Segment → target → profile are three zoom levels, not synonyms** (EK 2.1.B.1–B.3). A **segment** is a group with shared traits; **target customers** are the buyers *most likely to purchase*; a **profile** is one **fictional** person who stands for the target. One zoom level off is the most common error on this material.
5. **Targeting improves the odds; it never guarantees the sale** (EK 2.1.B.4). Designing for the buyers most likely to purchase is typically **more effective and less costly** than appealing to everyone.
6. **Relationships move two numbers in opposite directions** (EK 2.1.C.2–C.3): relationship tactics push **lifetime value (LTV)** up and **customer acquisition cost (CAC)** down. A referred customer arrives at zero marketing cost.

### Step 3 — LTV

**LTV = the estimated amount a customer will spend with you over time** (EK 2.1.C.3) — tracked in **margin**, not revenue.

| Source of value | Your number |
|---|---|

> **Model — Renee:** first bike margin $38 + two tune-ups $24 + sibling bike (40% × $65) $26 = **LTV $88**, against a **$5 CAC**. The repeat and the referral did the work, not the first sale.

## Before You Start: The Framework

**Six rules from the 2.1 lesson that this worksheet runs on:**

1. **Marketing ≠ advertising.** Marketing is *all* the activities that **identify** customers' problems, needs, and wants and **promote, sell, and deliver** products that meet them (EK 2.1.A.1). Advertising is one piece of it. If your answer to "what is your marketing?" is "we'll post on 小红书," you have named one activity out of four.
2. **Customer data feeds three decisions: who / what / how** (EK 2.1.A.2). Which customers to serve, which products to produce, how to market them. Every data point you collect should be traceable to one of those three.
3. **Traits of the person = demographic. Contents of the mind = psychographic** (EK 2.1.A.3). The **census test** settles it every time: *could a census form record it?* Age, sex, race, ethnicity, income, location — yes. Interests, activities, values, lifestyles — no.
4. **Segment → target → profile are three zoom levels, not synonyms** (EK 2.1.B.1–B.3). A **segment** is a group with shared demographic + psychographic traits. **Target customers** are the buyers *most likely to purchase*. A **profile** is one **fictional** person who stands for the target. One zoom level off is the most common error on this material.
5. **Targeting improves the odds; it never guarantees the sale** (EK 2.1.B.4). Designing for the buyers most likely to purchase is typically **more effective and less costly** than appealing to a wide audience — that is the whole argument, and it is an argument about *cost and fit*, not about certainty.
6. **Relationships move two numbers in opposite directions** (EK 2.1.C.2–C.3). Relationship tactics push **lifetime value (LTV)** up and **customer acquisition cost (CAC)** down. A referred customer arrives at zero marketing cost.

> **Strong-answer method (use it all through this worksheet):**
> **State the evidence → name the concept (LO/EK) → tie it to YOUR business.**

**A note on where your evidence comes from:** your Unit 1 interviews, surveys, and observation logs are still valid data. You do not need to start over — you need to **re-read them as data about a person** rather than data about a problem. The 鸣鸣很忙 clip in the lesson does exactly this at scale: 20,000 stores segmented by **location** (58% in 县城/乡镇), **income** ("帮月薪 3000 块的人实现零食自由"), and **values** (既要便宜又要爽) — and the profile that fell out of it produced **超大包辣条 and 5 斤装薯片.**

> **Privacy risk is sharpest in two situations** (EK 2.1.D.1): when customers are **unaware** that their data is being collected, and when they are **uninformed** about how it may be used. A "Y / N" of **N** in either column is a finding, not a formality.

### 4B. The four terms — classify your own exposure

| Term | Meaning | Where this could happen to us |
|---|---|---|
| **Privacy infringement** | collection or use that violates a customer's expectation | |

| **Identity theft** | stolen details used to impersonate someone | |

> **Model case from the lesson — 一张快递单:** a name, an address, and a phone number on one shipping label is the same "one line" as a notebook entry — data **kept without a stated purpose or a disclosed use.** What a stranger can do with it is **identity theft** and **fraud** — and the harm lands back on the seller.

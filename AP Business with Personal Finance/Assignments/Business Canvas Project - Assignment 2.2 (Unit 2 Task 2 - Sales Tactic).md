## Before You Start: The Framework

**Seven rules from the 2.2 lesson that this worksheet runs on:**

1. **Needs and wants start the decision; four tiebreakers end it** (EK 2.2.A.1). Consumers buy to meet basic needs and wants. When **rival goods** meet the same need, the choice is decided by **price · differentiated product features · availability · advertising.** Your tactic has to sit on one of those four — or on a fifth thing you can name.
2. **Match the depth of the process to the size of the decision** (EK 2.2.A.2). Consequential purchases (a home, a bike for your kid) run a **rational** step-by-step evaluation; small ones (coffee on the way to work) run **habitual**. Rational searching has a **cost — time spent gathering information** — and that cost is why the cheaper rival often loses anyway.
3. **Four families of influence, and the families are not interchangeable** (EK 2.2.A.3–A.6).
   **Personal** = who they are (age, sex, education, income, budget, occupation, lifestyle).
   **Psychological** = what they think and feel (values, beliefs, perceptions, learning, motivations).
   **Social & cultural** = what their groups expect (peers, family, social status, cultural norms, media).
   **Situational** = what's happening right now (store design — noise, lighting, organization — timing, product availability).
   > **The situational tell:** *if it changes when the shopper walks out the door, it's situational; if it travels home with the product, it isn't.* Warm lighting in the candle shop = situational. Soy wax = a product feature.
4. **A purchasing pattern is read in three dimensions** (EK 2.2.B.1): **timing** (when), **frequency** (how often), **quantity** (how much per purchase). Where a person lives and works, their income level, and the patterns of family and friends set all three.
5. **Laws and technology bend the pattern from outside** (EK 2.2.B.2, 2.2.B.3). Laws regulate **what, when, and where** people can buy; **consumer protection laws** exist to keep buyers away from fraudulent or unsafe products; when a product gets harder to obtain, the **need persists and substitutes arrive** (straw ban → paper and metal; motorcycle bans → e-bikes; the recall check is point 1 on Theo's inspection list). Technology makes new products accessible and changes **both sides of the sale** — seller reach *and* buyer information.
6. **Seven principles; sort by mechanism, never by keyword** (EK 2.2.C.1–C.8):

| Principle | Mechanism | The sorting question |
|---|---|---|
| **Scarcity** (C.2) | perceived rarity | Is it presented as **limited or one-of-a-kind**? |
| **Authority** (C.3) | expert credentials | Does an **expert or credential** endorse it? |
| **Consensus** (C.4) | many buyers' behavior | Do **many others** already buy or approve? |
| **Liking** (C.5) | rapport / relatability | Does the seller feel **friendly, like me**? |
| **Reciprocity** (C.6) | obligation from receiving | Did the buyer **get something free** first? |
| **Consistency** (C.7) | self-image match | Does it appeal to **who the buyer believes they are**? |
| **Unity** (C.8) | group membership | Does the buyer feel **part of a group**? |

> **The word in the answer choice is unreliable. The engine decides.** "Experts recommend it" is authority even if the sentence also says "everyone agrees."

7. **Every principle has an honest use and a manipulative misuse.** The CED asks you to **evaluate** tactics, not just list them — so the lesson gives you one test:

> **The face test:** say the tactic to the customer's face. **If the principle needs secrecy to function, it's manipulation. If it works in the open, it's persuasion.**

| Tactic | Face test |
|---|---|
| "This tag lists everything I checked" | **survives** — the buyer can read every point |
| "Come back within 30 days and the tune-up is free" | **survives** — it reverses the risk keeping used-bike buyers from saying yes |
| "Three other people are coming this afternoon" | **dies** the moment a buyer asks *"who?"* |

> **Strong-answer method (all through this worksheet):**
> **Name the mechanism → cite the EK → tie it to YOUR business and YOUR customer's exact words.**

**Model at scale — 茶颜悦色:** 500+ stores, and for **7 years the product was available in one city only.** Tourists planned trips around a cup. Scarcity (real: this flavor ends this season), consensus (1M+ genuine 小红书 posts), reciprocity (buy-6-get-1 stamp card, free festival gifts), consistency ("I'm a 茶颜 fan" decides the next order). **Every one of those tactics survives full disclosure.** That is why the brand scaled instead of collapsing — the honest version outsold the lie, and that is not luck, it is the psychology working as designed.

## The framework — six rules

1. **Needs and wants start the decision; four tiebreakers end it** (EK 2.2.A.1). When rival goods meet the same need, the choice is decided by **price · differentiated product features · availability · advertising.** Your tactic must attach to a tiebreaker you win.
2. **Match the depth of the process to the size of the decision** (EK 2.2.A.2). Consequential purchases run a **rational** step-by-step evaluation; small ones run **habitual**. Rational searching has a **cost — time spent gathering information** — and that cost is why the cheaper rival often loses anyway.
3. **Four families of influence, not interchangeable** (EK 2.2.A.3–A.6): **personal** = who they are · **psychological** = what they think and feel · **social & cultural** = what their groups expect · **situational** = what's happening right now. *The situational tell: if it changes when the shopper walks out the door, it's situational; if it travels home with the product, it isn't.*
4. **A purchasing pattern is read in three dimensions** (EK 2.2.B.1): **timing** (when) · **frequency** (how often) · **quantity** (how much per purchase). Where a person lives and works, their income, and the patterns of family and friends set all three.
5. **Laws and technology bend the pattern from outside** (EK 2.2.B.2–B.3). Laws regulate what, when and where people can buy — and when a product gets harder to obtain, **the need persists and substitutes arrive** (straw ban → paper and metal; 禁摩 → e-bikes). Technology changes **both sides**: seller reach *and* buyer information (a parent price-checks your tag on her phone while standing at your stall).
6. **Seven principles; sort by mechanism, never by keyword** (EK 2.2.C.1–C.8):

| Principle | Mechanism | The sorting question |
|---|---|---|
| **Scarcity** | perceived rarity | Is it presented as **limited or one-of-a-kind**? |
| **Authority** | expert credentials | Does an **expert or credential** endorse it? |
| **Consensus** | many buyers' behavior | Do **many others** already buy or approve? |
| **Liking** | rapport / relatability | Does the seller feel **friendly, like me**? |
| **Reciprocity** | obligation from receiving | Did the buyer **get something free** first? |
| **Consistency** | self-image match | Does it appeal to **who the buyer believes they are**? |
| **Unity** | group membership | Does the buyer feel **part of a group**? |

> **The face test** (the lesson's ethics check): say the tactic to the customer's face. **If the principle needs secrecy to function, it's manipulation. If it works in the open, it's persuasion.** "Three other buyers are coming this afternoon" dies the moment a buyer asks *"who?"*

## The framework — six rules

1. **Campaign = coordinated.** Several tools, one message, one target, one window. A single listing is a *promotion*; a planned week where every tool pushes the same product at the same customer is a *campaign*. "Coordinated" is the load-bearing word.
2. **Five tools** (LO 2.7.A): **media advertising** (paid, mass — one message to many at once) · **personal selling** (one-on-one, detailed, demos — livestream selling counts) · **sales promotion** (short-term incentives with an end date) · **direct marketing** (targeted messages to specific people — flyers, email, SMS) · **public relations** (earned image — press releases, events; nobody pays for the placement).
3. **Match the tool to the purchase** (LO 2.7.A.8): **consequential** purchases (expensive, high-involvement) need personal selling and detail; **routine** purchases respond to media advertising or direct marketing. Your 2.2 work already told you which yours is.
4. **Who paid decides the tool:** paid placement = advertising; unpaid earned coverage = PR. The content can look identical — the money flow decides. And a *permanent* low price is a 2.5 pricing strategy, not a sales promotion.
5. **Digital changed the measurement** (LO 2.7.B): traditional is broad, one-way and hard to track; digital is targetable, two-way and **measurable** — clicks, views, conversions, in real time. Big data lets a business personalize messages and optimize a campaign mid-flight.
6. **CAC arithmetic:** CAC = total campaign spend ÷ **customers acquired**. Views and taps never belong in the denominator. Theo's "Ready to Ride" week: **$36** spend → 1,200 views → 40 taps → 9 inquiries → **4 sales = $9 CAC** — still below his **$88 LTV**. Compute each stage's conversion rate and fix the weakest stage first.

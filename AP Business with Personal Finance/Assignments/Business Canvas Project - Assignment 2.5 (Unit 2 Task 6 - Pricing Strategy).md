## The framework — six rules

1. **Every price starts at the per-unit cost floor.** A price at or below cost is unprofitable whatever the strategy is called. Theo's commuter rebuild: cost **$57** (parts + labour + tag + stall share), price **$95**, margin **$38**. Compute yours from your 2.4 Section 2 — it must include your own labour.
2. **Six strategies** (LO 2.5.A): **value-based** (price = what the customer believes it's worth) · **competitive** (price = what rivals charge) · **cost-based** (price = cost + desired profit) · **penetration** (deliberately low, with a stated plan to raise later) · **dynamic** (real-time with demand) · **loss-leader** (below cost on one item, profit on the rest of the basket). No strategy is universally best — the right one depends on the product, the market, and the goal.
3. **Price lining** (bonus term): a small menu of distinct price points, one per segment — Theo's rack: kids' bikes **$60** / commuters **$95** / road bikes **$150**. The customer picks the tier, not the price.
4. **Pricing power** = raising price **without losing customers** (LO 2.5.B). It grows with brand strength, uniqueness (no close substitutes), switching costs, loyalty, and inelastic demand. **Elasticity** = how customers actually respond: **elastic** → a price raise cuts total revenue; **inelastic** → a price raise raises it. The exam never asks you to calculate — classify, then reason to the revenue consequence.
5. **Three illegal practices** (LO 2.5.C): **price fixing** (competitors agreeing on a price level) · **price gouging** (unfair price hikes during a crisis) · **price discrimination** (different prices by protected characteristics — legal forms like student tickets or advance-purchase rules are fine).
6. **A permanent low price is a pricing strategy, not a sales promotion** — a discount with an end date is 2.7's job.

## Important notes

1. **Same business idea, same team, same customer** as every prior assignment. If the price changed, document which checkpoint forced it — that is the design-thinking loop, not an inconsistency.

2. **The asterisk is on the pricing strategy.** The project rubric grades hypothesis testing of pricing as its own row: the hypothesis, the method, the evidence, and how the evidence shaped the decision. All four, every time.

3. **This feeds 2.6 directly.** The channel decision weighs margin per unit — your committed price minus your cost minus the channel's cut. Leave 2.5 without a committed price and 2.6 becomes opinion.

4. **Penetration pricing only pays if the discount buyers stay.** Xiaomi went from the **1,999 RMB** Mi 1 (2011) to the **4,499 RMB** Mi 15 (2024) — 13 years of earned trust. Write your raise-the-price plan *before* you discount.

5. **Ethics:** a price test run on classmates is run on people who trust you. No fake scarcity, no "was $X" claims that were never true, no crisis price hikes.

6. **Exam connection:** FRQ 1 (15% of your score) asks how hypothesis testing informed your project — Section 5 is that answer for price. Multiple choice will dress the six strategies in scenario costumes; classify by what the price is **anchored to** (customer perception / rivals / cost / a growth plan / real-time demand / the basket), never by the word in the stem.

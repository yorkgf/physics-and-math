## The framework — six rules

1. **Market research = systematically gathering, analyzing and interpreting information about a market — customers *and* competitors — to guide decisions** (EK 2.3.A.1). If a piece of research cannot change a decision you were about to make, do not pay for it.
2. **Two data types, and you need both** (EK 2.3.A.2): **quantitative** = numbers you can count and graph (*"72% prefer X"* — answers *how many?*); **qualitative** = words and observations (*"I like X because…"* — answers *why?*). **Quantitative identifies the pattern; qualitative explains it.**
3. **Three gates, in order** (EK 2.3.A.3): **desirability** (do customers want it?) → **feasibility** (can we build and deliver it?) → **viability** (does revenue exceed cost over time?). A product can be desirable but not feasible; feasible but not viable. Desirability is checked first.
4. **Secondary before primary** (EK 2.3.B.2–B.3). **Secondary = data that already exists** (government, commercial, academic, internal) — cheap, fast, broad, but you did not control how it was collected and it may not answer *your* question.
5. **A business hypothesis is an assumption stated so it can be tested** (EK 2.3.C.2) — written **before** the data, in **variable → measure → expected number** form. It focuses what to measure, makes results interpretable, and **prevents post-hoc rationalization.** *A hypothesis is not a wish.*
6. **Honest data has two pillars** (EK 2.3.C.8): a sample **large and representative enough** for your population, and **neutral question phrasing.** And always **report the base** — 30 of 120 is 25%, not 15%. Research **reduces** uncertainty; it never eliminates it (EK 2.3.C.9).

**Chart selection is decided by the question, never by the look** (EK 2.3.D.1–D.3):

| If you want to show… | Use | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| Values compared across categories | **Bar chart** | the workhorse of the exam |
| Total **and** composition | **Stacked bar chart** | segments = what's inside |
| Change over time | **Line graph** | **time on the x-axis** |
| Proportions of a whole | **Pie chart** | **3–5 slices max** |

## Section 3 — Task 3: hypotheses + method selection

*LO 2.3.C · CED Unit 2 Task 3 · the asterisked deliverable*

### 3A. Write two hypotheses — strict form

> **Template:** "If we **____________** (change one variable), then **____%** of **____________** (the named population) will **____________** (the measured behavior) within **____________**."

**H1 (customer preference — required):** ______________
**Success criterion, set NOW before collecting anything:** ______________ · **If it fails, we will:** ______________

**H2 (a second preference, or your 2.2 tactic's wording):** ______________
**Success criterion, set NOW:** ______________ · **If it fails, we will:** ______________

> **Model — Theo's:** *"If I add an inspection tag, at least 60% of bikes will sell within one week at $95."* Variable (the tag) + measure (sell rate) + number (60%) + clock (one week). All four, or it is a wish. **Hypothesis ≠ finding** — write the finding only in Section 4.

### 3B. Choose the method from the data the hypothesis needs

| Method | Data type | Best for | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Survey | Quantitative | preferences across a **large** group | social desirability bias |
| Interview (1-on-1) | Qualitative | deep motivation, with follow-up | small sample |
| Focus group (6–10) | Qualitative | attitudes, reactions; group dynamics | loudest voice distorts |
| Observation | Behavioral | what people **actually do** | never *why* |
| Experiment | Behavioral | cause and effect | hard to control |
| A/B test | Behavioral, real-world | **will they actually buy?** | needs enough units |

> **Write the justification, not the choice:** *the data I need is ____, therefore the method is ____, and I rejected ____ because ____.* Theo's chain: secondary sweep (free) → survey (200 commuters) → **A/B test: 17 bikes, 9 tagged at $95 vs 8 untagged at $75**, two Saturdays, same location → 12 follow-up interviews. Quantitative found the pattern; qualitative explained it.

| # | Your question or version | Leading? (Y/N) | If Y — the neutral rewrite |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | | | |
| 2 | | | |
| 3 | | | |

> **Rewrite rule:** delete *"don't you think," "wouldn't you prefer," "agree?"* and every superlative. Offer the options and let them rank. Theo's question that worked: *"What influenced your decision today?"* — not *"Didn't the tag make you feel safer?"*
> **If you run an A/B test, name the control explicitly:** the **one** variable that changed, what stayed identical, and the metric that decides the winner. Two Saturdays is a short window — say so.

### 3D. The sample plan

| Line | Your answer |
|---|---|

## Important notes

1. **Same business idea, same team, same customer.** You are not researching a new venture — you are testing the claims you committed to in the Unit 1 Final Canvas and profiled in 2.1. If the findings say the idea is wrong, **that is a success condition.** Document the revision.

2. **Secondary first, always** (EK 2.3.B.3). Section 2 before Section 3 — cheap reconnaissance sharpens the questions you then pay to ask. Teams that survey before they read arrive at 100 questions nobody needed.

3. **Pre-set or it doesn't count.** Success criteria (3A), the neutral rewrite pass (3C) and the sample plan (3D) must exist **before** the first response. A criterion written after seeing the data is a story, not a test — this is precisely how confirmation bias destroyed New Coke at 200,000-person scale.

4. **A small honest n beats a large dishonest one.** State your bias risk and lower your claims to fit — **the limit sentence is what gets the mark** (EK 2.3.C.8, C.9).

5. **The Appendix chart is permanent.** It travels with your canvas into 2.4, 2.5 and Unit 3–4, and it is one of the three Appendix elements the CED grades (with the pitch and the income statement, both due later). Build it once, correctly labeled, from **real** findings.

6. **This is the bridge between 2.2 and 2.4.** Your 2.2 sales tactic is now a **testable preference hypothesis** (H2 is its natural home), and your findings become the **MVP's success bar in 2.4** — the value proposition you write there should quote a number from Section 4, not a feeling.

7. **Team logistics:** split fieldwork by member, but every member must be able to explain the two hypotheses, the method choice and the verdict unaided — the exam-day FRQ is written **individually**.

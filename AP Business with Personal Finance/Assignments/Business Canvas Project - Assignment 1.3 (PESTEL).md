## Before You Start: The Framework

**PESTEL** = the six kinds of **outside forces** that shape the business landscape (EK 1.3.A.1):

| Letter | Factor | What it covers (EK 1.3.A.2–1.3.A.7) |
|---|---|---|
| **P** | Political | Trade policy, taxes, subsidies, mandates, bans, political stability |
| **E** | Economic | Economic stability, household income, inflation, unemployment, interest rates |
| **S** | Social | Demographics, cultural norms, lifestyle trends, population growth |
| **T** | Technological | Internet access, automation of production, pace of innovation |
| **E** | Environmental | Geography, access to resources, waste rules, consumer environmental attitudes |
| **L** | Legal | Employment, consumer protection, health & safety, environmental rules, IP, antitrust |

These forces decide which businesses are **viable** — able to **survive and stay successful in a market over time**.

**Three rules from this week's lesson:**

1. **Classify by the source of the force** — not by what it costs or what subject it touches. A solar-panel *subsidy* concerns the environment and technology, but the force acting on the market is a government policy choice → **political**.
2. **Political vs. legal:** a broad policy *choice* (tax, ban, subsidy) is **political**; a specific codified *rule to comply with* (license, safety code) is **legal**.
3. **The six factors are never equally important.** Identify the ones relevant to *your* product and weigh those — "all six matter equally" is always a weak analysis.

> **Strong-answer method (use it all through this worksheet):**
> **Name the factor → state its direction (helps or hurts) → tie it to YOUR specific business.**

# 3.7 DK Coffee Lab — The Balance Sheet and Net Worth

> **Topic:** 3.7 The Balance Sheet and Net Worth
> **Company:** DK Coffee Lab, Part 3
> **Continues from:** 3.4 (Business Expenses) and 3.5 (Financial Capital)

---

## Background

This case continues the story of **DK Coffee Lab**, the specialty coffee roaster and café founded by **Daniel Kim** in Portland, Oregon. (See the 3.4 and 3.5 handouts for the startup and financing history.)

After evaluating his funding options in mid-2025, Daniel made the following decisions:

| Decision | Outcome |
|---|---|
| SBA bank loan | **Accepted** — $100,000 at 10.5% interest, 5-year term, starting October 2025 |
| Michael Torres (friend loan) | **Accepted** — $50,000 at 8% interest, 3-year term, starting October 2025 |
| Sarah Park (angel investor) | **Declined** — Daniel decided not to give up equity; he wanted to retain full ownership |
| Entity conversion | **Completed** — DK Coffee Lab converted from an LLC to a **C-corporation** in July 2025 |

Daniel combined the $150,000 in loans with $95,000 of his own funds ($70,000 retained earnings + $25,000 personal investment) to fund all three expansion projects:

| Project | Cost | Status by Dec 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Café #4 (Pearl District) | $85,000 | **Open** — launched November 2025 |
| E-commerce website | $60,000 | **Live** — soft-launched December 2025; first orders shipping January 2026 |
| Roasting capacity upgrade (60 kg roaster) | $75,000 | **Operational** — installed November 2025 |

As of **December 31, 2025**, DK Coffee Lab operates **four cafés**, a roasting facility, and an e-commerce platform. The company employs **36 people** and is projecting **$2.4 million in annual revenue for 2026** (up from $1.8M in 2025, driven by Café #4 and e-commerce).

Daniel's accountant has prepared the company's first **balance sheet as a C-corporation** — dated December 31, 2025. Daniel is reviewing it for the first time.

## Questions

**1. The Balance Sheet Equation**
DK Coffee Lab's balance sheet shows $365,500 in assets, $180,000 in liabilities, and $185,500 in owners' equity. Assets = liabilities + owners' equity, explain what each side of the equation represents. If Daniel pays off $20,000 of the bank loan using cash, what happens to each component of the equation? Does it still balance?

**2. Asset Liquidity**
 (liquidity refers to the ease with which an asset can be converted into cash), rank DK Coffee Lab's assets from most liquid to least liquid. Why is liquidity important for a business that must pay weekly expenses like wages and supplies? Which of DK Coffee Lab's long-term assets would be the **hardest** to convert to cash, and why?

**3. Classifying Assets by Category**
Daniel's accountant lists assets in order of liquidity. **Using through 3.7.A.2.iv**, classify each of the following as a current asset, long-term asset, or intangible asset: (a) $62,000 in cash, (b) a commercial espresso machine, (c) the "DK Coffee Lab" trademark, (d) $38,500 in wholesale invoices not yet paid, and (e) the roasting facility's plumbing and electrical build-out. Explain why the order of listing matters to someone reading the balance sheet.

**4. Liabilities by Due Date**
DK Coffee Lab has $55,800 in current liabilities and $124,200 in long-term liabilities. Liabilities are grouped into current and long-term, in order of when payment is due,, and, explain the difference between current and long-term liabilities. Why does the $100,000 bank loan appear in **both** categories? What is the practical significance of this split for Daniel's cash flow planning over the next 12 months?

**5. Owners' Equity**
Owners' equity is the difference between assets and liabilities, often comprised of stock and retained earnings, answer: (a) What does DK Coffee Lab's owners' equity of $185,500 represent in practical terms? (b) The balance sheet shows $100,000 in common stock and $85,500 in retained earnings. Explain the difference between these two components. (c) Why is retained earnings described as "cumulative profits not paid out as dividends"?

**6. Year-over-Year Comparison**
The balance sheet includes data from both 2024 and 2025. Balance sheets typically provide comparable data from a previous year for purposes of comparison, analyze the changes: total assets grew from $243,000 to $365,500, while liabilities grew from $20,800 to $180,000 and owners' equity decreased from $222,200 to $185,500. What does this pattern tell you about how Daniel funded the 2025 expansion? Should Daniel be concerned about any of these trends?

**7. Stakeholder Uses of the Balance Sheet**
Internal stakeholders such as managers and owners, and external stakeholders such as lenders and investors, use balance sheets to evaluate financial condition, explain how **each** of the following stakeholders would use DK Coffee Lab's balance sheet: (a) Daniel (owner/manager), (b) Portland Community Bank (lender), (c) Michael Torres (lender), and (d) Sarah Park (potential investor). What specific line item would each stakeholder focus on most closely?

**8. Working Capital and Debt Assessment**
DK Coffee Lab's current assets are $134,500 and current liabilities are $55,800. A balance sheet indicates whether a business has positive net worth, sufficient working capital, and comparable debt levels, answer: (a) Calculate DK Coffee Lab's working capital and current ratio. (b) The debt-to-asset ratio is 49.2%. Explain what this means and whether it should concern Daniel. (c) What specific daily operations at DK Coffee Lab depend on having sufficient working capital?

**9. Negative Net Worth and Bankruptcy**
Business owners may shut down or pursue bankruptcy if the business cannot access sufficient current assets to fund operations, answer: (a) Under what circumstances would DK Coffee Lab's net worth turn negative? (b) Explain the difference between liquidating assets and reorganizing under bankruptcy. (c) If DK Coffee Lab's revenue dropped 40% for six months and the business could no longer pay its current liabilities, what options would Daniel have before considering bankruptcy?

**10. Personal Net Worth Calculation**
Daniel's personal net worth is $320,775. Personal net worth is calculated by adding all household assets and subtracting all liabilities, verify Daniel's net worth using the data in the Personal Net Worth table. Then answer: (a) What percentage of Daniel's personal net worth is tied to illiquid assets (retirement account + business ownership)? (b) Why does Daniel's personal net worth include his ownership in DK Coffee Lab, and what risk does this concentration create?

**11. Lenders and Personal Net Worth**
Lenders may require information about a consumer's household net worth as part of the loan application process, explain why Portland Community Bank required Daniel's personal financial statement when he applied for the $100,000 SBA loan. What specific information about Daniel's personal net worth gives the bank confidence that he can repay the loan if the business cannot?

**12. Net Worth and Retirement Planning**
Consumers and financial planners consider net worth when determining whether individuals have sufficient savings to retire, review Daniel's retirement data: he is 34 years old with $142,000 in a retirement account, $157,675 in business equity, $0 in annual retirement savings, and a target of $2,000,000 by age 65. (a) Why is Daniel's financial planner concerned, even though his current net worth of $320,775 is strong for his age? (b) What specific recommendation would you make for Daniel's retirement planning once the business generates sufficient cash flow?

# 3.8 AANE — The Cash Flow Statement

> **Topic:** 3.8 The Cash Flow Statement
> **Company:** AANE

---

## Background

**AANE** (the Asperger/Autism Network) is a **nonprofit organization** headquartered in Watertown, Massachusetts, dedicated to providing support, education, and community for individuals with autism and their families. Founded in 1996, AANE offers a range of programs including **social skills groups, parent coaching, employment support, educational workshops, and online community forums**. The organization serves over **10,000 individuals annually** across New England and nationally through virtual programming.

Unlike for-profit businesses that aim to generate profit for owners, AANE's mission is to **serve its community**. The organization generates revenue through a mix of **program fees** (paid by families), **grants** (from foundations and government agencies), **donations** (from individuals and corporations), and **fundraising events**. AANE does not have owners or shareholders — any surplus revenue is reinvested into programs and services.

In fiscal year 2024, AANE faces a critical **cash flow challenge**. The organization's annual operating budget is approximately **$1.8 million**, but a combination of **delayed grant payments, seasonal revenue fluctuations, and rising program costs** has created a situation where AANE's bank account dropped dangerously low — even though the organization is financially viable on paper. AANE's Executive Director, **Dr. Sarah Chen**, must understand the organization's cash flow statement to determine whether AANE can meet its payroll, pay its rent, and continue serving families without interruption.

## AANE Income Statement Summary — Fiscal Year 2024

*For comparison with the cash flow statement.*

| Line Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Total revenue (accrual basis) | $1,680,000 |
| Total expenses (accrual basis) | $1,703,000 |
| **Net income (loss)** | **($23,000)** |

> **The key insight:** AANE shows a **net loss of $23,000** on its income statement but generated **$255,200 in positive operating cash flow**. How is this possible? The income statement uses **accrual accounting** (recording revenue when earned and expenses when incurred), while the cash flow statement tracks **actual cash movement**. AANE received $120,000 in grant cash in August that was recorded as revenue in the prior fiscal year's accrual records, and AANE prepaid $36,000 in insurance for the following year. These timing differences explain the gap between the accounting loss and the positive cash flow.

## Questions

**1. Cash Flow Statement vs. Income Statement**
AANE's income statement shows a net loss of $23,000, but its cash flow statement shows positive operating cash flow of $255,200. A cash flow statement shows how cash inflows and outflows impact cash balance, explain why these two statements tell different stories. Which statement better reflects AANE's ability to pay staff next month?

**2. Cash Inflows**
Cash inflows include payments from customers, interest or dividends, proceeds from asset sales, and infusions of financial capital, identify all of AANE's cash inflows for FY2024 and classify each as operating, investing, or financing. Which inflow source is the most **unpredictable** in terms of timing, and why does this create a management challenge?

**3. Cash Outflows**
Cash outflows include payments to employees and suppliers, interest, taxes, asset purchases, debt repayment, and dividends, review AANE's cash outflows. (a) Which outflow is the largest, and why is it the most difficult to reduce in a cash crisis? (b) AANE is a nonprofit and does not pay dividends or income taxes — how does this change its cash outflow structure compared to a for-profit company like Hershey?

**4. Monitoring Cash Balances**
Businesses monitor cash balances to ensure sufficient cash for recurring expenses, debt repayment, and unforeseen expenses, AANE's cash balance fell to $18,200 in June 2024. AANE's biweekly payroll is approximately $35,000 and monthly rent is $8,500. How many weeks of operations could AANE fund with $18,200? What specific actions should Dr. Chen take when the cash balance falls below a certain threshold?

**5. Positive and Negative Cash Flow**
Cash flow may be positive or negative at the end of a period, review AANE's quarterly cash flow: Q1 was +$45,000, Q2 was −$52,000, Q3 was +$168,000, Q4 was +$62,500. (a) The annual total is positive ($223,500), yet AANE faced a crisis in Q2. Explain how a business can have positive annual cash flow but a negative quarterly cash flow that threatens operations. (b) What is the minimum cash balance AANE should maintain to avoid a crisis like the one in June 2024?

**6. Stakeholder Uses**
The cash flow statement is used to assess a business's ability to meet financial obligations, explain how **each** of the following stakeholders would use AANE's cash flow statement: (a) the Board of Directors, (b) a foundation considering a $100,000 grant, and (c) the bank that provides AANE's line of credit. What specific line item would each stakeholder focus on most closely?

**7. Cash Flow Warning Signs**
Negative cash flow may signal inability to pay current expenses, which could lead to shutdown or bankruptcy even if net income is positive, answer: (a) AANE's net income is negative (−$23,000) but its operating cash flow is positive (+$255,200). Should the Board of Directors be concerned about the net loss, or should they focus on the cash flow? Explain. (b) If AANE's operating cash flow had been **negative** instead of positive, what three specific actions could the organization take to continue operating ?

**8. The Nonprofit Cash Flow Challenge**
AANE's largest expense is payroll ($920,000/year), which must be paid every two weeks regardless of when grants or donations arrive. AANE's largest single revenue source is a $120,000 state grant that was paid 4 months late., explain why this timing mismatch between regular expenses and irregular revenue creates a uniquely dangerous situation for nonprofits. What structural changes could AANE make to reduce its vulnerability to future cash flow crises?

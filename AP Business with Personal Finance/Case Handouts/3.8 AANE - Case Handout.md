# 3.8 AANE — The Cash Flow Statement

> **Topic 3.8 The Cash Flow Statement** · Read before class. Be ready to discuss.

---

## Background

**AANE** (the Asperger/Autism Network) is a **nonprofit organization** headquartered in Watertown, Massachusetts, dedicated to providing support, education, and community for individuals with autism and their families. Founded in 1996, AANE offers programs including **social skills groups, parent coaching, employment support, educational workshops, and online community forums**. The organization serves over **10,000 individuals annually** across New England and nationally through virtual programming.

Unlike for-profit businesses that aim to generate profit for owners, AANE's mission is to **serve its community**. The organization generates revenue through **program fees** (paid by families), **grants** (from foundations and government agencies), **donations** (from individuals and corporations), and **fundraising events**. AANE does not have owners or shareholders — any surplus revenue is reinvested into programs and services.

In fiscal year 2024, AANE faces a **cash flow challenge**. The organization's annual operating budget is approximately **$1.8 million**, but a combination of **delayed grant payments, seasonal revenue fluctuations, and rising program costs** has created a situation where AANE's bank account dropped dangerously low — even though the organization is financially viable on paper. AANE's Executive Director, **Dr. Sarah Chen**, must understand the organization's cash flow statement to determine whether AANE can meet its payroll, pay its rent, and continue serving families without interruption.

## AANE Income Statement Summary — Fiscal Year 2024

*For comparison with the cash flow statement.*

| Line Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Total revenue (accrual basis) | $1,680,000 |
| Total expenses (accrual basis) | $1,703,000 |
| **Net income (loss)** | **($23,000)** |

> **Key observation:** AANE shows a **net loss of $23,000** on its income statement but generated **$255,200 in positive operating cash flow**. The income statement uses **accrual accounting** (recording revenue when earned and expenses when incurred), while the cash flow statement tracks **actual cash movement**. AANE received $120,000 in grant cash in August that was recorded as revenue in the prior fiscal year's accrual records, and AANE prepaid $36,000 in insurance for the following year. These timing differences explain the gap between the accounting loss and the positive cash flow.

## 1. Company Introduction

**Haidilao International Holding Ltd. (海底捞)** is China's largest hot pot restaurant chain, known worldwide for its **extreme customer service** — free manicures while waiting, birthday celebrations, noodle-pulling performances, and staff who anticipate customer needs before they are expressed.

- **Founded:** 1994 by **Zhang Yong (张勇)** in Jianyang, Sichuan Province, China
- **Product:** Sichuan-style hot pot dining experience — customers cook their own ingredients in a shared pot of broth at the table
- **Founder background:** Zhang Yong started with a single four-table restaurant. With no formal culinary training, he built the business by focusing obsessively on customer experience and employee satisfaction.
- **Core competency:** Haidilao's core competency is not cooking — it is employee training and empowerment. Staff are trained to anticipate customer needs and empowered to solve problems on the spot without manager approval. Employees who provide exceptional service receive bonuses and promotion opportunities. Zhang Yong's philosophy: "Take care of your employees first; they will take care of your customers."
- **Industry:** Restaurant / Food & Beverage / QSR (Quick Service Restaurant)

Haidilao went public on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange in 2018. As of 2024, it operates over **1,400 restaurants** in China and internationally (including the U.S., U.K., Japan, South Korea, Singapore, and Australia). Annual revenue exceeds **$5 billion.**

## 2. Key Events

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **1994** | Zhang Yong opens the first Haidilao restaurant — a four-table hot pot shop in Jianyang, Sichuan |
| **1999** | Opens first branch outside Sichuan — in Xi'an, Shaanxi Province |
| **2004** | Begins expanding to major Chinese cities; gains national reputation for extraordinary customer service |
| **2010s** | Becomes a cultural phenomenon in China — viral stories of Haidilao's service (staff offering free phone cases, charging cables, lap blankets for children, birthday song performances) spread on social media |
| **2018** | IPO on the **Hong Kong Stock Exchange**; market cap exceeds HK$100 billion |
| **2020** | COVID-19 forces restaurant closures; Haidilao pivots to **delivery hot pot** kits and takeout; demonstrates resilience |
| **2021** | Over-expansion during recovery leads to financial strain; Haidilao announces closure of ~300 underperforming restaurants |
| **2022–2024** | Refocuses on profitability and operational efficiency; international expansion continues (U.S., U.K., Southeast Asia) |

## Questions

**Q1.** Haidilao's hot pot is not fundamentally different from other hot pot restaurants. Using the concept of **differentiation** from Topic 1.2, explain what makes Haidilao's competitive advantage **different from a cost advantage.** Why are customers willing to pay more at Haidilao?

**Q2.** Haidilao's core competency is **employee training and empowerment** — not cooking. Explain the difference between a **core value** and a **core competency** (from Topic 1.5) using Haidilao as an example. Which of the following is a core value and which is a core competency: (a) "customer first" and (b) "the ability to train staff to anticipate customer needs"?

**Q3.** Zhang Yong said: "Take care of your employees first; they will take care of your customers." How does this illustrate the relationship between **internal stakeholders** (employees) and **external stakeholders** (customers) from Topic 1.6? What happens to Haidilao's competitive advantage if employee satisfaction declines?

**Q4.** In 2021, Haidilao closed ~300 restaurants after over-expanding. Compare this to **VANCL's** over-expansion (see the VANCL handout) and **ofo's** over-expansion (see the ofo handout). What is **one common lesson** about the relationship between growth and sustainability?

**Q5.** Haidilao expanded internationally to the U.S., U.K., Japan, and other countries. Using **PESTEL factors** from Topic 1.3, identify at least **two factors** that might make it harder for Haidilao to succeed in a foreign market than in China. How might cultural differences affect the effectiveness of Haidilao's service model?

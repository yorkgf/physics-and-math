# 3.5 DK Coffee Lab — Financial Capital

> **Topic:** 3.5 Financial Capital

---

## Background

This case continues the story of **DK Coffee Lab**, the specialty coffee roaster and café founded in 2022 by **Daniel Kim** in Portland, Oregon. (See the 3.4 handout for the full startup history and cost structure.)

By early 2025, DK Coffee Lab operates **three café locations** and a **dedicated roasting facility**, employs 28 people, and generates approximately **$1.8 million in annual revenue**. The business is profitable — net income is roughly 6–8% of revenue (~$108,000–$144,000/year). Daniel has repaid the $20,000 family loan from the original startup and has accumulated approximately **$95,000 in retained earnings** (profits saved inside the business over three years).

Now Daniel faces a pivotal decision. He sees three growth opportunities:

1. **Café #4** — a larger location in Portland's Pearl District with a kitchen for food service. Estimated build-out cost: **$85,000**.
2. **E-commerce platform** — a direct-to-consumer website selling roasted beans online, with national shipping. Estimated development and first-year operations cost: **$60,000**.
3. **Roasting capacity expansion** — upgrading the roasting facility with a larger roaster (60 kg) to serve a growing wholesale base. Estimated cost: **$75,000**.

The total investment needed is approximately **$220,000**. Daniel has $95,000 in retained earnings and $30,000 in personal savings — a combined **$125,000**. He is **$95,000 short**. This is where the question of **external financial capital** begins.

## Questions

**1. The Bootstrapping Ceiling**
Daniel bootstrapped DK Coffee Lab from $0 to $1.8M in revenue over three years without any outside funding., explain why bootstrapping is no longer sufficient for DK Coffee Lab's next phase of growth. What is the specific financial constraint that forces Daniel to seek external capital?

**2. Break-Even Analysis**
Café #4 has monthly fixed costs of $14,200, an average variable cost of $2.15 per drink, and an average drink price of $5.75. Entrepreneurs evaluate their need for external capital by calculating how many goods or services they would need to sell in a period to break even, calculate the monthly break-even point in number of drinks. If Café #4 has a capacity of 500 drinks/day, at what percentage of capacity does the café break even? What does this tell Daniel about the risk level of this expansion?

**3. Growth Investment and Cash Flow Timing**
Established businesses seek external financial capital for new product development, replacing fixed assets, and increasing sales volume and (businesses seek external capital to manage cash flow because revenue collection can be irregular while expenses must always be paid), answer both parts: (a) Which of Daniel's three expansion projects (Café #4, e-commerce, roasting capacity) represents new product development, which represents replacing fixed assets, and which represents increasing sales volume? (b) DK Coffee Lab's wholesale clients pay on Net-30 terms, but Daniel must buy beans and pay staff immediately. If DK Coffee Lab has $45,000/month in wholesale revenue and $18,000/month in wholesale costs, how much cash is "trapped" in accounts receivable at any given time? Why does this timing mismatch create a need for external capital?

**4. Debt vs. Equity Trade-off**
Daniel must choose between Sarah Park's $100,000 equity investment (15% ownership) and the bank's $100,000 loan (10.5% interest, 5 years)., calculate the total cost of each option over five years. Under what business outcome is the **loan cheaper**, and under what outcome is the **equity cheaper**?

**5. Friends and Family Financing**
Michael Torres offers to lend Daniel $50,000 at 8% interest — 2.5 percentage points below the bank's 10.5% rate. New businesses often borrow from friends and family or use equity financing, explain why Michael's rate is below market. What risks does this arrangement create that do not exist with a bank loan?

**6. Business Maturity and Funding Options**
Established businesses with proven revenue can obtain bank loans and (corporations can obtain funds by issuing bonds or shares of stock), answer both parts: (a) Why is DK Coffee Lab eligible for an SBA bank loan in 2025 when it would not have been eligible in 2022? What specific evidence of "proven revenue and capacity to repay" does the bank require? (b) Daniel's attorney suggests issuing corporate bonds to raise the $95,000. Explain why this is or is not a practical option for a business of DK Coffee Lab's size.

**7. Lender Benefits and Risks**
Portland Community Bank is considering Daniel's $100,000 loan request., explain: (a) What financial asset does the bank receive in exchange for providing the loan, and what income does it earn? (b) What is the bank's primary risk, and how does the bank protect itself against that risk? (c) If DK Coffee Lab defaults and the bank seizes Daniel's equipment (worth $60,000), how much would the bank lose on its $100,000 loan?

**8. Investor Returns and Capital Gain**
Sarah Park invests $100,000 for 15% of DK Coffee Lab., answer: (a) What two ways can Sarah earn a return on her investment? If Daniel reinvests all profits and pays no dividends for five years, how does Sarah earn a return? (b) If DK Coffee Lab's value grows from $667,000 to $2,000,000 over five years, what is Sarah's capital gain? What is her approximate annual rate of return, calculated (total dollars gained ÷ price of the asset)?

**9. Risk Tolerance**
Sarah Park expects a 15–30% annual return on her $100,000 investment. Portland Community Bank expects a 10.5% annual return on its $100,000 loan. Michael Torres expects only 8%. Lenders and investors vary in their risk tolerance; they expect a higher rate of return when they invest in riskier assets, explain why these three funders have different expected returns. What does this reveal about the relationship between risk and return?

**10. The Business Plan and Pitch**
Daniel must prepare a pitch to convince both the bank and Sarah to fund his expansion. Lenders and investors require a business plan that includes value proposition, market research, marketing strategy, and financial projections and (lenders and investors may consider the qualifications of the leadership team), answer: (a) What specific financial projections must Daniel include to justify a $95,000 funding request? (b) How does Daniel's team (see the Team Qualifications table) strengthen his pitch to Sarah, who is evaluating the business as an equity investor rather than a lender?

**11. Valuation and Product-Market Fit**
Sarah values DK Coffee Lab at $667,000 (based on her $100K for 15% offer). Daniel believes the company is worth $1,000,000. Lenders and investors rely on financial reports and external industry data to estimate business valuation and (a funding request is more likely to succeed when the business provides evidence of product-market fit), answer: (a) What specific evidence from DK Coffee Lab's track record demonstrates product-market fit? (b) If Daniel negotiates a $1,000,000 valuation, how much equity would Sarah's $100,000 buy? (c) How do comparable transactions in the specialty coffee industry support or challenge Daniel's $1,000,000 valuation claim?

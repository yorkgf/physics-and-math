# 3.5 DK Coffee Lab — Financial Capital

> **Topic 3.5 Financial Capital** · Read before class. Be ready to discuss.

---

## Background

This case continues the story of **DK Coffee Lab**, the specialty coffee roaster and cafe founded in 2022 by **Daniel Kim** in Portland, Oregon. (See the 3.4 handout for the full startup history and cost structure.)

By early 2025, DK Coffee Lab operates **three cafe locations** and a **dedicated roasting facility**, employs 28 people, and generates approximately **$1.8 million in annual revenue**. The business is profitable — net income is roughly 6–8% of revenue (~$108,000–$144,000/year). Daniel has repaid the $20,000 family loan from the original startup and has accumulated approximately **$95,000 in retained earnings** (profits saved inside the business over three years).

Daniel sees three growth opportunities:

1. **Cafe #4** — a larger location in Portland's Pearl District with a kitchen for food service. Estimated build-out cost: **$85,000**.
2. **E-commerce platform** — a direct-to-consumer website selling roasted beans online, with national shipping. Estimated development and first-year operations cost: **$60,000**.
3. **Roasting capacity expansion** — upgrading the roasting facility with a larger roaster (60 kg) to serve a growing wholesale base. Estimated cost: **$75,000**.

The total investment needed is approximately **$220,000**. Daniel has $95,000 in retained earnings and $30,000 in personal savings — a combined **$125,000**. He is **$95,000 short**.

## Questions

**1. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

Daniel bootstrapped DK Coffee Lab from $0 to $1.8M in revenue over three years without outside funding.

(A) Identify the specific financial constraint that prevents Daniel from funding the $220,000 expansion from internal resources alone.

(B) Explain why the growth strategies available to Daniel in 2025 differ from the growth strategies he used in 2022–2024.

(C) Calculate how much additional capital Daniel needs beyond his available funds, and identify at least two external sources he could approach.

---

**2. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

Cafe #4 has monthly fixed costs of $14,200, an average variable cost of $2.15 per drink, and an average drink price of $5.75. The cafe has a capacity of 500 drinks/day.

(A) Calculate how many drinks Cafe #4 must sell per month to cover all of its costs. Show your work.

(B) Calculate what percentage of the cafe's capacity this volume represents.

(C) Explain what your answer in part B tells Daniel about the risk level of opening Cafe #4.

---

**3. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

DK Coffee Lab's wholesale clients pay on Net-30 terms, but Daniel must buy beans and pay staff immediately. Wholesale revenue is $45,000/month and wholesale costs are $18,000/month.

(A) Calculate how much cash is tied up in unpaid wholesale invoices at any given time.

(B) Explain why this timing difference between receiving cash and paying expenses creates a need for external capital.

(C) Describe one specific action Daniel could take to reduce the cash flow gap caused by Net-30 payment terms.

---

**4. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

Daniel must choose between Sarah Park's $100,000 equity investment (15% ownership) and the bank's $100,000 loan (10.5% interest, 5-year term, ~$2,150/month payment, ~$29,000 total interest).

(A) Calculate the total dollar cost of the bank loan over five years.

(B) Under what business outcome would the loan cost Daniel less than giving up 15% equity? Under what outcome would the equity cost less?

(C) Explain why Daniel might prefer the loan even though it requires monthly payments, while the equity investment does not.

---

**5. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

Sarah Park expects a 15–30% annual return on her $100,000 investment. Portland Community Bank expects a 10.5% annual return on its $100,000 loan. Michael Torres expects 8%.

(A) Identify which of the three funders bears the most risk and which bears the least.

(B) Explain why the funder who bears the most risk expects the highest return.

(C) If DK Coffee Lab's value grows from $667,000 to $2,000,000 over five years, calculate Sarah's capital gain on her $100,000 investment for 15% ownership.

# 3.4 DK Coffee Lab — Business Expenses

> **Topic:** 3.4 Business Expenses
> **Company:** DK Coffee Lab

---

## Background

**DK Coffee Lab** is a specialty coffee roaster and café founded in 2022 in Portland, Oregon, by **Daniel Kim**, a former software engineer who left the tech industry to pursue his passion for specialty coffee. The company operates two business lines: **retail cafés** (selling brewed drinks and pastries directly to consumers) and a **wholesale roasting operation** (selling freshly roasted beans to local restaurants, offices, and grocery stores). As of 2025, DK Coffee Lab operates **three café locations** in Portland and a dedicated **roasting facility** that supplies beans to over 40 wholesale accounts.

Daniel started DK Coffee Lab with **$120,000** in personal savings and a small loan from a family member. He did not take outside investment — every dollar of startup funding came from his own pocket or close relationships. The first café opened in a 900-square-foot storefront in Portland's Alberta Arts District after five months of planning, legal setup, and build-out.

DK Coffee Lab's competitive advantage is **direct-trade sourcing**: Daniel travels to coffee farms in Colombia, Ethiopia, and Guatemala twice a year to personally select beans, negotiating directly with farmers rather than going through commodity brokers. This sourcing model produces higher-quality coffee but creates **higher and more variable direct costs** than competitors who buy commodity-grade beans on the open market.

As of 2025, DK Coffee Lab generates approximately **$1.8 million in annual revenue** across its three cafés and wholesale accounts. The company employs **28 people** — baristas, a head roaster, a small operations team, and Daniel himself. The business is **profitable but thin-margin**: net income is approximately 6–8% of revenue, which is typical for the specialty coffee industry.

## Questions

**1. Classifying Startup Costs**
Daniel spent $115,700 to launch DK Coffee Lab., classify each of the following as either a **one-time expenditure** or an **initial expense**: (a) the $3,500 attorney fee, (b) the $10,800 in green coffee beans, (c) the $28,000 roaster, and (d) the $4,200 in launch marketing. Why does the distinction matter for financial planning?

**2. Total Startup Capital**
Daniel funded his $115,700 startup with $100,000 in personal savings and a $20,000 family loan. Startup costs are one-time expenditures plus initial expenses incurred while establishing the business, calculate whether Daniel had enough startup capital. What happens if Café #1's first three months generate only $15,000/month in revenue while fixed costs are $9,570/month and variable costs are ~$8,000/month?

**3. Direct vs. Indirect Costs**
DK Coffee Lab's wholesale roasting operation buys green coffee beans, roasts them, and sells packaged bags to restaurants and grocery stores. Direct costs are tied to production of specific goods; indirect costs are costs of running the business, classify each of the following: (a) green coffee beans, (b) the roaster operator's salary, (c) the accounting software subscription, and (d) the roasting facility rent. Explain why the classification of roasting facility rent as a "direct cost" might surprise some students.

**4. Fixed vs. Variable**
During a slow month, DK Coffee Lab's Café #1 serves 300 drinks/day instead of the usual 500. Fixed expenses do not change with production levels; variable expenses increase as production increases, explain which of the following costs decrease and which stay the same: (a) rent, (b) green coffee beans, (c) barista wages (hourly), (d) insurance, and (e) cups and lids. What is the strategic implication of having high fixed costs during a slow month?

**5. COGS vs. Cost of Sales**
DK Coffee Lab operates two business lines: the **roasting operation** (selling packaged beans — a good) and the **cafés** (serving drinks — a service)., explain why the direct costs of the roasting operation are called **COGS** while the direct costs of the cafés are called **cost of sales**. What is the key difference between goods and services that drives this distinction?

**6. Insurance and Risk**
Daniel carries $17,400/year in insurance across five policies. He considered dropping product liability insurance ($2,400/year) to save money. Businesses buy insurance to protect against financial losses; businesses vary in their willingness to accept risk and/or pay for insurance, evaluate Daniel's decision to keep the policy. If DK Coffee Lab served 500 customers/day across three cafés, and one customer filed a food-contamination lawsuit costing $100,000, how many years of product liability premiums would it take to equal the cost of that single lawsuit?

**7. Operating Expenses in Practice**
DK Coffee Lab's company-wide operating expenses total approximately $51,250/month. Businesses face recurring indirect costs called operating expenses, which are typically fixed and include occupancy expenses, salaries, marketing, supplies, utilities, maintenance, and insurance, identify which of DK Coffee Lab's operating expenses from the table above are fixed and which are semi-variable. If Daniel's busiest café generates $45,000/month in revenue while his slowest generates only $22,000/month, which operating expenses does each café share equally, and what does this mean for the profitability of the slower location?

# 2.4 Dyson — Case Handout

> **Topic 2.4 Product** · Read before class. Be ready to discuss.

---

## Background

Dyson Ltd. is a British technology company founded by James Dyson in 1993. The company produces vacuum cleaners, hand dryers, hair care products, and air treatment devices. As of 2024, Dyson generates over $7 billion in annual revenue and sells products in more than 80 countries. James Dyson ranks 60th on the Bloomberg Billionaires Index with a net worth of $26.2 billion.

James Dyson was born in 1947 in Cromer, Norfolk, England. He studied at the Byam Shaw School of Art and then the Royal College of Art (RCA). He collaborated with entrepreneur Jeremy Fry on projects like the Dyson Sea Truck, an amphibious landing craft. Before pursuing the vacuum cleaner, he invented the **Ballbarrow** — a wheelbarrow with a molded plastic trough and a ball instead of a wheel. Despite initial UK success, the Ballbarrow design was replicated by US competitors, teaching Dyson about protecting innovation.

## Key Events

| Year | Event |
|------|-------|
| ~1975 | Invents the **Ballbarrow** (ball-wheelbarrow) while renovating his farmhouse; achieves UK success but loses to US copycats |
| 1978 | Disassembles his Hoover vacuum cleaner; identifies root cause: **clogged pores and reduced suction** from accumulated dust in the bag |
| 1978 | Observes a sawmill's **30-foot conical centrifuge** (dust removal system); recognizes the potential to miniaturize cyclone technology for a vacuum |
| 1979 | Begins building prototypes of a bagless vacuum in a barn behind his house |
| 1979-1984 | Builds **5,127 prototypes** over five years, enduring financial strain; wife supports the family by teaching art |
| 1984 | Perfects the **Dual Cyclone** technology (DC1 prototype) but **every domestic manufacturer refuses to license it** — they profit from selling replacement bags |
| ~1985 | Settles patent disputes with Amway over Dual Cyclone technology |
| 1986 | Secures a licensing deal with **Apex**, a Japanese manufacturer; launches the **G-Force** vacuum in Japan at ~$2,000 |
| 1986-1993 | G-Force wins design awards in Japan and Europe; validates that customers will pay a premium for bagless technology |
| 1993 | Founds **Dyson Appliances Limited** in the UK; launches the **DC01** with the campaign **"Say goodbye to the bag"** |
| 1995 | DC01 becomes the **#1 selling vacuum** in the UK, outselling established brands despite costing twice as much |
| 1996-2001 | Licenses technology to **Phantom Technologies** in North America; later takes direct control of US operations |
| ~2000 | Appoints **Martin McCord** as CEO so Dyson can refocus on design and engineering; McCord secures deals with **Best Buy, Target, Home Depot** |
| ~2000 | Launches the **Contra Rotator** washing machine (£1,000) — two drums rotating in opposite directions; positive reviews but commercial failure; discontinued after a few years |
| 2002 | Relocates manufacturing from Malmesbury, UK to **Malaysia** — frees up resources for R&D |
| 2006 | Launches the **Airblade** hand dryer — enters a completely new product category |
| 2016 | Launches the **Supersonic** hair dryer at $400 |
| 2018 | Launches the **Airwrap** multi-styler — becomes a social media phenomenon |
| 2019 | Attempts an **electric car project** — invests $2.5 billion but ultimately cancels |
| 2020 | Collaborates with **TTP** to develop ventilators during COVID-19 pandemic |
| 2023 | Launches the **Airstrait** wet-to-dry straightener; continues expanding the hair care portfolio |
| Ongoing | Dyson invests **15-20% of annual revenue** in R&D, with research labs in the UK, Singapore, and the US; the company holds approximately **6,000 granted and pending patents** |

## Questions

**1. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

Dyson built 5,127 prototypes before launching the G-Force in Japan at ~$2,000.

(A) Identify what made the G-Force launch different from the 5,127 prototypes that came before it.

(B) Describe what Dyson learned from selling the G-Force in Japan before launching the DC01 in the UK.

(C) Explain why Dyson launched in Japan first rather than in his home market of the UK.

---

**2. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

When Dyson approached vacuum manufacturers with his working prototype, every major brand refused to license it — they profited from selling replacement bags.

(A) Identify the reason vacuum manufacturers refused to license Dyson's bagless technology.

(B) Explain why manufacturers chose to protect their existing bag revenue rather than adopt a better product.

(C) Explain what this rejection reveals about how established companies evaluate new product ideas.

---

**3. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

Dyson vacuums sell for 2-3x the price of competitors. Some customers say, "I know I'm paying for the brand."

(A) Identify what allows Dyson to charge a price significantly higher than competitors.

(B) Explain what a customer means when they say "I know I'm paying for the brand."

(C) Evaluate whether this statement is a criticism or a sign of brand strength. Support your evaluation with evidence from the case.

---

**4. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

Dyson's vacuum line has been on the market since 1993 and faces established competitors. The Airwrap launched in 2018 and became a social media phenomenon. Dyson invests 15-20% of annual revenue in R&D.

(A) Describe the current market position of Dyson's vacuum line compared to its hair care products.

(B) Explain how Dyson's R&D investment strategy supports products at different stages of their life cycle.

(C) Recommend what Dyson should prioritize for its vacuum line versus its hair care line over the next five years. Support your recommendation with evidence from the case.

---

**5. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

Dyson launched the Contra Rotator washing machine (~2000) at £1,000 with positive reviews, but it was a commercial failure and was discontinued. The Airblade hand dryer (2006) entered a completely new product category and succeeded.

(A) Identify one reason the Contra Rotator failed despite positive reviews.

(B) Explain why the Airblade succeeded in a new product category while the Contra Rotator failed in an established one.

(C) Recommend what Dyson should consider before entering any new product category in the future. Support your recommendation with evidence from the case.

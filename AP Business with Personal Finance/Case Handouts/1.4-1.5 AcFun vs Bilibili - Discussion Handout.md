# AcFun vs Bilibili — Classroom Discussion

> **Chapters:** 1.4 How Business Ideas Originate + 1.5 Vision

---

## Case Background

AcFun and Bilibili are two Chinese video-sharing websites that both pioneered the **danmaku (弹幕)** comment system — where viewer comments scroll across the screen in real time, creating a shared viewing experience. Both started in the ACG (Anime, Comics, Games) subculture. Both attracted passionate young communities.

Yet today, **Bilibili has over 300 million monthly active users and a market capitalization in the billions**, while **AcFun is a niche platform with a fraction of that audience**, having changed ownership multiple times and nearly shut down.

**How did two platforms with nearly identical starting points end up so far apart?**

---

## Company Profiles

### AcFun

| | Details |
|---|---|
| **Founded** | 2007 |
| **Founder** | Xilin (西林), a college student |
| **Original concept** | China's first video platform with danmaku comments, modeled after Japan's Niconico |
| **Early community** | ACG enthusiasts — China's earliest danmaku users |
| **Key problems** | Frequent leadership changes, server crashes, funding shortages, management instability |
| **Major turning point** | Acquired by Kuaishou in 2018 as a complementary content platform |
| **Current status** | Still operating under Kuaishou, but with a much smaller user base; loyal niche community |

### Bilibili

| | Details |
|---|---|
| **Founded** | 2009 |
| **Founder** | Xu Yi (徐逸, username "⑨bishi"), a former AcFun user |
| **Original concept** | A more stable alternative to AcFun — same danmaku system, but with better servers and fewer crashes |
| **Early community** | ACG enthusiasts who left AcFun due to technical problems |
| **Key milestones** | 2014: Chen Rui became CEO, brought professional management and investment. 2018: IPO on NASDAQ. 2021: Secondary listing on Hong Kong Stock Exchange. |
| **Major turning point** | Expanded from ACG-only to mainstream content (education, science, lifestyle, vlogs, documentaries) while keeping its community culture |
| **Current status** | 300M+ monthly active users; one of China's top video platforms; diversified revenue (advertising, gaming, e-commerce, live streaming, premium memberships) |

## Key Events Timeline

| Year | AcFun | Bilibili |
|---|---|---|
| **2007** | Founded — China's first danmaku video site | — |
| **2009** | Experiencing server crashes and management issues | Founded by Xu Yi as a "more stable AcFun" |
| **2010–2012** | Struggles with funding; multiple leadership changes | Growing ACG community; improving technology |
| **2014** | Losing users to Bilibili | Chen Rui becomes CEO → professional management, outside investment |
| **2016–2017** | Near-shutdown; servers go offline for extended periods | Expanding content categories beyond ACG |
| **2018** | Acquired by Kuaishou | IPO on NASDAQ — raised $483 million |
| **2019–2020** | Operating under Kuaishou with limited resources | Launched premium membership; expanded into e-commerce and education |
| **2021** | Small but loyal community; limited growth | Secondary listing on Hong Kong Stock Exchange; 200M+ MAU |
| **2022–present** | Niche platform; content focused on ACG and Kuaishou ecosystem | 300M+ MAU; mainstream video platform competing with Douyin and iQiyi |

## Discussion Questions

### Question 1 — Sources and Gaps (1.4)

**(a)** As a former AcFun user, what **gap** did Xu Yi identify? Why were AcFun's own users the first to see this gap?

**(b)** Does Bilibili's founding fit **gap**, **opportunity**, or **existing business** — or more than one source? Explain.

> **Key terms:** gap, opportunity, existing business, unmet need, replication

### Question 2 — Risk and Return (1.4)

Compare the risk–return tradeoff faced by each founder:

| | Xu Yi (starting Bilibili in 2009) | AcFun's leadership (accepting Kuaishou's acquisition in 2018) |
|---|---|---|
| **Investment** | Limited resources, personal time | Giving up control of the company |
| **Potential return** | If successful, could become a mainstream platform | Lifeline funding, but loss of independence |
| **Risk** | Could fail just like AcFun | Company could be marginalized |

Which decision involved higher risk? Which had higher potential return? Justify using the **risk–return tradeoff** framework.

> **Key terms:** risk, return, risk–return tradeoff, viability

### Question 4 — The "Money Test" for Core Values (1.5)

In 2019, Bilibili introduced an **"entrance exam"** — new users must pass a test on ACG culture, community rules, and danmaku etiquette before becoming full members.

**(a)** What **core values** does this entrance exam reflect?

**(b)** Does this exam have a **business cost**? (Hint: how does it affect user growth?) If Bilibili ever **removed this exam** to chase growth, what would that mean under the 1.5 framework?

**(c)** Compare with AcFun: did AcFun have a similar test? Did AcFun's value of **"community culture"** survive commercial pressure? Use Seesaw Coffee as a parallel.

> **Core question:** "A value the business abandons the first time money appears was a decoration" — how does this apply to AcFun?

### Question 6 — Vision's "Survival Test" (1.4 + 1.5 Combined)

Seesaw Coffee's vision was "Bring specialty coffee into everyone's daily life," but when the 9.9-yuan price war arrived, it **abandoned craft** and eventually went bankrupt.

AcFun's vision (or implied vision) was "The home of ACG culture," but under funding pressure, leadership turmoil, and Bilibili's competition, this vision **failed to guide key decisions**.

**(a)** Using the Seesaw framework, analyze AcFun: did AcFun's vision/values **survive contact with revenue**? Give specific examples.

**(b)** Compare with Bilibili: when Bilibili faced similar pressures (expanding beyond ACG, accepting ads, going public), were its core values sacrificed — or did they survive in a different form?

**(c)** What makes the difference between a vision that **guides real decisions** and one that **becomes decoration**? What do you conclude from the AcFun vs Bilibili story?

> **Core framework:** "A value abandoned at the first price = decoration" vs "A value that survived every revenue test = real value"

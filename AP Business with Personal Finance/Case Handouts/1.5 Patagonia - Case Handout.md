# Patagonia — Case Handout

> **Topic 1.5 Vision** · Read before class. Be ready to discuss.

---

## Background

Patagonia is an American outdoor clothing and gear company founded in 1973 by Yvon Chouinard in Ventura, California. Chouinard was a lifelong climber and environmentalist who started by forging his own climbing pitons. The company sells outdoor clothing, climbing gear, camping equipment, and food products.

Patagonia's stated vision is: "We're in business to save our home planet." The company has made several decisions that prioritized environmental goals over short-term profit:

- In 1996, Patagonia switched 100% of its cotton line to organic cotton. This increased raw material costs by an estimated 50%, and the company had to discontinue one-third of its product line because organic cotton suppliers could not produce certain weaves.
- In 2011, Patagonia ran a full-page Black Friday ad in the New York Times: "Don't Buy This Jacket" — telling consumers to buy less and reduce waste. Despite the anti-consumption message, revenue grew approximately 30% the following year (from ~$540M to ~$700M).
- In 2012, the company launched the Worn Wear program, encouraging customers to repair and reuse clothing instead of buying new. Patagonia offers free lifetime repairs and operates the largest garment repair facility in North America.
- In 2013, Patagonia filed as a Benefit Corporation, legally committing to consider environmental and social impact alongside profit.

In September 2022, Chouinard transferred ownership: 98% of shares to Holdfast Collective (a nonprofit fighting climate change), 2% to a trust ensuring mission enforcement. All future profit goes to environmental causes. Chouinard's public statement: "Earth is now our only shareholder."

## Questions

**1. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

Patagonia's stated purpose is: "We're in business to save our home planet."

(A) Describe what this statement tells you about what Patagonia's leadership considers most important.

(B) Explain how this statement differs from a statement that describes what a company sells and how it makes money.

(C) Evaluate whether Patagonia's actions since 1996 are consistent with this stated purpose. Use specific evidence from the scenario.

**2. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

Patagonia switched to organic cotton in 1996, increasing raw material costs by approximately 50% and discontinuing one-third of its product line.

(A) Identify the short-term financial cost of this decision.

(B) Explain why Patagonia's leadership chose to accept this cost.

(C) Predict what would have happened to Patagonia's brand if the company had continued using conventional cotton to keep costs low.

---

**3. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

In 2011, Patagonia ran a Black Friday ad saying "Don't Buy This Jacket." Despite the anti-consumption message, revenue grew approximately 30% the following year.

(A) Identify the message Patagonia was sending to consumers with this ad.

(B) Explain why the ad may have actually increased sales despite telling consumers not to buy.

(C) Describe what this outcome suggests about the relationship between brand values and customer loyalty.

**4. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

(A) Identify what changed about Patagonia's ownership structure after the transfer.

(B) Explain what stayed the same about how the company operates after the transfer.

(C) Evaluate whether this ownership transfer makes Patagonia more or less likely to stay true to its stated purpose over the long term.

**5. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

Patagonia operates the Worn Wear program, which encourages customers to repair clothing instead of buying new items. The company offers free lifetime repairs.

(B) Explain how a program that discourages new purchases could still benefit Patagonia's business.

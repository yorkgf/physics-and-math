# 3.7.D — The Balance Sheet and Net Worth, read from real 10-Ks

**Purpose:** every knowledge point of lesson 3.7 (*The Balance Sheet and Net Worth*), illustrated with the **actual consolidated balance sheets** of eight public companies, one question sheet per knowledge point. Built the same way as the 3.6.D income-statement package: statements pulled from SEC EDGAR, question sheets in the house style, student + teacher PDFs ready to print.

**Eight real statements, three industries, four KP pairings** — all figures as reported, all verified line-by-line against the filings.

---

## 1. The knowledge points, and where each one is taught

| KP | Lesson LO | Knowledge point | Illustrated by | Sheet |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **KP1** | 3.7.A.1 | A balance sheet compares **assets** to **liabilities + owners' equity** — the net worth of the business to its owners — **at a specific point in time**; **A = L + OE** is the balance sheet (fundamental accounting) equation, and it is a *checking mechanism* | Apple vs Delta — same equation, opposite composition (a cash-rich tech company vs a leveraged airline) | KP1 |
| **KP2** | 3.7.A.2 | Assets are grouped **by liquidity** into **current** (cash, short-term investments, accounts receivable, inventory), **long-term** (fixed assets, long-term investments) and **intangible** (patents, brand names, trademarks); liquidity = ease of conversion to cash | Walmart (inventory-heavy retailer) vs Delta (aircraft-heavy airline) vs Coca-Cola (trademark + goodwill heavy) | KP2 |
| **KP3** | 3.7.A.3 | Liabilities are grouped **by due date**: **current** (AP, short-term debt, current portion of long-term debt, accrued expenses) vs **long-term** (mortgages, bank loans, long-term bonds) — one loan straddles the one-year line | Apple (term debt split) · Delta + United (current maturities, air traffic liability) · Nike (current portion 0 → 2,000) | KP3 |
| **KP4** | 3.7.A.4 | **Owners' equity = assets − liabilities** = net worth of the business; usually **stock + retained earnings** (cumulative profits not paid as dividends) | Apple (accumulated deficit → retained earnings) · Coca-Cola (reinvested earnings) · Nike (negative retained earnings) · McDonald's (negative *total* equity) | KP4 |
| **KP5** | 3.7.A.5 | A balance sheet shows **comparable data from a previous year/quarter** — one number is a fact, two are a trend | every statement prints two dates; KP5-7 sheet uses both years for all four companies | KP5-7 |
| **KP6** | 3.7.B.1/B.2 | Internal and external stakeholders read it at a point in time; it indicates **positive net worth**, **sufficient working capital** (current assets ≥ current liabilities), and **debt comparable to similar businesses** | Walmart vs Target (retail pair) · Delta vs United (airline pair) | KP5-7 |
| **KP7** | 3.7.B.3 | Without sufficient current assets, owners may **shut down or pursue bankruptcy** — a *legal process* to liquidate assets, eliminate or repay debts, and shut down **or reorganize under legal supervision** | the same four companies — all run *negative* working capital by design; the sheet turns that into the 3.7.B.3 nuance | KP5-7 |
| **KP8** | 3.7.C.1-C.3 | **Personal net worth** is a **household** figure (may include more than one individual): all assets — savings and investments, property, personal possessions — minus all liabilities; **lenders** require it in loan applications; **planners** use it for retirement sufficiency | No 10-K exists for a household (only companies file with the SEC) — the sheet bridges via the Chen household and Daniel Kim's business-equity line from the 3.7 case handout | KP8 |

## 4. Board-ready: the eight knowledge points in one table

| # | Point | One-line test |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Balance sheet = assets vs liabilities + equity **at a date** | "as of" not "for the year ended" |
| 2 | **A = L + OE** — the checking mechanism | both routes give the same number |
| 3 | Assets grouped **by liquidity**: current → long-term → intangible | cash first, patents last |
| 4 | Liabilities grouped **by due date**: current → long-term | one loan, two lines |
| 5 | Equity = **A − L** = net worth = stock + retained earnings | residual, not cash |
| 6 | Two dates = **comparable data** = a trend | one number is a fact |
| 7 | Three indications: **positive net worth · sufficient working capital · comparable debt** | current vs current; ratio vs peers |
| 8 | Net worth is a **household** number; lenders and planners use it | assets − liabilities, all of them |

## 5. Five-minute classroom sequence

1. **KP1, Apple vs Delta** — both statements balance to the dollar; one has 292,455 of debt, the other 60,464. Same equation, different risk. What date does each heading carry?
2. **KP2, the three-way mix** — project the current-asset share: Walmart 29.8%, Delta 13.5%, Coca-Cola 29.6%. Then PP&E: 47.8% / 48.9% / 9.2%. What business is each?
3. **KP3, Nike's 0 → 2,000** — nothing was borrowed; the calendar moved a loan across the one-year line. One loan, two lines.
4. **KP4, McDonald's −1,791** — negative equity, and nowhere near bankruptcy. Where did the equity go? (Treasury stock: 79,316 of buybacks.)
5. **KP5-7, four negative working capitals** — WMT −22,595, TGT −1,225, DAL −16,656, UAL −9,276. All four fail the CED's test; none is failing. Close: the test is about *access to current assets*, and retailers/airlines collect cash before they pay.

## ANSWERS (teacher · students stop here)

### A (8)
**A1.**

| Company | Assets − liabilities | Printed equity | Match? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apple | 404,548 − 292,455 = **112,093** | 112,093 | ✓ |
| Coca-Cola | 104,816 − 70,541 = **34,275** | 34,275 | ✓ |
| Nike | 38,410 − 23,545 = **14,865** | 14,865 | ✓ |
| McDonald's | 59,515 − 61,306 = **(1,791)** | (1,791) | ✓ (the seven components as printed sum to 61,307 — a one-unit rounding difference inside the filing; either figure returns the printed equity within rounding) |

**A2.** Liabilities exceed assets — owners would have nothing left if everything were sold at book value and all debts paid. It does **not** mean the company is bankrupt or insolvent: McDonald's still generated billions in cash flow in FY2025, and the negative figure comes from buybacks, not losses. (Negative equity is a *capital-structure choice*, not a verdict on operations.)

**A3.** **Owners' equity = total assets − total liabilities** (equivalently, the residual: A − L).

### B (10)
**B1.** Stock component: Apple — "common stock and additional paid-in capital" 104,815; Coca-Cola — common stock 1,760 + capital surplus 20,581; Nike — Class B common stock 3 + capital in excess of stated value 15,158; McDonald's — common stock 17 + additional paid-in capital 9,641. Retained earnings component: Apple "retained earnings/(accumulated deficit)" 13,700; Coca-Cola "**reinvested earnings**" 80,382; Nike "retained earnings (deficit)" (155); McDonald's "retained earnings" 70,282.

**B2.** Retained earnings = the business's **cumulative** net profits that have **not** been paid out to shareholders as **dividends** — every year's profit adds, every dividend subtracts.

**B3.** **Buybacks** (share repurchases). Coca-Cola's treasury stock grew (55,916 → 56,423) as it repurchased shares; Nike's negative retained earnings reflect years of repurchases and dividends exceeding the profits left inside the company. Profits fund the buybacks; the buybacks drain retained earnings.

**B4.** Apple's cumulative retained profits turned positive again — the company earned more than it returned to shareholders. Two transactions move the line: **net income** (adds) and **dividends paid / share repurchases charged to retained earnings** (subtract).

### C (12)
**C1.** 1 — **share buybacks** (treasury stock of 79,316 exceeds equity). 2 — **share buybacks** again, charged against retained earnings while paid-in capital stays positive. 3 — **profits retained** (net income exceeding dividends and repurchases over the year).

**C2.** Treasury stock = **the company's own shares that it has bought back** from the market. It sits inside equity as a subtraction because equity measures *outsiders'* claims: once the company buys its own shares, those shares no longer belong to outside owners, so the amount spent is deducted from total equity.

**C3.** **McDonald's** — negative equity of (1,791). Before predicting failure you would check: cash flow from operations, whether the debt is covered by earnings, the maturity schedule (39,973 of long-term debt is not due tomorrow), and whether the negative equity came from buybacks (a choice) rather than accumulated losses (a disease). Also note Nike's *retained earnings* are negative but its *total* equity is positive (14,865) — distinguish the line from the total.

### D (10)
**D1.** Retained earnings is an **accounting total of past profits**, not a pile of cash — the cash was already spent on operations, capex, dividends and buybacks, or is sitting in other assets (Coca-Cola's equity-method investments of 20,235 alone exceed its cash).

**D2.**

| Company | Equity ÷ assets |
|---|---|
| Apple | **27.7%** (112,093 / 404,548) |
| Coca-Cola | **32.7%** (34,275 / 104,816) |
| Nike | **38.7%** (14,865 / 38,410) |
| McDonald's | **−3.0%** ((1,791) / 59,515) |

**D3.** Any evidence that obligations are still covered: McDonald's holds 774 of cash plus 2,820 of investments in affiliates and earns royalty income from franchised restaurants (stable, contract-based cash flow); its 39,973 of long-term debt matures over years, not weeks; and negative equity from buybacks signals a company returning cash to owners, which is the opposite of distress. The key distinction for students: **negative equity ≠ negative cash flow**.

### Common errors
- Equating retained earnings with cash (the single most common error on this topic)
- Treating negative equity as automatic bankruptcy (3.7.B.3 requires *inability to fund operations*, not a negative subtotal)
- Counting treasury stock as an asset ("the company owns its own shares") — it is a *contra-equity* subtraction
- Forgetting Coca-Cola's label: "reinvested earnings" IS retained earnings

## ANSWERS (teacher · students stop here)

### A (10)
**A1.**

| | Apple | Nike |
|---|---|---|
| Total debt | 9,906 + 68,208 = **78,114** | 2,000 + 5,942 = **7,942** |
| Current portion | **9,906** | **2,000** |
| Current share | **12.7%** | **25.2%** |

**A2.** A loan is one instrument, but the balance sheet splits it **by due date**: the principal contractually payable within twelve months is a *current* liability; the remainder is *long-term*. Nike's line moving 0 → 2,000 means a slice of its long-term debt has crossed the one-year line — nothing was borrowed; the calendar moved. (Also note Apple's term debt line appears in both the current and non-current blocks for exactly this reason.)

**A3.** **Apple** — 9,906 of current term debt (plus another 1,997 of commercial paper), against United 4,426, Nike 2,000 and Delta 1,605. In *relative* terms the ranking flips: Nike has the largest current slice at 25.2% of its total debt.

**A4.** Fair only after "**as a share of total assets**" (or of equity): United's total assets are 76,448 vs Delta's 81,317, so 24,988 vs 14,112 is 32.7% vs 17.4% of assets — United is more leveraged, but the raw dollar gap overstates it because the airlines are similar in size. "Comparable" means *relative*, not absolute.

### B (10)
**B1.** No — the cash is collected but the flight is **not yet delivered**, so it is **unearned (deferred) revenue: a current liability**. It sits on the balance sheet until the flight operates, then moves to the income statement as revenue.

**B2.** Because the obligation will be settled **within one year** — the customer flies within months, and tickets sold but unused are typically redeemed or refunded inside twelve months. Anything not expected to convert within a year would be long-term.

**B3.** Any of: AppleCare / extended warranty, iCloud storage, App Store pre-orders, gift cards not yet redeemed — cash received before the service is provided.

**B4.** Miles expected to be redeemed **within twelve months** are a current liability (3,721); miles expected to be redeemed **beyond** a year are non-current (4,056). Same loyalty program, split by the one-year line — exactly like a loan.

### C (10)
**C1.** **Lenders** (and suppliers): the ordering shows what must be paid first, which is the input to the 3.7.B.2 working-capital test — can current assets cover current liabilities?

**C2.** Accrued salaries → accounts payable → current portion of long-term debt → long-term debt. (Wages are typically paid within days; supplier invoices within weeks-to-months; the loan slice within the year; the long-term remainder beyond it. Accept any order defended by due date.)

**C3.** That Delta must settle 27,624 within twelve months while holding only 10,968 of current assets — the airline depends on **next year's ticket sales and cash collections** (plus refinancing) to meet obligations that are already on the books. The ordering, not just the totals, is the warning.

**C4.** Because a manufacturer buys materials and components **on credit from many suppliers** and pays on invoice terms (e.g. 30–60 days) — the balance owed at any date is large and continuously revolving. Apple's 82,252 of payables is the accumulation of thousands of unpaid supplier invoices at the statement date.

### D (10)
**D1.** (i) **Receivable = asset** (customers owe Apple — arrow points in); **payable = liability** (Apple owes suppliers — arrow points out). (ii) Air traffic liability = **liability** (Delta owes flights to customers who already paid); Delta's accounts receivable = **asset** (other parties owe Delta money, e.g. corporate accounts).

**D2.** Accounts payable is money **the company owes its suppliers**; money customers owe the company is accounts **receivable**.

**D3.** Apple: current assets just cover current liabilities (a thin but positive cushion — working capital of about 3,942), so the coming year is funded from operations with little slack. Delta: current liabilities are 2.5× current assets, so the next twelve months depend on collecting this year's air traffic liability (7,157) and loyalty revenue (4,876) — cash that arrives *as the service is delivered* — and on rolling over or repaying 1,605 of current debt.

### Common errors
- Counting only the current portion as "the debt" (the non-current block is the same loans, further out)
- Treating unearned revenue as revenue because cash has arrived
- Comparing debt in dollars across companies of different size without scaling by assets or equity
- Forgetting that the same instrument straddles the one-year line — "one loan, two lines" is the knowledge point

## ANSWERS (teacher · students stop here)

### A (10)
**A1.**

| Company · year | Liabilities + equity | Total assets | Balances? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apple FY2026 | 292,455 + 112,093 = **404,548** | 404,548 | ✓ |
| Apple FY2025 | 285,508 + 73,733 = **359,241** | 359,241 | ✓ |
| Delta FY2025 | 60,464 + 20,853 = **81,317** | 81,317 | ✓ |
| Delta FY2024 | 60,079 + 15,293 = **75,372** | 75,372 | ✓ |

(Total liabilities = current + non-current: Apple FY2026 = 169,915 + 122,540 = 292,455; Delta FY2025 = 27,624 + 32,840 = 60,464.)

**A2.** Apple FY2026: 404,548 − 292,455 = **112,093** ✓. Delta FY2025: 81,317 − 60,464 = **20,853** ✓. Same answer by both routes — that is the equation working as a *checking mechanism*, not just a formula.

**A3.** Both companies grew their **net worth out of profits** (equity is where retained earnings accumulate) rather than by borrowing — lenders' claims grew slowly, owners' claims grew fast. (Any answer naming "equity = residual that profits add to; debt was paid down or flat" earns the mark.)

**A4.** A balance sheet is a **photograph at a single date** — a stock of what is owned and owed *on that day*. "For the year ended" describes a **period** of flows (revenue, expenses), which is what an income statement measures (3.6).

### B (10)
**B1.** **Cash** falls (asset side) and the **debt line** falls (liability side). Both sides fall by the same amount, so the equation still balances — assets down, liabilities down, equity unchanged.

**B2.** Any of: profits earned during the year (operating cash flow), selling investments (Apple also holds 80,213 of non-current marketable securities), issuing new stock, or selling assets. The point: a balance sheet can change in three ways — operations, investing, financing — not just by spending the cash line.

**B3.** No. "Current maturities" is only the slice of debt **due within 12 months**; the rest sits in non-current "debt and finance leases". You must check the *total* (current + non-current) — which fell from 16,194 to 14,112, so Delta's total debt did fall, but by less than the current line alone suggests.

**B4.** Net worth is **assets − liabilities** (owners' equity), not assets. Apple's net worth is 112,093; the other 292,455 of assets are claimed by lenders.

### C (10)
**C1.**

| | Apple | Delta |
|---|---|---|
| Total assets | **+12.6%** (359,241 → 404,548) | **+7.9%** (75,372 → 81,317) |
| Total liabilities | **+2.4%** (285,508 → 292,455) | **+0.6%** (60,079 → 60,464) |
| Owners' equity | **+52.0%** (73,733 → 112,093) | **+36.4%** (15,293 → 20,853) |

**C2.** Apple — +12.6% vs Delta's +7.9%, a gap of **4.7 percentage points** of asset growth.

**C3.** Because 3.7.A.5 requires **comparable data**: the second date turns one fact into a **trend** (did assets, debt or equity grow, and how fast). With a single date you can verify the equation but you cannot see direction.

### D (10)
**D1.** BS, IS, BS, BS, IS, IS. (Accounts payable and retained earnings are *stocks* at a date; revenue and net income are *flows* over a period.)

**D2.** Deferred revenue is **cash collected before the goods or services are delivered** — an obligation (a liability), because the company still owes the customer something. It becomes revenue on the income statement only when the delivery happens; until then the balance sheet must carry it as a claim against assets.

**D3.** The balance sheet date is a **snapshot** ("what we own and owe at this instant"); the income statement's period is a **video** ("what we earned and spent over these twelve months"). Same calendar day, two different kinds of statement.

### Common errors
- Adding only one liability group when verifying the equation (current + non-current both count)
- Reading "current maturities" as total debt
- Treating deferred revenue as revenue because of its name
- Quoting percent change on equity and calling it asset growth

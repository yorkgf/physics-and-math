# 4.1 Canva — Management and Leadership

> **Topic 4.1 Management and Leadership** · Read before class. Be ready to discuss.

## Background

**Canva** is an online graphic design platform that allows anyone — from professional designers to students to small business owners — to create visual content including presentations, social media graphics, posters, videos, and documents. Founded in 2013 in Sydney, Australia, by **Melanie Perkins**, **Cliff Obrecht**, and **Cameron Adams**, Canva has grown into one of the world's most valuable private technology companies, valued at approximately **$26 billion** as of 2024. The platform serves over **190 million monthly active users** across **190 countries** and employs more than **4,000 people** globally.

Canva's mission is to **"empower everyone in the world to design anything and publish anywhere."** This mission shapes management decisions — from hiring, to internal communication, to compensation and retention. Canva operates in a competitive market against Adobe, Microsoft, Google, and dozens of smaller design tools.

Melanie Perkins was 19 years old when she first conceived the idea for Canva while teaching design programs at university in Perth. She noticed that professional design tools like Adobe Photoshop were too complex for most people. Over the next several years, she and co-founder Cliff Obrecht built a prototype, faced over **100 rejections from investors**, and secured funding in 2012. The company launched publicly in 2013 and grew to 1 million users within its first year.

Canva's **management structure** has evolved as the company scaled from a small startup to a global enterprise. In 2024, Canva reorganized its 4,000+ employees into **"groups"** — small, autonomous teams of 8–12 people, each with ownership of a product area. This structure replaces the traditional corporate hierarchy with a model designed to keep teams agile, accountable, and aligned with the company's mission.

## Key Events

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **2007** | Melanie Perkins (age 19) begins teaching design software at university in Perth, Australia; notices that students struggle with complex tools like Adobe Photoshop and InDesign |
| **2008** | Perkins and Cliff Obrecht co-found **Fusion Books**, a yearbook design platform for Australian schools — the precursor to Canva |
| **2010–2012** | Perkins pitches Canva to over **100 investors** — all reject. She and Obrecht travel to Silicon Valley multiple times, sleeping on friends' couches |
| **2012** | Perkins meets **Bill Tai** (Silicon Valley investor) at a kite-surfing event; Tai introduces her to **Cameron Adams** (Google Maps engineer), who becomes the third co-founder and CTO |
| **2013** | Canva launches publicly; reaches **1 million users** in the first year |
| **2014** | Raises $3 million in Series A funding; hires first 20 employees; begins building the company's management culture around transparency and mission alignment |
| **2018** | Reaches 10 million users; raises $40 million; valued at $1 billion ("unicorn" status); employee count reaches 300 |
| **2019** | Launches **Canva for Enterprise** — requires hiring sales, customer success, and enterprise engineering teams; management structure begins shifting from flat startup to organized departments |
| **2020** | COVID-19 accelerates remote work; Canva's design tools become essential for remote teams; user count surges to 40 million; company hires 500+ employees in one year — management faces the challenge of scaling culture |
| **2021** | Raises $200 million at a $40 billion valuation; employee count exceeds 2,500; Perkins and Obrecht pledge to give away the **vast majority of their wealth** through the Canva Foundation |
| **2022** | Economic downturn forces Canva to lay off approximately **250 employees** (~10% of workforce) — the first layoffs in company history. CEO Perkins personally addressed all employees in a company-wide meeting, explaining the business rationale and answering questions. Affected employees received individual meetings with their managers, generous severance packages, and career transition support. Perkins also published a public letter explaining the decision to external stakeholders |
| **2023** | Launches **AI-powered design tools** (Magic Design, Magic Write) — requires rapid hiring of AI/ML engineers; Canva reorganizes teams around AI integration |
| **2024** | Reaches 190 million monthly active users and 4,000+ employees; reorganizes into **"groups"** (autonomous teams of 8–12) to maintain startup agility at scale. Each group is led by a group lead responsible for day-to-day decisions within the group, while department heads (such as the VP of Engineering overseeing all 1,600 engineers) set technical standards and allocate resources across groups. Valued at $26 billion |

# 4.1 Canva — Management and Leadership

> **Topic:** 4.1 Management and Leadership
> **Company:** Canva

---

## Background

**Canva** is an online graphic design platform that allows anyone — from professional designers to students to small business owners — to create visual content including presentations, social media graphics, posters, videos, and documents. Founded in 2013 in Sydney, Australia, by **Melanie Perkins**, **Cliff Obrecht**, and **Cameron Adams**, Canva has grown into one of the world's most valuable private technology companies, valued at approximately **$26 billion** as of 2024. The platform serves over **190 million monthly active users** across **190 countries** and employs more than **4,000 people** globally.

Canva's mission is to **"empower everyone in the world to design anything and publish anywhere."** This mission shapes every management decision the company makes — from who it hires, to how it communicates internally, to how it compensates and retains employees. Canva operates in a highly competitive market, competing against Adobe, Microsoft, Google, and dozens of smaller design tools. Its ability to attract, develop, and retain talented employees is central to its competitive advantage.

Melanie Perkins was 19 years old when she first conceived the idea for Canva while teaching design programs at university in Perth. She noticed that professional design tools like Adobe Photoshop were too complex for most people. Over the next several years, she and co-founder Cliff Obrecht built a prototype, faced over **100 rejections from investors**, and eventually secured funding in 2012. The company launched publicly in 2013 and grew to 1 million users within its first year.

Canva's **management structure** has evolved significantly as the company scaled from a small startup to a global enterprise. In 2024, Canva reorganized its 4,000+ employees into **"groups"** — small, autonomous teams of 8–12 people, each with clear ownership of a product area. This structure replaces the traditional corporate hierarchy with a model designed to keep teams agile, accountable, and aligned with the company's mission.

## Key Events

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **2007** | Melanie Perkins (age 19) begins teaching design software at university in Perth, Australia; notices that students struggle with complex tools like Adobe Photoshop and InDesign |
| **2008** | Perkins and Cliff Obrecht co-found **Fusion Books**, a yearbook design platform for Australian schools — the precursor to Canva |
| **2010–2012** | Perkins pitches Canva to over **100 investors** — all reject. She and Obrecht travel to Silicon Valley multiple times, sleeping on friends' couches |
| **2012** | Perkins meets **Bill Tai** (Silicon Valley investor) at a kite-surfing event; Tai introduces her to **Cameron Adams** (Google Maps engineer), who becomes the third co-founder and CTO |
| **2013** | Canva launches publicly; reaches **1 million users** in the first year |
| **2014** | Raises $3 million in Series A funding; hires first 20 employees; begins building the company's management culture around transparency and mission alignment |
| **2018** | Reaches 10 million users; raises $40 million; valued at $1 billion ("unicorn" status); employee count reaches 300 |
| **2019** | Launches **Canva for Enterprise** — requires hiring sales, customer success, and enterprise engineering teams; management structure begins shifting from flat startup to organized departments |
| **2020** | COVID-19 accelerates remote work; Canva's design tools become essential for remote teams; user count surges to 40 million; company hires 500+ employees in one year — management faces the challenge of scaling culture |
| **2021** | Raises $200 million at a $40 billion valuation; employee count exceeds 2,500; Perkins and Obrecht pledge to give away the **vast majority of their wealth** through the Canva Foundation |
| **2022** | Economic downturn forces Canva to lay off approximately **250 employees** (~10% of workforce) — the first layoffs in company history; management must handle the difficult process with transparency and empathy |
| **2023** | Launches **AI-powered design tools** (Magic Design, Magic Write) — requires rapid hiring of AI/ML engineers; Canva reorganizes teams around AI integration |
| **2024** | Reaches 190 million monthly active users and 4,000+ employees; reorganizes into **"groups"** (autonomous teams of 8–12); valued at $26 billion |

## Questions

**1. The Four Functions of Management**
Management is the process of planning, organizing, leading, and evaluating, identify one specific example of each function at Canva. For which function did Canva face its greatest challenge during the 2020–2024 period of rapid growth? Explain why.

**2. Management at All Levels**
Management includes decisions and actions from executive leaders to supervisors, describe what "management" looks like at three different levels of Canva: (a) the CEO, (b) the VP of Engineering, and (c) a group lead responsible for a team of 10 engineers. What decisions does each person make, and how do those decisions affect the employees they manage?

**3. Departmental Coordination**
Managers of specialized departments coordinate employees' work to align with the business's vision and mission, describe a specific scenario where Canva's marketing department and engineering department might have conflicting priorities. How should the department heads resolve this conflict while staying aligned with Canva's mission?

**4. Leadership in a Crisis**
In 2022, Canva laid off approximately 250 employees — the first layoffs in company history. Key leadership skills include articulating vision, building teams, negotiating conflicts, and motivating people, evaluate how Canva's leadership handled the layoffs. Which leadership skill was most critical during this moment, and why?

**5. Communication Skills**
Key communication skills include expressing ideas clearly, communicating persuasively, listening empathetically, and interpreting feedback, identify one example of each communication skill from Canva's history. Which skill was most important for Melanie Perkins during the period of 100+ investor rejections (2010–2012)? Explain.

**6. Core Competencies and Outsourcing**
Businesses need employees with a variety of core competencies, though some tasks may be outsourced if they are outside the business's core competencies, review Canva's employee breakdown by department. (a) Why does engineering represent 40% of Canva's workforce? (b) Identify one function that Canva outsources and explain why it makes sense to outsource rather than hire in-house. (c) What is the risk of outsourcing a function that is closer to the company's core competency?

**7. The Cost of Unskilled Employees**
Employees who lack skills may create flawed products or provide poor service, causing loss of customers, reputation, and revenue, explain how a single unskilled employee in each of the following roles could damage Canva: (a) a software engineer, (b) a salesperson, and (c) a customer success manager. Which of these three scenarios poses the greatest financial risk? Explain your reasoning.

**8. Compensation Schemes**
Employees may be paid using hourly wage, salary, commission, piece rate pay, or profit sharing, explain why Canva uses **different** compensation schemes for engineers and salespeople. What would happen if Canva paid its salespeople a flat salary with no commission? What would happen if it paid its engineers purely on commission?

**9. Benefits and Risk Management**
Employee compensation may include health insurance, retirement savings, HSA, disability insurance, educational reimbursement, and PTO, Canva's benefits package costs approximately $25,000–$35,000 per employee per year. (a) Identify which benefits connect to the Personal Finance standards for **Earning Income** and which connect to **Managing Risk**. (b) Why might an employee value educational reimbursement ($5,000/year) more than a $5,000 raise? Explain using the concept of human capital development.

**10. Retention Strategy**
Businesses use raises, promotions, bonuses, autonomy, flexibility, recognition, and positive culture to retain employees because retaining is more cost-effective than recruiting, calculate: If replacing a $150,000/year engineer costs 100% of their annual salary ($150,000), and Canva has 1,600 engineers with a 15% annual turnover rate, how much does Canva spend on engineer replacement per year? How does this compare to the cost of providing equity compensation to all 1,600 engineers ($10,000/engineer/year in stock options)?

**11. Motivation and Mission Alignment**
Motivated employees are more productive, more likely to stay, and more likely to build strong relationships with customers and teams, Canva's mission is "empower everyone in the world to design anything and publish anywhere." Explain how this mission motivates employees differently than a purely financial goal like "maximize shareholder value." Give specific examples of how a mission-driven employee at Canva might behave differently from an employee at a company with only financial goals.

**12. Training and Development**
Employee training may include postsecondary education, apprentice programs, on-the-job training, online training, and continuing education, Canva offers a $5,000/year educational reimbursement, a "Canva University" online learning platform, and a "Design Fellowship" apprentice program. (a) Which type of training is most effective for a new engineering hire with no professional experience? (b) Which type is most effective for a senior designer who wants to move into a leadership role? (c) Why is investing in training considered a management function, not just an HR function?

**13. Stakeholder Communication**
Leadership communicates effectively with a wide variety of stakeholders, identify five distinct stakeholders that Canva's leadership must communicate with. For each stakeholder, describe: (a) what information Canva's leaders need to communicate, and (b) how the communication method might differ (e.g., town hall for employees vs. quarterly earnings report for investors). Why is it important that Canva's message be consistent across all stakeholders, even when the communication format differs?

**14. Work Arrangements**
Businesses may use full-time, part-time, contract, and temporary employees depending on their needs, Canva employs approximately 3,500 full-time employees and 300 contractors. (a) Explain why Canva prefers full-time employees for core engineering roles but uses contractors for short-term projects like video production. (b) What are the advantages and disadvantages of hiring temporary customer support staff during a major product launch rather than permanently expanding the team? (c) How does the mix of work arrangements affect a company's ability to respond quickly to changes in demand?

**15. How Compensation Is Determined**
Compensation is determined by education, skills, productivity, and market benchmarks, Canva benchmarks its engineering salaries against Google, Atlassian, and Adobe. (a) Why is it important for Canva to compare its compensation to these specific companies rather than to the national average salary? (b) Two software engineers at Canva have identical job titles — one has a master's degree from Stanford and 8 years of experience, the other is self-taught with 4 years of experience but has built an open-source project used by 50,000 developers. Should Canva pay them the same salary? Use the factors to justify your answer. (c) How does location affect compensation decisions for a company with employees in 30+ countries?

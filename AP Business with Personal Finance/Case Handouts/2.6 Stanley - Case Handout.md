# 2.6 Stanley — Case Handout

> **Topic 2.6 Place and Channels** · Read before class. Be ready to discuss.

---

## Background

Stanley (officially Stanley 1913) is an American brand that has manufactured insulated drinkware and food storage products since **1913**, when inventor **William Stanley Jr.** patented the first all-steel vacuum bottle. For over a century, Stanley was known for its rugged, durable thermoses used by construction workers, military personnel, and outdoor enthusiasts.

By 2019, Stanley's annual revenue was approximately **$70 million**. The brand had a loyal but narrow customer base: men who valued durability over style. Then, in the span of just four years, Stanley grew from $70 million to approximately **$750 million** in annual revenue by 2023.

The catalyst was not a new product. It was a **new channel strategy** — selling the same type of product through different places, to a completely different customer.

---

## Key Events

| Year | Event |
|------|-------|
| 1913 | William Stanley Jr. patents the first all-steel vacuum bottle; Stanley brand founded |
| 1913-2015 | Stanley builds a reputation as a rugged, utilitarian brand for construction workers, military, and outdoorsmen |
| ~2016 | Stanley launches the **Quencher** tumbler — a large, cup-holder-friendly insulated tumbler. It does not sell well in hardware stores |
| 2019 | **The Buy Guide** (Ashlee LeSueur, Taylor Colange, Linley Hutchinson) — a lifestyle blog run by three women — discovers the Quencher |
| 2019 | The Buy Guide purchases **5,000 Quenchers** and resells them on their blog; sells out in hours |
| 2019 | The Buy Guide writes to Stanley urging them to keep making the Quencher; Stanley had been planning to **discontinue** it due to poor sales through existing channels |
| 2020 | Stanley hires **Terence Reilly** as president; Reilly previously transformed **Crocs** from a niche shoe into a fashion brand |
| 2020-2021 | Reilly identifies the Quencher's untapped potential among women; begins channel transformation |
| 2021 | Stanley launches colorful Quencher editions; begins selling through **Target** — a retailer whose core shoppers match the new target customer |
| 2021 | Stanley builds its **DTC (direct-to-consumer)** website (stanley1913.com) for exclusive drops and full brand control |
| 2022 | Stanley uses **influencer marketing** on TikTok and Instagram; Quencher becomes a social media phenomenon |
| 2023 | Revenue reaches approximately **$750 million** — a 10x increase from 2019 |
| Nov 2023 | A woman's car catches fire; her Stanley Quencher survives with ice still inside. She posts a **TikTok video** that goes viral. Stanley's president responds personally and offers to buy her a new car |
| Dec 2023 | **Starbucks x Stanley** limited-edition red Quencher launched exclusively at Target; sells out in minutes; resale prices reach $300+ |
| 2024 | Stanley faces **market saturation** — too many colors, too many drops, resale market frustration, criticism of artificial scarcity |
| 2024 | Stanley launches in international markets including China |

---

## Target Partnership — Key Facts

- Target's core shoppers are women aged 18-40 — exactly the Quencher's new target customer
- Target is known for trendy, design-forward, affordable products — aligns with Quencher's appeal
- Target's in-store displays and marketing aligned with Stanley's new brand image
- Hardware stores (traditional Stanley channel) cater to a male, construction-oriented demographic

## Questions

**1. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

Before 2019, Stanley sold Quenchers through hardware stores like Home Depot. After 2020, Stanley sold through Target and its own website (stanley1913.com).

(A) Classify each channel — hardware stores, Target, and stanley1913.com — as either a direct or indirect channel.

(B) Explain how selling through stanley1913.com gives Stanley more control over the customer experience than selling through Target.

(C) Explain why Stanley does not sell exclusively through its own website.

---

**2. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

Stanley's Quencher did not sell well in hardware stores. Target's core shoppers are women aged 18-40 — exactly the Quencher's new target customer.

(A) Identify the target customer for the Quencher tumbler.

(B) Explain why hardware stores were a poor channel match for the Quencher.

(C) Identify two specific factors that made Target a better channel for the Quencher than hardware stores.

**3. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

The Buy Guide — a lifestyle blog run by three women — purchased 5,000 Quenchers from Stanley and resold them on their blog. They sold out in hours. The Buy Guide then wrote to Stanley urging them to keep making the Quencher, which Stanley had been planning to discontinue.

(A) Identify what role The Buy Guide played in the Quencher's success.

(B) Explain what The Buy Guide's success selling 5,000 Quenchers revealed about customer demand that Stanley had not recognized.

(C) Explain what this story reveals about how businesses can identify new channel opportunities.

---

**4. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

By 2024, Stanley faced criticism for too many limited-edition drops, reseller markups, and artificial scarcity.

(A) Identify two problems that resulted from Stanley's channel strategy by 2024.

(B) Explain how a channel strategy that initially drove growth can become a problem when taken too far.

(C) Recommend one specific action Stanley could take to address these problems without losing the excitement around its brand.

---

**5. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

In November 2023, a woman's car caught fire and her Stanley Quencher survived with ice still inside. She posted a TikTok video that went viral. Stanley's president responded personally and offered to buy her a new car.

(A) Identify what made this event significant for Stanley's brand.

(B) Explain why Stanley's president personally responding to the video was a strategic decision, not just a kind gesture.

(C) Predict what would have happened to Stanley's brand if the president had ignored the video and made no public response.

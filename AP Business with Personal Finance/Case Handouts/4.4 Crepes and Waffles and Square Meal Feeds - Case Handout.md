## Background: Crepes & Waffles

**Crepes & Waffles** is one of Colombia's most recognized restaurant chains, founded in **1980** by **Beatriz Mejía Arango** and **Eduardo Macías Tofiño** in **Medellín, Colombia**. What began as a small creperie in a shopping center has grown into a major food service company with over **80 restaurants** across Colombia, **Panama**, **Mexico**, **Ecuador**, **Peru**, **Chile**, and **Spain**, employing more than **3,500 people**.

The company specializes in **sweet and savory crepes, waffles, salads, and beverages**, positioning itself in the **casual dining** segment with a focus on quality ingredients, attractive presentation, and a welcoming atmosphere. The restaurants are primarily located in **shopping centers and malls**, targeting middle-class families, young professionals, and shoppers.

Crepes & Waffles is notable for its **social impact hiring model**: the company has a long-standing practice of employing **single mothers** as its primary workforce, providing them with stable income, benefits, training, and career advancement opportunities. Approximately **70% of its workforce** are single mothers. This hiring model is central to the company's identity and brand.

## Background: Square Meal Feeds

**Square Meal Feeds** is a **livestock and pet feed manufacturer** founded in **2016** by **Jake Thompson** and **Sarah Nguyen** in **Stillwater, Oklahoma**. The company produces and sells **premium animal feed** — including feed for cattle, horses, poultry, goats, and dogs — through a direct-to-consumer model and regional farm supply stores.

Square Meal Feeds differentiates itself from large commodity feed companies (like Cargill, Purina, and Land O'Lakes) by offering **customized, nutritionist-formulated feed blends** tailored to the specific needs of individual farms and animals. Instead of selling generic "cattle feed," Square Meal works with each customer to analyze their animals' nutritional requirements and formulate a custom blend.

The company operates from a **15,000 sq ft production facility** in Stillwater and sources grain from local Oklahoma farmers. As of 2024, Square Meal Feeds serves **1,200 customers** across Oklahoma, Kansas, Texas, and Arkansas — primarily small and medium-sized farms (50–500 head of cattle) and horse owners.

## Questions

**1. Understanding the Five Forces**
Porter's Five Forces evaluates competitive intensity, attractiveness, and potential profitability; the five forces are competitive rivalry, threat of new entrants, threat of substitutes, customer power, and supplier power, explain why an entrepreneur considering opening a new restaurant should analyze Porter's Five Forces before investing. Which of the five forces is most important for a restaurant owner to evaluate, and why?

**2. Defining Each Force**
, define each of the five forces in your own words and provide one specific example from either Crepes & Waffles or Square Meal Feeds: (a) **Competitive rivalry** — what determines the intensity of rivalry? (b) **Threat of new entrants** — what determines how easy it is for new businesses to enter? (c) **Threat of substitute products** — what makes a product a "substitute" vs. a direct competitor? (d) **Customer power** — what factors give buyers power to drive prices lower? (e) **Supplier power** — what gives resource providers the ability to raise input costs? Which force do you think is most dangerous for Square Meal Feeds? Explain.

**3. Competitive Rivalry**
Businesses evaluate rivalry by identifying the number of direct competitors and the degree of product differentiation; rivalry is strong if there are many competitors with equivalent products and businesses have little pricing power, compare competitive rivalry for Crepes & Waffles vs. Square Meal Feeds. (a) Why does Crepes & Waffles face stronger rivalry despite having 80+ locations? (b) How does Square Meal Feeds' custom formulation process reduce the intensity of rivalry? (c) What could Square Meal Feeds do to further differentiate itself if large competitors like Cargill begin offering custom blends?

**4. Barriers to Entry**
Businesses evaluate threat of new entrants by assessing barriers to entry; the threat is strong if barriers are low and new businesses can easily capture market share, (a) identify three barriers to entry for Crepes & Waffles' industry (casual dining). (b) Identify three barriers to entry for Square Meal Feeds' industry (animal feed). (c) Which business is more vulnerable to new entrants? What specific actions could that business take to increase barriers?

**5. Substitute Products**
The threat is strong if customers have access to alternative products that are less expensive, easier to access, or higher quality, (a) list five substitutes for Crepes & Waffles and rate each as low, medium, or high threat. (b) List three substitutes for Square Meal Feeds' custom cattle feed and explain which is the most dangerous. (c) How does Crepes & Waffles' location in shopping centers both increase and decrease the threat of substitutes?

**6. Customer and Supplier Power**
customer power is strong if there are few customers, high acquisition costs, and low switching costs; supplier power is strong if there are few resource providers and high switching costs, Square Meal Feeds has only 1,200 customers and depends on volatile grain markets. (a) Calculate what would happen to Square Meal's operating profit if grain prices increased 15% (assume COGS increases proportionally and revenue stays constant). (b) What strategies could Square Meal use to reduce both customer power and supplier power simultaneously?

**7. SWOT Classification**
strengths are internal advantages; weaknesses are internal disadvantages; opportunities are external favorable factors; threats are external unfavorable factors, classify each of the following as S, W, O, or T for Square Meal Feeds: (a) "Grain prices increased 20% this year." (b) "Square Meal employs the only animal nutritionist in the Stillwater area." (c) "The USDA announced new grants for small agricultural businesses." (d) "Square Meal has no website or e-commerce capability." (e) "A severe drought is forecast for Oklahoma next year."

**8. Internal Capabilities**
SWOT evaluates internal capabilities such as financial, physical, and human resources, as well as intangible assets like brand recognition, reputation, and intellectual property, compare the internal capabilities of Crepes & Waffles and Square Meal Feeds. (a) Which business has stronger financial resources? How does this affect its strategic options? (b) Identify one intangible asset for each business and explain whether it is a strength or weakness. (c) Square Meal Feeds' production facility is only 65% utilized. Is this a strength or a weakness? Explain.

**9. External Factors and PESTEL**
SWOT evaluates external factors including potential market size, customer preferences, PESTEL factors, and Porter's Five Forces, identify one PESTEL factor (Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Environmental, or Legal) for each SWOT quadrant for Crepes & Waffles. For each factor, explain whether it creates an opportunity or a threat and how the business should respond.

**10. Strategic Decisions from SWOT**
Businesses use SWOT results to build on strengths, address weaknesses, capitalize on opportunities, and respond to threats, Square Meal Feeds' SWOT reveals a strength (custom formulation expertise) and an opportunity (organic feed market growing 8%/year). (a) Propose a specific strategy that combines this strength and opportunity. (b) Now consider Square Meal's weakness (no e-commerce) and the threat (large companies entering custom feed). Propose a strategy that addresses both simultaneously. (c) Which of your two proposed strategies should Square Meal prioritize? Justify using the financial data.

**11. Five Forces Comparison**
the five forces shape competition; an environment is more attractive when forces are weak, compare the overall attractiveness of the casual dining industry (Crepes & Waffles) vs. the specialty animal feed industry (Square Meal Feeds). (a) In which industry is it easier to earn a profit? Use the five forces data and the financial margins to support your answer. (b) If you were an entrepreneur choosing between these two industries, which would you enter? Explain using at least three forces. (c) Does a less attractive industry mean a business cannot succeed? Use Crepes & Waffles as an example.

**12. SWOT Limitations**
, a student argues: "SWOT analysis is just making lists — it doesn't actually help businesses make decisions." (a) Evaluate this argument. Is SWOT just a description tool, or is it a decision-making tool? (b) Using Square Meal Feeds' SWOT, demonstrate how the analysis leads to at least two specific, actionable decisions. (c) What are the limitations of SWOT? What information does it NOT capture?

**13. Applying Both Frameworks Together**
, Square Meal Feeds is considering expanding into the premium dog food market (currently 10% of revenue). (a) Apply Porter's Five Forces to the premium dog food market — how does each force compare to the cattle feed market? (b) Apply SWOT to evaluate Square Meal's ability to compete in premium dog food. (c) Based on your Five Forces and SWOT analysis, should Square Meal invest more heavily in dog food? Propose a specific strategy.

# 4.3 ExpressionMed — Strategy and Decision Making

> **Topic:** 4.3 Strategy and Decision Making
> **Company:** ExpressionMed

---

## Background

**ExpressionMed** is a small business founded in **2018** by **Meghan Sharkus** in **Chicago, Illinois**, when she was just 17 years old. The company designs and manufactures **decorative adhesive patches and accessories** for people who wear **continuous glucose monitors (CGMs)** — medical devices used by people with diabetes to track their blood sugar levels 24/7.

CGMs like the Dexcom G6/G7 and Abbott FreeStyle Libre are small sensors worn on the abdomen or arm that continuously measure glucose levels. While these devices are medically essential, many users — especially children and teenagers — feel self-conscious about wearing them. The devices look clinical and can draw unwanted questions or stares. Meghan, who was diagnosed with Type 1 diabetes at age 4, experienced this firsthand and created ExpressionMed to solve the problem.

ExpressionMed's products are **thin, colorful, waterproof adhesive overlays** that stick over the CGM sensor, allowing users to express their personality while wearing their medical device. The patches come in hundreds of designs — from sports teams and florals to abstract art and holiday themes. They also serve a practical function: the adhesive helps the CGM stay attached to the skin longer, reducing the frequency of sensor reattachments.

As of **2024**, ExpressionMed:
- Serves **150,000+ customers** in the U.S. and 30+ countries
- Offers **500+ designs** across CGM patches, insulin pump skins, and medical device accessories
- Employs **12 full-time employees** and 8 part-time production staff
- Operates from a **4,500 sq ft production facility** in Chicago
- Revenue: **$3.2 million** (2024)
- Products are sold through the company's website (expressiomed.com) and Amazon

## The Strategic Decision: December 2024

ExpressionMed is at a crossroads. The company has grown steadily for 6 years, but Meghan and her leadership team must decide how to invest the company's **$600,000 in available capital** to drive the next phase of growth. After extensive research, the team has identified **three strategic options**:

### Option A: Expand into Retail Distribution

Partner with major pharmacy chains (CVS, Walgreens) and durable medical equipment (DME) suppliers to sell ExpressionMed patches in physical stores.

| Factor | Details |
|---|---|
| Investment Required | $450,000 (retail packaging redesign, sales team hire, trade shows, slotting fees) |
| Projected Additional Revenue (Year 1) | $800,000 |
| Projected Additional Revenue (Year 2) | $1,500,000 |
| ROI (Year 1) | ($800,000 − $450,000) ÷ $450,000 = **78%** |
| Timeline | 9–12 months to first retail shelf |
| Risk Level | Medium — retail partnerships are competitive; slotting fees are non-refundable if products don't sell |

### Option B: Launch a Subscription Model ("Patch of the Month")

Offer customers a monthly subscription box with 3–4 exclusive designs delivered to their door for $19.99/month.

| Factor | Details |
|---|---|
| Investment Required | $200,000 (subscription platform development, exclusive design creation, packaging, marketing) |
| Projected Additional Revenue (Year 1) | $480,000 (2,000 subscribers × $19.99 × 12 months) |
| Projected Additional Revenue (Year 2) | $1,200,000 (5,000 subscribers) |
| ROI (Year 1) | ($480,000 − $200,000) ÷ $200,000 = **140%** |
| Timeline | 3–4 months to launch |
| Risk Level | Low-Medium — subscription model proven in adjacent markets; risk is subscriber churn |

### Option C: Develop Smart CGM Integration Technology

Invest in R&D to develop a patch with embedded NFC chip that connects to the ExpressionMed app, allowing users to track their CGM sensor placement, share glucose data visualizations with their care team, and receive reminders to change sensors.

| Factor | Details |
|---|---|
| Investment Required | $580,000 (R&D, app development, NFC chip sourcing, regulatory review) |
| Projected Additional Revenue (Year 1) | $150,000 (limited launch with 1,000 beta users at $149.99/product) |
| Projected Additional Revenue (Year 2) | $900,000 (full launch) |
| ROI (Year 1) | ($150,000 − $580,000) ÷ $580,000 = **−74%** (negative in Year 1) |
| Timeline | 12–18 months to market |
| Risk Level | High — technology development is uncertain; regulatory requirements may delay launch; competitive threat from CGM manufacturers adding their own features |

### PACED Decision Matrix (Preliminary Data)

| Criteria | Weight | Option A: Retail | Option B: Subscription | Option C: Smart Tech |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ROI (Year 1) | 25% | 78% | 140% | −74% |
| Revenue potential (Year 2) | 20% | $1,500,000 | $1,200,000 | $900,000 |
| Investment required | 15% | $450,000 | $200,000 | $580,000 |
| Timeline to market | 15% | 9–12 months | 3–4 months | 12–18 months |
| Risk level | 10% | Medium | Low-Medium | High |
| Mission alignment (helping people with diabetes) | 10% | Medium | Medium | High |
| Competitive advantage | 5% | Low | Medium | High |

## Questions

**1. Defining Strategy**
A strategy is a plan or approach to achieving a goal; businesses pursue differing strategies based on unique capabilities, competitive landscape, and industry, (a) identify ExpressionMed's overarching strategy. (b) How does ExpressionMed's strategy differ from Dexcom's strategy? (c) Why can two companies in the same industry have completely different strategies?

**2. Data-Driven Strategy**
Businesses identify, gather, and track specific data to define a strategy, evaluate effectiveness, and modify the strategy, ExpressionMed expanded into insulin pump skins after customer survey data showed 40% of CGM users also wear pumps. (a) Identify the data categories (financial, customer, competitor, market) that ExpressionMed used to make this decision. (b) What other data would you want before investing in the pump skin expansion? (c) How would ExpressionMed know if the pump skin strategy was successful after 12 months? What KPIs would you track?

**3. Resource Mobilization**
Defining a clear strategy facilitates the mobilization and alignment of resources, explain how ExpressionMed's financial, human, and physical resources would be mobilized differently under Option A (Retail) vs. Option C (Smart Tech). Which option requires the most significant reallocation of resources? Why?

**4. Strategy vs. Tactics**
Businesses use strategy to guide decision making and the development of tactics, ExpressionMed's strategy is "grow revenue by expanding into new sales channels." Identify three specific tactics that would advance this strategy under Option A (Retail) and three tactics under Option B (Subscription). Explain how each tactic directly supports the strategy.

**5. PACED Model Application**
A deliberative process includes defining the problem, developing alternatives, establishing criteria, and evaluating alternatives — the PACED model, walk through each step of the PACED model for ExpressionMed's decision. (a) State the problem clearly in one sentence. (b) Are the three options comprehensive, or is there a fourth alternative the team overlooked? (c) Which step of PACED do you think is most difficult for a founder like Meghan Sharkus, and why?

**6. Quantifiable vs. Intangible Criteria**
Decision-making criteria include both quantifiable costs/benefits and intangible costs/benefits, review the criteria in ExpressionMed's decision matrix. (a) Which criteria are quantifiable and which are intangible? (b) If ExpressionMed used ONLY quantifiable criteria, which option would win? (c) Why is it important to include intangible criteria even though they are harder to measure? Give a specific example from the case.

**7. ROI Analysis**
Decision-making criteria include financial considerations such as ROI, which measures additional profit generated by an investment divided by the cost, (a) calculate the Year 1 ROI for each option. (b) Calculate the Year 2 ROI for each option (using cumulative revenue and the same investment cost). (c) Which option has the best Year 1 ROI? The best Year 2 ROI? Why might they be different? What does this tell you about the tradeoff between short-term and long-term returns?

**8. Weighted Scoring Matrix**
Managers use strategic frameworks to systematically evaluate relevant variables against long-term goals, review the weighted scoring matrix. (a) If Meghan changed the weight of "mission alignment" from 10% to 25% (reducing "ROI" weight from 25% to 10%), which option would win? Show the new calculation. (b) What does this tell you about how the choice of weights affects the decision? (c) Who should decide the weights — the founder, the employees, the investors, or the customers? Justify your answer.

**9. Conflicting Criteria**
Managers sometimes must prioritize conflicting criteria with limited or imperfect data, resulting in imperfect decision making, the case identifies a conflict between short-term ROI and long-term vision. (a) Identify two other conflicts in ExpressionMed's decision. (b) If you were Meghan Sharkus, which option would you choose? Justify your decision using at least three criteria from the matrix. (c) What information would reduce the uncertainty in this decision?

**10. Competitive Advantage Through Strategy**
Businesses pursue strategies to achieve competitive advantage, ExpressionMed competes against other CGM patch companies (Pump Peelz, GrifGrips, Skin Grip). (a) What is ExpressionMed's current competitive advantage? (b) Which of the three options (A, B, C) would most strengthen this advantage? (c) Which option might weaken it? Explain.

**11. Market and Organizational Considerations**
Criteria may include market considerations, operational considerations, and organizational considerations, Option A requires selling through CVS and Walgreens. (a) What market considerations make retail attractive? (b) What operational challenges would ExpressionMed face in supplying retail stores that it doesn't face selling direct-to-consumer online? (c) How might hiring retail sales staff affect ExpressionMed's organizational culture?

**12. Imperfect Decision Making**
, suppose Meghan chooses Option C (Smart Tech) despite it scoring lowest on the weighted matrix. (a) What legitimate reasons might justify this choice? (b) What risks does she face if the technology development takes longer or costs more than projected? (c) How could she mitigate these risks while still pursuing Option C?

# 2.3 New Coke — Case Handout

> **Topic 2.3 Market Research** · Read before class. Be ready to discuss.

---

## Background

In the 1980s, **Coca-Cola** was losing the "Cola Wars" to Pepsi. After World War II, Coca-Cola had owned **60% of the cola market**; by 1983, that share had fallen to **less than 24%**. Pepsi intensified the pressure by launching the **"Pepsi Challenge"** — blindfolded taste tests in which people chose Pepsi over Coca-Cola.

Coca-Cola CEO **Roberto Goizueta** led the company's response. Born in Cuba and trained as a chemical engineer, Goizueta became president of Coca-Cola in 1979. Earlier in his career, he had successfully tweaked Coke's flavor for the Bahamas market — a past success that later influenced his judgment when he championed a much larger reformulation. Under his leadership, Coca-Cola launched **"Project Kansas"** — a secret plan to create a sweeter version of classic Coca-Cola that could defeat Pepsi in head-to-head taste tests.

---

## Key Events

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **1983** | Coca-Cola's market share falls below 24%, down from 60% after WWII. Pepsi's "Pepsi Challenge" campaign gains national attention |
| **1983-1985** | Project Kansas begins — Coca-Cola secretly develops a sweeter reformulation of classic Coca-Cola |
| **1984** | Coca-Cola spends approximately **$12 million** (in today's dollars) conducting market research on New Coke, enrolling **200,000 participants** in taste tests and focus groups. The taste test methodology gave participants only a small sip of each product — not a full can. Sip tests favor sweeter drinks (New Coke was sweeter), while drinking a full can favors less sweet, more familiar flavors (original Coca-Cola) |
| **April 23, 1985 — "Black Tuesday"** | Coca-Cola announces that New Coke will replace classic Coca-Cola. Pepsi had prepped **200+ reporters** with tough questions before the press conference |
| **April-July 1985** | Coca-Cola's Atlanta headquarters receives **8,000+ angry calls per day**. Demonstrations and letter-writing campaigns erupt across the country |
| **July 1985 — 3 months later** | Coca-Cola brings back the original formula as **"Coca-Cola Classic"** |

## Questions

**1. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

Coca-Cola collected both quantitative data (200,000 taste tests) and qualitative data (focus groups) before launching New Coke.

(A) Identify the two types of data Coca-Cola collected and describe how each was gathered.

(B) Explain why collecting both types of data was insufficient to predict consumer reaction to New Coke replacing Classic.

(C) Identify the critical variable that neither data type measured, and explain why measuring it would have changed Coca-Cola's decision.

---

**2. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

Pepsi's market share growth (Coca-Cola declining from 60% to less than 24%) and the Pepsi Challenge results were both secondary data that Coca-Cola used to inform its strategy.

(A) Identify the two secondary data sources described in the case.

(B) Explain how Coca-Cola interpreted these two data sources and what conclusion it drew.

(C) Identify an alternative explanation for Coca-Cola's market share decline that the company failed to consider.

---

**3. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

Coca-Cola's hypothesis was: "Consumers prefer a sweeter cola; therefore, they will accept New Coke replacing Classic."

(A) Identify what was being tested in this hypothesis.

(B) Explain what was missing from the hypothesis that made it incomplete.

(C) Explain how the incomplete hypothesis led to a flawed research design.

---

**4. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

The taste test gave participants a small sip of New Coke, not a full can. The test was conducted in a controlled setting, not in a real-life environment.

(A) Describe the conditions under which the taste test was conducted.

(B) Explain why results from a small sip test might differ from results when a consumer drinks a full can at home with a meal.

(C) Predict whether New Coke would have performed as well in taste tests if participants had been given a full can. Support your prediction with evidence from the case.

---

**5. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

CEO Roberto Goizueta had previously succeeded in tweaking Coke's flavor for the Bahamas market. He "believed that New Coke had to work so he ignored information that might have contradicted that belief."

(A) Describe how Goizueta's past experience may have influenced his interpretation of the New Coke research results.

(B) Explain how his personal investment in the outcome affected the research process.

(C) Identify two specific ways a neutral researcher — one without Goizueta's personal investment — might have designed the research differently.

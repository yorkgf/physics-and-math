# 2.6 To Summer (观夏) — Case Handout

> **Topic 2.6 Place and Channels** · Read before class. Be ready to discuss.

---

## Background

To Summer (观夏) is a Chinese niche fragrance brand founded in 2019 in Beijing by **Elvis Liu** (刘慧璞). The brand specializes in Chinese cultural fragrances — incense, candles, perfumes, and home scent products — all inspired by traditional Chinese aesthetics and natural ingredients.

The brand's Chinese name "观夏" means "observing summer," evoking a sense of contemplation and seasonal beauty. Its English name "To Summer" carries the same poetic feeling. The brand positions itself around **"东方人文香" (Eastern humanistic fragrance)** — fragrances that tell stories about Chinese landscapes, seasons, poetry, and cultural memory.

From its founding, To Summer took a specific approach to distribution: it **refused to sell on any third-party platform** (no Tmall, no JD.com, no Douyin Shop) and limited sales to its own **WeChat mini-program** (with scheduled drops) and a small number of **boutique experience stores**. This channel restriction became the brand's defining characteristic.

As of 2024, To Summer operates fewer than 10 physical stores across China, yet commands some of the highest prices in the Chinese fragrance market and has a devoted following among young, educated Chinese consumers.

## Key Events

| Year | Event |
|------|-------|
| 2019 | **Elvis Liu** founds To Summer in Beijing; launches with a small collection of home fragrance products |
| 2019 | Opens first boutique in **Beijing's Guozijian (国子监)** hutong — a restored traditional courtyard that blends fragrance retail with cultural experience |
| 2019 | Launches **WeChat mini-program** as the ONLY online sales channel — products available only during scheduled weekly "drops" |
| 2020 | Refuses offers from **Tmall, JD.com, and Douyin** to join their platforms — maintains direct-channel-only strategy |
| 2020 | Opens **Shanghai Hunan Road (湖南路)** boutique — a restored old Shanghai lane house with art installations and a fragrance library |
| 2021 | Products regularly **sell out within minutes** during WeChat drops; customers share screenshots on Xiaohongshu |
| 2021 | Launches signature fragrance collection inspired by Chinese landscapes: "昆仑煮雪" (Kunlun Snow), "颐和金桂" (Summer Palace Osmanthus) |
| 2022 | Opens **Chengdu Citang Street (祠堂街)** boutique — restored historical building; becomes a cultural landmark |
| 2022 | Media coverage of To Summer's stores as **cultural spaces** — generates PR without paid advertising |
| 2023 | Expands to additional cities including **Shenzhen, Hangzhou, Nanjing** — each store uniquely designed to reflect local culture |
| 2024 | Continues to operate **fewer than 10 stores** — deliberately slow expansion to maintain exclusivity |
| 2024 | Maintains **zero presence** on any third-party e-commerce platform; WeChat mini-program remains the only online channel |

## Questions

**1. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

To Summer sells only through its WeChat mini-program and its own boutique stores. It refuses to sell on Tmall, JD.com, or any third-party platform.

(A) Classify the WeChat mini-program and boutique stores as either direct or indirect channels.

(B) Explain what To Summer gains by refusing to sell on third-party platforms like Tmall.

(C) Explain what To Summer sacrifices by refusing to sell on third-party platforms.

---

**2. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

To Summer's WeChat mini-program only sells products during scheduled "drops" — most products sell out in minutes. Customers share screenshots on Xiaohongshu.

(A) Describe how To Summer's WeChat mini-program differs from a normal e-commerce store.

(B) Explain why scheduled drops that sell out in minutes serve To Summer's business strategy.

(C) Predict what would happen to To Summer's brand if it switched to always-available inventory on its mini-program.

**3. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

To Summer's boutique stores are restored historical buildings — a Beijing hutong courtyard, a Shanghai lane house, a Chengdu historical building — each uniquely designed to reflect local culture. Media covers the stores as "cultural spaces."

(A) Describe what makes To Summer's physical stores different from a typical retail store.

(B) Explain how investing in store design and location helps To Summer build its brand without paid advertising.

(C) Evaluate whether To Summer should prioritize opening more stores or launching on Tmall as its next growth step. Support your evaluation with evidence from the case.

**4. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

To Summer operates fewer than 10 stores and deliberately expands slowly. Products sell out during scheduled drops. Some products are released only as seasonal or limited editions.

(A) Identify two ways To Summer creates a sense of scarcity for its products.

(B) Explain how scarcity affects consumers' perception of To Summer's products and prices.

(C) Explain the risk of expanding too quickly — opening many stores or making products always available — for a brand built on exclusivity.

**5. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

To Summer and Stanley both use direct-to-consumer channels (their own websites) alongside physical retail. But their strategies are very different — To Summer restricts distribution while Stanley expanded from hardware stores to Target.

(A) Describe one similarity between To Summer's and Stanley's channel strategies.

(B) Describe one key difference between their approaches.

(C) Explain why two opposite channel strategies — restricting distribution versus expanding distribution — can both be successful. Use evidence from both cases.

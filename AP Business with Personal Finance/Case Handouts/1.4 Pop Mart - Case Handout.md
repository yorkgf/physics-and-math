# Pop Mart (泡泡玛特) — Case Handout

> **Topic 1.4 How Business Ideas Originate** · Read before class. Be ready to discuss.

---

## Background

Pop Mart is a Chinese designer toy company founded in 2010 by Wang Ning (王宁) in Beijing. The company pioneered the blind box model in China — collectible figurines sold in sealed packaging so buyers do not know which design they will receive until they open it.

Wang Ning started by opening a small retail shop selling lifestyle products. He noticed that collectible figurines were the store's best-selling category and pivoted the entire business to focus on designer toys. In 2016, Pop Mart signed an exclusive licensing deal with Hong Kong artist Kasing Lung for the Molly character and launched the blind box format. Sales grew rapidly.

Pop Mart operates over 400 retail stores and 2,000+ robotic vending machines across China and internationally, with presence in Japan, South Korea, Southeast Asia, the U.S., and Europe. The company went public on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange in December 2020; market capitalization exceeded HK$100 billion on the first day. In 2023–2024, the Labubu character (also designed by Kasing Lung) went viral internationally, driving a new wave of collector demand.

## Key Events

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **2010** | Wang Ning opens the first Pop Mart store in Beijing — a lifestyle retail shop selling trendy products |
| **2015** | Pivots to focus on designer toys after noticing collectible figurines were the best-selling category |
| **2016** | Signs exclusive licensing deal with Kasing Lung for the Molly character; launches blind box format; sales grow rapidly |
| **2017–2019** | Opens hundreds of stores and robotic vending machines across China; builds platform connecting independent toy designers with consumers |
| **2019** | Begins international expansion — enters Japan, South Korea, and Southeast Asia |
| **2020 (Dec)** | IPO on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange; market cap exceeds HK$100 billion on the first day |
| **2021–2023** | Continues global expansion; enters U.S. and European markets; launches pop-up stores worldwide |
| **2023–2024** | Labubu character goes viral internationally, especially in Southeast Asia; drives new wave of collector demand |

## Questions

**1. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

Wang Ning noticed that collectible figurines outsold other products in his lifestyle store and pivoted the entire business to focus on designer toys.

(A) Identify the source of the business idea that became Pop Mart's core business.

(B) Explain how Wang Ning used data from his own store to test whether designer toys would be a viable business.

(C) Describe one risk Wang Ning faced by pivoting his entire business based on sales data from a single store.

**2. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

The blind box model — where buyers do not know which design they will receive — was adapted from Japan's gashapon and trading card markets.

(A) Identify the original market where the blind box concept originated.

(B) Explain how Wang Ning adapted this concept for Chinese consumers.

(C) Describe one factor that made the blind box format more appealing in China than a traditional retail model where customers choose their product.

**3. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

Pop Mart signed exclusive licensing deals with artists like Kasing Lung for characters such as Molly and Labubu.

(A) Identify the benefit of exclusive licensing deals for Pop Mart.

(B) Explain how these deals helped Pop Mart build a platform that attracts both artists and consumers.

(C) Predict what would happen to Pop Mart's business if its licensing agreements with key artists were not renewed.

**4. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

# Pop Mart (泡泡玛特) — Case Handout

> **Topic 1.4 How Business Ideas Originate** · Read before class. Be ready to discuss.

---

## 1. Company Introduction

**Pop Mart** is a Chinese designer toy company that pioneered the **blind box** model — collectible figurines sold in sealed packaging so buyers do not know which design they will receive until they open it.

- **Founded:** 2010 by **Wang Ning** (王宁) in Beijing, China
- **Product:** Designer collectible toys (blind boxes), figurines, and IP-based merchandise
- **Founder background:** Wang Ning was a university graduate who noticed that young Chinese consumers had limited access to designer collectible toys — a category already popular in Japan and Hong Kong. He started by opening a small retail shop selling lifestyle products, then pivoted to focus entirely on designer toys.
- **Industry:** Designer toys / Consumer products

Pop Mart operates over 400 retail stores and 2,000+ robotic vending machines across China and internationally, with presence in Japan, South Korea, Southeast Asia, the U.S., and Europe. The company went public on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange in December 2020.

## 2. Key Events

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **2010** | Wang Ning opens the first Pop Mart store in Beijing — a lifestyle retail shop selling a variety of trendy products |
| **2015** | Pivots to focus on **designer toys** after noticing that collectible figurines were the store's best-selling category |
| **2016** | Signs an exclusive licensing deal with Hong Kong artist **Kasing Lung** for the **Molly** character — launches the blind box format; sales explode |
| **2017–2019** | Rapid expansion: opens hundreds of stores and robotic vending machines across China; builds a platform connecting independent toy designers with consumers |
| **2019** | Begins international expansion — enters Japan, South Korea, and Southeast Asia |
| **2020 (Dec)** | IPO on the **Hong Kong Stock Exchange**; market cap exceeds HK$100 billion on the first day |
| **2021–2023** | Continues global expansion; enters the U.S. and European markets; launches pop-up stores in major cities worldwide |
| **2023–2024** | The **Labubu** character (designed by Kasing Lung) goes viral internationally, especially in Southeast Asia; becomes a global pop culture phenomenon; drives a new wave of collector demand |

## Questions

**Q1.** Wang Ning noticed that designer toys outsold other products in his store and pivoted the entire business. Using the framework from Topic 1.4, identify **which source(s) of business ideas** apply to Pop Mart's origin. Is this an example of individual experience, market research, or identifying existing products in other markets? Explain.

**Q2.** The blind box model was adapted from Japan's gashapon and trading card markets. Explain how this illustrates the concept that **business ideas can originate from existing products and services in other markets**. What made the adaptation successful in China's market?

**Q3.** Pop Mart tested which characters sold best before investing heavily in IP partnerships. Explain how this process relates to the concept of **testing and refining a hypothesis** about a business idea. What was the hypothesis, and what data did Wang Ning use to test it?

**Q4.** Pop Mart's international growth was partly driven by the Labubu character going viral on social media. Identify at least **two external factors** (from Topic 1.3 PESTEL) that contributed to this growth. Were these factors within Pop Mart's control?

**Q5.** Compare Pop Mart's path to Copa Di Vino's path (see the Copa Di Vino handout). Both identified a gap in their market and built a business around it. What is **one similarity** and **one difference** in how each business originated? Which business faced greater **risk**, and why?

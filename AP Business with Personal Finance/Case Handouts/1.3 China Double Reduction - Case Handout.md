# China's "Double Reduction" Policy & New Oriental (新东方) — Case Handout

> **Topic 1.3 PESTEL Factors** · Read before class. Be ready to discuss.

---

## 1. Company Introduction

**New Oriental Education & Technology Group (新东方)** was China's largest private education company, offering after-school tutoring, test preparation, and English language training to millions of students.

- **Founded:** 1993 by **Yu Minhong (俞敏洪)** in Beijing, China
- **Product:** K-12 after-school tutoring, English language training, overseas test preparation (TOEFL, IELTS, SAT), and education technology
- **Founder background:** Yu Minhong (also known as "Yu Minhong") was a former Peking University English professor who started by teaching English to students preparing to study abroad. He built New Oriental into the dominant player in China's private education market.
- **Industry:** Education / EdTech

New Oriental went public on the New York Stock Exchange in 2006 and grew to operate over 100 learning centers across China with more than 50,000 teachers. At its peak, the company served millions of students annually and was valued at over $20 billion.

---

## 2. Key Events

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **1993** | Yu Minhong founds New Oriental in Beijing; starts by teaching English to students preparing to study abroad |
| **2006** | IPO on the **New York Stock Exchange**; becomes the first Chinese education company listed in the U.S. |
| **2006–2020** | Massive expansion: over 100 learning centers, 50,000+ teachers, millions of students; diversifies into K-12 tutoring, online education, and education technology |
| **2021 (July)** | Chinese government announces **"Double Reduction" (双减) policy** — banning for-profit K-12 academic tutoring; stock crashes **90%+** in days |
| **2021–2022** | New Oriental lays off **60,000+ employees**, closes most learning centers, and pivots to non-academic tutoring and other business lines |
| **2022** | Yu Minhong launches **Oriental Selection (东方甄选)** — a **live-stream e-commerce** platform where former teachers sell agricultural products and consumer goods on camera |
| **2022–2024** | Oriental Selection becomes a hit; Yu Minhong transforms from educator to live-stream host; new business grows rapidly |

**Transferable skills:** Former teachers brought specific skills from the classroom to live-streaming: audience engagement (holding attention for extended periods), clear communication (explaining product features as if teaching a lesson), on-camera presentation skills (comfort speaking to large audiences), and storytelling (connecting products to relatable narratives).

## Questions

**Q1.** The Double Reduction policy **banned for-profit K-12 tutoring** in China. Classify this as a **PESTEL factor** and explain why it is political rather than economic, social, or legal. What makes a government ban different from a change in consumer preferences?

**Q2.** New Oriental's stock crashed **90%+** after the Double Reduction announcement. Explain how a **single political factor** can affect a business's **revenue, costs, and viability** so dramatically. Use the chain from PESTEL to profitability in your answer.

**Q3.** Yu Minhong pivoted New Oriental from education to **live-stream e-commerce**. Identify the **core competencies** (from Topic 1.5) that transferred from the old business to the new business. Why were former teachers effective as live-stream hosts?

**Q4.** The Double Reduction policy affected **all** K-12 tutoring companies in China — not just New Oriental. Explain why the **same external factor** can affect different businesses in different ways. What determined whether a tutoring company survived or failed?

**Q5.** Compare the impact of China's Double Reduction policy on New Oriental with the impact of **the 9.9-yuan price war** on Seesaw Coffee (see the Seesaw handout, Topic 1.5). Both are examples of external forces that destroyed a business model. What is **one similarity** and **one difference** in how each company responded?

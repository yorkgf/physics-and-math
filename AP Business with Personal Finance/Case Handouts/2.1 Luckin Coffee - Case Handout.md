# Luckin Coffee (瑞幸咖啡) — Case Handout

> **Topic 2.1 Marketing to Customers** · Read before class. Be ready to discuss.

---

## 1. Company Introduction

**Luckin Coffee (瑞幸咖啡)** is a Chinese coffee chain that disrupted the market by combining technology-driven operations with aggressive pricing to challenge Starbucks in China.

- **Founded:** 2017 in Beijing, China
- **Stores:** 9,000+ locations across China — more stores in China than Starbucks
- **Model:** App-first ordering (no cash registers); all transactions happen through the Luckin app or WeChat mini-program
- **Industry:** Coffee / Food & Beverage

Luckin Coffee built its business around **data and technology** rather than traditional advertising. The company collects over **1,000 data tags per user** — including gender, iced/hot preference, social energy level, and coffee scenario (morning commute, afternoon break, meeting) — to drive personalized marketing and product decisions. This data-first approach allows Luckin to understand and serve customers at a level traditional coffee chains cannot match.

## 2. Key Events

| Year | Event |
|---|---|

| **2023** | Launches **Moutai Latte** collaboration (with Kweichow Moutai, China's most famous liquor brand); the product sells **millions of cups on day one** through social media buzz — not traditional advertising; repurchase rate at 3+ month stores exceeds **80%** |
| **2023–2024** | Implements **RFM user segmentation model** (Recency, Frequency, Monetary value) and **LBS (location-based) community targeting** for hyper-local marketing |

## 3. Luckin Coffee — Data-Driven Marketing Facts

### Data Collection

Luckin's app records every customer interaction. The company collects **1,000+ data tags per user**, including:

- **Demographic data** (who the customer is): age, gender, location
- **Psychographic data** (how the customer thinks and behaves): iced vs. hot preference, social energy level, coffee scenario (morning commute vs. afternoon break vs. meeting), flavor preferences, spending patterns

### Target Customer Profile

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Age | 20–29 years old (50% of user base) |
| Occupation | Office workers (60%+ of users) |
| Location | Tier 1 cities — Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou (30%+ of users) |
| Behavior | Tech-savvy, mobile-first, price-sensitive but brand-aware |

### RFM Segmentation Model

| RFM Dimension | What It Measures | How Luckin Uses It |
|---|---|---|
| **Recency** | When did the customer last order? | Identifies customers at risk of churning; sends targeted re-engagement offers |
| **Frequency** | How often does the customer order? | Distinguishes daily drinkers from occasional buyers; tailors rewards accordingly |
| **Monetary** | How much does the customer spend? | Identifies high-value customers for premium product promotions |

### Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) and Lifetime Value (LTV)

| Metric | Effect |
|---|---|
| Referral program impact on CAC | Reduces CAC — existing customers recruit new ones at the cost of one free cup per person |
| WeChat community impact on LTV | Monthly purchase frequency **+30%**; weekly repurchase **+28%**; at 3+ month stores, repurchase rate exceeds **80%** |

## Questions

**Q1.** Luckin Coffee built a 9,000+ store business with almost no traditional advertising. Using the concept that **marketing is not the same as advertising**, explain how Luckin's app-based model functions as its primary marketing tool. What specific data does the app collect that makes traditional advertising unnecessary?

**Q2.** Luckin collects **1,000+ data tags per user**, including demographic data (age, gender, location) and psychographic data (iced/hot preference, social energy level, coffee scenario). Using the distinction between **demographic and psychographic data**, explain why psychographic data is more valuable for personalized marketing. Give one example of how a psychographic tag (e.g., "prefers iced coffee in the afternoon") could be used to send a targeted promotion.

**Q3.** Luckin uses the **RFM model** (Recency, Frequency, Monetary value) to segment its customers. Using the concept of **market segmentation**, explain how Luckin might treat a customer who scored high on Recency (ordered yesterday) but low on Frequency (orders once a month) differently from a customer who scored high on Frequency (orders daily) but low on Recency (last ordered 3 weeks ago). What specific marketing action would you take for each?

**Q4.** The referral program ("Invite a friend, both get a free cup") doubled new user registrations and grew orders by 40% on launch day. Using the concepts of **Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)** and **Customer Lifetime Value (LTV)**, explain why this program is more cost-effective than traditional advertising. What must be true about LTV for this program to be profitable?

**Q5.** After joining Luckin's WeChat community groups, customers increased their monthly purchase frequency by **30%** and weekly repurchase by **28%**. Using the concept of **customer relationship tactics**, explain why a WeChat community group is more effective at building long-term customer relationships than a one-time coupon promotion. What role does the "Chief Welfare Officer Lucky" persona play in making the community feel personal rather than corporate?

**Q6.** Luckin Coffee's 2023 **Moutai Latte** collaboration sold millions of cups on day one through social media shares and word-of-mouth — not paid advertising. Using the concept of **social media as a marketing tool**, explain why this campaign went viral. What made the product shareable, and how could Luckin use data from the viral moment (new customer registrations, social media mentions) to convert one-time buyers into repeat customers?

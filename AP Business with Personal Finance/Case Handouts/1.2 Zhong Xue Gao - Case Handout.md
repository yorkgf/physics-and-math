# Zhong Xue Gao (钟薛高) — Case Handout

> **Topic 1.2 Markets and Competitive Advantage** · Read before class. Be ready to discuss.

---

## Background

Zhong Xue Gao (钟薛高) was a Chinese premium ice cream brand founded in 2018 by Lin Sheng (林盛) in Shanghai. The name sounds like a traditional Chinese name and is also a homophone for "中雪糕" (Chinese ice cream). The company sold ice cream bars priced at 13–66 yuan (~$2–$9 USD) per bar — 3 to 10 times the price of standard ice cream. The products were marketed as made with natural ingredients and featured a distinctive curved wafer-tile shape.

Lin Sheng was a marketing veteran who previously worked on branding for other food companies. The brand grew rapidly in 2019–2021 through social media marketing, and annual sales reportedly exceeded 1 billion yuan. The company raised venture capital funding and expanded distribution to convenience stores, supermarkets, and online platforms.

In 2022, a series of controversies led to a collapse. Consumers discovered that some products were sold at inflated prices in convenience stores and called Zhong Xue Gao an "雪糕刺客" (ice cream assassin) — products that look affordable but cost far more than expected at the register. Reports also emerged that some products did not melt even when left at room temperature for extended periods, raising questions about ingredient quality. By 2024, the brand had largely disappeared from the market, and the company faced lawsuits from suppliers and partners.

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **2018** | Lin Sheng founds Zhong Xue Gao in Shanghai; launches premium ice cream bars priced at 13–66 yuan |
| **2019** | Brand goes viral on social media; marketed as "Chinese luxury ice cream" with natural ingredients |
| **2020–2021** | Expands distribution; annual sales exceed 1 billion yuan; raises VC funding |
| **2022 (summer)** | Pricing backlash — consumers call the brand "雪糕刺客" (ice cream assassin); viral social media criticism |
| **2022** | Product quality controversy — reports that some products do not melt at room temperature; consumers question ingredient claims |
| **2023** | Lin Sheng publicly defends the brand but loses consumer trust; retail partners reduce orders; unsold inventory accumulates |
| **2024** | Brand effectively exits the market; products disappear from shelves; lawsuits from suppliers and partners |

## Questions

**1. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

Zhong Xue Gao priced its ice cream at 13–66 yuan — 3 to 10 times the price of standard ice cream brands.

(A) Identify what Zhong Xue Gao would need to deliver to customers to justify this premium price.

(B) Explain whether the brand delivered on this promise, using evidence from the scenario.

(C) Describe what happened to customer trust when the brand failed to deliver on its promise.

**2. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

Consumers called Zhong Xue Gao an "雪糕刺客" (ice cream assassin) — products that look affordable but shock you at the register.

(A) Describe the customer experience that led to this nickname.

(B) Explain how this experience affected customers' willingness to buy the product again.

(C) Predict what would have happened to Zhong Xue Gao's sales if the company had made its prices clearly visible on all packaging and store displays.

**3. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

Reports emerged that some Zhong Xue Gao products did not melt even when left at room temperature for extended periods.

(A) Identify the concern this raised among consumers.

(B) Explain how this controversy affected the brand's claim of using high-quality, natural ingredients.

(C) Evaluate whether a premium brand can survive when its product does not match its marketing claims.

**4. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

(B) Identify one way social media contributed to the brand's collapse in 2022.

(C) Explain what this pattern suggests about the relationship between social media marketing and business sustainability.

# Zhong Xue Gao (钟薛高) — Case Handout

> **Topic 1.2 Markets and Competitive Advantage** · Read before class. Be ready to discuss.

---

## 1. Company Introduction

**Zhong Xue Gao (钟薛高)** was a Chinese premium ice cream brand that positioned itself as a luxury alternative to mass-market ice cream. The name is a play on words — it sounds like a traditional Chinese name but is also a homophone for "中雪糕" (Chinese ice cream).

- **Founded:** 2018 by **Lin Sheng (林盛)** in Shanghai, China
- **Product:** Premium ice cream bars priced at **13–66 yuan** (~$2–$9 USD) per bar — marketed as high-quality, natural ingredients with sophisticated flavors and distinctive packaging (a curved瓦片/wafer-tile shape)
- **Founder background:** Lin Sheng was a marketing veteran who previously worked on branding for other food companies. He designed Zhong Xue Gao to be a "Chinese luxury ice cream" brand that could compete with Häagen-Dazs.
- **Industry:** Ice cream / Frozen desserts / Premium food

The brand grew rapidly in 2019–2021 through social media marketing and premium positioning. However, a series of controversies — including pricing backlash and product quality issues — led to a dramatic collapse. By 2024, the brand had largely disappeared from the market.

---

## 2. Key Events

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **2018** | Lin Sheng founds Zhong Xue Gao in Shanghai; launches premium ice cream bars priced at 13–66 yuan — **3–10× the price** of standard ice cream |
| **2019** | Brand goes viral on social media; marketed as "Chinese luxury ice cream" with natural ingredients; becomes popular among young urban consumers |
| **2020–2021** | Expands distribution to convenience stores, supermarkets, and online platforms; annual sales reportedly exceed **1 billion yuan**; raises venture capital funding |
| **2022 (summer)** | **Pricing backlash:** consumers discover that some products are sold at inflated prices in convenience stores; viral social media posts call Zhong Xue Gao an "雪糕刺客" (ice cream assassin) — products that **look affordable but shock you at the register** |
| **2022** | **Product quality controversy:** reports emerge that some products **do not melt** even when left at room temperature for extended periods — consumers question ingredient safety and quality claims |
| **2023** | Lin Sheng publicly defends the brand but loses consumer trust; multiple retail partners reduce orders; unsold inventory accumulates |
| **2024** | Brand effectively exits the market; most products disappear from store shelves; the company faces lawsuits from suppliers and partners |

## Questions

**Q1.** Zhong Xue Gao priced its ice cream at 13–66 yuan — 3–10× the price of standard brands. Using the concept of **differentiation** from Topic 1.2, explain what Zhong Xue Gao needed to deliver to justify this premium price. Did the brand ultimately deliver on its promise? Why or why not?

**Q2.** Consumers called Zhong Xue Gao an "雪糕刺客" (ice cream assassin) — products that look affordable but shock you at the register. How does this controversy relate to the concept of **trust** and **brand loyalty**? What happens to a differentiation strategy when customers feel deceived?

**Q3.** Reports that some Zhong Xue Gao products "do not melt" raised questions about ingredient quality. Explain how **product quality** relates to **competitive advantage.** Can a premium brand survive if its product does not match its marketing claims?

**Q4.** Social media built Zhong Xue Gao's brand — and then destroyed it. Using **PESTEL factors** from Topic 1.3, explain how **social and technological factors** contributed to both the brand's rise and its fall. Is social media a double-edged sword for businesses?

**Q5.** Compare Zhong Xue Gao's collapse with **Seesaw Coffee's** collapse (see the Seesaw handout, Topic 1.5). Both were premium brands that failed. What is **one similarity** and **one difference** in the reasons for their failure? What lesson do these cases teach about the relationship between **premium pricing and sustainability?**

# 2.5 Xiaomi (小米) — Case Handout

**Topic:** 2.5 Price

---

## Background

Xiaomi Corporation is a Chinese electronics company founded in 2010 by **Lei Jun** (雷军), a serial entrepreneur who had previously led Kingsoft (a software company) and served as an angel investor in numerous tech startups. Xiaomi entered the smartphone market in 2011 with a radical proposition: sell flagship-quality phones at half the price of competitors.

Xiaomi's founding team included former executives from Google, Microsoft, and Motorola — engineers who understood both technology and global markets. The company had no retail stores, no advertising budget, and no manufacturing facilities. Instead, it sold phones exclusively online, used social media for marketing, and outsourced production to contract manufacturers. This "asset-light" model kept costs extremely low.

By 2024, Xiaomi had grown into the world's third-largest smartphone maker, with products sold in over 100 countries. The company expanded beyond phones into smart home devices, wearables, electric vehicles, and an entire ecosystem of connected products.

## Key Events

| Year | Event |
|------|-------|
| 2010 | Lei Jun founds Xiaomi with a team of former Google, Microsoft, and Motorola engineers |
| 2011 | Launches the **Mi 1** at **1,999 RMB (~$310)** — competitors with similar specs charge 4,000–5,000 RMB |
| 2011 | Original plan was 1,499 RMB, but manufacturing costs exceeded expectations; sold at near-cost |
| 2011–2013 | Sells phones exclusively online — no retail stores, no advertising; uses social media and word-of-mouth |
| 2013 | Launches **Redmi** line at even lower prices (799 RMB) to capture the budget segment |
| 2014 | Becomes China's **#1 smartphone brand** by market share; expands to India and Southeast Asia |
| 2014 | Mi 4 launched — price held at 1,999 RMB for fourth consecutive year, reinforcing "value" positioning |
| 2018 | Xiaomi listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange; market cap ~$50 billion |
| 2019 | Spins off **Redmi as an independent sub-brand** — frees the Xiaomi brand to move upmarket |
| 2019 | Mi 9 launched at **2,999 RMB** — first time the flagship price exceeds 1,999 RMB |
| 2020 | Mi 10 launched at **3,999 RMB** — Xiaomi's first true premium flagship |
| 2020 | Lei Jun announces **5% hardware net profit margin cap** — written into company's articles of association |
| 2021 | Xiaomi announces entry into the **electric vehicle** market with a $10 billion investment |
| 2024 | Mi 15 launched at **4,499 RMB** — premium positioning fully established |
| 2024 | **SU7 Ultra** electric car launched at **814,900 RMB (~$113,000)** — competing with Tesla and Porsche |
| 2024 | Xiaomi's "Human × Car × Home" ecosystem connects phones, cars, and smart home devices |

## 3. Xiaomi Revenue Model — Key Facts

| Revenue Source | Gross Margin | Role |
|---------------|-------------|------|
| Hardware (smartphones, IoT devices) | ~5% (capped) | Customer acquisition tool — gets users into the ecosystem |
| Internet services (advertising, app store, games, fintech) | ~72% | Main profit driver — monetizes the user base |
| Ecosystem products (smart home, wearables, lifestyle) | ~15–20% | Growth driver — expands ecosystem stickiness |

> **Key insight:** Xiaomi makes most of its profit from internet services, not hardware. The 5% hardware cap is a deliberate strategy to maximize the user base, which is then monetized through high-margin internet services.

## 4. Penetration Pricing — Key Concepts

### Six Features of Penetration Pricing

| # | Feature | Definition |
|---|---------|-----------|
| 1 | **Low initial price** | Setting price lower than competitors to attract customers quickly |
| 2 | **Market share acquisition** | Gaining significant market share early to deter new entrants |
| 3 | **Volume sales and economies of scale** | Selling in large volumes reduces per-unit costs, maintaining margins over time |
| 4 | **Customer loyalty and retention** | Building a loyal base that continues purchasing even as prices increase |
| 5 | **Barrier to entry** | Low price point makes it difficult for competitors to enter without incurring losses |
| 6 | **Long-term profitability** | Sacrificing short-term profits to achieve long-term gains through scale and retention |

### Four Limitations of Penetration Pricing

| # | Limitation | Risk |
|---|-----------|------|
| 1 | **Low profit margins and cost recovery** | Initial low prices may not recover costs if volume doesn't materialize |
| 2 | **Quality perception and brand value** | Consumers may associate low price with low quality, damaging the brand |
| 3 | **Dependency on price competition** | Over-reliance on price makes it hard to compete on quality, innovation, or service |
| 4 | **Customer retention and loyalty** | Price-sensitive customers may switch when prices increase or competitors offer lower prices |

## Questions

**1. Penetration Pricing Features**
Xiaomi sold the Mi 1 at 1,999 RMB when comparable phones cost 4,000+ RMB. Using the six features of penetration pricing listed in the case, explain which feature was MOST critical to Xiaomi's success in the Chinese market.

**2. Penetration Pricing Limitations**
Which of the four limitations of penetration pricing was the biggest risk for Xiaomi, and how did the company address it?

**3. Strategy Shift**
Xiaomi used penetration pricing in 2011 but shifted to value-based pricing by 2020. What made this shift possible? Could Xiaomi have launched the Mi 1 at 4,499 RMB in 2011?

**4. Pricing Power Evolution**
In 2011, Xiaomi had almost no pricing power. By 2024, it could sell a car for 814,900 RMB. What specific factors increased Xiaomi's pricing power over time?

**5. The 5% Rule**
Lei Jun capped hardware profit at 5% — written into the company's legal documents. How does a company with a 5% profit cap still become one of the world's most valuable technology companies?

**6. Xiaomi vs. Costco**
Both Xiaomi and Costco use pricing strategies that sacrifice short-term profit for long-term gain. Compare their approaches: How are they similar? How are they different?

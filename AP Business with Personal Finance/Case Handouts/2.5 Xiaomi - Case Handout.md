# Xiaomi (小米) — Case Handout

> **Topic 2.5 Price** · Read before class. Be ready to discuss.

## Background

Xiaomi Corporation is a Chinese electronics company founded in 2010 by Lei Jun, who had previously led Kingsoft, a software company. Xiaomi's founding team included former executives from Google, Microsoft, and Motorola. The company entered the smartphone market in 2011, selling phones exclusively online with no retail stores, no advertising budget, and no manufacturing facilities — production was outsourced to contract manufacturers. This model kept costs low.

Xiaomi's first product, the Mi 1, launched at 1,999 RMB (~$310). Competing smartphones with similar specifications were priced at 4,000–5,000 RMB. Xiaomi's original plan was to price the Mi 1 at 1,499 RMB, but manufacturing costs exceeded expectations, and the phone was sold at near-cost. By 2014, Xiaomi was China's #1 smartphone brand by market share.

In 2020, Lei Jun announced that Xiaomi would cap its hardware net profit margin at 5% — a rule written into the company's articles of association. As of 2024, Xiaomi is the world's third-largest smartphone maker, selling products in over 100 countries. The company expanded into smart home devices, wearables, and electric vehicles, including the SU7 Ultra car launched at 814,900 RMB (~$113,000).

Xiaomi's revenue model is shown in Figure 1.

**Figure 1: Xiaomi Revenue Model**

| Revenue Source | Gross Margin | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Hardware (smartphones, IoT devices) | ~5% (capped) | Customer acquisition tool — gets users into the ecosystem |
| Internet services (advertising, app store, games, fintech) | ~72% | Main profit driver — monetizes the user base |
| Ecosystem products (smart home, wearables, lifestyle) | ~15–20% | Growth driver — expands ecosystem stickiness |

## Key Events

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **2010** | Lei Jun founds Xiaomi |
| **2011** | Mi 1 launched at 1,999 RMB; competitors charge 4,000–5,000 RMB for similar specs |
| **2011–2013** | Sells exclusively online; uses social media and word-of-mouth instead of paid advertising |
| **2013** | Launches Redmi line at 799 RMB to capture the budget segment |
| **2014** | Becomes China's #1 smartphone brand by market share; enters India and Southeast Asia |
| **2018** | Listed on Hong Kong Stock Exchange; market cap ~$50 billion |
| **2019** | Spins off Redmi as independent sub-brand; Mi 9 launched at 2,999 RMB — first price increase above 1,999 RMB |
| **2020** | Mi 10 launched at 3,999 RMB; Lei Jun announces 5% hardware profit cap |
| **2021** | Announces entry into electric vehicles with $10 billion investment |
| **2024** | Mi 15 launched at 4,499 RMB; SU7 Ultra electric car launched at 814,900 RMB |

# 2.2 Keep -- Consumer Behavior

> **Topic:** 2.2 Consumer Behavior
> **Company:** Keep (Beijing Calorie Technology Co., Ltd.)

---

## Background

Keep is a Chinese fitness and lifestyle app founded in 2014 in Beijing. The company went public on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange in 2023, making it "China's first fitness tech IPO." As of its IPO, Keep reported over 300 million registered users and more than 30 million monthly active users.

Keep operates four revenue streams:
- **Online fitness content** -- subscription-based access to workout classes and training plans
- **Smart fitness products** -- equipment and wearable devices sold under the Keep brand
- **Branded apparel** -- athletic clothing and accessories
- **Advertising** -- brand partnerships and in-app promotions

Keep's core users are 18--35 year olds in urban Chinese cities, with over 60% female users. Most are office workers with moderate incomes who find expensive gym memberships inaccessible. The brand's core values center on health, self-improvement, and discipline, and it has helped shift perceptions about home workouts from a lesser alternative to a legitimate fitness option.

The company's core mission is captured in its brand slogan: **"自律给我自由"** -- "Self-discipline gives me freedom."

## Key Events

- **2014:** Keep founded in Beijing; launches as a free fitness app offering guided home workouts.
- **2015--2017:** Rapid user growth as smartphone penetration in China surges. Online fitness users in China grow from approximately 100 million to a much larger base. Keep introduces streak counters and workout completion badges to drive daily engagement.
- **2018--2020:** Keep expands into smart fitness products (yoga mats, resistance bands, stationary bikes) and branded apparel. Training camps (训练营) and community features such as "Keep Running" local running clubs are introduced.
- **2021--2022:** Keep reaches 300 million registered users. The platform introduces leaderboards, user-generated content sharing to WeChat Moments, and group workout challenges. Premium subscription tiers are formalized at approximately 19 USD/month or 178 USD/year.
- **2023:** Keep completes its IPO on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange, becoming China's first publicly listed fitness technology company. Monthly active users exceed 30 million. Online fitness users in China have grown to approximately 400 million.

## Questions

**Question 1**:
Before Keep launched, fitness participation in China required a gym membership costing 50--100 USD per month plus commute time and a fixed schedule. By 2023, online fitness users in China had grown to approximately 400 million. Using Keep as an example, explain how smartphone technology changed both the seller side (Keep's reach) and the buyer side (consumers' access to fitness). Why was this a "both sides" transformation rather than just one side gaining an advantage?

**Question 2**:
Keep's brand slogan is "自律给我自由" -- "Self-discipline gives me freedom." The app uses streak counters ("连续打卡天数") and workout completion badges to reinforce user identity. Explain how the consistency principle operates in Keep's case: how does a user's self-image as a "self-disciplined person" influence their purchasing and usage behavior over time? What happens when a user breaks their streak, and what does this reveal about the power of consistency?

**Question 3**:
Keep offers training camps (训练营) with group goals and leaderboards, "Keep Running" local running clubs, and easy sharing of workout screenshots to WeChat Moments. Analyze how these features apply the unity principle. In what ways does Keep transform what could be a solitary activity (exercising at home) into an in-group identity experience? How might this in-group identity affect a user's decision to subscribe to premium or purchase Keep-branded products?

**Question 4**:
Keep's product ecosystem includes free app usage, a premium subscription (19 USD/month or 178 USD/year), and smart fitness equipment. Classify each of these three products as either habitual or rational purchasing. Then explain how Keep's app design -- reminders, streak counters, daily workout plans -- is specifically engineered to shift users from rational consideration to habitual use. Why is this shift strategically valuable for Keep?

**Question 5**:
Keep provides a large library of free workout content that delivers real value to users. The company also offers free trial periods of 7--14 days for its premium subscription tier, allowing users to access all features before committing to a paid plan. Apply the reciprocity principle to explain why users who have benefited from free content are more likely to eventually pay for a subscription or purchase Keep-branded equipment. Does a free trial period for premium strengthen or weaken the reciprocity effect? Justify your answer.

## Face Test Application

Keep's marketing practices can be evaluated against the face test -- would the company be comfortable if consumers fully understood the tactic being used?

- **"自律给我自由" (Self-discipline gives me freedom):** Survives the face test. The slogan is aspirational and reflects the genuine value proposition of the app. It does not mislead consumers about what the product delivers.
- **Streak counters and completion badges:** Survive the face test. They display real data -- actual days of consecutive use and actual workouts completed. Users can see exactly what the numbers represent.
- **"Only 3 spots left in this training camp!" (when spots are unlimited):** Would fail the face test. This creates false scarcity to pressure immediate sign-up. If users understood that spots were not actually limited, they would feel manipulated.

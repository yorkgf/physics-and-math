## ANSWERS (teacher · students stop here)

### A (8)
**A1.** Processed meat 235.27 (**39.7%**), cost 148.10; slaughtering 292.50 (**49.4%**), cost 278.06; other 120.63 (**20.4%**), cost 115.24. The three add to more than 100% because of an **internal elimination of 55.65** between segments — segment revenue never simply sums to consolidated revenue.

**A2.** Most revenue: **slaughtering, 292.50**. Most gross profit: **processed meat, 87.17** (235.27 − 148.10); slaughtering contributes only 14.44.

**A3.** Slaughtering is the **upstream volume and supply channel**: it secures raw material, spreads fixed costs and keeps bargaining power with farms; the profit is taken downstream in processed meat. Low margin, but it is the cost gateway, not the profit centre.

### B (12)
**B1.** Total volume 342 (2024 ≈ 317, +7.81%); revenue 595 (−0.43%); fresh pork 155.60 vs 134.34 (**+15.83%**); processed meat 136.11 vs 141.43 (**−3.76%**); poultry 40.62 vs 33.83 (**+20.07%**).
**Reason: prices fell.** Volume +7.8% with revenue −0.4% implies average selling price down roughly **7–8%**. The report says it plainly: revenue was hit by **lower hog and meat prices year on year**.

**B2.** Shrank: **packaged processed meat (−3.76%)**. Fastest growth: **fresh poultry (+20.07%)** and **fresh pork (+15.83%)**. The mix therefore tilts from branded processed product toward fresh/upstream — which is why the overall gross margin only crept from 17.69% to 18.05% instead of improving sharply.

**B3.** Gross profit 107.00 (2024: 105.37); gross margin **18.05%** (2024: **17.69%**); net margin **8.6%**.

### C (10)
**C1.** Raw material supply and price (live hogs) → **cost of sales**. Rising social costs — labour, food-safety and environmental compliance → **cost of sales plus selling/administrative expenses**.

**C2.** **Supply chain (input prices).** Two steps: (1) higher live-hog prices raise procurement cost at the slaughterhouse → cost of sales up; (2) if retail prices cannot follow (pork is transparent and competitive), gross margin is squeezed. Note the symmetry: a hog-price rise is a *cost* to Shuanghui but *revenue* to a farm — which is why the group wants industrial coordination.

**C3.** (a) A **13 cash outflow in investing activities** next year, not an expense. (b) Later it becomes **depreciation** inside cost of sales, lowering profit but raising capacity and efficiency.

**C4.** Model answer: "Revenue ≈ external volume × average price, so 2026 revenue grows only if volume growth outruns the fall in hog prices. Testable version: *if external volume grows 5% and hog prices fall another 5%, revenue will be roughly flat to −1%.*" The point is to convert a qualitative promise into a **volume × price** statement.

### D (10)
**D1.** (1) **Scale and turnover**: 592.74 of revenue on 3.42 million tonnes — a thin-margin, high-velocity model. (2) **Segment margins**: processed meat earns **37.05%**, which is a normal branded-consumer level; only slaughtering drags the average down. Useful contrast: processed-meat gross profit (87.17) is larger than the whole company's net profit (51.05).

**D2.** Model answer: fund **processed meat** — each yuan of revenue there produces about 7.5 times the gross profit of slaughtering — but first check (1) processed-meat volume already fell 3.76%, so demand must be confirmed; (2) the brand and channel spend that supports it (selling expenses already +11.2%); (3) whether slaughtering investment would actually deliver the farm-to-fork coordination benefit.

**D3.** Customers (supermarkets, restaurants, distributors) are making a **routine purchase** of an input or semi-finished good — low involvement, substitutable, price-compared. So the promotional mix leans on **direct marketing and sales promotion (rebates, free cases) plus account-level personal selling**, not mass-media advertising.

### Common errors
- Adding segment revenues and calling it consolidated revenue (ignoring the 55.65 elimination)
- Assuming volume growth must raise revenue (here it did the opposite)
- Judging the business by the 18% blended margin alone
- Treating a hog-price rise as bad for every part of the group (it is revenue upstream)

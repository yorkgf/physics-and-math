## ANSWERS (teacher · students stop here)

### A (8)
**A1.** No. 1,159.31 is *total revenue* (includes other operating income); the consolidated income statement's revenue is 115,636,231,250.05 yuan = **1,156.36**. The gap is **2.95**. Always state which measure you use, and use the same one for both years.

**A2.** Net profit 115.14 vs profit attributable to shareholders **115.65**: the 0.51 difference is **non-controlling interests** (here negative — minorities absorbed a loss). Shareholders use the attributable figure.

**A3.** The segment margins are a *weighted* average, not a simple one; and the segment table is on a **main-business basis** (excluding other operating income), which differs from the consolidated measure.

**A4.** Highest margin: **milk powder and dairy, 41.58%**. Most revenue: **liquid milk, 704.22 — about 62% of total**.

### B (12)
**B1.** Cost of sales **756.54** (2024: 762.99); gross profit **399.82** (2024: 390.94); gross margin **34.6%** (2024: **33.9%**, +0.7 pt).

**B2.** Revenue **+0.21%**; profit **+36.82%**; net margin **10.0%** (2024: **7.3%**).

**B3.** Liquid milk 704.22 = **62.0%**; milk powder 327.69 = **28.8%**; cold drinks 98.22 = **8.6%**.

**B4.**
| Line | 2025 | 2024 | Change | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gross profit | 399.82 | 390.94 | **+8.88** | helps |
| Selling expenses | 215.45 | 219.82 | **−4.38** | helps |
| Administrative expenses | 46.96 | 44.65 | **+2.30** | hurts |
| Investment income | 1.47 | 24.17 | **−22.70** | hurts |
| Asset impairment loss | (7.30) | (46.76) | **+39.45** (smaller charge) | **helps the most** |

**Answer: the asset impairment loss.** 2024 carried a 46.76 charge; 2025 carried only 7.30 — a 39.45 swing. So the profit jump is mostly **"we impaired less this year"**, not "we sold more". This is exactly the 3.6 nonrecurring idea: before reading a profit growth rate, ask what one-off items moved.

### C (10)
**C1.** Revenue 1,159.31 → 1,215 = **+4.8%**; pretax profit 129.94 → 136 = **+4.7%**.

**C2.** The phrase is, in translation, *"based on industry development conditions"* — that is **trends** (3.6.D.3), with pricing implied through product mix and price levels.

**C3.** Because it separates a *target* from a *forecast*: a target is set by management and carries no liability. Read it as a **budget** (3.6.C), then interrogate the path (volume? price? mix?).

**C4.** Target 1,190 vs actual **1,159.31** → **97.4% achieved** (missed); pretax profit target 126 vs actual 129.94 (beaten). So a business plan is management intent, not a precise prediction: use it as a **base case** and build your own upside and downside.

**C5.** **Planned expenses / capital expenditure.** It enters the income statement later as **depreciation**, and only after capacity comes online can it support revenue.

### D (10)
**D1.** Raw materials → **cost of sales → gross profit**. Exchange rates and tariffs → **cost of sales** (imported inputs) and **revenue** (overseas prices). Product quality → **revenue** first (recalls, delisting), then **expenses** (compensation).

**D2.** Revenue 1,215 × 65% = cost of sales ≈ **790**; materials at 60% ≈ **474**; +5% = **+23.7**; pretax profit falls from 136 to about **112** — a **17% cut**. Conclusion: the profit target is highly sensitive to input prices, which is why the risk exists.

**D3.** It is a **budget** — a plan management can revise — not a **forecast** of what conditions will deliver. 3.6.C says budgets exist to plan costs, identify funding needs and protect cash; this paragraph is exactly that.

### Common errors
- Mixing total revenue with revenue (a 2.95 gap, but a wrong concept)
- Using net profit instead of profit attributable to shareholders
- Reading +36.8% as an operating improvement (ignoring the 46.76 impairment base)
- Copying the business plan into a scenario as if it were a forecast

## ANSWERS (teacher · students stop here)

### A (12)
**A1.**
| | WMT | TGT |
|---|---|---|
| Total operating expenses | 147,943 | 24,152 (21,535 + 2,617) |
| Gross profit | 177,768 | 29,269 |
| Gross margin | **24.9%** | **27.9%** |
| Operating margin | **4.2%** | **4.9%** |
| Net margin | **3.1%** | **3.5%** |

**A2.** WMT 4.2% vs TGT 4.9% — within 0.7 points despite a 6.8× size gap. Operating margin is scale-neutral: it measures how much of each revenue dollar survives the whole cost stack, so it can be compared across companies of very different sizes (unlike dollars of profit).

### B (12)
**B1.**
| | WMT | TGT | Higher? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gross margin | 24.9% | 27.9% | **Target** (+3.0 pts) |
| Operating expense ÷ revenue | 20.7% | 23.1% | **Target** (+2.4 pts) |

Target is higher on **both** — and that is the whole story: it charges more per item and spends more to sell it. Walmart earns less per item and spends less to sell it.
**B2.** Everyday-low-price: Walmart accepts a thinner gross margin (24.9%) and compensates with a leaner cost structure (20.7%), winning on volume. Differentiated merchandise: Target earns a wider margin per item (27.9%) but must spend more on selling, marketing and store labour (23.1%) to keep the assortment attractive. The two strategies meet at almost the same operating margin.
**B3.** Walmart — its operating-expense ratio is 2.4 points lower, so a given percentage cut in operating expenses saves more as a share of revenue; and its scale means absolute savings are larger. But the deeper answer: **Target's SG&A contains more discretionary spend** (marketing, store labour, remodel costs), so it has more that *can* be cut in a bad year — at the cost of the differentiation it sells. Either answer defended with a line item earns the mark.

### C (10)
**C1.**
| | WMT | TGT |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue growth | **+4.7%** | **−1.7%** |
| Net income growth | **+12.6%** | **−9.4%** |

**C2.** The two levers are **volume** (units sold) and **price/mix** (what each unit sells for). Cost of sales falling *with* revenue falling points to **price/mix and input costs**, not volume: if units had held up, cost of sales would not have dropped. Target's own disclosure for this pattern is a shift in mix and lower selling prices.
**C3.** Something **below the operating line** improved. Operating income rose only 1.6% (29,825 vs 29,348), but **other (gains) and losses** swung from a 794 loss in FY2025 to a 2,075 **gain** in FY2026 — a 2,869 swing, six times the rise in operating income. Walmart's profit jump came from investment gains, not from running stores better: the same "growth ≠ operations" lesson as Yili's impairment base, in another costume.

> **Exam link:** the CED keeps only *interest* as a non-operating item on the income statement (3.6.A.7) — but real statements carry more lines below operating profit. When you read one, classify it: financing or investment, not the business of selling goods.

### D (6)
**D1.** WMT 2,431/713,163 = **0.34%**; TGT 445/104,780 = **0.42%**. Both are a fraction of the airlines' 1.1–2.3%: retail stores and inventory are largely financed by payables and cash flow, while airlines finance aircraft with debt. Interest ÷ revenue is a quick proxy for how capital-intensive (and how leveraged) a business is.
**D2.** The ~377 difference is profit attributable to **noncontrolling interests** — subsidiaries Walmart does not fully own. It sits **below** net income on the ladder: consolidated net income includes 100% of every subsidiary; the attributable line removes the share belonging to outside shareholders, leaving the owners of the parent. Target, with no material noncontrolling stake, reports a single net earnings line.

### One-line summary (for the board)
**Same shelf, different strategies: Target earns more per item and spends more to sell it; Walmart earns less per item and spends less to sell it — and the two meet at almost the same operating margin. Compare ratios, never dollars, across different-sized companies.**

### Common errors
- Comparing Target's SG&A to Walmart's operating line without adding Target's D&A
- Concluding "Walmart is 6.8× better" from profit dollars — use margins
- Reading Target's falling revenue as a volume collapse without checking the cost lines
- Forgetting that attributable net income ≠ consolidated net income when noncontrolling interests exist

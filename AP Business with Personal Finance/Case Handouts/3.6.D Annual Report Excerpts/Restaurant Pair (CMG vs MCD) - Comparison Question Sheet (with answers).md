## ANSWERS (teacher · students stop here)

### A (10)
**A1.**
- **CMG:** above the COGS line — food, beverage & packaging; labour; occupancy; other operating costs (these four are the restaurant operating costs block). Below — G&A; D&A; pre-opening costs; impairment/closure costs.
- **MCD:** above — food & paper; payroll & employee benefits; occupancy & other operating expenses; other restaurant expenses (these attach to company-operated restaurants). Below — franchised occupancy expenses; other (G&A); D&A; other operating (income) expense. *(Franchised occupancy is arguably indirect — it supports the franchise system, not a specific product — but a franchisor's only "product" is the franchise, so either answer defended with a reason earns the mark.)*

**A2.**
| | CMG | MCD |
|---|---|---|
| Gross profit | 3,026.2 (11,925.6 − 8,899.4) | 18,052.0 (26,885.0 − 8,833.0) |
| Gross margin | **25.4%** | **67.1%** |
| Operating margin | **16.2%** | **46.1%** |
| Net margin | **12.9%** | **31.8%** |

*(Direct costs: at CMG the four restaurant operating lines — food/beverage/packaging 3,527.0 + labour 2,991.7 + occupancy 624.9 + other operating 1,755.8 = 8,899.4. At MCD the four company-restaurant lines — food & paper 3,006.0 + payroll 2,905.0 + occupancy & other 2,358.0 + other restaurant 564.0 = 8,833.0. A narrower boundary (food only) gives CMG 70.4%; adding MCD's franchised occupancy to direct costs gives 57.4%. Accept either if the boundary is stated — drawing the COGS line is the judgement the CED leaves to you.)*

### B (12)
**B1.** A franchisee pays royalties (and usually rent) for the brand, the system and the supply chain — McDonald's sells the *right to operate*, not the food. Franchise revenue is therefore a fee stream: no ingredients, no kitchen labour sits inside it.
**B2.** Only 36% of MCD's revenue is company-operated, so food & paper (which only exists at company-operated restaurants) is spread over the whole revenue base. At CMG 100% of revenue carries food cost, so the ratio is nearly 3× higher. Mix, not efficiency.
**B3.** It is the rent McDonald's pays (or guarantees) on sites it leases and then sublets to franchisees — the franchisor carries the property cost of the network and collects it back through franchise rent. A pure fee franchisor would not have this line; a company-operated chain like CMG books its own occupancy instead.
**B4.** CMG holds more cash than debt, so it earns interest on investments (income). MCD carries ~$50bn of debt (the interest line is net of capitalized interest), so it pays interest (expense). Both are the financing rung of the ladder — the sign depends on which side of the lend/borrow line the company sits.

### C (12)
**C1.** Mix, then cost structure: 62% of MCD's revenue is franchise fees with almost no direct cost attached, which lifts both its gross and operating margin; CMG carries the full restaurant cost stack (food, labour, occupancy) on 100% of its revenue. Same industry, different business.
**C2.** CMG +5.4% (11,925.6/11,313.9); MCD +3.7% (26,885.0/25,920.0). CMG grew faster.
**C3.** (1) Total operating costs rose 6.3% (9,989.8 vs 9,397.5) — faster than revenue (+5.4%), so operating income rose only 1.0%; (2) other income/expense and a slightly higher effective tax rate trimmed the rest. Net income 1,535.8 vs 1,534.1 is essentially flat.
**C4.** Unfair: the two revenue models are not comparable line-for-line (see B1–B2) — MCD's margin is structurally higher because a third of its revenue is fees. Before judging you would need: multi-year margins for both, cash flow, same-store sales growth, and unit economics (per-restaurant revenue and margin).

### D (6)
**D1.**
- CMG: 11,925.6 − 8,899.4 (restaurant operating costs) = 3,026.2 gross − 1,090.4 (G&A 652.0 + D&A 361.4 + pre-opening 49.5 + impairment 27.5) = 1,935.8 operating + 73.7 interest & other = 2,009.5 pretax − 473.8 tax = **1,535.8 net**
- MCD: 26,885.0 − 8,833.0 (company-restaurant costs) = 18,052.0 gross − 5,659.0 (franchised occupancy 2,618.0 + other/G&A 2,583.0 + D&A 457.0; the filing's own subtotal implies 5,659.0, its lines sum to ±1 rounding) = 12,393.0 operating − 1,582.0 interest + 87.0 non-operating = 10,898.0 pretax − 2,334.0 tax = **8,564.0 ≈ 8,563.0 net** (±1 rounding)
**D2.** Chipotle's is closer: every revenue dollar carries the same cost stack, so the direct/indirect boundary is unambiguous. MCD's two revenue streams need the boundary drawn twice (company-operated vs franchised), which is exactly the judgement the exam tests.

### One-line summary (for the board)
**Same industry, opposite models: a company-operated chain books food cost on every dollar; a franchisor books fees on most of them. Compare margins only after you compare revenue models.**

### Common errors
- Comparing gross margin across the two without adjusting for the franchise mix
- Calling MCD's franchised-occupancy line "rent expense, so indirect" without noticing the franchisor's product *is* the franchise
- Reading Chipotle's +73.7 interest line as a typo — cash-rich companies earn interest
- Using one year of margin to rank "better business"

## ANSWERS (teacher · students stop here)

### A (6)
1. **Two**: Products and Services.
2. A **segment gross margin** — which half of the business is carrying the margin. One blended cost line can only give the company average.
3. **Operating expense** (indirect; not COGS).
4. **Operating expense** (selling + general and administrative).
5. **Out of scope** — 3.6 keeps only interest among non-operating items. It sits just above pretax income, so students must notice it and set it aside.
6. **Income before provision for income taxes** (159,237).

### B1 (2)
Total net sales 355,533 + 123,301 = **478,834**　Total opex 45,765 + 29,774 = **75,539**
Operating income 233,602 − 75,539 = **158,063**　Pretax 158,063 + 1,174 = **159,237**

### B2 (3)
| | Products | Services |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 355,533 | 123,301 |
| Cost of sales | 215,874 | 29,358 |
| Gross margin | **39.3%** | **76.2%** |

**Services** carries the margin: 25.8% of revenue but **40.2%** of gross profit (93,943 of 233,602).

### B3 (5)
| Measure | FY2026 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Gross margin | **48.8%** | 46.9% |
| Operating margin | **33.0%** | 32.0% |
| Net margin | **27.6%** | 26.9% |
| Effective tax rate | **17.0%** | 15.6% |

Revenue **+15.1%**　Net income **+18.0%**

### C (5)
(a) FY2026 **17.0%**; FY2025 **15.6%**.
(b) Gross margin **46.9% → 48.8%** (+1.9 pt): more services in the mix plus a better hardware cost structure.
(c) Profit rose because revenue grew **15.1%** and gross margin improved **1.9 pt**; operating expenses also grew (14.9% → **15.8%** of revenue), absorbing part of the gain. The **effective tax rate rising 15.6% → 17.0%** then pulled net income growth (18.0%) below operating growth (18.8%).

### D (5)
| Line | FY2027 |
|---|---|
| Revenue | 478,834 × 1.08 = **517,141** |
| Gross margin | 517,141 × 48.8% = **252,365** |
| Total opex | 75,539 × 1.08 = **81,582** |
| Operating income | **170,783** |
| Tax | 170,783 × 17.0% = **29,033** |
| **Net income** | **141,750** |

"Most work": **holding the 48.8% gross margin** — gross margin is nearly half of revenue, so one point of margin is worth about 5,171 of revenue; next is opex growing in line with revenue.

### E (4)
1. Gross margin is not operating margin: operating margin = 158,063/478,834 = **33.0%** (the gap is the 75,539 of opex).
2. R&D is an **operating expense**; moving it into cost of sales would cut the gross margin from 48.8% to about 44.2%.
3. The denominator must be the **initial** value: (478,834 − 416,161) ÷ **416,161** = **15.1%**.
4. Other income of 1,174 was omitted; pretax = 158,063 + 1,174 = **159,237**.

### F (5)
1. Services have almost **no material cost** (one build, low marginal delivery cost) while hardware carries a bill of materials and manufacturing; as services grow, **gross margin keeps rising** — but growth then depends on the installed base and the ecosystem.
2. Its sign flipped (+1,174 / −321), which distorts the **growth rate** of pretax and net income; disclose it separately or exclude it when comparing.
3. Use **net margin** (or operating margin); avoid **gross margin**, because a supermarket's cost of sales includes the goods themselves — the definition is not comparable across business models.

### Common errors
- Reporting gross margin as operating margin
- Putting R&D in COGS
- Dividing percent change by the new value
- Reporting pretax income without other income
- Comparing gross margins across business models

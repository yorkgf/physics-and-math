## ANSWERS (teacher · students stop here)

### A (12)
**A1.**
| | Yili | Shuanghui | Haitian |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gross profit | 399.82 | 107.00 | 116.13 |
| Gross margin | **34.6%** | **18.1%** | **40.2%** |
| Rank | 2 | 3 | 1 |

**A2.**
| | Yili | Shuanghui | Haitian |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue growth | **+0.21%** | **−0.48%** | **+7.32%** |
| Profit growth | **+36.82%** | **+2.32%** | **+10.95%** |
| Net margin | **10.0%** | **8.6%** | **24.4%** |
| Rank by net margin | 2 | 3 | 1 |

**Answer: Yili grew fastest (+36.8%), but not operationally** — the jump comes mostly from a 46.76 asset impairment in 2024 against only 7.30 in 2025, while gross profit contributed just +8.88. This is the "growth ≠ quality" point.

### B (12)
**B1.** **Yili (most verifiable) > Shuanghui > Haitian (least).** Yili gives a number and a stated basis; Shuanghui gives no target but a checkable volume-and-price decomposition; Haitian is qualitative only.

**B2.**
| Company | Source | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Yili | **trends** (+ pricing) | "Based on industry development conditions, the company plans total revenue of 121.5 billion yuan for 2026", plus the product table of revenue and margins |
| Shuanghui | **pricing** (volume × price) | "Total external sales volume 3.42 million tonnes, +7.81%; hit by lower hog and meat prices, total revenue 59.5 billion, −0.43%" |
| Haitian | **market research / trends** (qualitative) | Business plan: users first, "value-for-money and emotional value per yuan", deeper penetration of consumption occasions |

**B3.**
| Company | Revenue ≈ |
|---|---|
| Yili | Volume by product line × average price, plus mix effect; capped by industry supply and demand and the 1,215 target |
| Shuanghui | External volume (slaughtering + processed meat + poultry) × average price, with hog prices driving slaughtering prices |
| Haitian | Category volume × price (price rises and mix); no company guidance, so scenarios must be built |

### C (10)
**C1.**
| Company | Input | Page |
|---|---|---|
| Yili | **Raw milk / imported materials** | p.31–32: imported raw and packaging material prices are uncertain |
| Shuanghui | **Live hogs (meat prices)** | p.42: a large rise in hog prices would pressure production costs |
| Haitian | **Soybean, sugar, packaging, energy** | p.45: agricultural prices move with supply, demand and climate |

**C2.**
| | Yili | Shuanghui | Haitian |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5% of cost of sales | **37.8** | **24.3** | **8.6** |
| % of profit attributable | **32.7%** | **47.6%** | **12.3%** |

**C3.** **Shuanghui** — the most exposed: a single 5% input shock wipes out nearly half of profit, because cost of sales is 81.9% of revenue. Its report says so directly (p.42).

### D (6)
**D1.** (1) **Different business models**: half of Shuanghui's revenue is slaughtering (a processing margin of 4.94%), while Haitian is a branded condiment business at 40%+. (2) **Different cost structures**: Shuanghui's input is a traded commodity whose price customers can see and compare; Haitian's recipe and brand create pricing power.

**D2.** **Net margin**, or all three margins together. Net margin is the only measure that survives a change of business model — it already contains cost structure, expense efficiency and tax. Cross-industry gross-margin comparisons are close to meaningless.

**D3.** A single year is dominated by base effects, price cycles and one-offs (Yili's +36.8% is largely an impairment base; Shuanghui's −0.5% is a hog-price cycle). Quality shows up in a **multi-year margin trend and in cash flow**, not in one year's growth.

### One-line summary (for the board)
**Revenue ≈ volume × price × trend; cost ≈ input price × quantity + planned spend. A plan you can write as an equation is a forecast; one you cannot is only an intention.**

### Common errors
- Ranking businesses by gross margin alone (18% vs 40%)
- Adding segment revenues as consolidated revenue (Shuanghui eliminates 55.65)
- Judging management by one year of profit growth (ignoring impairment and pig-price cycles)
- Reading "no numeric target" as "no plan"

# Ctrip (携程) — Case Handout

> **Topic 1.6 Business Ethics** · Read before class. Be ready to discuss.

---

## Background

Ctrip International (携程) is China's largest online travel agency (OTA), founded in 1996 by James Liang (梁建章), Neil Shen (沈南鹏), Ji Qi (季琦), and Fan Min (范敏) in Shanghai. The platform offers hotel bookings, flights, train tickets, vacation packages, car rentals, and travel services. Ctrip went public on NASDAQ in 2003 — one of the first Chinese internet companies listed in the U.S.

Ctrip dominates China's online travel market with a market share exceeding 50%. In 2016, Ctrip acquired its main competitor Qunar (去哪儿), further consolidating its market power. The company is also listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange. As of 2024, Ctrip serves hundreds of millions of users annually and operates internationally under the Trip.com brand.

Ctrip's stated vision is: "To become the world's leading travel service company by providing travelers with the most comprehensive and reliable travel products and services at the best prices."

Since 2016, reports of "大数据杀熟" (big data price discrimination) have surfaced — loyal, frequent users notice they are charged higher prices than new users for the same hotel room or flight. Ctrip denied the practice but pledged to improve pricing transparency. Chinese media widely reported on the issue in 2019. Regulators have since begun investigating algorithmic pricing practices across the tech industry.

## Questions

**1. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

Reports indicate that Ctrip's algorithm charges loyal, frequent users higher prices than new users for the same hotel room or flight.

(A) Describe the practice described in this scenario.

(B) Explain why this practice creates a conflict between Ctrip's short-term revenue goals and its relationship with loyal customers.

(C) Evaluate whether this practice is consistent with Ctrip's stated vision of providing "the best prices" to travelers.

**2. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

(A) Identify how Ctrip's market dominance gives it the ability to implement the pricing practice described in this scenario.

(B) Explain why customers who notice higher prices may continue using Ctrip despite the practice.

(C) Describe one specific consequence Ctrip could face if regulators ban algorithmic price discrimination.

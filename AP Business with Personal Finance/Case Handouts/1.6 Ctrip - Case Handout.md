## 1. Company Introduction

**Ctrip International (携程)** is China's largest online travel agency (OTA), offering hotel bookings, flights, train tickets, vacation packages, and corporate travel services.

- **Founded:** 1996 by **James Liang (梁建章)**, **Neil Shen (沈南鹏)**, **Ji Qi (季琦)**, and **Fan Min (范敏)** in Shanghai, China
- **Product:** Online travel booking platform — hotels, flights, trains, vacation packages, car rentals, and travel services
- **Industry:** Online travel / E-commerce / Technology

Ctrip dominates China's online travel market with a market share exceeding 50%. In 2016, Ctrip acquired its main competitor Qunar (去哪儿), further consolidating its market power. The company is listed on NASDAQ and the Hong Kong Stock Exchange. As of 2024, Ctrip serves hundreds of millions of users annually and has expanded into international markets under the Trip.com brand.

**Vision:** Ctrip's stated vision is "to become the world's leading travel service company by providing travelers with the most comprehensive and reliable travel products and services at the best prices."

## 2. Key Events

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **1996** | Four co-founders establish Ctrip in Shanghai as one of China's first online travel booking platforms |
| **2003** | IPO on NASDAQ; becomes one of the first Chinese internet companies listed in the U.S. |
| **2003–2013** | Grows into China's dominant OTA through aggressive marketing, partnerships with hotels and airlines, and superior technology |
| **2016** | Acquires **Qunar (去哪儿)** — its main competitor — creating a near-monopoly in China's online travel market |
| **2016–2019** | Reports of **大数据杀熟** (big data price discrimination) begin surfacing — loyal users notice they are charged higher prices than new users for the same hotel room or flight |
| **2019** | Chinese media widely reports on 大数据杀熟; Ctrip denies the practice but pledges to improve pricing transparency |
| **2020–2021** | COVID-19 devastates travel industry; Ctrip's revenue drops sharply; company pivots to domestic travel and livestream commerce |
| **2022–2024** | Travel recovery; Ctrip's revenue rebounds; 大数据杀熟 complaints continue on social media; regulators begin investigating algorithmic pricing practices across the tech industry |

## Questions

**Q1.** Ctrip's algorithm charges loyal users higher prices than new users for the same product. Classify this situation: is it an **ethical dilemma**, **unethical behavior**, or **both**? Explain your reasoning using the definitions from class. What **core value** conflicts with what **business goal**?

**Q2.** Apply the **stakeholder arithmetic** framework to Ctrip's 大数据杀熟. Create a table showing the **benefits and costs** for each stakeholder group (internal: company/investors; external: loyal customers, new customers, competitors). What does the arithmetic suggest about the **long-term** vs. **short-term** impact?

**Q3.** Ctrip's market dominance (after acquiring Qunar) gave it the power to implement price discrimination. Using **PESTEL factors** from Topic 1.3, explain how **political and legal factors** eventually constrained Ctrip's behavior. What role did government regulators play as an **external stakeholder**?

**Q4.** Compare Ctrip's ethical dilemma with **Seesaw Coffee's** dilemma (see the Seesaw handout, Topic 1.5). Both companies made decisions that conflicted with their stated values. What is **one similarity** and **one difference** in how each company's decision affected its relationship with customers?

**Q5.** If you were Ctrip's CEO, which approach would you take: (1) keep 大数据杀熟 for maximum short-term revenue, (2) stop the practice and reward loyalty with lower prices, or (3) adopt transparent dynamic pricing with open loyalty discounts? Apply **both lenses** from class — stakeholder arithmetic AND consistency with the company's vision — to justify your answer.

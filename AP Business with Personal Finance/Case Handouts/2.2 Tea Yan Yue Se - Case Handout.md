# 2.2 Tea Yan Yue Se (茶颜悦色) — Case Handout

> **Topic 2.2 Consumer Behavior** · Read before class. Be ready to discuss.

---

## Background

**Tea Yan Yue Se (茶颜悦色)** is a Chinese tea drink brand founded in 2013 in Changsha, Hunan Province. The brand is known for its **Chinese-style (国风) cup designs** featuring classical poetry, traditional illustrations, and cultural references. Signature products include the **Youlan Latte (幽兰拿铁)**, **Shengsheng Oolong (声声乌龙)**, and **Zhixiao (栀晓)** — each named with poetic, literary references that reflect the brand's fusion of traditional tea culture with modern drink trends.

For **seven years**, Tea Yan Yue Se was available **only in Changsha**. This geographic exclusivity turned the brand into a cultural phenomenon: tourists planned trips to Changsha around visiting the brand. The phrase **"排队2小时买一杯茶颜"** ("queuing 2 hours for one cup of Tea Yan") became a social media topic and a marker of dedication. The brand began expanding to other cities — Wuhan, Chongqing, Nanjing — starting in 2020, but Changsha remains the **"spiritual home"** of the brand. As of 2025, Tea Yan Yue Se operates over **500 stores**, the vast majority still in Hunan Province.

**Target customer profile:** Young Chinese consumers (18-35) who value cultural identity, aesthetic experience, and social participation. A typical customer visits Tea Yan Yue Se not only for the drink but for the experience: reading the poetry on the cup, sharing a photo on Xiaohongshu, and telling friends "I went to Changsha and got Tea Yan." The purchase decision is shaped by scarcity, social proof, cultural identity, and emotional connection.

## Key Events

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **2013** | Founded by Lü Liang (吕良) in Changsha, Hunan; opens first store with Chinese-style decor and hand-written product descriptions |
| **2014-2019** | Expands within Changsha only; builds a following among locals and tourists; limited geographic footprint creates scarcity — no stores outside Hunan |
| **2017** | Social media attention grows — "queuing 2 hours for Tea Yan" becomes a topic on Weibo and Xiaohongshu; Changsha travel guides begin listing Tea Yan Yue Se as a destination |
| **2019** | Seasonal limited flavors (季节限定) become a core strategy — rotating flavors create urgency and repeat visits; fans track release dates |
| **2020** | First expansion outside Changsha — opens stores in Wuhan; social media coverage accompanies each new city launch |
| **2021** | Expands to Chongqing and Nanjing; loyalty stamp card (积点卡) program formalized — buy 6 cups, get 1 free; Xiaohongshu posts about the brand exceed 1 million |
| **2022** | Some seasonal flavors designated as **Changsha-exclusive** — even after expansion, certain products remain available only in Changsha, reinforcing the city's special status |
| **2023** | Store count exceeds 500; WeChat mini-program ordering rolled out across locations; fan communities on WeChat and Weibo grow to millions of members |
| **2024** | Continues measured expansion; maintains focus on quality and cultural identity over rapid scaling |

In addition to its stamp card loyalty program, the brand uses other reciprocity tactics: stores provide free tasting samples of new teas to walk-in customers, and the brand gives small gifts during Chinese festivals, such as red envelopes during Spring Festival.

## Questions

**1. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

Tea Yan Yue Se was available only in Changsha for over seven years, and tourists planned entire trips around visiting the brand.

(A) Identify the type of scarcity that drove demand for Tea Yan Yue Se during its Changsha-only period.

(B) Explain how this scarcity — where supply was genuinely limited by geography — differs from a store displaying "Only 10 left!" when 100 exist.

(C) Explain which type of scarcity builds long-term trust with customers and which type erodes it.

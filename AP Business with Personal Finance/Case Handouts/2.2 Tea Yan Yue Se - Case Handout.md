# 2.2 Tea Yan Yue Se (茶颜悦色) — Consumer Behavior

> **Topic:** 2.2 Consumer Behavior
> **Company:** Tea Yan Yue Se (茶颜悦色 / Modern China Tea Shop)

---

## Background

**Tea Yan Yue Se (茶颜悦色)** is a Chinese tea drink brand founded in 2013 in Changsha, Hunan Province. The brand is known for its **Chinese-style (国风) cup designs** that feature classical poetry, traditional illustrations, and hand-written-style cultural references on every cup. Signature products include the **Youlan Latte (幽兰拿铁)**, **Shengsheng Oolong (声声乌龙)**, and **Zhixiao (栀晓)** — each named with poetic, literary flair that reflects the brand's fusion of traditional tea culture with modern drink trends.

For **seven years**, Tea Yan Yue Se was available **only in Changsha**. This geographic exclusivity turned the brand into a cultural phenomenon: tourists literally planned trips to Changsha around getting a cup. The phrase **"排队2小时买一杯茶颜"** ("queuing 2 hours for one cup of Tea Yan") became a social media meme and a badge of dedication. The brand began expanding to other cities — Wuhan, Chongqing, Nanjing — starting in 2020, but Changsha remains the **"spiritual home"** of the brand. As of 2025, Tea Yan Yue Se operates over **500 stores**, the vast majority still in Hunan Province.

**Target customer profile:** Young Chinese consumers (18–35) who value cultural identity, aesthetic experience, and social participation. These customers are not just buying milk tea — they are buying into a **cultural narrative**. A typical customer visits Tea Yan Yue Se not only for the drink but for the experience: reading the poetry on the cup, sharing a photo on Xiaohongshu, and telling friends "I went to Changsha and got Tea Yan." The purchase decision is shaped by scarcity, social proof, cultural identity, and emotional connection — not just taste or price.

## Key Events

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **2013** | Founded by Lü Liang (吕良) in Changsha, Hunan; opens first store with Chinese-style decor and hand-written product descriptions |
| **2014–2019** | Expands within Changsha only; builds a cult following among locals and tourists; limited geographic footprint creates extreme scarcity — no stores outside Hunan |
| **2017** | Social media buzz intensifies — "queuing 2 hours for Tea Yan" becomes a viral topic on Weibo and Xiaohongshu; Changsha travel guides begin listing Tea Yan Yue Se as a must-visit destination |
| **2019** | Seasonal limited flavors (季节限定) become a core strategy — rotating flavors create urgency and repeat visits; fans track release dates like product launches |
| **2020** | First expansion outside Changsha — opens stores in Wuhan; massive queues and social media coverage accompany each new city launch |
| **2021** | Expands to Chongqing and Nanjing; loyalty stamp card (积点卡) program formalized — buy 6 cups, get 1 free; Xiaohongshu posts about the brand exceed 1 million |
| **2022** | Some seasonal flavors designated as **Changsha-exclusive** — even after expansion, certain products remain available only in Changsha, reinforcing the city's special status |
| **2023** | Store count exceeds 500; WeChat mini-program ordering rolled out across locations; fan communities on WeChat and Weibo grow to millions of members |
| **2024** | Continues measured expansion; maintains focus on quality and cultural identity over rapid scaling; Changsha remains the brand's cultural anchor |

In addition to its stamp card loyalty program, the brand uses other reciprocity tactics: stores provide free tasting samples of new teas to walk-in customers, and the brand gives small gifts during Chinese festivals, such as red envelopes during Spring Festival.

## Questions

**Q1.** Tea Yan Yue Se was available only in Changsha for over seven years, and tourists planned entire trips around visiting the brand. Scarcity increases perceived value when supply is limited, explain how geographic scarcity — not artificial scarcity — drove consumer demand. Why does real scarcity (verifiable, consistent) build trust, while manipulative scarcity ("Only 10 left!" when 100 exist) erodes it? Apply the face test to both Tea Yan's scarcity and a manipulative example.

**Q2.** Founder Lü Liang personally writes product descriptions, and every Tea Yan cup features a hand-written-style poem or cultural reference. The brand positions itself as an authority on "authentic Chinese tea." Authority influences consumer decisions when a source is perceived as credible or expert, explain how Tea Yan Yue Se builds authority without celebrity endorsements or professional certifications. Is cultural authority different from professional authority? Use specific case facts to support your answer.

**Q3.** Tea Yan Yue Se has over one million Xiaohongshu posts, and "Tea Yan guide" is a standard section in Changsha travel guides. Social media shows massive queues, reinforcing the perception that "everyone is drinking it." Consensus or social proof influences decisions when people observe others' behavior, explain how the queue itself functions as social proof beyond just being a consequence of popularity. How does the feedback loop between social media posts and real-world queues amplify consensus?

**Q4.** Tea Yan Yue Se uses Chinese-style cup designs with classical poetry, literary product names like "Orchid Latte" and "Singing Oolong," and staff trained to explain each drink's cultural reference. Liking increases influence when people find a brand attractive, relatable, or culturally aligned, evaluate which element of the liking strategy — visual design, product naming, or staff interaction — is most effective at creating an emotional connection with customers. How does each element contribute differently to the overall sense of liking?

**Q5.** Tea Yan Yue Se's loyalty stamp card offers a free drink after six purchases, stores provide free tasting samples, and the brand gives small gifts during Chinese festivals such as red envelopes during Spring Festival. Reciprocity creates a sense of obligation to return a favor, analyze which of these three reciprocity tactics is most powerful in driving repeat purchases. Consider whether culturally meaningful gifts (red envelopes) create stronger reciprocity than transactional rewards (stamp cards). Use case facts to justify your reasoning.

**Q6.** Fans of Tea Yan Yue Se publicly identify as "茶颜粉" (Tea Yan fans), post check-in photos on social media, and participate in online fan communities with millions of members. Changsha locals describe the brand as "ours." Consistency leads people to act in ways that align with their past behavior and self-image and (unity strengthens when people feel part of an in-group), explain how consistency and unity work together to create brand loyalty that is difficult for competitors to break. Would a customer who has publicly identified as a Tea Yan fan and posted multiple check-in photos be more or less likely to switch to a new competitor? Why?

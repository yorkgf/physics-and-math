# Labubu Goes Global (2024) — Case Handout

> **Topic 1.3 PESTEL Factors** · Read before class. Be ready to discuss.

---

## Background

Labubu is a character designed by Hong Kong-born, Netherlands-based artist Kasing Lung (龙家升). It is a mischievous forest creature with a wide grin and spiky ears, part of a character series called "The Monsters." Labubu is sold as a collectible figurine by Pop Mart (泡泡玛特), China's leading designer toy company.

Labubu figurines are sold as blind boxes — sealed packaging with random designs. Collectors do not know which version they will get until they open the box. In 2024, Labubu went viral globally, driven by social and cultural trends rather than a direct marketing campaign by Pop Mart. The character became a pop culture phenomenon, particularly in Southeast Asia. Resale prices on the secondary market reached 5–10 times the retail price for rare designs. Fans lined up overnight at Pop Mart stores; some locations required police crowd control.

## Key Events

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **2015** | Kasing Lung creates "The Monsters" character series, including Labubu |
| **2019** | Pop Mart signs exclusive licensing deal with Kasing Lung; begins selling Labubu blind boxes in China |
| **2019–2023** | Labubu grows steadily in China as part of Pop Mart's collectible toy ecosystem |
| **2024 (early)** | Labubu goes viral in Southeast Asia — Thailand, Malaysia, Singapore, Philippines — driven by social media and collector communities |
| **2024 (mid)** | Pop Mart stores across Southeast Asia see overnight queues and stockouts; resale prices reach 5–10× retail; Labubu keychains become a fashion accessory and status symbol |
| **2024 (late)** | Trend spreads to Europe and the U.S.; international celebrities and influencers seen with Labubu; Pop Mart's international revenue surges |

## Questions

**1. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

In 2024, Labubu went viral because of social trends — collecting culture, the "surprise" element of blind boxes, and status signaling among young consumers.

(A) Identify the type of external factor (political, economic, social, technological, environmental, or legal) that best describes these trends.

(B) Explain why these trends are classified this way rather than as technological or economic factors.

(C) Explain how a social trend that a company did not create can still benefit the company financially.

**2. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

The Labubu craze spread across Southeast Asia, Europe, and the U.S. within months in 2024.

(A) Identify two specific factors that enabled the trend to spread across countries so quickly.

(B) Explain how each factor you identified contributed to the speed and scale of the trend.

(C) Predict whether the Labubu trend would have reached the same scale without social media platforms.

**3. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

Pop Mart did not create the Labubu viral trend, but the company benefited from it.

(A) Identify one specific action Pop Mart took before 2024 that positioned the company to benefit from the trend.

(B) Explain how that action prepared Pop Mart to respond when demand surged.

(C) Recommend one specific action Pop Mart should take now to prepare for a possible decline in Labubu's popularity.

**4. Respond to parts A, B, and C.**

During the Labubu craze, resale prices on secondary markets reached 5–10 times the retail price.

(A) Identify what the gap between retail and resale prices indicates about the relationship between supply and demand.

(B) Explain why Pop Mart chose not to raise its retail prices to match resale prices.

(C) Predict what would happen to Pop Mart's brand perception and customer loyalty if the company had raised retail prices to match secondary market prices.

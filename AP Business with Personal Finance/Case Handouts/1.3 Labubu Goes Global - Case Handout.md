# Labubu Goes Global (2024) — Case Handout

> **Topic 1.3 PESTEL Factors** · Read before class. Be ready to discuss.

---

## 1. Background Introduction

**Labubu** is a character designed by Hong Kong-born, Netherlands-based artist **Kasing Lung (龙家升)**. It is a mischievous forest creature with a wide grin and spiky ears — part of a series of characters called "The Monsters." Labubu is sold as a collectible figurine by **Pop Mart (泡泡玛特)**, China's leading designer toy company.

In 2024, Labubu went viral — not because of a marketing campaign, but because of **social and cultural trends** that the company did not directly control. The character became a global pop culture phenomenon, particularly in Southeast Asia.

**Key context:**
- Labubu figurines are sold as **blind boxes** — sealed packaging, random designs, collectors do not know which version they will get until they open the box
- The "surprise" element of blind boxes taps into **collecting culture, dopamine-driven purchasing, and social media sharing**
- Resale prices on the secondary market reached **5–10× the retail price** for rare designs
- Fans lined up overnight at Pop Mart stores; some locations required police crowd control

## 2. Key Events

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **2015** | Kasing Lung creates the "The Monsters" character series, including Labubu |
| **2019** | Pop Mart signs an exclusive licensing deal with Kasing Lung; begins selling Labubu blind boxes in China |
| **2019–2023** | Labubu grows steadily in China as part of Pop Mart's collectible toy ecosystem — popular but not yet a global phenomenon |
| **2024 (early)** | Labubu goes viral in **Southeast Asia** — particularly Thailand, Malaysia, Singapore, and the Philippines — driven by social media, celebrity endorsements, and collector communities |
| **2024 (mid)** | Pop Mart stores across Southeast Asia see **overnight queues** and stockouts; resale prices on secondary markets reach **5–10× retail**; Labubu keychains become a fashion accessory and status symbol |
| **2024 (late)** | The trend spreads to **Europe and the U.S.**; international celebrities and influencers are seen with Labubu; Pop Mart's international revenue surges |

## Questions

**Q1.** Labubu went viral because of **social trends** (collecting culture, unboxing dopamine, status signaling), not because of a Pop Mart marketing campaign. Classify these trends as **PESTEL factors** and explain why they are **social** rather than technological or economic. What makes a social trend different from a marketing strategy?

**Q2.** The Labubu craze was amplified by **celebrities and social media**. Identify the **technological factors** that enabled the trend to spread across countries. Without social media platforms, would the trend have reached the same scale? Why or why not?

**Q3.** Pop Mart did not create the Labubu viral trend — but the company **benefited enormously** from it. Explain how a business can take advantage of an **external social factor** it does not control. What did Pop Mart do right (before and during the trend) that positioned it to benefit?

**Q4.** During the Labubu craze, resale prices reached **5–10× the retail price**. Explain what this tells you about the **demand** for the product. If Pop Mart had raised its retail prices to match resale prices, what might have happened to customer loyalty and brand perception?

**Q5.** Compare the impact of **social factors** on Labubu's success with the impact of **political factors** on New Oriental's collapse (see the Double Reduction handout). Both are examples of external PESTEL forces that dramatically changed a business's fortunes. What is **one similarity** and **one difference** between how social factors and political factors affect businesses?

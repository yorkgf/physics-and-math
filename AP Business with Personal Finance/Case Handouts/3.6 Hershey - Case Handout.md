# 3.6 Hershey — The Income Statement

> **Topic:** 3.6 The Income Statement
> **Company:** Hershey

---

## Background

**The Hershey Company** is one of the largest chocolate and confectionery manufacturers in the world, headquartered in Hershey, Pennsylvania. Founded in 1894 by **Milton S. Hershey**, the company produces iconic brands including Hershey's Kisses, Reese's Peanut Butter Cups, Kit Kat (under license in the US), Twizzlers, and Ice Breakers. As of 2024, Hershey generates approximately **$11.2 billion in annual revenue**, employs over **19,000 people**, and sells products in more than **80 countries**.

Hershey's business model is straightforward: the company **manufactures and sells confectionery products** (chocolate, candy, mints, and snacks) through retail channels including grocery stores, convenience stores, vending machines, and increasingly through e-commerce. Approximately **90% of revenue** comes from North America.

In 2024, Hershey faces a **major financial challenge**: the global price of **cocoa beans** — the primary raw material for chocolate — surged to record highs. Cocoa prices reached over **$10,000 per metric ton** in early 2024, more than **tripling** from $3,000 in late 2022. This is the most severe commodity cost increase in the chocolate industry in decades, driven by poor harvests in West Africa (which produces ~70% of the world's cocoa) caused by climate change, disease, and aging trees.

This cocoa price surge directly impacts Hershey's **Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)** and compresses the company's **profit margins**. Hershey's management must decide how to respond: absorb the cost increase (reducing profits), raise prices (risking lower sales volume), reformulate products (reducing cocoa content), or some combination of all three.

## Key Events

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **1894** | Milton S. Hershey founds the Hershey Chocolate Company in Lancaster, Pennsylvania |
| **1900** | Introduces the Hershey's Milk Chocolate Bar — the first mass-produced chocolate in America |
| **1907** | Introduces Hershey's Kisses — now produces over **70 million Kisses per day** |
| **1928** | H.B. Reese invents Reese's Peanut Butter Cups — later acquired by Hershey in 1963 |
| **1970** | Hershey becomes a publicly traded company on the NYSE (ticker: **HSY**) |
| **2018** | Acquires **SkinnyPop** parent company Amplify Snack Brands for $1.6 billion — diversifying beyond chocolate into healthier snacks |
| **2021** | Revenue exceeds $8 billion for the first time; acquires **Pirate's Booty** and other snack brands |
| **2022** | Revenue reaches **$10.4 billion** (+14% year-over-year) driven by price increases and strong demand; net profit reaches $1.64 billion |
| **2023** | Cocoa prices begin rising sharply; Hershey implements additional price increases; revenue grows to **$11.2 billion** but COGS rises faster, compressing margins; net profit falls to $1.45 billion |
| **Early 2024** | Cocoa prices surge past **$10,000/metric ton** (up from $3,000 in late 2022); Hershey announces further price increases of 5–7% and product size reductions; analysts project margin pressure for 2024–2025 |
| **2024** | Revenue slightly declines to **$11.16 billion** as price increases reduce consumer demand; COGS reaches **$6.58 billion**; net profit falls to **$1.50 billion**; Hershey begins exploring cocoa alternatives and supply chain hedging strategies |

## Questions

**1. Income Statement Structure**
Review Hershey's income statement for 2022–2024. An income statement organizes information into revenue, COGS, and operating expenses, plus interest and taxes, identify the three largest line items by dollar amount on Hershey's 2024 income statement. Why is it important to separate costs into these categories rather than listing all expenses in a single total?

**2. Gross Profit and COGS**
Gross profit = revenue − COGS, calculate Hershey's gross profit margin for each year (2022–2024). What specific cost driver caused gross profit to decline from $4.74B to $4.58B even as revenue grew? Why is gross profit margin the first margin analysts examine when evaluating a company facing rising input costs?

**3. Operating Expenses and Operating Profit**
, explain the difference between COGS and operating expenses using specific Hershey examples. If Hershey's advertising spending (a selling expense) increased by $100 million in 2024, would this affect gross profit, operating profit, or both? Explain.

**4. Interest, Taxes, and the Bottom Line**
, trace the calculation from operating profit to net profit for Hershey's 2024 data. Interest expense increased from $0.16B (2022) to $0.21B (2024). What does this suggest about Hershey's use of debt? How does this increase affect pretax income and, ultimately, net profit?

**5. Profit Margin Analysis**
, calculate all three profit margins (gross, operating, net) for both 2022 and 2024. Which margin declined the most in percentage-point terms? What does this tell you about where in the income statement Hershey's financial performance deteriorated most severely?

**6. Benchmarking and Percent Change**
, answer both parts: (a) Hershey's 2024 gross profit margin (41.0%) is above the industry average (~40%). Does this mean Hershey has no reason to be concerned? Why or why not? (b) Using the percent change formula, calculate the rate of change in COGS from 2022 to 2024. Compare this to the rate of change in revenue over the same period. What does the gap between these two percentages reveal?

**7. Financial Uncertainty and PESTEL**
Businesses face financial uncertainty because incomes and expenses fluctuate in response to competitive pressure and PESTEL forces, identify which specific PESTEL force (Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Environmental, Legal) is primarily responsible for the cocoa price surge. How does this external force propagate through Hershey's income statement — which specific line items are affected, and in what order?

**8. Projected Income Statements**
, review the projected 2025 income statement for Hershey. (a) The projection assumes revenue grows 3% while COGS grows 3.3%. Why would Hershey accept a scenario where costs grow faster than revenue? (b) What are three specific risks that could cause Hershey's actual 2025 results to be significantly worse than the projection?

**9. Consumer Budgeting Parallel**
, draw a direct parallel between Hershey's income statement and a consumer's monthly budget. If a consumer earns $5,000/month after taxes (net pay), spends $2,000 on essentials (rent, food, transportation), $1,500 on discretionary items (entertainment, dining out), and $300 on student loan payments, calculate their "net profit margin" (savings ÷ income). How does this compare to Hershey's 2024 net profit margin? What lesson from Hershey's margin compression applies to personal budgeting?

**10. Revenue Estimation**
Future revenues are estimated based on pricing strategies and market research about customer demand and industry trends, explain how Hershey would estimate its 2025 revenue. If Hershey implements a 4% price increase but market research predicts a 1% decline in unit volume, what is the projected revenue growth rate? Under what conditions could this estimate be significantly wrong?

**11. Expense Estimation and Budgeting**
Future COGS and operating expenses are estimated based on planned production processes, supply chain costs, and projected expenses, answer: (a) Hershey's COGS projection for 2025 is $6.80B. What specific data inputs would Hershey use to arrive at this number? (b) Why is R&D spending projected to increase 8% — faster than any other operating expense category? How does this spending relate to Hershey's response to the cocoa cost challenge?

**12. Developing a Projected Income Statement**
, imagine you are developing a projected income statement for a new chocolate startup that plans to sell artisan chocolate bars for $8 each. You expect to sell 50,000 bars in Year 1. Your COGS is $3.50 per bar. Fixed operating expenses (rent, salaries, marketing) total $120,000 per year. Interest expense is $5,000. The tax rate is 24%. Calculate the projected: (a) revenue, (b) gross profit and gross profit margin, (c) operating profit and operating profit margin, and (d) net profit and net profit margin.

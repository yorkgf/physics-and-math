## 1. How the AP Exam Asks These (0:00)

- On the AP exam, many multiple-choice questions are **attached to a stimulus** — a short business scenario that feeds several questions in a row — and the rest **stand alone**.
- This video: **3 stimulus questions + 3 standalone**, each testing a different concept.
- Recommended approach: read → pause → **commit to your own answer** → then see why the right answer is right and where the traps are.

## 2. The Stimulus: LumiDesk (0:32)

> **LumiDesk** is a startup that designs height-adjustable study desks for students living in small apartments. Each desk costs LumiDesk $90 to manufacture. Market research shows customers value the desk at about $260 because it converts from a sitting desk to a standing desk and folds flat against the wall when not in use. LumiDesk currently sells the desk for $180. Recently, several tutoring centers have begun purchasing desks for their classrooms, where enrolled students use them during lessons.

Three numbers to remember:

- Manufacturing cost $C=\$90$
- Customer value (willingness to pay) $=\$260$
- Selling price $P=\$180$

## 3. Q1–Q3 (shared LumiDesk scenario)

### Q1 (1:21) — Who is the consumer?

> For the desks purchased by tutoring centers, which best identifies the **consumer**?
>
> A. the tutoring centers  B. LumiDesk  C. the students who use the desks during lessons  D. the parents who pay tuition to the tutoring centers

**Answer: C.**
**Why:** The consumer is the individual who **uses** the good, whether or not they bought it. The tutoring center **purchased** (customer); the students **use** them (consumers).
**Traps:**

- A is the **customer** placed in a consumer question — to catch anyone who blurs the two roles.
- D points at a **real but different transaction** (parents paying tuition), not the exchange the question asked about.

> **Tip: Answer the transaction the question points at — don't get pulled into a nearby, plausible-looking exchange.**

### Q2 (2:19) — Compute value captured

> Based on the scenario, what is the amount of **value captured** by LumiDesk per desk at its current price?
>
> A. $80  B. $90  C. $170  D. $260

**Answer: B.**

$$
\text{Value Captured}=\text{Price}-\text{Cost}=180-90=\$90 \text{ per desk}
$$

**Trap: A ($80).** $80 = $260 − $180, the gap between what customers think the desk is worth and what they actually pay. That number is **real but belongs to the customer** — the benefit the buyer keeps. It's built from the **same three numbers**, which is exactly why it's there.

> **Tip: In a capture question, write price − cost before you look at the choices.**

### Q3 (3:22) — Why is it value creation?

> Which best explains why LumiDesk's product demonstrates **value creation**?
>
> A. the desk is sold at a price higher than its manufacturing cost  B. the desk responds to the space problem faced by students in small apartments  C. the desk generates revenue from both individual customers and tutoring centers  D. the desk's manufacturing cost is low relative to competitors

**Answer: B.**
**Why:** Value creation occurs when a product **responds to a customer's problem, need, or want**. The small-apartment space problem is what this product was built for.
**Trap: A** — that's the **definition of value capture** placed inside a value-creation question. The video says **this swap is the most common trap in this topic**, so expect it again on exam day.

> **Tip: Creation questions point at the customer's problem; capture questions point at price and cost.**

## 4. Q4–Q6 (standalone, one concept each)

### Q4 (4:28) — problem-solution fit

> A snack company notices that gym members want high-protein food they can eat immediately after workouts, and it develops a refrigerated protein snack sold in vending machines inside gyms. Which concept does this best illustrate?
>
> A. value capture  B. a barrier to entry  C. problem-solution fit  D. a need being converted into a want

**Answer: C.**
**Why:** A specific want was identified, a product was developed that matches it, and it's placed at the exact point where the want occurs. That match between problem and product = problem-solution fit.
**Trap: B.** Barrier to entry is **real vocabulary from Topic 1.2**, used here too early. The exam borrows terms from neighboring topics to build wrong answers.

> **Tip: Recognizing a term is never enough on its own — it has to match what the scenario describes.**

### Q5 (5:42) — goods vs services

> HomeGuard sells home security packages. Customers pay a one-time price for a camera and doorbell unit plus a monthly fee for 24-hour monitoring through an app. Which best describes HomeGuard's products?
>
> A. goods only  B. services only  C. both goods and services  D. neither

**Answer: C.**
**Why:** The camera is a tangible item (good); monitoring is work performed for compensation (service). The company sells both, and the scenario prices them separately to make the split visible.
**Trap: D** — it **mixes two different vocabulary sets**.

> **Tip:** Needs/wants describe **why** customers buy; goods/services describe **what** businesses sell. A product can address a need and still be a good, a service, or both.

### Q6 (6:45) — choosing customers (market opportunity)

> A new meal-delivery startup has limited funding, and its founders are deciding which customers to serve first. Which approach best reflects how businesses address market opportunities?
>
> A. serve every possible customer in the city to maximize total sales  B. select a specific customer group whose problem the startup can solve better than competitors  C. target the customers with the mildest problems since they are easiest to satisfy  D. delay choosing customers until the product is fully perfected

**Answer: B.**
**Why:** A business **cannot satisfy all potential customers**, so it selects which problems and which customers to focus on. The selection questions from the concept video (severity, how many share it, can solve better) point directly to B.
**Trap: A** — "serving everyone" reads as ambition but functions as the **wrong answer**. The video says some version of it **will appear on the real exam**.

## 5. Trap Summary (patterns across all six)

| # | Trap type | Question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Putting the customer definition in a consumer question (or vice versa) | Q1 |
| 2 | Using the "customer's kept benefit" number (value − price) to distract from the "business capture" calc (price − cost) | Q2 |
| 3 | Putting the value-capture definition inside a value-creation question (**most common trap**) | Q3 |
| 4 | Borrowing a real term from a neighboring topic (1.2) as a distractor | Q4 |
| 5 | Confusing needs/wants (why buy) with goods/services (what's sold) | Q5 |
| 6 | "Serve everyone" disguised as ambition — the wrong answer | Q6 |

**Three cross-question tips:**

- **Capture questions:** write $\text{price}-\text{cost}$ first, then look at choices.
- **Creation questions:** look for the customer's problem, not price and cost.
- **Any option:** the term must **match the scenario** — recognizing the word isn't enough.

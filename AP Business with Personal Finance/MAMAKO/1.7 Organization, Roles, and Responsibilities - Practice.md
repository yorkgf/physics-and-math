## 2. The Stimulus: Tumble Route (0:19)

> **Aiko Mori** and **Jordan Lee** started **Tumble Route**, a laundry pickup-and-delivery service, **with a spoken agreement and no legal filings.** Aiko manages the routes, machines, and daily schedule, while Jordan handles pricing, advertising, and the accounts — a split they chose because it matches what each does best. Last month a **customer's designer jacket was ruined in a machine**, and the customer is demanding **\$800.** The partners are now weighing two changes: (1) **reorganizing as a limited liability company**, or (2) **incorporating** so they can sell shares to fund **twenty new delivery vans.**

Note the artifacts: a **partnership** (spoken agreement, no filings), a **role split by strengths**, and two **structural-change** options (LLC vs. corporation).

## 3. Q1–Q3 (shared Tumble Route scenario)

### Q1 (1:02) — liability for the ruined jacket

> Which best describes the partners' **current** legal situation regarding the ruined jacket?
>
> A. Only Aiko is liable, because the machines are her responsibility.  B. The business alone is liable, because Tumble Route made the agreement with the customer.  C. Neither partner is liable until the business incorporates.  D. Both partners are **personally liable** for the business's debts and obligations, including this claim.

**Answer: D.** Tumble Route is a partnership, and **partners are personally liable for all business debts and obligations** — so the \$800 claim can reach **both partners' personal money** regardless of whose task went wrong.
**Traps:**

- **A divides liability by job duties** — the law does no such thing for a partnership: the **internal split of roles decides who does the work and changes nothing about who owes the debts.**
- **B describes the protection an LLC or corporation provides** (owners avoid personal liability because debts belong to the business) — exactly the protection **Tumble Route has skipped building.**

### Q2 (2:19) — classify the Aiko/Jordan split

> Aiko running operations while Jordan handles pricing and accounts best illustrates which?
>
> A. Partners dividing responsibilities according to each partner's strengths and interests.  B. The formation of specialized departments.  C. Outsourcing of business functions.  D. A transfer of ownership between partners.

**Answer: A.** Partners carry full responsibility together and typically **divide the roles by strengths and interests** — precisely the route-versus-accounts split.
**Trap: B.** Departments are how **large** businesses organize **many employees** with specific skills into functional teams; **two owners splitting hats** is the small-business version of the same instinct, **without any departments existing yet.**

> **Scale is the tell: two partners *divide roles*; a large staff *organizes into departments*.**

### Q3 (3:23) — the tradeoff of incorporating

> If the partners **incorporate** to fund the twenty new vans, which best describes the tradeoff they accept?
>
> A. They keep full control of decisions while gaining access to funding.  B. They become personally liable for a larger share of business debts.  C. They **cede control** to shareholders and an elected board of directors in exchange for greater access to funding and room to grow.  D. They lose the ability to earn profit from the business.

**Answer: C.** A corporation typically gains **greater access to funding**, and the **price is control** — owners cede decision-making power to shareholders and an elected board, and the **company itself controls the profits and carries the liability.**
**Traps:**

- **A promises the funding without the price.** The structures **do** trade against each other — **control and funding pull in opposite directions** across the four types.
- **B inverts the liability rule** — incorporating **shrinks** personal exposure (the corporation itself becomes the liable party).

## 4. Q4–Q6 (standalone)

### Q4 (4:37) — identify the department

> A department at a mid-size company **tracks the year's expenditures and earnings and prepares the financial statements** that management reviews each quarter. Which best identifies the department?
>
> A. Finance  B. Accounting  C. Operations  D. Research and development

**Answer: B.** **Accounting** tracks expenditures and earnings and prepares financial statements to monitor financial health — the question restates that job **almost word for word.**
**Trap: A** — the **swap pair** for this topic. **Finance** secures and manages funds and uses financial data to recommend strategies **going forward.**

> **Records look backward, funding looks forward:** a department preparing statements about money that **already moved** is **accounting** — **even when a scenario sprinkles the word "financial" everywhere.**

### Q5 (5:36) — data table

The table describes three businesses:

| Business | Owners | Liability | Stock / funding |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **1 owner** | owner **personally liable** | savings + a bank loan |
| **B** | **4 owners, called "members"** | owners **not** personally liable | **cannot issue stock** |
| **C** | **~3,000 shareholders** | (corporate shield) | funded partly by **issuing shares** + a board of directors |

> Which correctly identifies businesses **A, B, and C, in that order?**
>
> A. Sole proprietorship, corporation, LLC  B. Partnership, LLC, corporation  C. LLC, partnership, corporation  D. Sole proprietorship, LLC, corporation

**Answer: D.**

- **A** = one owner + personal liability → **sole proprietorship**
- **B** = owners called "members" + liability shield + no stock → **LLC**
- **C** = shareholders + board + stock funding → **corporation**

**Trap: B** — the **first row is the tell:** a partnership requires **two or more owners**, and business A has **one**, so the whole option **dies on a single word in the table.**

> **Read every row against every feature** — these identification tables **hide the decisive detail in the least glamorous column.**

### Q6 (7:09) — outsourcing a support line

> A software company **hires an outside firm** to run its customer-support phone lines because **support wages in its city are unusually high.** Which best describes this decision?
>
> A. The company has made customer support one of its **core competencies.**  B. The company has converted the support staff into internal stakeholders.  C. The company is **outsourcing** a function because doing so is likely to reduce costs.  D. The company has reorganized as a corporation.

**Answer: C.** Outsourcing = hiring another business to handle a function; the two classic triggers are **missing skills** and **high labor costs** — high support wages are the second trigger **verbatim.**
**Trap: A** — **points the concept backward.** Core competencies are capabilities a business **develops itself** (they help it outperform rivals). **Handing a function to an outside firm is the opposite move** — the company has decided support is a **cost to manage**, so the function moves outside.

## 5. Trap Summary (patterns across all six)

| # | Trap type | Question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Liability in a partnership is **shared and personal** — **not** divided by who did the work; the LLC/corp shield is what they *haven't* built yet | Q1 |
| 2 | **Partners dividing roles ≠ departments** — scale is the tell (2 owners vs. a large staff) | Q2 |
| 3 | Corporation: **control and funding pull opposite directions** (can't keep both); incorporating **shrinks** personal liability | Q3 |
| 4 | **Accounting (records, backward) vs. Finance (funds, forward)** — the word "financial" is unreliable | Q4 |
| 5 | ID tables: check **every feature in every row**; the killer detail hides in a **boring column** (partnership needs 2+ owners) | Q5 |
| 6 | **Outsourcing ≠ core competency** — handing a function *out* (cost-driven) vs. developing one *in-house* are opposite moves | Q6 |

**Three cross-question tips:**

- **Partnership liability:** personal and **shared** — the role split is about *work*, not *debt*.
- **The accounting/finance swap:** records look **backward**, funding looks **forward** — ignore the word "financial."
- **Structure tradeoffs run in opposite directions:** more control → less funding; sole/partner → personal liability, LLC/corp → shielded. And never import "core competency" into an outsourcing scenario.

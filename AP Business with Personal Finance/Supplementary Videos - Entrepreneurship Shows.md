# Supplementary Videos — Entrepreneurship Reality Shows

> For classroom use alongside AP Unit 1 (especially 1.4 How Business Ideas Originate).
> Each pitch is tagged with the **AP concept** it illustrates.
>
> **Suggested use:** Play a 5–10 minute pitch in class → students identify the source, viability, and risk–return → class discussion.

---

## Shark Tank (创智赢家) — Individual Pitches

### 1. Bombas — "Better Socks, Bigger Mission"

- **YouTube:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RlALuStBplY
- **Bilibili (full season):** https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1ox411G79J (S1)
- **What happens:** Two founders pitch premium athletic socks with a "buy one, donate one" model for the homeless.
- **AP 1.4 concepts:**
  - **Gap:** Existing socks were uncomfortable and poorly designed → unmet need for performance socks
  - **Opportunity:** "Buy one, donate one" social enterprise trend (cf. TOMS Shoes)
  - **Existing business:** Adapted the existing sock market with a differentiated product
  - **Risk–return:** Asked for $200K for 5% equity → deal with Daymond John → now $300M+ revenue
- **Best for:** Gap identification, social enterprise, risk–return tradeoff

---

### 2. Scrub Daddy — "The Smile Face Sponge"

- **YouTube:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ae5MssJ8en4
- **Bilibili analysis:** https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1LxiYB3EkK (4 min, 18K views)
- **What happens:** An inventor pitches a sponge that changes texture based on water temperature, shaped like a smiley face.
- **AP 1.4 concepts:**
  - **Gap:** Existing sponges were either too soft or too hard → no adaptive cleaning tool
  - **Opportunity:** New material technology (temperature-responsive texture)
  - **Risk–return:** Asked for $100K for 10% → deal with Lori Greiner → now $300M+ in sales, most successful Shark Tank product ever
- **Best for:** Innovation-driven opportunity, how technology creates new products

---

### 3. Ring (Doorbell Camera) — "Rejected on Shark Tank"

- **YouTube:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=um-iVXiXedc
- **What happens:** Founder Jamie Siminoff pitches a WiFi-enabled video doorbell. All five sharks pass. He leaves with no deal.
- **AP 1.4 concepts:**
  - **Gap:** Homeowners couldn't see who was at their door when away → unmet security need
  - **Opportunity:** WiFi + smartphone technology made video doorbells possible for the first time
  - **Risk–return:** Rejected on the show → founder kept going → Amazon acquired Ring for **$1 billion**
  - **Key lesson:** Rejection doesn't mean the idea isn't viable — the sharks were wrong
- **Best for:** Risk–return (high risk, massive return), viability (market validation after rejection), perseverance

---

### 4. Copa Di Vino — "The Wine Pitch That Came Back"

- **YouTube:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lh21LcjB1Nk
- **What happens:** Founder James Martin pitches single-serve premium wine in a sealed glass. He appears on Shark Tank **twice** — and is rejected both times.
- **AP 1.4 concepts:**
  - **Gap:** Wine drinkers wanted convenience (single-serve, portable) without sacrificing quality
  - **Existing business:** Adapted the single-serve beverage concept (like juice boxes) for wine
  - **Risk–return:** Rejected twice on national TV → still built a $60M+ business independently
- **Best for:** Gap vs. opportunity distinction, persistence, "no deal" risk–return

---

### 5. The Comfy — "The Wearable Blanket"

- **YouTube:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IK_JDKOLP8g
- **What happens:** Two brothers pitch an oversized hoodie-blanket hybrid. They get a deal with Barbara Corcoran.
- **AP 1.4 concepts:**
  - **Gap:** People wanted to be warm and comfortable at home/outdoors → no product combined a blanket with wearable clothing
  - **Social trend:** "Cozy lifestyle" trend + social media virality
  - **Risk–return:** Asked for $50K for 20% → deal → now $150M+ in sales
- **Best for:** Gap identification, social trends creating demand, low-risk/low-investment success

---

### 6. Groovebook — "Photo Books from Your Phone"

- **YouTube:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wO8LZ1bONh4
- **What happens:** A husband-and-wife team pitch a subscription photo book service. They get a deal with Mark Cuban and Kevin O'Leary.
- **AP 1.4 concepts:**
  - **Gap:** Smartphone users had thousands of photos but never printed them → unmet need for easy, affordable photo printing
  - **Opportunity:** Smartphone camera technology + subscription model trend
  - **Risk–return:** $2.4M deal on the show → Shutterfly acquired Groovebook for $14.5M
- **Best for:** Technology opportunity, subscription business model, exit strategy

## Quick Reference — Which Pitch for Which Concept?

| AP Concept | Best Pitch(es) |
|---|---|
| **Gap** (unmet need) | Bombas, Scrub Daddy, The Comfy, Groovebook |
| **Opportunity** (technology/change) | Scrub Daddy, Ring, Groovebook |
| **Existing business** (replication) | Copa Di Vino, Bombas |
| **Risk–return** (high risk → high return) | Ring (rejected → $1B), Copa Di Vino (rejected twice → $60M) |
| **Viability** (will it survive?) | All pitches — sharks evaluate viability in real time |
| **Social enterprise** | Bombas (buy one, donate one) |
| **"No deal" case study** | Ring, Copa Di Vino |

# Review Case Of Unit 1

## Case: Xibei (西贝莜面村) — A Trust Premium Under Pressure

**Xibei Youmian Village (西贝莜面村)** is a Chinese casual-dining chain serving northwestern food built around oat noodles (莜面). What it actually sells is **trust**: the brand promise is *食材安全、亲子友好* — safe ingredients and child-friendly service — and customers have accepted a **trust premium** on that basis. In 2025 that promise was tested in public.

**Founded** — 1988, by Jia Guolong (贾国龙) in Inner Mongolia  
**Scale** — ≈370 direct-operated stores, ≈18,000 employees, revenue ≈¥6.2 billion (2023)  
**Price** — about 40% more per head than comparable family restaurants such as 外婆家 and 绿茶 (≈¥110 vs ≈¥75)  
**Legal form** — a **private limited-liability company** (有限责任公司); unlisted, no stock-market shareholders, controlled by its founder  
**Vision (long-term goal)** — “to be China’s most trusted family restaurant”  
**Mission (what it does every day)** — safe ingredients, child-friendly service, and dishes finished fresh in the store, at a premium price  
**Production** — a **central kitchen** model: ingredients are cut, marinated and partly cooked at large central kitchens, cold-chain delivered, then finished in store (heated, assembled, seasoned, plated). Kitchens are open to view (明厨亮灶), but customers see only the final step. The model is legal, standard in the industry, and lowers cost per dish while keeping taste consistent and food safety controllable.  
**Cost base** — central kitchens, a fixed menu and volume purchasing hold food and labour cost per dish below a scratch kitchen’s, while the premium price sits on top  
**Control** — one founder, one family: decisions were historically made inside the company, not with outside owners or the public

**2025-09-10** Luo Yonghao (罗永浩) posts that Xibei’s dishes are “almost all pre-made” while prices are high, and calls for a law requiring restaurants to disclose it.  
**2025-09-11** Xibei denies the claim, cites the official six-department 2024 definition (a chain’s own central-kitchen preparation is **not** 预制菜), and announces a defamation lawsuit.  
**Within days** revenue falls ≈¥1 million **per day**; frontline staff report being insulted by customers; the company pays a ¥500 subsidy to store staff and calls an all-staff meeting (“the battle of 18,000 employees vs Luo Yonghao”).  
**2025-09-14/15** Jia admits “my way of responding was wrong”, apologises, pledges “a completely transparent Xibei” and “to learn thoroughly from Pangdonglai”, and switches nine dishes to fully in-store cooking.  
**2025-11** the dispute reignites; business is down 40–60% over four months; more than ¥300 million in coupons are issued to win customers back.  
**2026-01-15** Xibei confirms **102 store closures — about 30% of all locations — affecting roughly 4,000 employees**; January revenue is down ≈50% year-on-year.  
**Both sides** — Jia: the damage came from a fight over a definition, and the company acted legally. Luo: “No company fails just because it was criticized.” The dispute is contested; no court has ruled.

**Key dates:** 1988 founded · 2024 official 预制菜 definition · 2025-09 public dispute · 2025-11 relapsed · 2026-01 store closures

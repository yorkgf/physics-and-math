# Mid-Term Exam Instructions

- **Time allowed: 120 minutes. Total: 100 points.**
- Answer **all** questions and write in the spaces provided.
- **Section A — Multiple choice (45 points).** Choose the ONE best answer for each question and fill in the circle ◯ before that option. If you fill in more than one circle for a question, that question is not marked.
- **Section B — Short answer (35 points).** Answer about **your own Business Canvas Project**. For each question: **name the concept, define it briefly, then apply it to your own business** using specific evidence from your own customer interviews, market research and product decisions. A conclusion with no explanation earns no credit.
- **Section C — Case analysis (20 points).** The Patagonia case is on the separate sheet provided at the start of this section. Answer both questions below using the case information; the key terms printed under each question are the terms you should use.
- No calculators, phones or other electronic devices.

## How to mark your answers in Section A (example)

Fill the circle **completely** with a dark pen or pencil, so that ◯ becomes ●, and leave every circle you are **not** choosing empty.

**Example.** Which action best protects a small business from running out of raw materials?

| Mark | Option |
|---|---|
| **●** | **A.** Sign a contract with a second supplier as a backup. — *the answer you chose: the circle is fully filled in* |
| **◯** | **B.** Buy all materials from the cheapest supplier. — *not chosen: the circle is left empty* |
| **◯** | **C.** Store two years of materials in the stockroom. |
| **◯** | **D.** Stop tracking supplier delivery times. |

- ✔ **Right:** one circle completely filled in (◯ → ●).
- ✘ **Wrong:** a tick ✓, a slash /, or a half-filled ring ⊘ in the circle; or two or more circles filled in on the same question — that question then scores **0**.
- If you change your mind, cross out the whole line of the old answer and write your new letter in the margin next to the question.

# SECTION A — MULTIPLE CHOICE (45 points)




**Q1.** A product's sales have been flat for two years, most customers in the market already own one, and new rivals are cutting prices. The product is in the ______ stage of the product life cycle, and the most appropriate strategy is to:

◯ A. Introduction — focus on raising brand awareness.

◯ B. Maturity — defend market share through differentiation, lower prices, and innovation.

◯ C. Decline — cut costs or discontinue the product.

◯ D. Growth — expand distribution and scale production.



**Q2.** A tea shop posts: "Only 12 tins of this single-origin oolong are left at this price — one tin per customer." Customers immediately feel urgency to buy. Which principle of influence is the shop using?

◯ A. Consistency — customers want to stay consistent with their earlier purchases.

◯ B. Authority — customers obey the shop because of its expertise.

◯ C. Scarcity — the rarer an item seems, the more people want it.

◯ D. Consensus — customers follow what other buyers have chosen.



**Q3.** A customer buys a ¥12 coffee on the way to work without comparing options, but carefully compares three laptops before buying one. Which statement is most accurate?

◯ A. The coffee purchase is habitual buying behavior, while the laptop purchase is likely to follow the rational decision-making process.

◯ B. Both purchases are habitual, because the customer is loyal to both brands.

◯ C. Both purchases are rational, because the customer spends money in both cases.

◯ D. The coffee purchase is rational because it meets a need, and the laptop purchase is habitual because laptops are expensive.



**Q4.** A shopper always buys the unbranded version of ibuprofen because it costs half as much and works the same. This product is best described as:

◯ A. A differentiated product, because the shopper prefers the plain version.

◯ B. A generic product, sold to customers who prioritise cost savings over brand recognition.

◯ C. A brand with high brand equity, because the shopper is loyal to it.

◯ D. An example of a barrier to entry protecting the generic manufacturer.



**Q5.** Before buying a restaurant meal, a customer now checks three apps for the lowest price and reads reviews from other customers. Which statement best describes this change?

◯ A. A situational influence, because the customer happened to be standing in the street.

◯ B. A personal factor, because the customer's budget has changed.

◯ C. Technology and innovation influencing consumer purchasing patterns, on both the buyer and the seller side.

◯ D. A psychological factor, because the customer has learned to prefer reviews.



**Q6.** A tutoring company has no factory and no warehouse, yet it still has a supply chain. Which combination makes up its service supply chain?

◯ A. Suppliers, distributors, and retailers

◯ B. Raw materials, manufacturing, and inventory

◯ C. Advertising, market research, and pricing

◯ D. Employees, resources, and delivery systems



**Q7.** After two rounds of research a team receives contradictory answers: some students love the app's design, others say the price is too high, and usage data shows most users drop off after one week. Which is the best professional response?

◯ A. Keep only the finding that supports the original idea and ignore the rest.

◯ B. Reject the research as invalid, because a business must never act on contradictory data.

◯ C. Accept the limitation — businesses often cannot accurately predict customer responses and must respond using limited, unclear or contradictory data.

◯ D. Rerun the same survey with the same sample and questions until the answers agree.



**Q8.** A company competes by charging the lowest price in its market. Which supply chain design is most consistent with that strategy?

◯ A. Artisan production using highly skilled labor and exclusive long-term supplier contracts.

◯ B. Exclusive agreements that prevent suppliers from selling to rivals.

◯ C. Mass production with a cost-focused chain that uses cheaper resources and more efficient production processes.

◯ D. A supply chain built on high-quality resources and premium materials.



**Q9.** A company's logo is recognized and trusted by customers, who choose it over identical products sold by other businesses. The added financial and competitive value created by that recognition and trust is best described as:

◯ A. Product messaging

◯ B. A value proposition

◯ C. Brand equity

◯ D. Market saturation



**Q10.** A shopper buys a coat partly because her colleagues all own similar coats, and partly because the fitting room is warm and the store is playing music. Which is the best classification?

◯ A. The colleagues are a psychological factor; the store atmosphere is a personal factor.

◯ B. Both are personal factors, because they come from the shopper's own life.

◯ C. The colleagues are a personal factor; the store atmosphere is a psychological factor.

◯ D. The colleagues are a social and cultural factor; the store atmosphere is a situational influence.



**Q11.** After a city bans the sale of energy drinks to minors, a new brand of caffeinated drinks for adults appears, and teenagers switch to it. Which concept best explains the new brand's existence?

◯ A. A purchasing pattern, because teenagers now buy drinks more frequently.

◯ B. Market segmentation, because the new brand serves a different demographic.

◯ C. A barrier to entry, because the ban protects the new brand from competitors.

◯ D. Substitute products — the underlying need persists, so alternatives emerge when one option is restricted.



**Q12.** A startup has built a working prototype that customers say they want, but the team cannot find a supplier able to produce the materials at any price, and projected revenue would not cover costs even if it could. Which statement best applies the desirability–feasibility–viability test?

◯ A. The product is viable because customers want it, so the startup should begin mass production immediately.

◯ B. The product appears desirable, but it fails the feasibility test (it cannot be produced) and the viability test (it cannot profit), so it should not be pursued.

◯ C. The product is feasible because a prototype exists, so the only question left is marketing.

◯ D. The product fails only the viability test, since the prototype already exists.



**Q13.** A team sketches a meal-kit box and shows the sketch to 20 classmates, who say the box looks nice. The team then builds a simple version with only five core features and sells it to 50 real customers at full price to see whether they buy. According to the six stages of product development, the sketch belongs to the design stage, and the sellable five-feature version is:

◯ A. A minimum viable product, used in the validation stage to assess product-market fit.

◯ B. A prototype, used in the messaging stage to build brand awareness.

◯ C. A prototype, used in the ideation stage to generate new ideas.

◯ D. A minimum viable product, used in the production stage to test suppliers.



**Q14.** According to the CED, a business develops its brand identity in order to be consistent with which of the following?

◯ A. Its vision statement and the product's value proposition, so that the brand appeals to the target customer.

◯ B. Its product's cost structure, so that the brand signals good value.

◯ C. Its annual profit target, so that investors recognise the brand.

◯ D. Its competitors' brand identities, so that customers can compare easily.



**Q15.** A bakery's supply chain includes local wheat farms, a mill, the bakery itself, a storage room for finished loaves, and two shops that sell them. Which correctly orders the stages?

◯ A. Raw materials → manufacturing → warehouse → distribution/retail → customer

◯ B. Supplier → distributor → retailer → manufacturing → customer

◯ C. Manufacturing → raw materials → warehouse → customer → retail

◯ D. Raw materials → distribution center → manufacturing → warehouse → customer



**Q16.** A customer buys the same brand of shampoo every month because it is the cheapest and the store is on the way home. This is best described as:

◯ A. A purchasing pattern based on psychographic characteristics.

◯ B. Brand loyalty, because the customer buys the same brand every time.

◯ C. A repeat purchase rather than brand loyalty, because there is no emotional attachment to the brand.

◯ D. A habit driven by brand equity, which is why the customer will pay more in future.



**Q17.** A business doubles its output. Its revenue rises by 100% while its total cost rises by only 60%, because its fixed costs are now spread across twice as many units. Which statement is correct?

◯ A. Scaling can only be achieved by building exclusive agreements with suppliers.

◯ B. Scaling failed, because total costs always rise when output doubles.

◯ C. Scaling succeeded, because revenue grew faster than cost and each unit now costs less to produce.

◯ D. The business has a cost problem, because costs rose at all.



**Q18.** A business spends ¥12,000 on marketing, advertising, and sales in a month and acquires 400 new customers. Those customers are expected to spend an average of ¥150 with the business over the next three years. Which statement is correct?

◯ A. CAC is ¥12,000 and LTV is ¥150, so the business should cut marketing spending.

◯ B. CAC is ¥30 and LTV is ¥150, but the business should judge CAC alone, since LTV cannot be estimated.

◯ C. CAC is ¥150 per customer and LTV is ¥30, so the business should raise advertising spending.

◯ D. CAC is ¥30 per customer and LTV is ¥150, so building strong relationships is profitable.



**Q19.** To size the market for running shoes and identify its main competitors, a business reads an industry report and visits competitors' websites. This is best described as:

◯ A. Primary-source research, because the business collected the information itself.

◯ B. Secondary-source research, because the business is using information that already exists to map the market landscape.

◯ C. Qualitative research, because the report is full of descriptions rather than numbers.

◯ D. A business hypothesis, because the report is an assumption the business will test.



**Q20.** A clothing brand can buy fabric from three mills: Mill A is the cheapest but sits in a flood-prone region; Mill B is mid-priced with reliable delivery; Mill C is the most expensive and the only Fair Trade–certified supplier. Which statement best reflects how a business should choose a supplier?

◯ A. Choose Mill B, because supplier reputation and quality are not supply chain factors.

◯ B. Choose Mill A, because the lowest cost always produces the highest profit.

◯ C. Weigh cost, quality, efficiency, convenience and risk together; choosing on price alone ignores the risk of delays and higher costs.

◯ D. Always choose Mill C, because quality is the most important factor in every situation.



**Q21.** A product launched three months ago has low sales and negative profit, and the company is spending heavily on advertising to build awareness. Which product life cycle stage is it in, and what should the marketing focus be?

◯ A. Introduction — sales and revenue are typically low, so marketing focuses on tactics that raise brand awareness.

◯ B. Decline — sales are falling, so marketing should cut costs or discontinue the product.

◯ C. Growth — sales are rising quickly, so marketing should focus on expanding distribution.

◯ D. Maturity — the market is saturated, so marketing should defend market share with lower prices.



**Q22.** A snack brand designs one product line and one message for busy high-school students, instead of trying to appeal to everyone. Which statement best explains the reasoning?

◯ A. Targeting a specific segment is typically more effective and less costly than appealing to a broad audience.

◯ B. Designing for one segment is always more profitable, because a broad audience can never be profitable.

◯ C. Broad audiences always respond better to marketing than narrow segments do.

◯ D. Narrow targeting reduces the number of competitors the business has to face.



**Q23.** A business believes students will pay more for a study-planner app with a shared calendar feature than for the same app without it. To test this, it builds both versions, randomly shows each version to real students visiting its site, and records which version produces more paid sign-ups. Which description is most accurate?

◯ A. The business conducted a survey, because it asked a large number of students for their preference.

◯ B. The business ran an observation study, because it watched students browse the site without interacting.

◯ C. The business conducted secondary-source research, because it used data that already existed.

◯ D. The business tested a hypothesis with an A/B test, a form of primary-source research, and the result is which version produced more sign-ups.



**Q24.** A student researcher surveys only classmates and finds that 90% would buy an energy drink before class, then charts her monthly website visits. Which statement best identifies both problems?

◯ A. The sample is not representative of the target market, and a pie chart is the wrong tool for trends over time.

◯ B. The data is qualitative rather than quantitative, so it cannot be charted at all.

◯ C. There is no problem: classmates are genuine target customers, and any chart can show a trend.

◯ D. The sample is too small, and a bar chart should be used to compare discrete data points.



**Q25.** A shop gives every customer a free repair kit with their purchase. A week later many of those customers return and buy more, saying they felt they owed the shop something. Which principle of influence is at work?

◯ A. Reciprocity — people feel obligated to give something back after receiving something of value.

◯ B. Consistency — customers want to stay true to their earlier purchase.

◯ C. Consensus — customers follow what most other buyers have done.

◯ D. Liking — customers are persuaded by people they find similar to them.



**Q26.** A small online shop collects customers' phone numbers for delivery but never tells customers that it also sells those numbers to a marketing partner. Which statement best describes the problem?

◯ A. A privacy violation, because data is collected without the customer's awareness or disclosure; the business must weigh the benefits of customer data against the risks to customers and to its own reputation.

◯ B. A data breach, because stored customer information has been exposed to outsiders.

◯ C. No problem, because the customers provided the numbers voluntarily.

◯ D. Effective targeting, because customer data always lowers customer acquisition cost.



**Q27.** A bakery fills one order at a time from a handwritten recipe book, while a chain of cafés buys a machine that shapes 2,000 identical buns an hour. Which statement best describes the two production processes?

◯ A. The production process is determined by how many products a business sells, not by customer priorities.

◯ B. The bakery uses artisan production (skilled labor, attention to detail, smaller quantities) and the café chain uses mass production (technology, assembly lines, machinery, larger quantities).

◯ C. The bakery uses mass production because it produces the same bun every time, and the café chain uses artisan production because the machine is new.

◯ D. Both are mass production, because only artisan production requires skilled labor.



**Q28.** A fitness app wants to market to customers most likely to pay for coaching. It groups users by age, income, and location (demographic) and by whether they value convenience and self-improvement (psychographic), then writes a description of "Lin, 24, a busy graduate student who exercises before work and values quick, guided sessions." Lin's description is best called:

◯ A. A market segment — the group of users sharing demographic and psychographic traits.

◯ B. A purchasing pattern — the timing, frequency, and quantity of a consumer's purchases.

◯ C. A customer profile — a fictional individual who stands for the target segment.

◯ D. Customer data — the raw information collected about users.



**Q29.** A founder wants to know how many students would pay ¥30 a month for a study-planner app, and why the others would not. Which research design best fits the two questions?

◯ A. Observation of existing students only, because what people do matters more than what they say.

◯ B. A focus group for both questions, because group discussion always gives more reliable numbers.

◯ C. A survey for the 'how many' question and interviews or a focus group for the 'why' question.

◯ D. An experiment for both questions, because experiments measure behaviour rather than opinions.



**Q30.** Which pairing of customer data tools is correct?

◯ A. Subscriber lists and tracking apps are traditional research tools; surveys and interviews are digital tools.

◯ B. Surveys and focus groups are the only sources of quantitative customer data.

◯ C. Click-tracking software and social media monitoring are digital tools; surveys and interviews are traditional research tools.

◯ D. Buying customer data from another business is not a legitimate way to collect customer data.

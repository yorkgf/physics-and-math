# 4.2 Evaluating Performance Using KPIs

## Core Terms

| # | Term | Definition | Connects To 
|---|---|---|---
| 1 | Key performance indicator (KPI) | A data point used to measure a business's performance, including progress toward short- and long-term goals and the effectiveness of strategy | 4.2.A.1 
| 2 | Revenue | The total amount of money a business earns from its normal business activities (sales of goods or services) | 4.2.B.1, 3.6 
| 3 | Gross profit | Revenue minus cost of goods sold (COGS) — the profit remaining after deducting direct production costs | 4.2.B.1, 3.6 
| 4 | Gross profit margin | Gross profit divided by revenue, expressed as a percentage — measures production efficiency | 4.2.B.1, 3.6 
| 5 | Operating profit | Gross profit minus operating expenses — profit from core business operations before interest and taxes | 4.2.B.1, 3.6 
| 6 | Cost of goods sold (COGS) | The direct costs attributable to the production of goods sold, including materials and direct labor | 4.2.B.1, 3.4, 3.6 
| 7 | Operating expenses | Costs required to run the business that are not directly tied to production, such as rent, salaries, and marketing | 4.2.B.1, 3.4, 3.6 
| 8 | Cash flow | The net amount of cash moving in and out of a business during a specific period | 4.2.B.1, 3.8 
| 9 | Customer acquisition cost (CAC) | The total cost of marketing and sales efforts divided by the number of new customers acquired | 4.2.B.2 
| 10 | Customer lifetime value (CLV) | The total revenue a business expects to earn from a single customer over the entire duration of their relationship | 4.2.B.2 
| 11 | Customer retention rate | The percentage of customers who continue doing business with the company over a given period | 4.2.B.2 
| 12 | Benchmark | A reference point used to compare data to a standard, based on internal historical data or external industry standards | 4.2.C.1 
| 13 | Market share | The percentage of total sales in an industry captured by a specific business | 4.2.B.2, 1.3 
| 14 | Net Promoter Score (NPS) | A customer satisfaction metric measuring how likely customers are to recommend the business to others (scale: −100 to +100) | 4.2.B.2 

## Additional Terms

| # | Term | Definition | Connects To |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 | Cash burn rate | The rate at which a business spends its cash reserves, typically expressed as a monthly figure | 4.2.B.1 |
| 16 | Cash runway | The amount of time (in months) a business can continue operating before running out of cash, calculated as cash on hand ÷ monthly burn rate | 4.2.B.1 |
| 17 | Operating profit margin | Operating profit divided by revenue, expressed as a percentage — measures overall operational efficiency | 4.2.B.1, 3.6 |
| 18 | Per-unit cost | The total cost to produce one unit of a product, calculated as total production costs ÷ number of units produced | 4.2.B.3, 3.4 |
| 19 | On-time delivery percentage | The percentage of orders delivered to customers by the promised date | 4.2.B.3 |
| 20 | Order accuracy | The percentage of orders fulfilled correctly without errors (wrong item, wrong quantity, damage) | 4.2.B.3 |
| 21 | Internal benchmark | A reference point based on the business's own historical data (e.g., comparing this quarter to last quarter) | 4.2.C.1 |
| 22 | External benchmark | A reference point based on industry standards or competitor data (e.g., comparing to industry average) | 4.2.C.1 |
| 23 | Diminishing returns | The principle that as investment in one area increases, the marginal benefit eventually decreases | 4.2.B.2 |

## Trap Pairs

| Pair | Term A | Term B | Key Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Revenue** | **Profit** | Revenue = total money earned from sales. Profit = what remains after subtracting costs. A business can have high revenue but no profit (or a loss). |
| 2 | **Gross profit margin** | **Operating profit margin** | Gross profit margin = (Revenue − COGS) ÷ Revenue. Operating profit margin = (Revenue − COGS − Operating Expenses) ÷ Revenue. Operating margin is always lower because it includes more costs. |
| 3 | **CAC** | **CLV** | CAC = what it costs to GET a customer. CLV = what a customer is WORTH over their lifetime. A healthy business has CLV > CAC (ideally 3:1 or higher). |
| 4 | **Internal benchmark** | **External benchmark** | Internal = compared to your own past performance. External = compared to industry or competitor standards. Both are needed for a complete picture. |
| 5 | **Cash flow** | **Cash burn rate** | Cash flow = net cash in minus cash out (can be positive or negative). Cash burn rate = how fast you're spending cash (always expressed as a positive monthly figure). A negative cash flow means a positive burn rate. |
| 6 | **KPI** | **Data point** | A KPI is a data point selected because it directly measures progress toward goals. Not all data points are KPIs — only those tied to mission, profitability, or viability. |

## Formulas

| Formula | Equation | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Gross Profit | Revenue − COGS | $1,296,000 − $388,800 = $907,200 |
| Gross Profit Margin | Gross Profit ÷ Revenue × 100% | $907,200 ÷ $1,296,000 × 100% = 70% |
| Operating Profit | Gross Profit − Operating Expenses | $907,200 − $2,555,000 = $(1,647,800) |
| Operating Profit Margin | Operating Profit ÷ Revenue × 100% | $(1,647,800) ÷ $1,296,000 × 100% = −127% |
| CLV:CAC Ratio | Customer Lifetime Value ÷ Customer Acquisition Cost | $560 ÷ $135 = 4.1:1 |
| Cash Runway | Cash on Hand ÷ Monthly Burn Rate | $1,850,000 ÷ $163,000 = 11.3 months |
| Customer Retention Rate | (Customers End − New Customers) ÷ Customers Start × 100% | (12,100 − 1,000) ÷ 11,500 × 100% ≈ 96.5% |
| Market Share | Business Revenue ÷ Total Market Revenue × 100% | Varies by market |

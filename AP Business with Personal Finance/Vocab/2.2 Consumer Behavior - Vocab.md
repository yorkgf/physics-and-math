## Core Terms (14)

| # | Term | One-sentence definition | CED anchor 
|---|---|---|---
| 1 | **Rational decision-making process** | A deliberate, step-by-step approach in which a consumer identifies a need, gathers information, evaluates alternatives, makes a purchase, and reviews the outcome | 2.2.A.2 
| 2 | **Habitual buying behavior** | Low-involvement, routine purchasing in which a consumer repeats a familiar choice with little conscious evaluation | 2.2.A.2 
| 3 | **Personal factors** | Individual characteristics—such as age, income, lifestyle, and personality—that shape what a consumer wants and can afford | 2.2.A.3 
| 4 | **Psychological factors** | Internal mental processes—motivation, perception, learning, and beliefs—that influence how a consumer interprets and reacts to marketing | 2.2.A.4 
| 5 | **Social and cultural factors** | External forces—family, reference groups, culture, and social class—that set norms and expectations for what a consumer considers acceptable or desirable | 2.2.A.5 
| 6 | **Situational influences** | Temporary, context-specific conditions—such as time pressure, store atmosphere, or mood—that affect a purchase decision at the moment | 2.2.A.6 
| 7 | **Purchasing pattern** | The observable regularity in a consumer's buying, defined by timing, frequency, and quantity of purchases | 2.2.B.1 
| 8 | **Consumer protection laws** | Government regulations that safeguard buyers against unfair, deceptive, or unsafe business practices | 2.2.B.2 
| 9 | **Substitute products** | Alternative goods or services that satisfy the same underlying need, giving consumers a choice when one option is restricted or unavailable | 2.2.B.2 
| 10 | **Scarcity** | The perception that a product is limited in availability, which increases its psychological appeal and urgency to buy | 2.2.C.2 
| 11 | **Authority** | The tendency to defer to people perceived as experts or credible sources when making purchase decisions | 2.2.C.3 
| 12 | **Consensus/social proof** | The tendency to follow the actions of others, assuming that if many people buy something, it must be a good choice | 2.2.C.4 
| 13 | **Reciprocity** | The felt obligation to give something back after receiving a favor, gift, or concession from a seller | 2.2.C.6 
| 14 | **Consistency** | The desire to act in ways that are aligned with commitments one has already made, even small ones | 2.2.C.7

## Trap Pairs (pairs that look similar but mean different things)

| Pair | Why they get confused | How to tell them apart |
|---|---|---|
| **Rational decision-making** vs. **Habitual buying** | Both describe how consumers buy | Rational = deliberate, step-by-step evaluation · Habitual = automatic, low-involvement routine |
| **Personal factors** vs. **Psychological factors** | Both operate inside the individual | Personal = who you **are** (age, income, lifestyle) · Psychological = what you **think and feel** (motivation, perception, beliefs) |
| **Social/cultural factors** vs. **Situational influences** | Both are external forces on a decision | Social/cultural = permanent norms and group expectations · Situational = temporary, moment-specific conditions (time, mood, store atmosphere) |
| **Consensus/social proof** vs. **Authority** | Both rely on others' behavior or status | Consensus = "many **people** bought this" (quantity of buyers) · Authority = "an **expert** endorses this" (credibility of one source) |
| **Reciprocity** vs. **Liking** | Both involve a positive connection to the seller | Reciprocity = obligation from a **favor received** (give-back) · Liking = rapport from **attraction or similarity** (warmth) |
| **Consistency** vs. **Unity** | Both deepen commitment over time | Consistency = alignment with **your own past actions** (self-image) · Unity = alignment with **a group's identity** (belonging) |

## Key Relationships

```
Four families of consumer influence (broadest → most immediate)
  1. Personal factors (who you are)
       │
  2. Psychological factors (what you think)
       │
  3. Social & cultural factors (what your groups expect)
       │
  4. Situational influences (what's happening right now)
       │
       └──► All four converge on the purchase decision
```

```
Consumer protection laws
  └──► restrict or ban a product
        └──► need persists → substitute products emerge
              └──► consumers redirect spending
```

```
Technology
  ├──► Seller side: wider reach, richer data, personalized offers
  └──► Buyer side: easier price comparison, peer reviews, informed decisions
        └──► Both sides shift purchasing patterns
```

```
7 Cialdini Principles of Influence (each has an honest use AND a manipulative misuse)
  1. Scarcity       — honest: real limited stock · manipulative: fake countdown timers
  2. Authority       — honest: genuine expertise · manipulative: borrowed or false credentials
  3. Consensus       — honest: authentic reviews · manipulative: fake testimonials
  4. Liking          — honest: genuine rapport · manipulative: manufactured flattery
  5. Reciprocity     — honest: real value first · manipulative: unsolicited "gifts" with strings
  6. Consistency     — honest: small honest commitments · manipulative: foot-in-the-door traps
  7. Unity           — honest: shared community · manipulative: manufactured tribalism
```

## Must-Know Formulas / Lists

| Formula / List | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Purchasing pattern** = timing + frequency + quantity | Three observable dimensions of how a consumer buys | A customer buys coffee every Monday morning (timing), weekly (frequency), one large cup (quantity) |
| **Four tiebreakers** when choosing between similar products | Price, features, availability, advertising — the criteria consumers use when two options seem equal | A shopper comparing two laptops uses price and features to decide |
| **Face test** = "Does the tactic survive full disclosure?" | An ethical check: would you be comfortable if the customer saw exactly what you were doing? | Sending a fake "limited time" offer fails the face test |

## Sorting Test

If the factor describes **who the consumer is** (age, income, lifestyle) → **personal factor**
If the factor describes **what the consumer thinks or feels** (motivation, beliefs) → **psychological factor**
If the factor describes **what the consumer's group expects** (family, culture, social class) → **social/cultural factor**
If the factor describes **what's happening right now** (time pressure, mood, store layout) → **situational influence**

If many **people** are doing it → **consensus / social proof**
If an **expert** endorses it → **authority**
If you feel **obligated to give back** → **reciprocity**
If you feel **drawn to the person** → **liking**
If you want to stay **true to a past commitment** → **consistency**
If you feel **"they are my people"** → **unity**

# 1.8 Supply Chains — Vocabulary

## Core Terms

| # | Term | Definition | Key Detail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Artisan production** | A production process that relies on **skilled labor** and close attention to detail, producing goods in **smaller quantities** | Chosen when customers prize customization; quality can be high but output is limited |
| 2 | **Mass production** | A production process that relies on **technology, assembly lines, and machinery** to produce goods in **far larger quantities** | Chosen when customers prize low price; output is high |
| 3 | **Supply chain** | All of the individuals and businesses involved at **each stage of producing and distributing a product** — from raw materials to final customer delivery | Can be local, regional, or global |
| 4 | **Raw materials** | The basic natural resources or inputs used to manufacture a product | First stage of a good's supply chain |
| 5 | **Manufacturing facility** | The location where workers and equipment turn raw materials into **finished goods** | Second stage of a good's supply chain |
| 6 | **Warehouse** | A facility that emphasizes **storage** of finished goods | Comes **after** manufacturing, **before** distribution |
| 7 | **Distribution center** | A facility that stores goods **and moves them onward** to stores and customers | Differs from a warehouse by actively forwarding goods |
| 8 | **Supplier** | A business that buys materials/goods from one business and sells them to another | Feeds the production process; sits early in the chain |
| 9 | **Distributor** | A business that buys **finished products** from manufacturers and sells them onward to **retail stores** | Sits between manufacturing and retail |
| 10 | **Scaling** | Building higher-capacity, more efficient supply chains so that **revenue increases outrun cost increases** | Fixed costs spread across more units → each unit costs less to produce |
| 11 | **Service supply chain** | Acquiring the **employees, resources, and delivery systems** needed to provide a service (in person or virtually) | No factory, no warehouse — three parts: people, resources, delivery system |

## Trap Pairs

| # | Pair | Why They Get Swapped | How to Tell Apart |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Artisan** vs. **Mass production** | Both produce physical goods | Artisan = skilled labor, small quantities; Mass = technology, large quantities |
| 2 | **Warehouse** vs. **Distribution center** | Both store goods | Warehouse = **storage only**; Distribution center = **stores and moves onward** |
| 3 | **Supplier** vs. **Distributor** | Both move goods between businesses | Supplier = materials/goods **into** production; Distributor = finished goods **out to** retail |
| 4 | **Buying inputs** vs. **Outsourcing** | Both involve paying another business | Buying inputs = purchasing raw materials (every producer does this); Outsourcing = handing an entire **function** to another business |
| 5 | **Service supply chain** vs. **No supply chain** | Services have no visible factory or warehouse | Services DO have supply chains — made of **people, resources, and delivery systems** |
| 6 | **Cost increase** vs. **Scaling failure** | Both show rising total costs | Scaling succeeds when **revenue growth > cost growth**; costs always rise when output doubles — the question is whether revenue rose faster |

## Key Relationships

```
Production process choice:
├── Customer priorities (customization → artisan; low price → mass)
├── Core competencies (from 1.5)
└── Competitive landscape (from 1.2)

Good's supply chain (5 stages):
Raw materials → Manufacturing → Warehouse → Distribution/Retail → Customer

Service supply chain (3 parts):
People + Resources + Delivery system

Supplier selection (5 factors):
├── Cost (visible — on price sheet)
├── Quality (visible — on specs)
├── Efficiency (visible — on delivery schedule)
├── Convenience (visible — on delivery schedule)
└── Risk (HIDES — disasters, instability, errors)

Strategy → Chain design:
├── Low price → mass production + cost-cutting (± scaling)
├── High quality → high-quality resources + methods
└── Barriers to entry → exclusive/restrictive agreements

Scaling mechanism:
Revenue growth (+100%) > Cost growth (+60%)
→ Fixed costs spread across more units
→ Profit grows faster than either revenue or cost
```

## Must-Know Formulas / Rules

| Rule | Application |
|---|---|
| **Chain order** | Raw materials → Manufacturing → **Warehouse** → Distribution/Retail → Customer |
| **Warehouse vs. distribution center** | Warehouse = storage; Distribution center = storage + forwarding |
| **Five supplier factors** | Cost, quality, efficiency, convenience, **risk** (the one that hides) — always weigh all five |
| **Scaling success test** | Revenue growth % > Cost growth % → scaling works |
| **Strategy → chain** | Be specific: cheaper input / better input / locked-up input — name the mechanism |
| **Service chain** | People + resources + delivery system — no factory, no warehouse |
| **Three question shapes** | (1) Order the stages, (2) Choose a supplier, (3) Match strategy to chain |

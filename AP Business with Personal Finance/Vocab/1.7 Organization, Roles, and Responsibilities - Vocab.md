# 1.7 Organization, Roles, and Responsibilities — Vocabulary

## Core Terms

| # | Term | Definition | Key Detail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sole proprietorship** | A business with **one owner** who has full control, keeps all profits, and is **personally liable** for all debts | Funding is limited (savings + loans); the owner wears every hat |
| 2 | **Partnership** | A business with **two or more owners** who share control and profits and are **personally liable** for all debts | Partners typically divide roles by strengths and interests |
| 3 | **Limited liability company (LLC)** | A business structure where owners **retain control and profits** but are **shielded** from personal liability | Cannot issue stock; funding is limited |
| 4 | **Corporation** | A business structure owned by **shareholders** who **cede control** to an elected board of directors in exchange for **greater access to funding** | The company itself is liable (not the owners personally) |
| 5 | **Personal liability** | The owner's **personal assets** are at risk for business debts and obligations | Applies to sole proprietorships and partnerships |
| 6 | **Limited liability** (liability shield) | Business owners are **not personally responsible** for business debts — only the business's assets are at risk | Applies to LLCs and corporations |
| 7 | **Organizational structure** | The arrangement of roles, responsibilities, authority, and communication within a business | Drawn as an org chart; authority flows down, accountability flows up |
| 8 | **Department** | A specialized functional unit within a larger business (e.g., accounting, marketing, operations) | Six common departments: sales/marketing, R&D, operations, accounting, finance, HR |
| 9 | **Accounting** | The department that **tracks** expenditures and earnings and **prepares financial statements** to monitor financial health | Looks **backward** — records money that already moved |
| 10 | **Finance** | The department that **secures and manages funds** and uses financial data to **recommend strategies** for improving performance | Looks **forward** — decides what to fund next |
| 11 | **Outsourcing** | Hiring **another business** to handle a function instead of doing it internally | Triggers: (1) lack of in-house skills, (2) high labor costs; "buy vs. build" |

## Trap Pairs

| # | Pair | Why They Get Swapped | How to Tell Apart |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sole proprietorship** vs. **LLC** | Both have one or few owners who retain control | Sole prop = **personal liability**; LLC = **liability shield** |
| 2 | **Partnership** vs. **Corporation** | Both have multiple people involved in ownership | Partnership = owners **retain control**, personal liability; Corporation = owners **cede control**, liability shielded |
| 3 | **Accounting** vs. **Finance** | Both deal with money and financial data | Accounting = **backward** (records, statements); Finance = **forward** (funds, strategies) |
| 4 | **Personal liability** vs. **Limited liability** | Both describe who pays business debts | Personal = owners pay **from their own pocket**; Limited = only the **business's assets** are at risk |
| 5 | **Roles** vs. **Departments** | Both describe who does what work | Roles = how **small teams** (sole prop, partners) divide work; Departments = how **large businesses** organize specialized functions |
| 6 | **Outsourcing** vs. **Core competency** | Both involve a business function | Outsourcing = moving a function **out** (cost-driven); Core competency = developing a function **in-house** (competitive advantage) |

## Key Relationships

```
Legal structure decides:
├── Control (who makes decisions)
├── Liability (who pays debts)
└── Funding (how growth is paid for)

Sole prop ──→ personal liability, full control, limited funding
Partnership ──→ personal liability, shared control, limited funding
LLC ──→ shielded liability, retained control, limited funding
Corporation ──→ shielded liability, ceded control, greatest funding

3-question ID method:
├── How many owners? (1 vs. 2+)
├── Who pays debts? (personal vs. shielded)
└── How funded? (savings/loans vs. shares)

As business grows:
├── One owner = 5 hats (CEO, marketer, product dev, finance, operations)
├── Partners = divide roles by strengths
├── Large staff = specialize into 6 departments
└── Missing skills or high costs → outsource

Accounting (backward) ←── SWAP PAIR ──→ Finance (forward)
```

## Must-Know Formulas / Rules

| Rule | Application |
|---|---|
| **Control vs. funding tradeoff** | More control (sole prop, partnership, LLC) → less funding; less control (corporation) → more funding |
| **Liability boundary** | Sole prop + partnership = owners pay personally; LLC + corporation = only business assets at risk |
| **3-question method** | Owners? → Debts? → Funding? → Structure identified |
| **Accounting vs. finance** | If the scenario describes **preparing statements or tracking past money** → accounting, not finance |
| **Outsourcing triggers** | Missing skills OR high labor costs → "buy the function" |

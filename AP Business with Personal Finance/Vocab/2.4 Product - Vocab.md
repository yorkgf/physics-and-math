# 2.4 Product — Vocab

| # | Term | Definition | Connects To 
|---|------|-----------|-------------

| 11 | Brand Equity | The added financial and competitive value a product earns because customers recognize, trust, and prefer the brand. | LO 2.4.B, LO 2.2 
| 12 | Product Life Cycle (PLC) | A model describing the four stages every product passes through: introduction, growth, maturity, and decline. | LO 2.4.C

| 16 | Product Messaging | The stage of product development focused on crafting the value proposition and product-positioning strategy to communicate the product's unique value to target customers | EK 2.4.A.5

## Additional Terms (Frequently Tested)

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| Growth Stage | The PLC stage where sales rise rapidly, profits begin to appear, and new competitors enter the market. |
| Decline Stage | The final PLC stage where sales drop and businesses must decide whether to reduce investment, discontinue, or reinvent the product. |
| Product Features | The specific attributes or functions of a product that deliver value to the customer. |
| Customer Feedback | Information collected from users about their experience with a product, used to guide refinements and improvements. |
| Target Market | The specific group of consumers a product is designed and marketed to serve (cross-topic: 2.1). |
| Market Saturation | A condition in which most potential customers already own or use a product, making further sales growth difficult. |
| Price Wars | Aggressive rounds of price cutting among competitors, most common during the maturity stage of the PLC. |

## ⚠️ Trap Pairs — Know the Difference

| Pair | How They're Different |
|------|-----------------------|
| Brand vs. Branding | A brand is the identifiable mark itself (name, logo, symbol); branding is the strategic process of shaping how customers perceive that mark over time. |
| Prototype vs. MVP | A prototype is a model built to explore and test design ideas; an MVP is the simplest sellable version released to real customers to validate willingness to pay. |
| Idea Screening vs. Concept Testing | Idea screening is an internal evaluation of feasibility (costs, competition, regulations); concept testing involves presenting the idea to actual customers to gauge real-world interest. |
| Value Proposition vs. Brand Equity | A value proposition is a company's own claim about why its product is superior; brand equity is the market's valuation of that product based on customer recognition and loyalty. |
| Maturity Stage vs. Decline Stage | In maturity, sales are at their peak and the strategic challenge is defending market share; in decline, sales are falling and the key decision is whether to invest, pivot, or exit. |
| Market Research vs. Concept Testing | Market research is broad data gathering about customers and competitors; concept testing is a focused activity where a specific product idea is presented to potential buyers for feedback. |

## 📐 Product Development Flow

| Stage | Key Question | Output |
|-------|-------------|--------|
| 1. Generate Idea | What customer problem can we solve? | List of product concepts |
| 2. Screen & Refine | Is this idea feasible given market size, competition, costs, and regulations? | Shortlist of viable ideas |
| 3. Develop Prototype | What will the product look and function like? | Visual or working model |
| 4. Test Concept with Customers | Does this idea solve a real problem for real people? | Validated customer interest |
| 5. Develop MVP | What is the simplest version customers will pay for? | Basic sellable product |
| 6. Refine Based on Feedback | What do early users want changed or improved? | Improved product ready for scaling |

## 🔁 Product Life Cycle — Strategy by Stage

| Stage | Sales Trend | Profit | Strategy Focus |
|-------|------------|--------|----------------|
| Introduction | Low and slowly rising | Negative (losses common) | Build awareness, educate early adopters, invest in promotion |
| Growth | Rapidly increasing | Begins to turn positive | Expand distribution, differentiate from new competitors, scale production |
| Maturity | Peak but growth slows | High but under pressure | Defend market share, optimize costs, consider product extensions |
| Decline | Falling steadily | Shrinking or disappearing | Reduce investment, discontinue, or reinvent the product for a new cycle |

## Core Terms (AP Exam)

| # | Term | Definition | Connects To |
|---|------|-----------|-------------|
| 1 | **Ideation** | **Stage 1** of product development: generating new or improved product ideas through market research, technical R&D, and brainstorming. | EK 2.4.A.2 |
| 2 | **Idea screening** | An internal filter that evaluates ideas against market size, competition, cost, and regulatory barriers before further investment. (Part of ideation/validation — a management step, not a separate CED stage.) | LO 2.4.A |
| 3 | **Validation** | **Stage 2**: testing product ideas with potential customers; the stage in which an **MVP** is used to assess **product-market fit**. | EK 2.4.A.3 |
| 4 | **Concept testing** | Showing a product idea (or sketch/prototype) to potential customers to confirm the problem is real and they want a solution. (Happens in validation.) | LO 2.4.A |
| 5 | **Minimum Viable Product (MVP)** | The simplest version of a product with **only core features** (may be a sketch, a description, or a model) that customers are willing to pay for; used to test whether demand is sufficient to generate profit. | EK 2.4.A.3, EK 1.4.C.3 |
| 6 | **Product-market fit** | The condition in which **customer demand is sufficient to generate profit**. | EK 2.4.A.3 |
| 7 | **Design** | **Stage 3**: sourcing materials, creating prototypes, and estimating production and delivery costs; determines functionality (how it works), user experience, and basic attributes (size, color, quality). | EK 2.4.A.4 |
| 8 | **Prototype** | A preliminary model built to explore and test design ideas; it may be a simple sketch or a functional working version. (Built in the design stage.) | EK 2.4.A.4 |
| 9 | **Messaging** | **Stage 4**: creating the product's overall marketing strategy — the **value proposition**, branding, and product positioning. | EK 2.4.A.5 |
| 10 | **Value proposition** | A statement of **who** the product serves, **what problem or need** it addresses, and **how it is superior to alternatives**, based on research insights. | EK 2.4.A.5, LO 1.1.B |
| 11 | **Production** | **Stage 5**: building the product, incorporating design-stage feedback, determining the production process and supply chain, and matching production levels with customer demand. | EK 2.4.A.6 |
| 12 | **Manufacturing planning** | Deciding how and where the product will be produced; it grows out of the production stage and the validated MVP. (Not a separate CED stage.) | LO 2.4.A |
| 13 | **Launch** | **Stage 6**: taking orders and distributing the final product to customers, with marketing aimed at the target segment. | EK 2.4.A.7 |
| 14 | **Market research** | The process of gathering and analyzing data about customers, competitors, and market conditions to guide product decisions. | LO 2.3.A, LO 2.1.A |
| 15 | **Brand** | A **name, idea, term, symbol, design, or combination** of these that identifies and differentiates a business's products from those of competitors; often protected by trademark. | EK 2.4.B.1, EK 2.4.B.3 |
| 16 | **Branding** | The process of developing a brand's **identity** so it distinguishes the product, raises customer awareness, and generates loyalty; built to stay consistent with the vision and value proposition. | EK 2.4.B.1, EK 2.4.B.2 |
| 17 | **Brand equity** | The added financial and competitive value a product earns because customers **recognize, trust, and prefer** the brand. | LO 2.4.B |
| 18 | **Generic product** | A product sold **without branding**, targeting customers who prioritize cost savings over brand recognition. | EK 2.4.B.4 |
| 19 | **Product life cycle (PLC)** | The four stages every product passes through: **introduction → growth → maturity → decline**, dictated by changes in customer demand over time. | EK 2.4.C.1 |
| 20 | **Introduction stage** | Low sales and revenue; marketing focuses on tactics that raise **brand awareness** and generate initial demand. | EK 2.4.C.3 |
| 21 | **Growth stage** | Sales and revenue grow at an increasing rate and production typically increases; rivals enter, so marketing focuses on **differentiation, quality, and promotion**. | EK 2.4.C.4 |
| 22 | **Maturity stage** | Sales and revenue **flatten** in a saturated market; the business seeks to **retain market share** through brand loyalty, continued differentiation, lower prices, and innovation. | EK 2.4.C.5 |
| 23 | **Decline stage** | Sales and revenue fall as customers turn to rivals or substitutes; marketing focuses on **cutting costs** or **product redesign**; the product may be discontinued. | EK 2.4.C.6 |
| 24 | **Product portfolio management** | Managing multiple products at different life-cycle stages to balance risk and maintain steady revenue. | LO 2.4.C |

## 📐 Product Development Flow — the CED six stages

> **Ideation → Validation → Design → Messaging → Production → Launch** (EK 2.4.A.1)

| Stage | Key question | What happens | Typical student evidence |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------------------------|
| 1. **Ideation** | What customer problem could we solve? | Market research, technical R&D, brainstorming generate new or improved ideas; ideas are screened for market size, competition, costs, regulations. | List of product concepts + why we picked one |
| 2. **Validation** | Does a real customer actually want this? | Test the idea with potential customers; build an **MVP** (core features only, may be a sketch/description/model) and check for **product-market fit**. | Interview/survey results, MVP, the change we made because of feedback |
| 3. **Design** | What will it look like and how will it work? | Source materials, build **prototypes**, estimate production and delivery costs; set functionality (how it works), user experience, and basic attributes (size, color, quality). | Prototype, feature list, cost estimate, prototype feedback |
| 4. **Messaging** | How will we talk about it? | Set the overall marketing strategy: **value proposition** (who + what problem + why us), **branding/brand identity**, and product positioning versus rivals. | Value proposition statement, brand name/logo/tagline |
| 5. **Production** | How do we build it and get it to customers? | Build the product incorporating design-stage feedback; determine the production process and supply chain; match production levels with demand. | Production process, supply chain plan, supplier list |
| 6. **Launch** | How do we sell and deliver it? | Take orders and distribute the final product to customers; marketing aimed straight at the target segment. | Channel plan, launch marketing, early sales data |

### Where the "extra" terms fit (mapping to CED)

| Extra term | Fits inside which CED stage | Why it is NOT a stage of its own |
|-------------|----------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Idea Generation | **Ideation (1)** | It is the activity that *fills* the ideation stage. |
| Idea Screening | **Ideation (1) / Validation (2)** | An internal go/no-go filter; it does not involve the customer. |
| Concept Testing | **Validation (2)** | Asking potential customers whether the idea solves a real problem they have. |
| Prototype | **Design (3)** | Built to explore and test the design; feedback on it comes from design. |
| MVP | **Validation (2)** | The simplest *sellable* version; feedback on it comes from validation. |
| Value Proposition | **Messaging (4)** | The core message built from research. |
| Branding / Brand Identity | **Messaging (4)** | Identity elements are part of the messaging strategy. |
| Manufacturing Planning | **Production (5)** | How and where it gets made. |
| Market Research | Runs across **all six stages** | Feeds ideation, validation, and messaging. |

> ⚠️ **Do not memorize "six stages" from any other list.** The CED list is *Ideation → Validation → Design → Messaging → Production → Launch*. Older versions of this file listed "Generate Idea → Screen & Refine → Prototype → Concept Testing → MVP → Refine", which is **not** the CED list and does not match the 2.4 slides.

## Additional Terms (Frequently Tested)

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| Product features | The specific attributes or functions of a product that deliver value to the customer. |
| Customer feedback | Information collected from users about their experience with a product, used to guide refinements and improvements. |
| Target market | The specific group of consumers a product is designed and marketed to serve (cross-topic: 2.1). |
| Market saturation | A condition in which most potential customers already own or use a product, making further sales growth difficult. |
| Price wars | Aggressive rounds of price cutting among competitors, most common during the maturity stage of the PLC. |
| Brand awareness | The extent to which customers recognize and recall a brand; the main objective of the introduction stage. |
| User experience | How a product feels and works for the user — set during the design stage. |
| Supply chain link to product | Stage 5 connects the product to 1.8: production process and supply chain plan determine cost, quality, and delivery. |

## ⚠️ Trap Pairs — Know the Difference

| Pair | How They're Different |
|------|-----------------------|
| **Prototype vs. MVP** | A prototype is a model built to explore and test **design** ideas; an MVP is the simplest **sellable** version released to real customers to validate willingness to pay (and product-market fit). |
| **Idea Screening vs. Concept Testing** | Idea screening is an **internal** evaluation (costs, competition, regulations); concept testing **shows the idea to customers** to gauge real-world interest. |
| **Validation vs. Design stage** | Validation asks *do they want it?* (MVP, product-market fit); design asks *what will it look like and how will it work?* (prototypes, features, costs). |
| **Brand vs. Branding** | A brand is the identifiable mark itself (name, logo, symbol); branding is the strategic process of shaping how customers perceive that mark over time. |
| **Value Proposition vs. Brand Equity** | A value proposition is the company's own **claim** about why its product is superior; brand equity is the **market's valuation** of that product based on customer recognition and loyalty. |
| **Value Proposition vs. Product Messaging** | The value proposition is the *content* (who/what problem/why us); messaging is the *stage and process* that carries it to the market along with branding and positioning. |
| **Maturity Stage vs. Decline Stage** | In maturity, sales are at their peak and the challenge is **defending market share**; in decline, sales are falling and the key decision is whether to invest, pivot, or exit. |
| **Product Development Stages vs. Product Life Cycle** | Development stages build the product (once, before launch); the PLC tracks the product's sales over time (after launch). Never mix them up. |
| **Market Research vs. Concept Testing** | Market research is broad data gathering about customers and competitors; concept testing is a focused activity where a specific product idea is presented to potential buyers. |

## 🔁 Product Life Cycle — Strategy by Stage

| Stage | Sales trend | Profit | Strategy focus | CED |
|-------|------------|--------|----------------|------|
| Introduction | Low and slowly rising | Negative (losses common) | Build awareness, educate early adopters, invest in promotion | EK 2.4.C.3 |
| Growth | Rapidly increasing | Begins to turn positive | Expand distribution, differentiate from new competitors, scale production | EK 2.4.C.4 |
| Maturity | Peak but growth slows | High but under pressure | Defend market share (brand loyalty, lower prices, innovation) | EK 2.4.C.5 |
| Decline | Falling steadily | Shrinking or disappearing | Reduce investment, discontinue, or redesign the product for a new cycle | EK 2.4.C.6 |

## Must-Know Rules

| Rule | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **Stage order** | Ideation → Validation → Design → Messaging → Production → Launch. Nothing is taught "out of order" on the exam. |
| **MVP → Validation, prototype → Design** | If feedback came from an MVP, it belongs to validation; if from a prototype, it belongs to design. |
| **Value proposition formula** | Who + what problem/need + how we are superior (grounded in research). |
| **Brand elements** | Name, idea, term, symbol, design — or a combination — often protected by trademark. |
| **PLC order** | Introduction → Growth → Maturity → Decline; sales low → rising → peak/flat → falling. |
| **Strategy at each PLC stage** | Awareness → differentiate/scale → defend share → cut costs or redesign. |
| **Generic vs. branded** | Some customers buy generics because they prioritise cost savings over brand recognition. |

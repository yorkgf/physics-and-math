# 1.6 Business Ethics — Vocabulary

## Core Terms

| # | Term | Definition | Key Detail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Unethical behavior** | Actions that violate moral or professional standards | Three forms: falsifying/concealing info, misusing company property, causing harm |
| 2 | **Code of conduct** | A written document outlining expected ethical behavior and standards | One of four tools to encourage ethical behavior; sets clear boundaries |
| 3 | **Training** | Teaching employees to follow ethical guidelines through ongoing instruction and practice scenarios | Helps employees recognize dilemmas before they act |
| 4 | **Internal repercussion** | A consequence imposed **after** an ethical transgression (warning, suspension, termination) | Pushes **backward** against behavior; must be consistent and proportional |
| 5 | **Modeling** (ethical behavior) | A leader **demonstrates** ethical behavior in front of employees, so the standard is learned by observation | Most powerful when it costs the leader something |
| 6 | **Incentive** | A reward that encourages a particular action, offered **before** the behavior | Pulls **toward** behavior; can unintentionally push toward cutting corners |
| 7 | **Ethical dilemma** | A situation where a core value conflicts with another core value or with a business goal or practice | Not the same as unethical behavior — a dilemma has trade-offs on both sides |
| 8 | **Internal stakeholder** | Individuals or groups with **direct involvement** in a business's operations, decisions, and outcomes | Owners, managers, employees |
| 9 | **External stakeholder** | Individuals or groups **not directly employed or involved** but with a vested interest in the business's decisions and outcomes | Customers, government agencies, community members |
| 10 | **Brand loyalty** | Ongoing customer preference for a business's products based on trust and positive experience | Built by ethical behavior; destroyed by unethical behavior |

## Trap Pairs

| # | Pair | Why They Get Swapped | How to Tell Apart |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Ethical dilemma** vs. **unethical behavior** | Both involve ethical situations | Dilemma = two sides with real trade-offs; unethical behavior = a specific wrong act (lying, hiding, taking) |
| 2 | **Incentive** vs. **internal repercussion** | Both influence employee behavior | Incentive = reward **before** behavior (pulls toward); repercussion = consequence **after** behavior (pushes back) |
| 3 | **Modeling** vs. **internal repercussion** | Both involve a leader taking action | Modeling = leader **demonstrates** the standard at personal cost; repercussion = leader **punishes** someone for a transgression |
| 4 | **Internal stakeholder** vs. **external stakeholder** | Both are affected by business decisions | Internal = **direct involvement** in operations (owners, managers, employees); external = **vested interest** only (customers, government, community) |
| 5 | **Greatest total benefit** vs. **one-column total** | Both involve adding numbers | Must sum across **every** stakeholder group (internal + external); stopping at one column is the trap |
| 6 | **Right decision + broken reason** vs. **right decision + valid reason** | Both reach the same verdict | A conclusion supported by "always" or "guarantee" is **wrong reasoning** even if the verdict is correct |

## Key Relationships

```
Ethical behavior → trust → brand loyalty → profitability (revenue ↑, costs ↓)
Unethical behavior → lost trust → customers leave, employees disengage → profitability ↓

Response to ethical lapse → shapes relationships + public perception → affects revenue + costs

Incentive ──(before)──→ behavior ←──(after)── Repercussion
    (pulls toward)                          (pushes back)

Ethical dilemma: core value ↔ core value, or core value ↔ business goal
  → resolve by: stakeholder arithmetic OR consistency with vision/goals
```

## Must-Know Formulas

- **Net impact** = (benefits to internal stakeholders + benefits to external stakeholders) − (costs to internal stakeholders + costs to external stakeholders)
- **Decision rule:** choose the option with the **greatest total benefit** or the **least total harm** across **all** stakeholder groups
- **Internal stakeholder** = direct involvement (owners, managers, employees)
- **External stakeholder** = vested interest, no direct involvement (customers, government, community)

# 2.5 Price — Vocab

## Core Terms (AP Exam)

| # | Term | Definition | Connects To 
|---|------|-----------|-------------
| 1 | Price | The amount of money a customer pays in exchange for a good or service. | LO 2.5.A 
| 2 | Pricing Strategy | A deliberate method a business uses to set the price of its products in order to meet revenue and profit goals. | LO 2.5.A 
| 3 | Value-Based Pricing | Setting a price based on the customer's perceived value — what they are willing to pay — rather than on costs alone. | LO 2.5.A; connects to 1.1 (customer needs) 
| 4 | Competitive Pricing | Setting a price by looking at what competitors charge for similar products and positioning accordingly. | LO 2.5.A; connects to 1.2 (competitive advantage) 
| 5 | Cost-Based Pricing | Calculating the total cost per unit and then adding a markup to determine the selling price (cost + desired profit = price). | LO 2.5.A; connects to 2.6 (costs) 
| 6 | Penetration Pricing | Setting a low initial price to attract customers quickly and gain market share, then gradually raising the price. | LO 2.5.A; connects to 1.2 (market share) 
| 7 | Dynamic Pricing | Adjusting the price of a product in real time based on current demand, supply, or other market conditions. | LO 2.5.A 
| 8 | Loss-Leader Pricing | Selling a specific product below cost to draw customers into the store or platform, expecting they will also buy profitable items. | LO 2.5.A 
| 9 | Pricing Power | The ability of a business to raise its prices without losing customers to competitors. | LO 2.5.B; connects to 1.2 (brand loyalty) 
| 10 | Price Elasticity of Demand | A measure of how sensitive customers are to a change in price — whether demand rises or falls significantly when price shifts. | LO 2.5.B; connects to 2.2 (supply and demand) 
| 11 | Price Fixing | An illegal agreement between competitors to set prices at a certain level, eliminating genuine competition. | LO 2.5.C; connects to 1.6 (ethics) 
| 12 | Price Gouging | Raising prices to unfair levels during a crisis or emergency, which is illegal in many states and countries. | LO 2.5.C 
| 13 | Price Discrimination | Charging different prices to different customer segments for the same product; illegal when based on protected characteristics. | LO 2.5.C 

## Additional Terms (Frequently Tested)

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| Elastic Demand | Demand that changes significantly when price changes — customers are highly price-sensitive. |
| Inelastic Demand | Demand that stays relatively stable even when price changes — customers are less price-sensitive. |
| Markup | The amount added to the cost of a product to determine its selling price and generate profit. |
| Switching Costs | The time, money, or effort a customer must spend to change from one product or brand to another. |
| Price War | A situation where competing businesses continuously lower prices to undercut each other, often hurting profits industry-wide. |
| Perceived Value | The customer's belief about what a product is worth to them, influenced by brand, quality, and experience. |

## ⚠️ Trap Pairs — Know the Difference

| Pair | How They're Different |
|------|-----------------------|
| Value-Based vs. Cost-Based Pricing | Value-based starts with what the customer is willing to pay; cost-based starts with what it costs the business to produce and adds a profit margin. |
| Penetration vs. Loss-Leader Pricing | Penetration sets a low price on the main product to build market share over time; loss-leader sells one product below cost specifically to lure customers into buying other profitable items. |
| Competitive vs. Dynamic Pricing | Competitive pricing sets a fixed price relative to rivals; dynamic pricing constantly changes based on real-time demand and conditions. |
| Elastic vs. Inelastic Demand | Elastic demand means customers are very responsive to price changes (small price increase = big drop in sales); inelastic demand means customers keep buying even when prices rise. |
| Pricing Power vs. Price Elasticity | Pricing power is the business's ability to raise prices without losing customers; price elasticity is the measurement of how customers actually respond. High pricing power usually corresponds to inelastic demand. |
| Price Fixing vs. Competitive Pricing | Competitive pricing means independently setting prices based on the market; price fixing means illegally colluding with competitors to set prices together. |

## 💰 Pricing Strategies at a Glance

| Strategy | How It Works | Best When... |
|----------|-------------|-------------|
| Value-Based | Price reflects what customers believe the product is worth to them. | The brand is strong and the product offers unique benefits that competitors cannot easily copy. |
| Competitive | Price is set in line with — or slightly above/below — what rivals charge. | The market has many similar products and customers compare prices before buying. |
| Cost-Based | Total cost per unit is calculated, then a markup is added for profit. | The business wants a simple, predictable pricing method and knows its costs accurately. |
| Penetration | A low initial price attracts early customers and builds market share, then price rises over time. | The business is entering a crowded market and needs to win customers away from established brands. |
| Dynamic | Price shifts in real time based on demand, time of day, inventory, or other factors. | Demand fluctuates frequently (e.g., ride-sharing, airline tickets, hotel bookings). |
| Loss-Leader | One product is sold below cost to bring customers in, who then buy other profitable items. | The business sells many products and can afford to take a loss on one to increase total basket size. |

## ⚖️ Pricing Power Spectrum

| More Pricing Power | Less Pricing Power |
|-------------------|-------------------|
| Strong, recognizable brand identity | Weak or unknown brand |
| Unique or differentiated product with no close substitutes | Generic product with many close substitutes |
| High switching costs make it hard for customers to leave | Low switching costs — customers can easily change brands |
| Inelastic demand — customers keep buying even when price rises | Elastic demand — customers leave quickly when price rises |
| Loyal customer base built on trust and quality | Price-sensitive customers who shop purely on cost |
| Examples: Apple, luxury goods, patented pharmaceuticals | Examples: commodity products, generic groceries, budget airlines |

# 3.5 Financial Capital — Vocab

## Core Terms (AP Exam)

| # | Term | Definition | Connects To 
|---|---|---|---
| 1 | **bootstrapping** | Using personal savings, personal bank loans, and/or personal credit to fund startup costs | 3.5.A.1 
| 2 | **external financial capital** | Cash obtained from outside sources to fund business operations | 3.5.A.2, 3.5.A.3 
| 3 | **break even** | The point at which a business covers all costs for a period; calculated by determining how many goods or services need to be sold | 3.5.A.2 
| 4 | **loans** | A classification of financial capital that must be repaid with interest; interest on loans is a business expense | 3.5.B.1.i 
| 5 | **equity financing** | A type of business funding that requires issuing ownership shares and ceding some control over decisions and a portion of future profits to investors, who become part owners | 3.5.B.1.ii 
| 6 | **bonds** | A financial instrument representing a loan from an investor to a business; can be issued by businesses organized as corporations | 3.5.B.4 
| 7 | **stock** | An ownership share in a business; can be sold privately or publicly; can be issued by businesses organized as corporations | 3.5.B.4 
| 8 | **secondary market** | A market where financial assets (loans, bonds, stock) can be resold after the initial issuance | 3.5.C.1 
| 9 | **financial assets** | Loans (including bonds) or shares of stock obtained by lenders and investors in exchange for financial capital provided to businesses | 3.5.C.1 
| 10 | **dividends** | Income representing an investor's share of a business's profits; some corporations reinvest earnings and do not pay dividends | 3.5.C.3 
| 11 | **capital gain** | Profit achieved when an investor sells an asset at a higher price than they paid; prices change in response to business performance, investor demand, and PESTEL forces | 3.5.C.4 
| 12 | **annual rate of return** | Determined by dividing the total dollars gained (income + capital gain) by the price of the asset | 3.5.C.5 
| 13 | **risk tolerance** | Willingness of lenders and investors to take financial risks; higher risk tolerance → higher expected returns | 3.5.C.7 
| 14 | **business plan** | A document including value proposition, market research, marketing strategy, and financial projections used to justify a funding request | 3.5.D.1 
| 15 | **pitch** | A polished presentation summarizing key business information to persuade potential lenders and investors to provide capital | 3.5.D.1 
| 16 | **valuation** | An estimate of what an ownership share is worth; based on financial reports, projections, and external industry data | 3.5.D.2 
| 17 | **product-market fit** | Evidence that a business's product meets a market need; a funding request is more likely to succeed when demonstrated | 3.5.D.3 

## Additional Terms (Frequently Tested)

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **ownership shares** | Portions of ownership in a business issued through equity financing; investors become part owners |
| **business loans** | Loans to businesses similar to personal loans; must be repaid with interest; interest is a business expense |
| **business bank loans** | Loans available to established businesses (typically 2+ years in operation) with proven revenue and capacity to repay |
| **shareholder (investor)** | An individual who buys a share of stock and becomes an owner in the business |
| **financial projections** | Estimates of future financial performance included in a business plan; used to justify funding requests |
| **institutional lenders and investors** | Organizations (banks, venture capital firms) as opposed to individuals that provide financial capital |

## Trap Pairs — Know the Difference

| Pair | How They're Different |
|---|---|
| **loans** vs. **equity financing** | Loans = debt (must be repaid with interest; lender has no ownership). Equity = ownership (no repayment; investor shares in profits and losses permanently). |
| **bonds** vs. **stock** | Bonds = a loan to the company (you are a lender; you earn interest). Stock = ownership in the company (you are an owner; you earn dividends and capital gains). |
| **dividends** vs. **capital gain** | Dividends = regular income payments from profits (like interest). Capital gain = profit from selling an asset for more than you paid (one-time). Both are ways investors earn returns. |
| **business plan** vs. **pitch** | The business plan is the full written document (detailed projections, market research, strategy). The pitch is a condensed presentation summarizing the key points to persuade funders. |
| **valuation** vs. **revenue** | Revenue = total income from sales (what the business earns). Valuation = estimated total worth of the business (what the whole company is worth). A company with $1M revenue might have a $3M valuation. |
| **bootstrapping** vs. **external capital** | Bootstrapping = funding from personal savings and retained earnings (no outside help). External capital = loans or equity from outside sources (bank, investors). Bootstrapping is cheaper but limits growth speed. |

# 1.3 PESTEL Factors — Vocabulary

> Key terms for Topic 1.3. Companion to [[1.3 PESTEL Factors - Summary]] · [[1.3 PESTEL Factors - Slides]].
> Definitions aligned to the CED. Running story: the candy seller's hallway market is **shut down by a memo** from the new principal.

## Core terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **PESTEL** | An acronym for the six kinds of **outside forces** that shape the business landscape: **P**olitical, **E**conomic, **S**ocial, **T**echnological, **E**nvironmental, **L**egal. |
| **Viability** | The ability to **survive and stay successful** in a market over time. |
| **Political factors** | Policies and political dynamics that affect market activity: trade policy, taxes, subsidies, mandates, bans, political stability. |
| **Economic factors** | Conditions of the economy itself: economic stability, household income, inflation, unemployment, interest rates. |
| **Social factors** | Trends in society and culture that affect consumers: demographics, cultural norms, lifestyle trends, population growth. |
| **Technological factors** | Features of a market tied to the availability of technology: internet access, automation, pace of innovation. |
| **Environmental factors** | External environmental conditions that promote or limit market activity: geography, access to resources, waste rules, customer attitudes toward the environment. |
| **Legal factors** | Specific laws, rules, and regulations that impact market activity: employment laws, consumer protection, health and safety rules, IP protection, antitrust laws. |

## Political tools (CED list)

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Tax** | Funds government; often discourages the activity being taxed. |
| **Subsidy** | Supports / incentivizes a specific activity. |
| **Mandate** | Requires a business to do something (e.g., seat belts in every new car). |
| **Ban** | Prohibits an activity entirely. |
| **Tariff** | A tax on imported goods — raises costs for importers. |

## Easily confused (trap pairs)

| Pair | The difference |
|---|---|
| **Political vs. Legal** | Broad policy **choice** (tax, ban, subsidy) vs. specific **law** to comply with (safety code, licensing requirement). Source decides: government *decision* = political; codified *rule* = legal. |
| **Economic vs. Social** | Condition of the **economy itself** (income, inflation, unemployment) vs. **what people choose to value** (lifestyle trends, demographics, cultural norms). A fitness trend costs money but the *source* is social, not economic. |
| **Economic factor vs. one company's finances** | Economic factors describe the **whole economy** — never a single company's revenue or profit. |
| **Subject matter vs. source** | Classify by the **source of the force**, not the subject it touches. A solar-panel *subsidy* concerns technology and environment, but the force acting is a government policy choice → **political**. |

## Must-know rules

- **Rivals live inside the market; PESTEL factors press on it from the outside.**
- **Classify by the source of the change** — not by what it costs, what it touches, or its subject matter.
- **Political vs. legal:** broad policy *choice* → political; specific *rule to follow* → legal.
- **Overlap is real** — pick the **best fit** by the **source** of the force.
- **Factors move** — when they move, they can rewrite an existing market overnight.

> Trap to remember: **"Treat all six factors as equally important"** sounds thorough but is wrong — relevance depends on the specific business.

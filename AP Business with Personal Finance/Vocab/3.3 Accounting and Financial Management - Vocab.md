# 3.3 Accounting and Financial Management — Vocab

## Core Terms (AP Exam)

| # | Term | Definition | Connects To 
|---|---|---|---
| 1 | **financial transactions** | Business activities such as buying resources, receiving payment from customers, distributing profits to owners, and saving and borrowing funds that affect assets, liabilities, and owners' equity | 3.3.A.1 
| 2 | **assets** | Items of value owned by a business or individual | 3.3.A.1, 3.3.A.2, 3.7 
| 3 | **liabilities** | Debts or obligations owed by a business or individual | 3.3.A.1, 3.3.A.2, 3.7 
| 4 | **owners' equity** | The value of the business to its owners; the residual interest after subtracting liabilities from assets | 3.3.A.1, 3.7 
| 5 | **net worth** | For consumers, the difference between assets and liabilities | 3.3.A.2, 3.7.C 
| 6 | **financial statements** | Reports that summarize a business's financial performance, used to monitor financial health, guide decision making, provide accurate information to shareholders/investors/lenders, and ensure compliance with laws and reporting regulations | 3.3.A.3, 3.6–3.8 
| 7 | **generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP)** | Require corporations that sell ownership shares to the public to consistently disclose all positive and negative financial information for each reporting period, typically quarterly or annually | 3.3.A.4, 3.9 
| 8 | **budget** | An organized system for tracking financial transactions used by consumers to monitor finances and make decisions aligned to financial goals | 3.3.A.5, 3.6.C 
| 9 | **accounting departments** | Departments that identify and record all financial transactions during a time period and prepare financial statements | 3.3.B.1 
| 10 | **managerial accountants** | Accountants who provide financial information and analysis to managers and other internal stakeholders for business planning and decision making | 3.3.B.2 
| 11 | **financial accountants** | Accountants who provide financial information and analysis primarily to external stakeholders (shareholders, investors, and lenders) | 3.3.B.3 
| 12 | **finance departments** | Departments responsible for analyzing financial data compiled by accounting departments and recommending strategies for maintaining or improving a business's financial performance | 3.3.B.4 
| 13 | **internal stakeholders** | Managers and other individuals within the business who use financial information | 3.3.B.2 
| 14 | **external stakeholders** | Shareholders, investors, lenders, and others outside the business who use financial information | 3.3.B.3 
| 15 | **financial advisors** | Professionals consumers may hire to help with financial planning, decision making, and tax preparation | 3.3.B.5 

## Trap Pairs — Know the Difference

| Pair | How They're Different |
|---|---|
| **assets** vs. **liabilities** | Assets = what you OWN (items of value). Liabilities = what you OWE (debts). The difference is owners' equity (business) or net worth (consumer). |
| **owners' equity** vs. **net worth** | Both mean assets minus liabilities. Owners' equity applies to a business; net worth applies to consumers/households. Same formula, different contexts. |
| **managerial accountants** vs. **financial accountants** | Managerial accountants serve INTERNAL stakeholders (managers, planning). Financial accountants serve EXTERNAL stakeholders (shareholders, investors, lenders). |
| **accounting department** vs. **finance department** | Accounting = records and reports financial transactions (what happened). Finance = analyzes the data and recommends strategy (what to do next). |
| **GAAP** vs. **internal reporting** | GAAP = standardized rules for external financial reporting (required for public companies). Internal reporting = informal reports for management decisions (not standardized). |

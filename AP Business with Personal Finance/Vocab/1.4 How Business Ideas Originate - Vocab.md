# 1.4 How Business Ideas Originate — Vocabulary

> Key terms for Topic 1.4. Companion to [[1.4 How Business Ideas Originate - Summary]] · [[1.4 How Business Ideas Originate - Slides]].
> Definitions aligned to the CED. Running example: the **candy seller** starting over after the hallway closes.

## Core terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Gap** | A need or want that is **not currently being met** — an unmet need in a market. Can exist even when competitors are present, if a **specific** need goes unserved. |
| **Opportunity** | A **new or better** way to do something — often triggered by a change in technology, society, rules, or resources. |
| **Existing business** | What is **already being sold** in a market — a source of ideas through **replication**, adaptation, or improvement. |
| **Replication** | Copying a business model that works in one market and applying it in **another** — one of the most common sources of business ideas worldwide. |
| **Observation** | The skill of **watching** what people struggle with, complain about, or can't find — the primary way entrepreneurs discover gaps. |
| **Risk** | The chance that things will **not go as planned** — the possibility of unwanted outcomes like losing money, time, or effort. Can be **managed** but never fully eliminated. |
| **Return** | The **gain** (usually money) that an entrepreneur hopes to earn from a business. Never guaranteed. |
| **Risk–return tradeoff** | A situation where taking more risk gives the chance of a **higher return**, but also a higher chance of **losing everything**. |
| **Viability** | The ability to **survive and stay successful** in a market over time (carried forward from 1.3). |
| **Entrepreneur** | A person who **takes on the risk** of starting and running a business. |
| **Entrepreneurial motivation** | The reasons entrepreneurs bear risk, including **potential profits**, **satisfaction of solving a problem**, and **pursuing a passion** — not all motivation is financial. |

## Easily confused (trap pairs)

| Pair | The difference |
|---|---|
| **Gap vs. Opportunity** | An unmet **need** (what's missing) vs. a new or better **way** (what changed). A gap exists now; an opportunity appears when something **changes**. |
| **Risk vs. Uncertainty** | A **known** danger you can name and plan for vs. an **unknown** you can't predict. Most exam scenarios describe **risk**. |
| **Risk vs. Return** | The **chance of loss** vs. the **potential gain**. They move together — higher potential return usually means higher risk. |
| **Replication vs. Gap** | Copying an **existing model** into a new place vs. filling a **need nobody is meeting**. Replication uses existing businesses as the source; gaps come from observation. |
| **Viability vs. Return** | Whether the business **can survive** vs. what the entrepreneur **hopes to earn**. A business can be viable but earn low returns. |
| **Observation vs. Opportunity** | **How** entrepreneurs find gaps (watching people struggle) vs. **what** they find (the gap itself). Observation is the method; the gap is the result. |

## Key relationships

- **Gaps, opportunities, and existing businesses** are the three sources of business ideas — most real ideas combine more than one
- **Strengths must match the opportunity** — a good idea in the wrong hands fails
- **Higher risk ↔ higher potential return** — but return is never guaranteed
- **Viability** requires willing and able buyers, survivable competition, and manageable costs — a good idea that isn't viable stays an idea
- **"High return, zero risk"** = a trap answer — if it sounds too good to be true, it is
- **"Guaranteed"** and **"always"** = trap words in risk–return questions

## Must-know formulas

- **Return = Revenue earned − Amount invested** (the entrepreneur's gain)
- **Profit = Revenue − Total Cost** (carried forward from 1.1)

> Trap to remember: a scenario showing **high investment + no proven demand** = **high risk**, regardless of how exciting the idea sounds.

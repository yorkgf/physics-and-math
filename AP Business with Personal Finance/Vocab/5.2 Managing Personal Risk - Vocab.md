# 5.2 Managing Personal Risk

## Core Terms

| # | Term | Definition | Connects To 
|---|---|---|---
| 1 | Insurable risk | A risk that involves a potential loss due to chance, is quantifiable, and is statistically predictable, allowing an insurer to estimate cost and likelihood | 5.2.A.2 
| 2 | Personal risk | An insurable risk involving the health and well-being of the insured individual, such as injury from an accident or suffering from illness | 5.2.A.3 
| 3 | Property risk | An insurable risk involving loss or damage to the insured individual's property, such as their home or car | 5.2.A.4 
| 4 | Liability risk | An insurable risk involving damage caused to another person's property or health, such as damage from reckless driving | 5.2.A.5 
| 5 | Health insurance | Insurance that reimburses policyholders for medically necessary healthcare and sometimes preventive care; may be provided as an employee benefit | 5.2.B.1, 5.2.B.3 
| 6 | Auto insurance | Insurance covering financial losses from car accidents, damage to the insured's vehicle, and liability for damage caused to others | 5.2.B.1, 5.2.B.4 
| 7 | Homeowner's insurance | Insurance covering financial losses to a home and its contents, as well as liability for injuries that occur on the property | 5.2.B.1, 5.2.B.4 
| 8 | Renter's insurance | Insurance covering a renter's personal property and liability, but not the building itself (which is the landlord's responsibility) | 5.2.B.1, 5.2.B.4 
| 9 | Life insurance | Insurance that provides funds to designated beneficiaries in the event of the insured person's death, intended to replace lost income or fund dependents' needs | 5.2.B.1, 5.2.B.5 
| 10 | Premium | The amount an insurance buyer pays (annual, semi-annual, or monthly) for an insurance policy | 5.2.B.2 
| 11 | Claim | A formal request filed by the policyholder to the insurance company for reimbursement after a covered financial loss | 5.2.B.2 
| 12 | Risk tolerance | An individual's willingness and ability to accept uncertainty or potential financial loss; affects insurance and investment decisions | 5.2.C.1, 5.2.C.3, 5.3.C.3 
| 13 | Insurance fraud | A crime involving misrepresentation or falsified claims by policyholders, or misrepresentation of policies/benefits by insurance sellers | 5.2.C.6 
| 14 | Predatory lending | Deceptive or aggressive lending practices that exploit borrowers, often through high fees, hidden terms, or pressure tactics | 5.2.D.1, 5.2.D.2 
| 15 | Personal financial risk | The risk of financial loss due to events such as car accidents, illness, property damage, or liability — individuals face both financial and physical risks in daily life | 5.2.A.1 
| 16 | Legal insurance requirement | Insurance coverage mandated by law, such as auto liability insurance required by most U.S. states and property insurance required by mortgage lenders | 5.2.C.2 
| 17 | Dependent coverage | Additional insurance needs that arise when an individual has dependents (spouse, children), including family health insurance, multiple vehicle policies, and higher life insurance amounts | 5.2.C.4 
| 18 | Risk reduction | Actions taken to lower insurance premiums by reducing risky behavior — e.g., maintaining a good driving record (auto insurance) or not smoking (life insurance) | 5.2.C.5 

## Additional Terms

| # | Term | Definition | Connects To |
|---|---|---|---|
| 19 | Deductible | The amount the policyholder must pay out of pocket before insurance begins covering costs; higher deductibles typically mean lower premiums | 5.2.C.3 |
| 20 | Coverage | The total amount of protection provided by an insurance policy; the maximum the insurer will pay for a covered loss | 5.2.B.2 |
| 21 | Beneficiary | The person designated to receive funds from a life insurance policy upon the insured person's death | 5.2.B.5 |
| 22 | Extended warranty | A service contract on an expensive purchase (car, appliance) that extends the manufacturer's warranty and covers repairs beyond the original terms | 5.2.B.6 |
| 23 | Identity theft | The fraudulent acquisition and use of someone's personal information (SSN, credit card numbers) for financial gain | 5.2.D.1, 5.2.D.3 |
| 24 | Phishing | A fraudulent attempt to obtain personal or financial information by disguising as a trustworthy entity in electronic communications | 5.2.D.1 |

## Trap Pairs

| Pair | Term A | Term B | Key Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Premium** | **Deductible** | Premium = what you PAY regularly to have insurance. Deductible = what you PAY out of pocket when you FILE a claim. Higher deductible → lower premium (and vice versa). |
| 2 | **Personal risk** | **Property risk** | Personal risk = harm to YOUR BODY (injury, illness). Property risk = harm to YOUR THINGS (home, car). Both are insurable, but different policies cover each. |
| 3 | **Liability risk** | **Property risk** | Liability = damage YOU cause to OTHERS or THEIR property. Property = damage to YOUR OWN property. Auto insurance covers both: collision (your car) + liability (the other car). |
| 4 | **Tax credit** | **Insurance claim** | Tax credit = reduces taxes you owe to the government. Insurance claim = request for reimbursement from your insurance company after a loss. Both give you money back, but from different sources. |
| 5 | **Predatory lending** | **Insurance fraud** | Predatory lending = the LENDER exploits the borrower. Insurance fraud = the POLICYHOLDER or INSURER commits a crime. Both are illegal and harmful to consumers. |
| 6 | **Homeowner's insurance** | **Renter's insurance** | Homeowner's = covers the BUILDING + contents + liability. Renter's = covers CONTENTS + liability only (not the building). The landlord's policy covers the building for renters. |

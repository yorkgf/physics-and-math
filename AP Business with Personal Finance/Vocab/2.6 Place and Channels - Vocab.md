# 2.6 Place and Channels — Vocab

## Core Terms (AP Exam)

| # | Term | Definition | Connects To 
|---|------|-----------|-------------
| 1 | **Place** | Where and how customers access a business's products, whether through physical locations, online platforms, or membership-based models | 2.6.A 
| 2 | **Marketing channel** | All the individuals and businesses involved in moving a finished product from the producer to the final customer | 2.6.A; 1.8 (supply chain) 
| 3 | **Distribution channel** | A synonym for marketing channel — the path a product follows from production to the end user | 2.6.A 
| 4 | **Supply chain** | The full network of people and businesses that produce and distribute a product, from raw materials through final delivery; the marketing channel is the final stage of this chain | 1.8; 2.6.A 
| 5 | **Direct channel** | A marketing path that connects the business straight to its customers with no intermediaries in between | 2.6.A; 2.6.B 
| 6 | **Indirect channel** | A marketing path that includes intermediaries such as wholesalers or retailers between the business and its customers | 2.6.A; 2.6.B 
| 7 | **Intermediary** | A third party — such as a wholesaler, retailer, or distributor — that sits between the producer and the final customer in a channel | 2.6.A 
| 8 | **Wholesaler** | A business that buys products in bulk from manufacturers and resells them in smaller quantities to retailers or other businesses | 2.6.A 
| 9 | **Retailer** | A business that sells products directly to the final consumer, either in physical stores or online | 2.6.A 
| 10 | **B2C channel** | A marketing channel designed to deliver products from businesses directly to individual consumers, such as websites or retail stores | 2.6.A 
| 11 | **B2B channel** | A marketing channel designed to deliver products from one business to another, often using industrial distributors | 2.6.A 
| 12 | **Legally mandated channel** | A distribution path required by law for certain products — such as prescription medications or health-and-safety goods — that restricts how and where the product can be sold | 2.6.A

## Additional Terms (Frequently Tested)

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Industrial distributor** | A middleman that sells products to other businesses rather than to individual consumers; the standard B2B channel |
| **Company-owned store** | A retail location operated directly by the brand, giving the business full control over pricing, merchandising, and customer experience |
| **Club membership model** | A retail format (e.g., Costco, Sam's Club) where customers pay a fee to access products, often at lower prices in bulk |
| **Channel partner** | Any external business — wholesaler, retailer, or distributor — that helps move a product to the final customer through an indirect channel |
| **Channel conflict** | Tension that arises when two or more channel partners compete for the same customers, such as a brand's website competing with its own retail partners |

## Trap Pairs — Know the Difference

| Pair | How They're Different |
|------|-----------------------|
| **Direct vs. indirect channel** | Direct = producer sells straight to the customer (no middleman). Indirect = one or more intermediaries sit between producer and customer. |
| **Wholesaler vs. retailer** | Wholesaler = sells in bulk to other businesses (B2B). Retailer = sells in smaller quantities to the final consumer (B2C). |
| **Marketing channel vs. supply chain** | Supply chain = the entire production and distribution network, starting from raw materials. Marketing channel = only the final stage, from finished product to the end customer. |
| **B2B channel vs. B2C channel** | B2B = business sells to another business (industrial distributors). B2C = business sells to individual consumers (stores, websites). |
| **Intermediary vs. distributor** | Intermediary = any third party in the channel (wholesaler, retailer, agent). Distributor = a specific type of intermediary that buys finished products from manufacturers and sells them onward to retailers. |

## Channel Selection Decision Framework

| Factor | Direct Channel | Indirect Channel |
|--------|---------------|-----------------|
| **Control** | High — business sets pricing, branding, and customer experience | Low — partners influence how the product is presented and sold |
| **Cost to establish** | Higher — must build or buy stores, websites, sales teams, and distribution expertise | Lower — leverages partners' existing infrastructure and networks |
| **Reach** | Limited — constrained by the business's own footprint and resources | Broader — taps into partners' established customer bases and geographic coverage |
| **Expertise needed** | Must acquire or develop sales, logistics, and distribution capabilities internally | Partners bring existing expertise in warehousing, selling, and last-mile delivery |
| **Customer experience** | Consistent and branded end-to-end | May vary depending on partner quality and priorities |
| **Profit margin per unit** | Higher — no intermediary takes a cut | Lower — intermediaries claim a share of revenue |
| **Best for** | Premium or differentiated products where brand experience matters; businesses with resources to invest | Products that need wide availability quickly; businesses that lack distribution infrastructure |

> **Rule of thumb:** A business with limited resources and a need for fast, wide market access should lean toward indirect channels. A business that prioritizes brand control and can afford the investment should lean toward direct channels. Many businesses use a mix of both.

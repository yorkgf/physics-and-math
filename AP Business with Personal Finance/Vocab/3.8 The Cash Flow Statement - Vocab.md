# 3.8 The Cash Flow Statement — Vocab

## Core Terms (AP Exam)

| # | Term | Definition | Connects To 
|---|---|---|---
| 1 | **cash flow statement** | A financial statement that shows how cash inflows and outflows impact a business's cash balance over a financial reporting period | 3.8.A.1 
| 2 | **cash inflows** | Payments that increase a business's cash balance; typically include customer payments, interest/dividends earned, proceeds from asset sales, and infusions of financial capital (e.g., new loans) | 3.8.A.3 
| 3 | **cash outflows** | Payments that decrease a business's cash balance; typically include payments to employees and suppliers, interest expense, taxes, asset purchases, debt repayment, and dividends | 3.8.A.4 
| 4 | **cash balance** | The amount of cash a business has on hand at a given time; monitored to ensure sufficient cash for recurring expenses, repaying lenders, and managing unforeseen expenses | 3.8.A.2 
| 5 | **positive cash flow** | When cash inflows exceed cash outflows at the end of a period | 3.8.A.5 
| 6 | **negative cash flow** | When cash outflows exceed cash inflows at the end of a period; may signal inability to pay current expenses, which could lead to shut down or bankruptcy even if net income is positive | 3.8.A.5, 3.8.B.2 
| 7 | **financial obligations** | Commitments a business must meet, including paying employees, suppliers, creditors, and shareholders | 3.8.B.1 

## Trap Pairs — Know the Difference

| Pair | How They're Different |
|---|---|
| **cash flow statement** vs. **income statement** | Cash flow statement tracks ACTUAL CASH movement (when money enters or leaves the bank account). Income statement uses ACCRUAL accounting (records transactions when earned or incurred, regardless of when cash moves). A business can be profitable on the income statement but have negative cash flow, and vice versa. |
| **cash inflows** vs. **revenue** | Revenue = income earned (accrual basis — recorded when the sale occurs). Cash inflow = cash actually received (cash basis — recorded when payment arrives). A $100K sale in December with Net-30 terms = $100K revenue in December but $100K cash inflow in January. |
| **positive cash flow** vs. **net profit** | Positive cash flow = more cash came in than went out (cash basis). Net profit = revenue exceeded all expenses (accrual basis). Both can be positive, both can be negative, or they can differ — and that's why both statements matter. |
| **operating cash flow** vs. **investing cash flow** vs. **financing cash flow** | Operating = cash from core business activities (selling, paying employees). Investing = cash from buying/selling long-term assets (equipment, property). Financing = cash from borrowing, repaying debt, or issuing stock. All three are sections of the cash flow statement. |
| **negative cash flow** vs. **bankruptcy** | Negative cash flow for one period does not mean bankruptcy. It means outflows exceeded inflows that period. Bankruptcy occurs when a business persistently cannot meet its obligations. A seasonal business may have negative quarterly cash flow but positive annual cash flow. |

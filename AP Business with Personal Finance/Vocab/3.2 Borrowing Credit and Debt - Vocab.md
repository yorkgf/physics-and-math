# 3.2 Borrowing, Credit, and Debt — Vocab

## Core Terms (AP Exam)

| # | Term | Definition | Connects To 
|---|---|---|---
| 1 | **borrowing** | The act of obtaining money from a lender with the obligation to repay it with interest | 3.2.A 
| 2 | **loan** | Borrowed money that must be repaid with interest | 3.2.A.2 
| 3 | **interest rate** | The percentage charged on borrowed money; varies based on the lender, type of loan, amount borrowed, and borrower's credit history | 3.2.A.2 
| 4 | **repayment terms** | The conditions under which a loan must be repaid (schedule, duration, penalties) | 3.2.A.2 
| 5 | **secured loans** | Loans used to buy items that can serve as collateral, such as cars and houses; typically have lower interest rates | 3.2.A.2 
| 6 | **unsecured loans** | General consumer loans that are not backed by collateral; typically have higher interest rates than secured loans | 3.2.A.2 
| 7 | **collateral** | An asset that can serve as security for a loan; the lender can seize it if the borrower defaults | 3.2.A.2 
| 8 | **default** | When a borrower fails to repay a loan according to the agreed terms | 3.2.B.1 
| 9 | **creditworthiness** | A consumer's ability and likelihood to repay a loan, assessed by lenders based on income, savings, existing debt, and credit reports | 3.2.B.2 
| 10 | **credit reports** | Documents created by credit bureaus that detail a consumer's past use of credit; created each time a consumer interacts with a financial institution | 3.2.B.3 
| 11 | **credit bureaus** | Organizations that collect information each time a consumer interacts with a financial institution and create credit reports (also called credit reporting agencies) | 3.2.B.3 
| 12 | **credit score** | A numerical reflection of a consumer's past use of credit, included in a credit report; can be shared with potential lenders, employers, landlords, insurance companies, and government agencies | 3.2.B.4 
| 13 | **bankruptcy** | A legal process that eliminates some debts and helps the borrower set up a repayment plan for other debts; available to individuals who face negative consequences from unmanageable debt | 3.2.C.6 
| 14 | **consumer protection laws** | Laws that require lenders to communicate credit terms clearly and explicitly to borrowers; govern debt collection tactics and prohibit discriminatory lending practices | 3.2.A.5 

## Additional Terms (Frequently Tested)

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **liability / debt** | An obligation to repay borrowed money; borrowing creates a personal liability or debt (connects to 3.3, 3.7) |
| **credit card companies** | Entities that lend money to consumers through credit cards |
| **mortgage lenders** | Entities that lend money to consumers for home purchases |
| **alternative financial services** | Services such as payday loans, check-cashing services, and instant tax refunds that lend money to consumers |
| **payday loans** | A type of alternative financial service — short-term, high-interest loans against a consumer's next paycheck |
| **down payment** | Using some income or saved money to pay part of the cost of a major purchase like a home or car; helps obtain better loan terms |
| **debt management assistance** | Help available to individuals who face negative consequences from unmanageable debt, such as property seizures |
| **property seizures** | A negative consequence of unmanageable debt — a lender can take the borrower's property if a secured loan is not repaid |

## Trap Pairs — Know the Difference

| Pair | How They're Different |
|---|---|
| **secured loans** vs. **unsecured loans** | Secured loans are backed by collateral (car, house) → lower interest rates. Unsecured loans have no collateral → higher interest rates because the lender takes on more risk. |
| **credit report** vs. **credit score** | The credit report is the full document detailing your credit history. The credit score is a single number (typically 300–850) derived from that report. Lenders check both. |
| **default** vs. **bankruptcy** | Default = failing to repay one loan. Bankruptcy = a legal process for handling unmanageable overall debt; may eliminate some debts but has severe long-term credit consequences. |
| **interest rate** (savings) vs. **interest rate** (borrowing) | When saving, interest rate = what you earn. When borrowing, interest rate = what you pay. Higher rates are good for savers but bad for borrowers. (connects to 3.1) |
| **down payment** vs. **collateral** | A down payment is cash you pay upfront to reduce the loan amount. Collateral is an asset the lender can seize if you default. Both reduce lender risk, but in different ways. |

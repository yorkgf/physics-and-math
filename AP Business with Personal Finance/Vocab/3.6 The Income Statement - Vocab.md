# 3.6 The Income Statement — Vocab

## Core Terms (AP Exam)

| # | Term | Definition | Connects To 
|---|---|---|---
| 1 | **income statement** | A financial statement that compares total revenue to total costs over a time period to determine net profit or loss; also called the statement of profit and loss; typically provides multiple periods for comparison | 3.6.A.1 
| 2 | **revenue** | Income generated by a business's core activities, such as sales of goods and services | 3.6.A.3 
| 3 | **cost of goods sold (COGS)** | Direct costs of production; one of three major categories on the income statement | 3.6.A.2, 3.4.B.3 
| 4 | **operating expenses** | Indirect costs of running the business; one of three major categories on the income statement | 3.6.A.2, 3.4.B.5 
| 5 | **gross profit** | Profit earned after deducting only direct costs of production; calculated as revenue minus COGS | 3.6.A.4 
| 6 | **operating profit** | Profit earned after deducting COGS and operating expenses; represents net income before interest and taxes; calculated as gross profit minus operating expenses | 3.6.A.6 
| 7 | **interest expense** | The cost of borrowing money through loans (including bonds) | 3.6.A.7, 3.5 
| 8 | **pretax income** | Calculated by subtracting interest expense from operating profit | 3.6.A.7 
| 9 | **tax expense** | Taxes paid on positive pretax income; included on the income statement | 3.6.A.8 
| 10 | **net profit** | The final dollar amount on the income statement; calculated as pretax income minus taxes; known as the "bottom line"; represents profit earned by owners at the end of a period | 3.6.A.9 
| 11 | **gross profit margin** | Calculated as gross profit ÷ total revenue; evaluates how successfully a business sets prices and manages direct costs | 3.6.B.2 
| 12 | **operating profit margin** | Calculated as operating profit ÷ total revenue; evaluates how successfully a business markets, sells, and controls operating expenses | 3.6.B.3 
| 13 | **net profit margin** | Calculated as net profit ÷ total revenue; evaluates overall profitability; the percentage of revenue that flows to owners as income | 3.6.B.4 
| 14 | **percent change** | Calculated as [(Current Value − Initial Value) ÷ Initial Value] × 100; used to determine the rate of change for any data point | 3.6.B.6 

## Additional Terms (Frequently Tested)

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **statement of profit and loss** | Another name for the income statement |
| **bottom line** | Another name for net profit — the last line on the income statement |
| **selling expenses** | A category of operating expenses: advertising costs, salespeople's salaries, trade promotions |
| **general and administrative expenses** | A category of operating expenses: office salaries, rent on office space, insurance, IT |
| **nonrecurring expenses** | One-time costs that are not expected to repeat (e.g., lawsuit settlement, asset write-down) |
| **projected income statements** | Estimates of future revenues and expenses; help businesses plan for costs, identify funding needs, and maintain cash to meet obligations |
| **budgets** | Plans for revenues, costs, and resulting profit/loss for a period; for consumers, plans for net pay, savings, and expenses |
| **net pay** | Income after taxes and other deductions; used in consumer budgets |
| **revenue projection** | An estimate of future revenue based on planned pricing strategies and market research about customer demand and industry trends |

## Formulas (Know These Cold)

| Formula | Calculation |
|---|---|
| **Gross Profit** | Revenue − COGS |
| **Operating Profit** | Gross Profit − Operating Expenses |
| **Pretax Income** | Operating Profit − Interest Expense |
| **Net Profit** | Pretax Income − Tax Expense |
| **Gross Profit Margin** | Gross Profit ÷ Revenue |
| **Operating Profit Margin** | Operating Profit ÷ Revenue |
| **Net Profit Margin** | Net Profit ÷ Revenue |
| **Percent Change** | [(Current − Initial) ÷ Initial] × 100 |

## Trap Pairs — Know the Difference

| Pair | How They're Different |
|---|---|
| **revenue** vs. **net profit** | Revenue = total income from sales (top line). Net profit = what's left after ALL costs (bottom line). Revenue can be $10M while net profit is only $500K. |
| **gross profit** vs. **net profit** | Gross profit = revenue minus ONLY production costs (COGS). Net profit = revenue minus ALL costs (COGS + operating expenses + interest + taxes). Gross profit is always ≥ net profit. |
| **gross profit margin** vs. **net profit margin** | Gross profit margin measures production efficiency. Net profit margin measures overall profitability. A company can have a high gross margin but a low net margin if operating expenses are high. |
| **operating profit** vs. **pretax income** | Operating profit = before interest and taxes. Pretax income = after interest, before taxes. The difference is interest expense — the cost of debt. |
| **income statement** vs. **cash flow statement** | Income statement uses accrual accounting (records when earned/incurred). Cash flow statement tracks actual cash movement. Revenue ≠ cash received. (connects to 3.8) |
| **income statement** vs. **balance sheet** | Income statement covers a PERIOD of time (e.g., one year). Balance sheet is a SNAPSHOT at a single point in time (e.g., Dec 31). (connects to 3.7) |

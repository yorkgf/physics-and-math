# 3.7 The Balance Sheet and Net Worth — Vocab

## Core Terms (AP Exam)

| # | Term | Definition | Connects To 
|---|---|---|---
| 1 | **balance sheet** | A financial statement that compares a business's assets to its liabilities and owners' equity at a specific point in time | 3.7.A.1 
| 2 | **balance sheet equation** | Assets = Liabilities + Owners' Equity; also called the fundamental accounting equation | 3.7.A.1 
| 3 | **assets** | Every item of value owned by a business, grouped by liquidity into current assets, long-term assets, and intangible assets | 3.7.A.2 
| 4 | **liquidity** | The ease with which an asset can be converted into cash | 3.7.A.2.i 
| 5 | **current assets** | Highly liquid assets including cash, short-term investments, accounts receivable, and inventory; used to fund day-to-day operations | 3.7.A.2.ii 
| 6 | **accounts receivable** | Money owed to a business by its customers; a current asset | 3.7.A.2.ii 
| 7 | **inventory** | Goods held by a business for sale or production; a current asset | 3.7.A.2.ii 
| 8 | **long-term assets** | Assets considered less liquid, including fixed assets and long-term investments | 3.7.A.2.iii 
| 9 | **fixed assets** | Long-term assets such as production plants, equipment, and vehicles used to run business operations | 3.7.A.2.iii 
| 10 | **intangible assets** | Non-physical items such as patents, brand names, and trademarks that have value because they represent potential revenue | 3.7.A.2.iv 
| 11 | **liabilities** | Debts or obligations owed by a business, grouped into current and long-term liabilities in order of when payment is due | 3.7.A.3 
| 12 | **current liabilities** | Debts due within one year: accounts payable, short-term debt, current payments on long-term debt, and accrued operating expenses | 3.7.A.3.i 
| 13 | **accounts payable** | Money owed to a business's suppliers; a current liability | 3.7.A.3.i 
| 14 | **long-term liabilities** | Obligations to pay beyond one year, such as mortgages, bank loans, and long-term bonds | 3.7.A.3.ii 
| 15 | **owners' equity** | The difference between assets and liabilities; the net worth of a business to its owners; often comprised of stock and retained earnings | 3.7.A.4 
| 16 | **retained earnings** | The business's cumulative profits not paid out as dividends; a component of owners' equity | 3.7.A.4 
| 17 | **working capital** | Current assets minus current liabilities; measures whether a business has sufficient short-term resources to fund day-to-day operations | 3.7.B.2 
| 18 | **bankruptcy** | A legal process through which a business can liquidate assets, eliminate or repay debts, and either shut down or reorganize under legal supervision | 3.7.B.3 
| 19 | **personal net worth** | Typically calculated for a household; determined by adding all household assets and subtracting all liabilities | 3.7.C.1 

## Additional Terms (Frequently Tested)

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **accrued operating expenses** | Expenses that have been incurred but not yet paid (e.g., wages earned but not yet disbursed, utilities used but not yet billed); a current liability |
| **patents** | An intangible asset — legal protection for an invention |
| **brand names** | An intangible asset — the value of customer recognition and reputation |
| **trademarks** | An intangible asset — legally protected symbols, names, or designs |
| **positive net worth** | When a business's assets exceed its liabilities |
| **liquidate assets** | Converting assets to cash as part of the bankruptcy process |
| **household** | May include more than one individual; the unit for which personal net worth is typically calculated |

## Formulas (Know These Cold)

| Formula | Calculation |
|---|---|
| **Balance Sheet Equation** | Assets = Liabilities + Owners' Equity |
| **Owners' Equity** | Assets − Liabilities |
| **Working Capital** | Current Assets − Current Liabilities |
| **Current Ratio** | Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities |
| **Personal Net Worth** | Total Household Assets − Total Household Liabilities |

## Trap Pairs — Know the Difference

| Pair | How They're Different |
|---|---|
| **current assets** vs. **long-term assets** | Current assets are highly liquid and fund day-to-day operations (cash, receivables, inventory). Long-term assets are less liquid and used to run the business (equipment, buildings). |
| **current liabilities** vs. **long-term liabilities** | Current liabilities are due within one year. Long-term liabilities are due beyond one year. Both are debts, but the payment timeline differs. |
| **accounts receivable** vs. **accounts payable** | Receivable = money OWED TO you (asset). Payable = money YOU OWE (liability). They are mirror images on opposite sides of the balance sheet. |
| **owners' equity** vs. **retained earnings** | Owners' equity is the total (stock + retained earnings). Retained earnings are just the cumulative profits reinvested in the business. Retained earnings are a COMPONENT of owners' equity. |
| **working capital** vs. **owners' equity** | Working capital = current assets − current liabilities (short-term health). Owners' equity = total assets − total liabilities (long-term net worth). Both are "what's left" but over different time horizons. |
| **balance sheet** vs. **income statement** | Balance sheet = SNAPSHOT at one point in time (Dec 31). Income statement = PERIOD of time (Jan 1 – Dec 31). The balance sheet shows position; the income statement shows performance. (connects to 3.6) |
| **personal net worth** vs. **business net worth** | Personal net worth = household assets − household liabilities (for individuals). Business net worth = owners' equity = business assets − business liabilities. Daniel's personal net worth INCLUDES his business ownership as an asset. |

# 3.1 Saving for Future Purchases — Vocab

## Core Terms (AP Exam)

| # | Term | Definition | Connects To 
|---|---|---|---
| 1 | **saving** | Setting aside a portion of income for future use; creates a personal asset and may earn interest | 3.1.A 
| 2 | **income** | Money earned by consumers, typically by working for an organization; can also come from self-employment, rental properties, government programs, investments, or retirement accounts | 3.1.A.1 
| 3 | **interest** | Payment earned on savings, determined by the interest rate, the amount of savings, the type of savings vehicle, and conditions in the economy | 3.1.A.3 
| 4 | **interest rate** | The percentage at which savings earn interest | 3.1.A.3 
| 5 | **savings vehicle** | The type of account or instrument used to hold savings (e.g., savings account, CD, money market account) | 3.1.A.3, 3.1.C 
| 6 | **automated savings plans** | Programs that allocate a set amount of income to savings during each pay period | 3.1.A.4 
| 7 | **retirement savings plans** | Programs that allow individuals to save on taxes when they save for retirement (e.g., 401k, IRA) | 3.1.A.4 
| 8 | **health savings accounts (HSAs)** | Accounts that allow individuals to save on taxes when they allocate money for health-related expenses | 3.1.A.4 
| 9 | **inflation** | An increase in the prices of goods and services; erodes the purchasing power of savings because money will buy fewer goods and services in the future | 3.1.B.2 
| 10 | **purchasing power** | The value of money in terms of what it can buy; eroded by inflation | 3.1.B.2 
| 11 | **financial institutions** | Organizations such as commercial banks and credit unions that offer savings vehicles | 3.1.B.4, 3.1.C.2 
| 12 | **federally insured** | Protection provided by the federal government on deposits at financial institutions (up to $250,000), meaning the consumer does not lose their deposit if the bank goes out of business | 3.1.C.4–3.1.C.6 

## Additional Terms (Frequently Tested)

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **barriers to saving** | Factors that make saving difficult, including inconsistent income, recurring expenses that exceed income, instant gratification, lifestyle inflation, and impulse buying |
| **instant gratification** | A psychological force that makes saving difficult — the desire to spend now rather than save for the future |
| **lifestyle inflation** | A psychological force that makes saving difficult — the tendency to increase spending as income rises |
| **impulse buying** | A psychological force that makes saving difficult — unplanned purchases driven by emotion |
| **savings account** | A deposit account at a financial institution that typically pays interest and is federally insured up to $250,000; features vary by institution |
| **money market account** | Similar to a savings account, federally insured; may require a larger minimum deposit, charge higher fees, pay higher interest rates, and provide easier access to cash |
| **certificate of deposit (CD)** | Similar to a savings account, federally insured; typically pays higher interest than savings or money market accounts, but depositors are restricted from withdrawing funds for a set period |
| **mobile payment accounts** | Vehicles for storing savings that make money easy to spend; unless offered by insured institutions, not federally insured and typically do not pay interest |
| **cryptocurrency accounts** | Vehicles for storing savings that users hope will gain value as an asset; not federally insured and typically do not pay interest |
| **cost of living** | The amount of money needed to cover basic expenses; affected by economic conditions |
| **PESTEL factors** | Political, economic, social, technological, environmental, and legal forces that can impact the value of savings and incentives to save |

## Trap Pairs — Know the Difference

| Pair | How They're Different |
|---|---|
| **savings account** vs. **money market account** | Both are federally insured and pay interest. Money market accounts typically require higher minimum deposits, may charge higher fees, but offer higher interest rates and easier access to cash. |
| **savings account** vs. **certificate of deposit (CD)** | Both are federally insured. CDs pay higher interest but lock your money for a set period (1 month to 5 years) — early withdrawal incurs penalties. Savings accounts allow withdrawals at any time. |
| **interest rate** vs. **purchasing power** | Interest rate determines how much your savings grow; purchasing power determines what that money can actually buy. A 3% interest rate with 4% inflation means purchasing power is shrinking. |
| **saving** vs. **investing** | Saving = setting aside money in low-risk, federally insured vehicles (savings account, CD). Investing = putting money into assets (stocks, bonds) with higher potential returns but also higher risk. (connects to 3.5) |
| **automated savings plan** vs. **retirement savings plan** | Automated savings plans allocate income to general savings each pay period. Retirement savings plans (401k, IRA) are tax-advantaged accounts specifically for retirement, often with employer matching. |

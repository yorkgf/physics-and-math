# 1.2 Markets and Competitive Advantage — Vocabulary

> Key terms for Topic 1.2. Companion to [[1.2 Markets and Competitive Advantage - Summary]] · [[1.2 Markets and Competitive Advantage - Slides]].
> Definitions aligned to the CED. Running example: the **candy seller's hallway** (now with two sellers).

## Core terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Market** | Any **physical or virtual space** where sellers interact with their buyers; can be local, regional, or global. |
| **Voluntary exchange** | A transaction in which **both sides** walk away better off — sellers earn revenue, buyers receive value. |
| **Prevailing market price** | The going price for a good or service in a competitive market that **no individual seller can hold themselves above**. |
| **Competitive advantage** | The ability to **outperform rivals**, leading to **increased market share** and potentially increased profits. |
| **Market share** | The portion of a market's **total sales** that one business controls. |
| **Rival businesses** | Businesses competing for the **same customers** in the same market. |
| **Differentiation** | Building products with **distinguishing features** that set them apart from competitors' offerings. |
| **Barrier to entry** | An obstacle that makes it **difficult for new firms** to compete in a market. |
| **Monopoly** | A market **without competition**, in which only **one business** operates and produces a **unique** good or service. |
| **Cost leadership** | A strategy of achieving competitive advantage by **producing as efficiently as possible** to charge the **lowest price** — common in commodity and agricultural markets where products are undifferentiated. |

## Barriers to entry — the five types (CED list)

| Barrier | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Intellectual property rights** | Legal protections (patents, trademarks, copyrights) that prevent copies. |
| **Regulations** | Government rules that limit or condition who can enter a market. |
| **Limited access to resource suppliers** | New firms can't obtain the inputs they need. |
| **High startup costs** | The capital required to enter is prohibitively large (e.g., airlines). |
| **Low prices from large-scale operation** | Incumbents' scale-driven prices are below what a new entrant can profitably match. |

## Differentiation — the five routes (CED list)

| Route | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Higher quality** | The product works better or lasts longer. |
| **Unique product features** | Capabilities or design no rival offers. |
| **Better customer service** | Support, returns, or experience that competitors don't match. |
| **Lower prices** | Same type of product, cheaper — works when the product is differentiated enough that price isn't the only comparison. |
| **More effective marketing** | Communicating the product's value more convincingly than rivals. |

## Easily confused (trap pairs)

| Pair | The difference |
|---|---|
| **Competitive advantage vs. Monopoly** | **Outperforming rivals** vs. having **no rivals at all**. Advantage wins a contest; monopoly is the absence of the contest. |
| **Differentiation vs. Barrier to entry** | Uniqueness a rival **can copy tomorrow** vs. an obstacle a rival **can't easily get past**. |
| **Market share vs. Revenue** | A **percentage** of market sales vs. **dollar amount** earned. Share can fall while revenue rises (if the whole market grew). |
| **Cost advantage vs. Differentiation** | **Cost** lives inside the business (cheaper to produce); **differentiation** lives inside the product (visible distinguishing features to the buyer). |
| **Identical-product market vs. Differentiated-product market** | Buyers choose **purely on price** vs. buyers consider **features, quality, service, and price**. |

## Must-know mechanisms

- **Market price:** sellers push up · buyers push down · competitive markets settle at a prevailing price.
- **Identical-product market** → win on **efficiency / cost**.
- **Differentiated-product market** → win on **superiority** (one or more of the five routes).
- **Differentiation** is how a business **exits a pure price contest**.

> Trap to remember: answer choices with **"always" / "never" / "guarantee"** are almost never correct — they convert a market-specific outcome into a fake universal law.

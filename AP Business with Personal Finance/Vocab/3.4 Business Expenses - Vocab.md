# 3.4 Business Expenses — Vocab

## Core Terms (AP Exam)

| # | Term | Definition | Connects To 
|---|---|---|---
| 1 | **startup costs** | One-time expenditures associated with launching a new business or product as well as initial expenses incurred while establishing the business | 3.4.A.1 
| 2 | **one-time expenditures** | Costs incurred once at launch, including legal fees, incorporation and licensing fees, and in some cases equipment purchases | 3.4.A.2 
| 3 | **initial expenses** | Expenses incurred while establishing the business (occupancy, R&D, marketing, insurance, initial inventories) that become ongoing once the business begins operation | 3.4.A.3 
| 4 | **direct costs** | Costs tied to the production or delivery of specific goods or services | 3.4.B.1 
| 5 | **indirect costs** | Costs associated with running the business, not tied to specific goods or services; also called operating expenses | 3.4.B.1 
| 6 | **fixed expenses** | Costs that do not change with production or service levels (e.g., rent, insurance, base salaries) | 3.4.B.2 
| 7 | **variable expenses** | Costs that increase as production/service levels increase (e.g., raw materials, hourly wages, packaging) | 3.4.B.2 
| 8 | **cost of goods sold (COGS)** | Direct costs incurred by businesses that produce goods; includes raw materials, production supplies, production-related wages, and facility operating costs; components can be fixed or variable | 3.4.B.3 
| 9 | **cost of sales** | Direct costs for businesses that provide services, including direct labor expenses, travel costs, and materials associated with delivering the service | 3.4.B.4 
| 10 | **operating expenses** | Recurring indirect costs that are typically fixed; include occupancy, office/sales salaries, marketing, supplies, utilities, maintenance, and insurance | 3.4.B.5 
| 11 | **insurance** | Protection purchased by businesses (and consumers) against financial losses from accidents, injuries, or property damage; some types are required, many are optional | 3.4.B.6 
| 12 | **risk tolerance** | Willingness to accept risk and/or pay for insurance to minimize risk; varies among businesses and consumers | 3.4.B.6 

## Additional Terms (Frequently Tested)

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **legal fees** | A one-time startup expenditure for attorneys, contracts, and business formation |
| **incorporation and licensing fees** | One-time fees paid to government entities to legally establish the business and obtain operating permits |
| **occupancy expense** | Cost of physical space for the business (rent, lease payments); becomes an ongoing operating expense |
| **research and development (R&D)** | Initial and ongoing expenses for developing new products or improving existing ones |
| **raw materials** | A variable component of COGS — the basic materials used to produce goods |
| **production supplies** | A component of COGS — materials used in the production process but not part of the final product |
| **direct labor expenses** | A component of cost of sales for service businesses — wages paid to workers who directly deliver the service |
| **workers compensation insurance** | A type of insurance required by law for businesses; covers employee injuries on the job |

## Trap Pairs — Know the Difference

| Pair | How They're Different |
|---|---|
| **one-time expenditures** vs. **initial expenses** | One-time expenditures are paid once and never again (legal fees, equipment). Initial expenses are paid during startup but become ONGOING costs (rent, inventory, marketing). |
| **direct costs** vs. **indirect costs** | Direct costs are tied to producing a specific product (beans, cups). Indirect costs run the business itself (rent, insurance, accounting). Same money, different classification. |
| **COGS** vs. **cost of sales** | COGS = direct costs for goods-producing businesses (manufacturing). Cost of sales = direct costs for service businesses (consulting, cafes). Both are "direct costs" but for different business types. |
| **fixed expenses** vs. **variable expenses** | Fixed = same amount regardless of production (rent, insurance). Variable = changes with production volume (raw materials, hourly wages). Note: COGS can contain BOTH fixed and variable components. |
| **operating expenses** vs. **COGS** | Operating expenses = indirect costs of running the business. COGS = direct costs of producing goods. Both appear on the income statement but in different categories. (connects to 3.6) |

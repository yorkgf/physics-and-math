# 2.1 Marketing to Customers — Vocabulary

## Core Terms (12)

| #   | Term                                   | One-sentence definition                                                                                                                                   | CED anchor 
| --- | -------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- 
| 1   | **Marketing**                          | All activities a business undertakes to identify customers' problems, needs, and wants, and to promote, sell, and deliver products that meet them         | 2.1.A.1    
| 2   | **Customer data**                      | Information about customers that helps marketers decide which customers to serve, which products to produce, and how to market effectively and profitably | 2.1.A.2    
| 3   | **Demographic characteristics**        | Measurable qualities used to describe a population, such as age, sex, race, ethnicity, income, and location                                               | 2.1.A.3.i  
| 4   | **Psychographic characteristics**      | Cognitive and behavioral factors within a population, including individuals' interests, activities, values, and lifestyles                                | 2.1.A.3.ii 
| 5   | **Market segmentation**                | Aggregating potential customers into groups (market segments) based on shared demographic and psychographic characteristics                               | 2.1.B.1    
| 6   | **Market segment**                     | A group of potential customers who share demographic and psychographic characteristics                                                                    | 2.1.B.1    
| 7   | **Target customers**                   | The buyers most likely to purchase a specific product because of their wants, needs, and preferences                                                      | 2.1.B.2    
| 8   | **Customer profile**                   | A fictional description of a specific sample customer, incorporating demographic and psychographic data as well as wants, needs, and preferences          | 2.1.B.3    
| 9   | **Personalized service**               | A relationship tactic that treats a customer as a known individual                                                                                        | 2.1.C.1    
| 10  | **Customer acquisition cost (CAC)**    | Total marketing, advertising, and sales costs used to acquire customers ÷ number of customers acquired                                                    | 2.1.C.2    
| 11  | **Lifetime value of a customer (LTV)** | The estimated amount of money a customer will spend on the business's products over time                                                                  | 2.1.C.3    
| 12  | **Data breach**                        | An incident that exposes stored customer information to outsiders                                                                                         | 2.1.D.2

## Trap Pairs (pairs that look similar but mean different things)

| Pair | Why they get confused | How to tell them apart |
|---|---|---|
| **Demographic** vs. **Psychographic** | Both describe customers | Demographic = measurable/census traits (who the person **is**) · Psychographic = cognitive/behavioral (what the person **cares about**) |
| **Market segment** vs. **Customer profile** | Both describe target customers | Segment = the **group** sharing traits · Profile = one **fictional person** who stands for the group |
| **CAC** vs. **LTV** | Both are per-customer numbers | CAC = what you **spend to get** a customer · LTV = what that customer **spends on you** over time |
| **Advertising costs** vs. **Acquisition costs** | Both involve spending to get customers | Advertising is **one component**; acquisition costs = marketing **+** advertising **+** sales (all of them) |
| **Brand loyalty** vs. **Repeat purchase** | Both describe coming back | Loyalty = **emotional attachment** to the brand · Repeat = buying again (may be convenience, not loyalty) |
| **Privacy violation** vs. **Data breach** | Both involve customer data problems | Privacy = collecting/storing **without awareness or disclosure** · Breach = data **exposed to outsiders** |

## Key Relationships

```
Customer data
  ├── demographic (who they are)
  └── psychographic (what they care about)
        │
        ▼
Market segmentation (group by shared traits)
        │
        ▼
Target customer (most likely to buy)
        │
        ▼
Customer profile (one fictional person)
        │
        ▼
Better targeting → more effective + less costly
```

```
Customer relationship tactics
  ├── personalized service
  ├── rewards programs
  └── feedback opportunities
        │
        ├──→ Lower CAC (satisfied customers refer new ones)
        └──→ Higher LTV (brand loyalty + repeat purchases)
```

```
Data collection
  ├── Benefits: targeting, LTV, CAC, product improvement
  └── Risks: privacy violation, breach, identity theft, fraud
        │
        ▼
Balance benefits vs. risks → lost customers, violated values, reputation harm
```

## Must-Know Formulas

| Formula | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **CAC** = (marketing + advertising + sales costs) ÷ customers acquired | What it costs to win one customer | $20 in flyers/fees ÷ 4 buyers = $5 per customer |
| **LTV** = estimated total spending over time | What a customer is worth to you | $38 margin + $24 tune-ups + $26 sibling chance = $88 |
| **LTV − CAC** | Net value per customer | $88 − $5 = $83 net |

> **Rule:** Compute CAC from **ALL** costs, then judge it against LTV — never on its own. A higher CAC can still be the better buy when LTV grows faster.

## Sorting Test

If a **census form** could record the trait → **demographic**
If it describes **what a person cares about** or **how they spend their time** → **psychographic**

# 5.1 Taxes, Net Income, and Budgeting

## Core Terms

| # | Term | Definition | Connects To 
|---|---|---|---
| 1 | Income tax | A percentage of income paid to federal, state, and/or local governments; for employees, a portion is withheld from each paycheck by the employer | 5.1.A.1, 5.1.A.2 
| 2 | Capital gains tax | A tax paid on the profit earned from selling an asset (e.g., stocks, property) at a higher price than the purchase price; typically taxed at a lower rate than income tax | 5.1.A.1, 5.1.A.3, 5.3.B.5 
| 3 | Payroll tax | Taxes withheld from employee paychecks that fund specific government insurance programs, including Social Security, Medicare, Medicaid, and unemployment benefits | 5.1.A.1, 5.1.A.4 
| 4 | Property tax | A tax based on the value of an individual's property (houses, land, cars), paid to local or state government | 5.1.A.1, 5.1.A.5 
| 5 | Sales tax | A tax based on the sales price of an item, collected by the business at the point of sale and submitted to the government | 5.1.A.1, 5.1.A.6 
| 6 | Progressive tax | A tax system in which individuals earning higher incomes pay higher tax rates; the U.S. federal income tax is progressive | 5.1.B.2 
| 7 | Tax deduction | An expense that reduces an individual's taxable income, thereby reducing the taxes owed (e.g., mortgage interest, retirement contributions, charitable donations) | 5.1.B.1, 5.1.B.3 
| 8 | Tax credit | A direct reduction in the amount of taxes owed (not taxable income); more valuable than a deduction of the same amount | 5.1.B.1, 5.1.B.4 
| 9 | Gross income | The total amount of income earned during a pay period before any deductions | 5.1.C.1, 5.1.C.2 
| 10 | Net income (net pay) | The amount of money an individual receives after all deductions (taxes, benefits, retirement) are subtracted from gross income | 5.1.C.1, 5.1.C.6 
| 11 | Pay stub | A document provided by an employer showing gross income, mandatory and voluntary deductions, and net income for a pay period | 5.1.C.1 
| 12 | Mandatory deduction | Amounts required by law to be withheld from a paycheck, including federal/state income taxes and payroll taxes | 5.1.C.3 
| 13 | Voluntary deduction | Amounts an employee chooses to have withheld for benefits such as health insurance, retirement savings, and life insurance | 5.1.C.4 

## Additional Terms

| # | Term | Definition | Connects To |
|---|---|---|---|
| 14 | Taxable income | Gross income minus tax deductions; the amount of income on which taxes are actually calculated | 5.1.B.1, 5.1.B.3 |
| 15 | Tax refund | Money returned to a taxpayer when the amount withheld from paychecks exceeds the actual tax owed for the year | 5.1.A.2 |
| 16 | Withholding | The practice of an employer deducting estimated taxes from each paycheck and sending them directly to the government | 5.1.A.2 |
| 17 | Pretax deduction | A deduction taken from gross income before taxes are calculated, reducing taxable income (e.g., 401(k) contributions, HSA contributions) | 5.1.C.5 |
| 18 | Social Security | A federal program providing retirement, disability, and survivor benefits, funded by payroll taxes shared between employer and employee | 5.1.A.4 |
| 19 | Medicare | A federal health insurance program for people 65+ and certain younger individuals with disabilities, funded by payroll taxes | 5.1.A.4 |
| 20 | Self-employment tax | The full payroll tax (both employer and employee portions) that self-employed individuals and contractors must pay themselves | 5.1.A.4 |
| 21 | Filing status | A classification (single, married filing jointly, head of household, etc.) that determines tax brackets and standard deduction amounts | 5.1.B.1 |
| 22 | Budget | A plan for how an individual or household will allocate income toward expenses, savings, and debt repayment over a specific period | 5.1.C.6, 3.1 |

## Trap Pairs

| Pair | Term A | Term B | Key Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Tax deduction** | **Tax credit** | Deduction reduces TAXABLE INCOME (you save your tax rate × the deduction). Credit reduces TAXES OWED directly (a $1,000 credit saves you exactly $1,000). Credits are more valuable. |
| 2 | **Gross income** | **Net income** | Gross = total earnings BEFORE deductions. Net = what you actually TAKE HOME after all deductions. Net can be significantly lower than gross. |
| 3 | **Mandatory deduction** | **Voluntary deduction** | Mandatory = required by law (income tax, payroll tax). Voluntary = chosen by employee (health insurance, 401(k), union dues). You can change voluntary deductions; you cannot opt out of mandatory ones. |
| 4 | **Income tax** | **Payroll tax** | Income tax = general tax on earnings funding government operations. Payroll tax = specific tax funding Social Security and Medicare. Both are withheld, but they fund different things. |
| 5 | **Progressive tax** | **Flat tax** | Progressive = higher income → higher tax rate (U.S. federal system). Flat = same rate for all income levels. The U.S. uses progressive; some states use flat tax. |
| 6 | **Pretax deduction** | **Post-tax deduction** | Pretax = deducted BEFORE taxes calculated → reduces taxable income (e.g., 401(k)). Post-tax = deducted AFTER taxes calculated → does not reduce taxable income (e.g., Roth 401(k)). |

# 1.5 Vision — Vocabulary

> Key terms for Topic 1.5. Companion to [[1.5 Vision - Summary]] · [[1.5 Vision - Slides]].
> Definitions aligned to the CED. Running example: the **snack box** choosing between two offers.

## Core terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Core values** | The **defining beliefs and principles** that guide an individual's or business's actions. Examples: creativity, excellence, transparency, empathy, reliability. |
| **Core competencies** | The **capabilities, skills, and expertise** that help an individual or business **outperform rivals and achieve competitive advantage.** Examples: innovation, customer service, communication, ethical behavior, efficiency. |
| **Vision statement** | A concise description of the **future** the business is trying to create — its **aspirations** and **core values.** Describes a future worth wanting, **not** the work. |
| **Mission statement** | Describes **what the business does** and **how** it will achieve its long-term goals. Describes the **work** — the operation, not the aspiration. |
| **Business** | An organization that seeks to **achieve and increase profits**, fulfill its mission/goals, and remain competitive and viable. Owners keep or reinvest profit. |
| **Social enterprise** | An organization that seeks **profit while also, by design, achieving social objectives.** Impact travels through products, operations, or financial model. Owners keep profit. |
| **Nonprofit organization** | An organization that serves the **public good** rather than generating profit for owners. Any surplus **must be reinvested**; no payment to owners. |
| **Grant** | Funding awarded for a stated purpose, usually by a government or foundation; earned **by application, not by sale.** |
| **Donation** | Money or resources given to an organization **with no product attached** in return. |
| **Competitive advantage** | The ability to **outperform rivals** in the same market, leading to increased market share and potentially profits. Core competencies **feed** competitive advantage. |

## Easily confused (trap pairs)

| Pair | The difference |
|---|---|
| **Core value vs. Core competency** | What the business **believes** vs. what it is **good at.** Scenario signal: "believes" → value; "good at" → competency. |
| **Vision statement vs. Mission statement** | **Future/aspiration** vs. **what it does and how.** Test: a future worth wanting = vision; a description of the work = mission. Classify by **content**, not by the label. |
| **Social enterprise vs. Nonprofit** | Both may serve social goals, but a social enterprise's owners **keep profit**; a nonprofit's surplus **must be reinvested** with no payment to owners. |
| **Social enterprise vs. Business** | Both seek profit, but a social enterprise has a **built-in social objective** through products, operations, or financial model; a traditional business does not. |
| **Grant vs. Donation** | A grant is awarded **by application** for a stated purpose; a donation arrives **with no product attached** and no application required. |
| **Value vs. Decoration** | A real value **costs the business** at a real decision; a decoration is abandoned the moment money appears. |

## Key relationships

- **Core competencies feed competitive advantage** (1.2) — trace an advantage backward and you find a capability somebody developed
- **Values guide decisions** — test any stated value by asking: what did it cost the business at the last real decision?
- **Pursue opportunities your strengths can win** — spend resources where competencies compound
- **Profit improves only two ways:** increase revenues or decrease costs
- **Organization decision tree:** owners keep profit? → No = nonprofit; Yes → social objective = social enterprise; else = business
- **Nonprofit edge case:** can sell goods, can run a surplus — but **no surplus to owners**, ever

## Must-know formulas

- **Profit = Revenue − Total Cost** (carried forward from 1.1)
- **Total Revenue = Sales revenue + Grants + Donations** (for nonprofits — never judge a surplus from sales revenue alone)

> Trap to remember: a scenario showing **sales revenue < total costs** but **total revenue > total costs** with **no owner payment** = **nonprofit running a healthy surplus**, not a failing business.

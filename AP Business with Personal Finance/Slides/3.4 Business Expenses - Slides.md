# Part 1: Before you sell anything
### LO 3.4.A — Startup costs and initial expenses

---

## Startup costs are two different things

**3.4.A.1** **Startup costs** are the one-time expenditures associated with launching a new business or product **as well as initial expenses incurred while establishing the business**.

> **Read that definition carefully — it contains *both* categories.**
>
> | Category | Behaviour after launch |
> |---|---|
> | **One-time expenditures** | Paid once, never again |
> | **Initial expenses** | Paid at startup, then **become ongoing** |

**This is the trap the CED is built around: not everything that is a "startup cost" is a one-time cost.**

---

## One-time expenditures (3.4.A.2)

**One-time expenditures include:**

| Item | DK Coffee Lab example |
|---|---|
| **Legal fees** | $1,500 to an attorney for the operating agreement |
| **Incorporation and licensing fees** | $200 state filing + $300 health permit |
| **Equipment purchases** (in some cases) | $9,000 espresso machine and grinder |

> **"In some cases" matters.** Some equipment is paid once; some is leased or financed, which turns it into a recurring payment. Read the scenario.

---

## Initial expenses (3.4.A.3)

**A business's initial expenses include:**

| Initial expense | DK Coffee Lab example | Becomes ongoing as… |
|---|---|---|
| **Occupancy expense** | First month's rent + deposit | Monthly rent |
| **Research and development** | Recipe testing, menu development | Ongoing product development |
| **Marketing** | Opening-week flyers and social ads | Ongoing advertising |
| **Insurance** | Liability policy before opening day | Monthly premiums |
| **Production or acquisition of initial inventories** | First order of beans, milk, cups | Ongoing inventory purchases |

> **All of these expenses become ongoing once the business begins operation** — that is the defining sentence of 3.4.A.3.

---

## The startup cost sheet, side by side

| | **One-time expenditure** | **Initial expense** |
|---|---|---|
| **Paid** | Once, at launch | At launch — **and every period after** |
| **Examples** | Legal fees, incorporation and licensing fees, some equipment | Occupancy, R&D, marketing, insurance, initial inventory |
| **Where it shows up later** | Nowhere (sunk cost) | As **operating expenses** or **COGS** on the income statement (3.6) |

> **Why this distinction is exam-critical: only the *ongoing* ones appear on next year's income statement. One-time expenditures are sunk — they cannot be recovered, so they should not drive future decisions.**

---

## Your turn — build DK Coffee Lab's startup sheet

> Costs given: attorney $1,500 · state filing $200 · health permit $300 · espresso machine $9,000 · first month rent + deposit $3,600 · opening marketing $800 · liability insurance $450 · initial inventory $2,400

**Classify each as a one-time expenditure or an initial expense**, then total both. Which total is "money you will never see again," and which one predicts next month's costs?

## Your turn — answer

| Item | Amount | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Attorney | $1,500 | One-time expenditure |
| State filing | $200 | One-time expenditure |

# Part 2: The cost of running it
### LO 3.4.B — Direct/indirect and fixed/variable

---

## Two classifications, two questions

**3.4.B.1 / 3.4.B.2** Recurring costs can be sorted on **two independent axes**. Getting this wrong is the most common 3.4 error.

| Axis | Question it answers | Categories |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct vs. indirect** | Is this cost tied to producing a **specific product**? | **Direct costs** / **indirect costs** (= operating expenses) |
| **Fixed vs. variable** | Does this cost change when **volume** changes? | **Fixed expenses** / **variable expenses** |

> **They are not the same axis, and a cost is described by *both*.** Factory rent is **indirect-fixed**? No — factory rent is **direct-fixed**. Always name both labels.

---

## Direct and indirect costs (3.4.B.1)

| | **Direct costs** | **Indirect costs** (operating expenses) |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | Tied to the production or delivery of **specific goods or services** | Costs associated with **running the business** |
| **Coffee shop examples** | Beans, milk, cups, barista wages | Rent, utilities, insurance, marketing, owner's salary |
| **The test** | Remove the product → the cost disappears | Remove the product → the cost **stays** |

> **The "remove the product" test is the fastest way to classify in an exam scenario.**

---

## Fixed and variable costs (3.4.B.2)

| | **Fixed expenses** | **Variable expenses** |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | Do **not** change with production or service levels | **Increase as production/service levels increase** |
| **Coffee shop examples** | Rent, insurance, salaried manager | Beans, milk, cups, hourly barista hours |
| **Per-unit behaviour** | Falls per unit as volume rises | Roughly constant per unit |

> **Fixed costs are a bet on volume.** The more you sell, the thinner each fixed cost is spread — which is exactly why 3.6's margins move with sales volume.

---

## COGS — the direct costs of goods (3.4.B.3)

**3.4.B.3** Businesses that produce goods incur direct costs called the **cost of goods sold (COGS)**. It typically includes:

| Component | Example |
|---|---|
| **Raw materials** | Coffee beans, milk |
| **Production supplies** | Filters, cleaning supplies used in production |
| **Production-related wages and benefits** | Baristas making the drinks |
| **Cost to operate manufacturing facilities** | The roastery's rent and power |

> **Critical sentence: COGS components can be either fixed or variable.** Rent for a factory is **fixed**; raw materials are **variable**. So "COGS = variable costs" is false — and that is a standard trap.

---

## Cost of sales — the service version (3.4.B.4)

**3.4.B.4** For businesses that **provide services**, the direct costs are called **cost of sales** and include:

- **direct labor expenses**
- **travel costs**
- **any materials associated with delivering the service**

| Business type | Direct cost label |
|---|---|
| **Produces goods** | **COGS** (cost of goods sold) |
| **Provides services** | **Cost of sales** |

> **Same idea, different name: COGS for goods, cost of sales for services. A question that says "a consulting firm's direct costs" wants *cost of sales*.**

---

## Operating expenses (3.4.B.5)

**3.4.B.5** Businesses face recurring **indirect costs**, called **operating expenses**. These are **typically fixed** and include:

| Operating expense | Note |
|---|---|
| **Occupancy expenses** | Rent, lease |
| **Salaries and benefits for office and sales staff** | Not production wages — those are direct |
| **Marketing and advertising** | Ongoing campaigns |
| **Supplies** | Office supplies, not production supplies |
| **Utilities** | Power, water, internet |
| **Maintenance** | Repairs, servicing |
| **Insurance** | Liability, property |

> **Watch the boundary: production wages → direct (COGS). Office and sales salaries → indirect (operating expenses).**

---

## Insurance is a cost decision, not a formality (3.4.B.6)

**3.4.B.6** Businesses (and consumers) buy **insurance** to protect against financial losses from **accidents, injuries, or property damage**.

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Some insurance is required** | For example, **workers compensation insurance** |
| **Many are optional** | Property, business interruption, cyber |
| **Behaviour varies** | Businesses and consumers differ in their **willingness to accept risk and/or pay for insurance** — that is **risk tolerance** |

> **Insurance converts an uncertain large loss into a certain small cost.** Low risk tolerance → buy more insurance → higher fixed costs. That trade-off is the EK.

---

## Two axes, one matrix

|  | **Fixed** | **Variable** |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct** | Factory rent for the roastery; salaried production manager | Beans, milk, cups, hourly production wages |
| **Indirect** | Occupancy rent, insurance, office salaries, maintenance | Utilities that scale with hours; sales commissions |

> **In an FRQ, always give both labels if the question allows: "direct variable" is a stronger answer than "variable."**

---

## 🔑 Exam points to remember — Business expenses

| # | Point |
|---|-------|
| 1 | **Startup costs** include **both** one-time expenditures **and** initial expenses |
| 2 | One-time expenditures: legal fees, incorporation and licensing fees, some equipment |
| 3 | Initial expenses (occupancy, R&D, marketing, insurance, initial inventory) **become ongoing** once operations begin |
| 4 | **Direct** = tied to a specific product; **indirect** = running the business (= **operating expenses**) |
| 5 | **Fixed** = unchanged by volume; **variable** = rises with volume |
| 6 | Direct/indirect and fixed/variable are **two different axes** — a cost has both labels |
| 7 | **COGS** = direct costs for **goods**: raw materials, production supplies, production wages, facility operation |
| 8 | **COGS can contain fixed *and* variable components** — factory rent (fixed) vs. raw materials (variable) |
| 9 | **Cost of sales** = direct costs for **services**: direct labor, travel, service materials |
| 10 | **Operating expenses** are indirect and typically fixed: occupancy, office/sales salaries, marketing, supplies, utilities, maintenance, insurance |
| 11 | **Insurance** protects against accidents, injuries, property damage; some is required (workers comp), much is optional |
| 12 | **Risk tolerance** explains why similar businesses buy different amounts of insurance |

---

## Three exam tells — business expenses

**Tell 1 — "Does it disappear with the product?"** If the cost vanishes when you stop making the product, it is **direct** (COGS or cost of sales). If it stays, it is **indirect** (operating expense). This resolves most classification questions in one step.

**Tell 2 — "Does it change when volume changes?"** Fixed vs. variable. Note the CED's favourite wrinkle: **COGS is not automatically variable** — factory rent inside COGS is fixed.

**Tell 3 — "Paid once, or paid again?"** One-time expenditures are paid once and never appear again. Initial expenses are paid at launch **and** recur. Any option that says "startup costs are all one-time" is wrong.

## Practice — Stimulus

**Sunrise Smoothies** is preparing to open. Planned costs:

- $1,200 in legal and licensing fees
- $6,500 for blenders and refrigeration
- $2,800 first month's rent
- $900 opening marketing
- $600 insurance premium
- $1,400 initial fruit and cup inventory
- monthly: $2,800 rent, $1,100 for two part-time staff on hourly wages, $700 manager salary, $260 utilities, $600 fruit, $80 maintenance

---

## Practice — Q1

> Which group contains **only** one-time expenditures?

> A. Legal and licensing fees, blenders, refrigeration
> B. Initial fruit inventory, first month's rent, insurance
> C. Rent, utilities, maintenance
> D. Opening marketing, legal fees, manager salary

---

## Practice — Q1 answer

**Answer: A.**

**3.4.A.2** lists legal fees, incorporation and licensing fees, and **in some cases equipment purchases**. All three items in A are paid once.

**Traps:**
- **B** — inventory, rent, and insurance all **recur**; these are initial expenses (3.4.A.3)
- **C** — these are operating expenses, not startup costs at all
- **D** — the manager's salary is a recurring operating expense

> **"One-time" is a claim about the *second* payment, not the first.**

---

## Practice — Q2

> The $1,100 paid to two part-time staff who blend the smoothies is best classified as:

> A. Indirect fixed cost
> B. Direct variable cost
> C. Indirect variable cost
> D. One-time expenditure

---

## Practice — Q2 answer

**Answer: B.**

Two labels. **Direct** — wages for staff who produce the product (3.4.B.3, production-related wages). **Variable** — hourly wages rise with the number of smoothies sold (3.4.B.2).

**Traps:**
- **A** — production wages are not indirect; office and sales salaries are
- **C** — indirect would apply to staff who do not make the product
- **D** — wages are paid every period, so they are not one-time

> **Name both axes: direct + variable.**

---

## Practice — Q3

> The operator says: "COGS is our variable cost, and rent is our fixed cost." What is the error in this statement?

> A. Rent is a direct cost, not a fixed cost
> B. COGS can contain fixed costs too — for example rent on a production facility
> C. COGS is always an operating expense
> D. There is no error

---

## Practice — Q3 answer

**Answer: B.**

**3.4.B.3** states explicitly: **"The components of COGS can be either fixed or variable; rent for a factory is fixed, while raw materials costs are variable."** Treating COGS as purely variable is the trap.

**Traps:**
- **A** — rent is fixed; if it is production space, it is also direct
- **C** — COGS is a direct cost, distinct from operating expenses (3.4.B.1, 3.4.B.5)
- **D** — the statement is wrong precisely on the fixed/variable question

> **Two axes, again. COGS answers "direct?"; it does not answer "variable?"**

---

## Practice — Q4

> Sunrise is quoted a required workers compensation policy and an optional business-interruption policy. Which explanation best fits 3.4.B.6?

> A. Both are legally required, so both must be purchased
> B. Workers compensation is required; the optional policy depends on the owner's risk tolerance and willingness to pay
> C. Optional insurance is always a waste of money for a small business
> D. Insurance is a one-time startup cost

---

## Practice — Q4 answer

**Answer: B.**

**3.4.B.6**: some types of insurance are **required** — workers compensation is the CED's example — while **many are optional**. Businesses vary in their **willingness to accept risk and/or pay for insurance**, which is risk tolerance.

**Traps:**
- **A** — only some insurance is required
- **C** — optional insurance converts a large uncertain loss into a small certain cost; whether that is worth it depends on risk tolerance
- **D** — premiums recur; the opening premium was an initial expense that becomes an operating expense

> **Required vs. optional, then risk tolerance — in that order.**

---

## Trap patterns to watch

| # | Trap type | Where it appears |
|---|-----------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Startup = one-time only** — initial expenses are startup costs that recur | Q1 |
| 2 | **Initial expense treated as one-time** — rent, insurance, inventory come back | Q1 |
| 3 | **Production wages = indirect** — they are direct (COGS/cost of sales) | Q2 |
| 4 | **Only one label** — a cost is direct/indirect *and* fixed/variable | Q2 |
| 5 | **COGS = variable** — COGS can contain fixed costs | Q3 |
| 6 | **COGS for services** — services use **cost of sales** | throughout |
| 7 | **All insurance is required** — only some is | Q4 |
| 8 | **Insurance as a one-time cost** — premiums recur | Q4 |

## Wrap-up — 3.4 Business Expenses

**What we covered:**
- **Startup costs** = **one-time expenditures** (legal, incorporation/licensing, some equipment) **+ initial expenses** (occupancy, R&D, marketing, insurance, initial inventory)
- Initial expenses **become ongoing** once the business operates
- Recurring costs sort on **two independent axes**: **direct/indirect** and **fixed/variable**
- **Direct costs** are tied to a specific product; **indirect costs** are operating expenses
- **COGS** = direct costs for goods, and it can contain **fixed and variable** components
- **Cost of sales** = direct costs for services
- **Operating expenses** are indirect and typically fixed
- **Insurance** protects against accidents, injuries, and property damage; some is required, much is optional, and **risk tolerance** explains the difference

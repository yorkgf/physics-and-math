# Part 1: Why anyone records money
### LO 3.3.A — Tracking and evaluating financial data

---

## Every business activity is a financial transaction

**3.3.A.1** Businesses engage in a variety of financial transactions. Every one of them moves the same three quantities:

| Transaction | Example at DK Coffee Lab |
|---|---|
| **Buying resources** | Purchasing beans, milk, cups |
| **Receiving payment from customers** | A $5 latte paid by card |
| **Distributing profits to owners** | Paying a dividend, or the owner drawing money |
| **Saving and borrowing funds** | A loan to buy the espresso machine |

> **These transactions affect the business's assets, liabilities, and owners' equity** — where **owners' equity is the value of the business to its owners.**

**The three quantities are the spine of this entire unit.** 3.6 measures them, 3.7 snapshots them, 3.8 tracks cash moving between them.

---

## Consumers run the same three quantities

**3.3.A.2** Consumers engage in financial transactions too: **receiving income, buying goods and services, saving, and borrowing.**

| | Business | Consumer |
|---|---|---|
| What you own | **Assets** | **Assets** |
| What you owe | **Liabilities** | **Liabilities** |
| What is left | **Owners' equity** | **Net worth** |

> **Same equation, different name for the remainder.** Owners' equity (business) = net worth (consumer) = assets − liabilities. The exam swaps these labels to test whether you know they are the same thing.

---

## Recording → reporting → deciding

**3.3.A.3** Businesses **record all financial transactions** and use this data to prepare reports and **financial statements** that summarize financial performance.

Financial statements are used to:

| Use | Who it serves |
|---|---|
| **Monitor financial health** | Managers, owners |
| **Guide decision making** | Managers |
| **Provide accurate information to shareholders, investors, and lenders** | External capital providers |
| **Ensure compliance with laws and reporting regulations** | Government, regulators |

> **Four uses — memorise the list. Questions often ask "who uses financial statements and why," and the answer is always one of these four.**

---

## GAAP: the rules for public companies

**3.3.A.4** **Generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP)** require corporations that **sell ownership shares to the public** to:

- **consistently disclose all positive and negative financial information**
- for **each reporting period** — typically **quarterly** (every three months) or **annually**

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Who must follow** | Corporations selling ownership shares to the public |
| **What must be disclosed** | **All** information — positive **and** negative |
| **How often** | Quarterly or annually |

> **Two exam-favourite details: "positive *and* negative" (no selective good news), and "typically quarterly or annually."**

---

## Consumers are not required — but should anyway

**3.3.A.5** Consumers are **not usually required** to record or report financial transactions.

**However**, having an organized system — such as a **budget** — for tracking financial transactions helps consumers:

- **monitor their finances**
- **make decisions aligned to their financial goals**

> **This is the bridge back to 3.1/3.2.** A budget is the consumer version of a financial statement: voluntary, but it is what turns "I think I'm fine" into "I know I'm fine."

# Part 2: Accounting vs. finance
### LO 3.3.B — Who does what, for whom

---

## The accounting department: record everything

**3.3.B.1** **Accounting departments**:

1. **identify and record all financial transactions** during a time period
2. **prepare financial statements**

> **Accounting is backward-looking.** It answers: *what happened, in numbers, this period?*

---

## Two kinds of accountant — split by audience

**3.3.B.2 / 3.3.B.3** The same records serve two different audiences:

| | **Managerial accountants** | **Financial accountants** |
|---|---|---|
| **Audience** | **Internal** stakeholders — managers | **External** stakeholders — shareholders, investors, lenders |
| **Purpose** | Business planning and decision making | Reporting financial position and performance |
| **Output** | Analysis and information for internal use | Formal financial statements |

> **One word decides the answer: internal or external.** If the scenario says "the board wants a cost analysis to decide next quarter's pricing," that is managerial. If it says "investors receive the annual report," that is financial.

---

## The finance department: what to do next

**3.3.B.4** **Finance departments** are responsible for:

- **analyzing the financial data compiled by accounting departments**
- **recommending strategies** for maintaining or improving the business's financial performance

| Department | Question it answers | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| **Accounting** | What happened, and how do we report it accurately? | Backward-looking |
| **Finance** | Given that, what should we do? | Forward-looking |

> **Accounting records and reports; finance analyses and recommends.** Schools blur this constantly — the exam does not.

---

## Consumers hire the same expertise (3.3.B.5)

**3.3.B.5** Consumers may hire **financial advisors and/or accountants** to help with:

- **financial planning and decision making**
- **tax preparation**

> **Note the parallel with 3.3.B.2–B.4:** an individual can outsource exactly the two functions a company has departments for — *the analysis* (advisor) and *the record-keeping/tax* (accountant).

---

## One table that settles the whole LO

| Term | Audience | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Accounting department** | Internal process | Records all transactions; prepares statements |
| **Managerial accountant** | **Internal** | Provides analysis to managers for planning and decisions |
| **Financial accountant** | **External** | Provides information to shareholders, investors, lenders |
| **Finance department** | Internal process | Analyzes the data; recommends strategy |
| **GAAP** | External reporting rules | Requires public corporations to disclose all info each period |
| **Budget** | Consumer (and business) tool | Voluntary tracking that aligns spending with goals |

> **If you can fill this table from memory, LO 3.3.B is complete — it is only five EKs deep.**

## Your turn — DK Coffee Lab's month

> DK Coffee Lab had these events in March:
> 1. Paid $1,800 for green coffee beans
> 2. Took in $9,400 from customers
> 3. Borrowed $12,000 to buy a second espresso machine
> 4. Paid $500 in dividends to the owner
> 5. The manager asks for a per-drink cost breakdown to set next month's prices

**Which events are (a) transactions affecting assets/liabilities/equity, (b) managerial or financial accounting, (c) finance department work?**

---

## Your turn — answer

**(a) All four are financial transactions** (3.3.A.1). Events 1–4 each change assets, liabilities, or owners' equity: cash out, cash in, liability up, equity down.

**(b) Event 5 is managerial accounting** (3.3.B.2) — internal stakeholders, planning and decision making. Preparing the March statements for the bank that issued the loan would instead be **financial accounting** (3.3.B.3), because lenders are external.

**(c) Recommending whether DK should raise prices or cut the bean supplier** is **finance** (3.3.B.4) — analysis of accounting data turned into a strategy.

> **Sort by audience first, then by direction (backward vs. forward).**

---

## 🔑 Exam points to remember — Accounting and financial management

| # | Point |
|---|-------|
| 1 | Business transactions — buying resources, receiving payment, distributing profits, saving/borrowing — affect **assets, liabilities, and owners' equity** |
| 2 | **Owners' equity (business) = net worth (consumer)** = assets − liabilities |
| 3 | Financial statements have **four uses**: monitor health, guide decisions, inform shareholders/investors/lenders, ensure legal compliance |
| 4 | **GAAP** applies to corporations **selling ownership shares to the public** |
| 5 | GAAP requires disclosure of **all** information — **positive and negative** — **quarterly or annually** |
| 6 | Consumers are **not required** to record transactions, but a **budget** helps monitor finances and align decisions with goals |
| 7 | **Accounting department** records transactions and prepares statements |
| 8 | **Managerial accountants → internal** stakeholders (planning, decisions) |
| 9 | **Financial accountants → external** stakeholders (shareholders, investors, lenders) |
| 10 | **Finance department** analyzes accounting data and **recommends strategy** |
| 11 | Consumers may hire **financial advisors and accountants** for planning and tax preparation |

---

## Three exam tells — accounting and finance

**Tell 1 — "Internal or external?"** This single question resolves every managerial-vs-financial distinction. Internal (managers, planning) → managerial. External (shareholders, investors, lenders) → financial.

**Tell 2 — "Record, or recommend?"** Recording and reporting = accounting. Analyzing and recommending strategy = finance. An option that says "the accounting department recommends raising prices" is wrong on the verb alone.

**Tell 3 — "Required, or voluntary?"** For businesses selling shares to the public, GAAP reporting is **required**. For consumers, tracking is **voluntary** — but a budget is still the tool that aligns decisions with goals.

## Practice — Stimulus

**Northline Bikes** is a corporation whose shares are sold to the public. In the last quarter it:

- sold 1,200 bikes
- paid $310,000 to suppliers
- **lost** $40,000 after a recall
- borrowed $200,000 from a bank

The CFO has asked the accounting team for the quarterly statements, and a separate team is preparing a recommendation on whether to discontinue the recalled model.

---

## Practice — Q1

> Under GAAP, which statement about Northline's reporting is correct?

> A. It may omit the recall loss because it is a one-time event
> B. It must disclose the recall loss along with positive results for the period
> C. It reports only annually, because recalls are unusual
> D. GAAP applies only to businesses with more than 500 employees

---

## Practice — Q1 answer

**Answer: B.**

**3.3.A.4**: GAAP requires public corporations to **consistently disclose all positive and negative financial information** for each reporting period. A recall loss is negative information — it must be disclosed, not buried.

**Traps:**
- **A** — "all" explicitly includes negative information; omitting it is the classic violation
- **C** — reporting is **typically quarterly or annually**; the event's unusualness does not change the schedule
- **D** — the trigger is **selling ownership shares to the public**, not headcount

> **GAAP is about consistency and completeness, not about good news.**

---

## Practice — Q2

> The team preparing a recommendation on discontinuing the recalled model is performing work that belongs to:

> A. The accounting department, because it involves financial data
> B. The finance department, because it analyzes data and recommends strategy
> C. Financial accountants, because the decision affects external stakeholders
> D. An independent auditor, because the recall involves compliance

---

## Practice — Q2 answer

**Answer: B.**

**3.3.B.4**: finance departments analyze the data compiled by accounting and **recommend strategies** for maintaining or improving financial performance. Recommending whether to discontinue a product is exactly that.

**Traps:**
- **A** — accounting records and reports; it does not recommend strategy
- **C** — financial accountants serve external stakeholders with reported information, not internal strategy
- **D** — auditors review records for accuracy and compliance, not product decisions

> **The verb gives it away: *recommend* = finance.**

---

## Practice — Q3

> Northline's bank requests the quarterly financial statements before extending further credit. Which role does producing those statements belong to?

> A. Managerial accounting
> B. Financial accounting
> C. Finance department
> D. GAAP itself

---

## Practice — Q3 answer

**Answer: B.**

**3.3.B.3**: **financial accountants** provide financial information and analysis primarily to **external stakeholders** — specifically **shareholders, investors, and lenders**. A bank is a lender.

**Traps:**
- **A** — managerial accounting serves internal stakeholders
- **C** — the finance department analyzes and recommends; it does not prepare the statements
- **D** — GAAP is a set of **rules**, not a role or a department

> **Audience decides: lender = external = financial accounting.**

---

## Practice — Q4

> Which statement best explains why DK Coffee Lab, a small business with no public shares, still keeps careful records?

> A. GAAP legally requires it, because all businesses must comply
> B. Recording transactions supports monitoring health, guiding decisions, informing lenders, and complying with applicable laws
> C. Records are only useful when preparing tax returns
> D. Small businesses are exempt from keeping any records

---

## Practice — Q4 answer

**Answer: B.**

**3.3.A.3** lists the four uses of financial statements generally: monitoring health, guiding decisions, informing shareholders/investors/**lenders**, and **compliance with applicable laws and reporting regulations**. GAAP's *specific* requirement targets public corporations, but the four uses apply to any business that wants to survive.

**Traps:**
- **A** — GAAP's requirement applies to corporations that sell ownership shares to the public
- **C** — tax preparation is one use, not the only one
- **D** — no exemption exists; small businesses still face tax and lending requirements

> **Separate "what GAAP *requires*" from "why a business *should* keep records."**

## Trap patterns to watch

| # | Trap type | Where it appears |
|---|-----------|-----------------|
| 1 | **GAAP = all businesses** — it applies to public corporations | Q1, Q4 |
| 2 | **Omit bad news** — GAAP requires positive *and* negative disclosure | Q1 |
| 3 | **Recommend = accounting** — recommending strategy is finance | Q2 |
| 4 | **External audience = managerial accounting** — external means financial accounting | Q3 |
| 5 | **Owners' equity ≠ net worth** — they are the same formula, different context | throughout |
| 6 | **Financial statements are only for taxes** — four uses, not one | Q4 |
| 7 | **Accounting and finance are synonyms** — record/report vs. analyze/recommend | Q2, Q3 |

## Wrap-up — 3.3 Accounting and Financial Management

**What we covered:**
- Every business transaction moves **assets, liabilities, or owners' equity** — the same three quantities consumers track as **net worth**
- Financial statements serve **four uses**: monitor health, guide decisions, inform capital providers, ensure compliance
- **GAAP** requires public corporations to disclose **all** information, positive and negative, **quarterly or annually**
- Consumers are not required to record transactions — but a **budget** is what aligns spending with goals
- **Accounting** records and reports; **finance** analyzes and recommends
- **Managerial accountants serve internal** stakeholders; **financial accountants serve external** ones
- Consumers can hire **advisors and accountants** for planning and tax preparation

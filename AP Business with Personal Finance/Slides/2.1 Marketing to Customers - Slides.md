## Opening question

**Theo, 16** — rebuilds bikes in the garage: **Trued Cycles.**

One receipt request → a **$2 notebook** → six weeks later, **six lines from the same buyer.**

> **A ledger full of buyers is customer data.** Today: how to read it.

---

## What is marketing? (2.1.A.1)

**Marketing** = all activities to **identify** customers' problems, needs, wants — and **promote, sell, deliver** products that meet them.

| Theo's activity | Step |
|---|---|
| Reads marketplace listings | **Identify** |
| Writes "21-point safety inspection" | **Promote** |
| Adjusts the seat before handoff | **Deliver** |

> **Marketing ≠ advertising.** Advertising is one piece of it.

---

## Customer data feeds three decisions (2.1.A.2)

| Decision | Theo's notebook |
|---|---|
| **Which customers to serve** | Parents of middle schoolers (6 of 6 sales) |
| **Which products to produce** | Safe commuters ($95) |
| **How to market** | Listings: safety inspection + tune-up promise |

> **Data → who / what / how.**

---

## Two kinds of customer data (2.1.A.3)

| | Demographic (2.1.A.3.i) | Psychographic (2.1.A.3.ii) |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | **measurable** population traits | **cognitive & behavioral** factors |
| Examples | age, sex, race, ethnicity, income, location | interests, activities, values, lifestyles |
| Census test | *can a census form record it?* | *what they care about / how they spend time* |

> **Traits of the person = demographic · Contents of the mind = psychographic.**

---

## Where data comes from (2.1.A.4)

| Source | Tools |
|---|---|
| **Digital** | subscriber lists · online accounts · click-tracking · tracking apps · social media monitoring |
| **Traditional** | surveys · interviews |
| **Purchased** | bought from other businesses |

> **The question asked — not the tool — decides the data type.**

# 2.1.B — Segmentation → Target → Profile

**Aggregate the market. Then aim at one buyer.**

---

## Market segmentation (2.1.B.1)

**Market segmentation** = aggregating potential customers into **segments** by **shared demographic + psychographic** characteristics.

| Theo's segments | Buys | Cares about |
|---|---|---|
| Parents of younger kids | $60 bikes | working brakes |
| **Parents of middle schoolers** | **$95 commuters** | safety, test ride, guarantee |
| Adult hobbyists | $150 road bikes | parts list, negotiate |

> **Each segment's needs → how the product might meet them.**

---

## Target customers (2.1.B.2)

**Target customers** = buyers **most likely to purchase** — their **wants, needs, preferences** already point at the product.

| Broad | Targeted |
|---|---|
| "Used bikes, all sizes, good prices" | "Inspected commuter bikes for middle schoolers — 21-point check, test rides, 30-day tune-up" |

> **Targeting improves the odds — it never guarantees the sale.**

---

## Customer profile (2.1.B.3)

**Customer profile** = a **fictional** description of **one sample customer** — demographic + psychographic + wants/needs/preferences.

> **Renee, 41.** Safe first commuter bike for her middle schooler. Budget $90. Values the test ride and tune-up more than price.

> **Segment = the group · Profile = one invented person who stands for it.**

---

## Why targeting pays (2.1.B.4)

| One target population | Everyone |
|---|---|
| product / branding / pricing / advertising **fit the buyer** | generic appeal |
| **more effective** | weaker |
| **less costly** | costlier |

> **Aim at one buyer, not the whole market.**

# 2.1.C — Customer Relationships

**Turn one sale into a relationship.**

---

## Building relationships (2.1.C.1)

| Tactic | Theo |
|---|---|
| **Personalized service** | 30-day free tune-up; greets the kid by name |
| **Rewards programs** | $10 off a sibling's bike |
| **Customer feedback** | two questions at every pickup |

> **Social media & the internet make all three cheaper to run.**

---

## Customer acquisition cost (2.1.C.2)

**CAC** = (marketing + advertising + sales costs) ÷ customers acquired

$$\text{CAC} = \frac{\$20}{4} = \$5 \text{ per customer}$$

> **Satisfied customers refer — a referred buyer arrives at zero marketing cost.**

---

## Lifetime value (2.1.C.3)

**LTV** = estimated amount a customer spends **over time** (track in **margin**).

| Renee | Value |
|---|---|
| First bike margin | $38 |
| Two tune-ups ($12 each) | $24 |
| Sibling bike (40% × $65) | $26 |
| **LTV** | **$88** |

> **Loyalty + repeat purchases raise LTV. $88 LTV − $5 CAC = $83 net.**

---

# 2.1.D — Data Risks

**Collection creates risk.**

---

## Privacy (2.1.D.1)

> Collecting, compiling, storing data can violate **privacy** — especially when customers are **unaware** of collection or **uninformed** about its use.

---

## Breach, identity theft, fraud (2.1.D.2)

| Risk | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Data breach** | stored information exposed to outsiders |
| **Identity theft** | stolen details used to impersonate |
| **Fraud** | deception to take money or property |

---

## Balancing benefits vs. risks (2.1.D.3)

| Benefits | Risks to the business |
|---|---|
| better targeting · higher LTV · lower CAC · better products | **loss of customers** · **violation of core values** · **damaged reputation** |

> **The gym:** a "trusted community space" stores card numbers in an unsecured spreadsheet.

---

## Exam points to remember

| # | Point |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Marketing ≠ advertising** — identify → promote → sell → deliver; data feeds **who / what / how** |
| 2 | **Demographic** (census traits) vs. **psychographic** (contents of the mind) |
| 3 | **Segmentation → target customer → profile** — targeting is more effective and less costly |
| 4 | **Relationship tactics** move **LTV up** and **CAC down** |
| 5 | **CAC** = all acquisition costs ÷ customers; **LTV** = spend over time |
| 6 | **Data risks:** privacy · breach · identity theft · fraud → lost customers, values, reputation |

# Practice — 6 questions

**4 on one stimulus (Riverbend Suds)** · **2 standalone**

Read → pause → **commit to an answer** → then reveal.

---

## Stimulus: Riverbend Suds

**Dana Whitfield** owns **Riverbend Suds** — a Saturday farmers market stall + a small online store. Stall shoppers who **join her email list** get a free sample. The store requires an **account** and logs **what each account browses and buys.** A checkout **survey** asks **age range, zip code, and what matters most.**

Two clusters emerge:

| Cluster | Buys | Ranks first |
|---|---|---|
| Shoppers **over 50** | single unscented bars | sensitive-skin ingredients |
| Shoppers **late 20s–30s** | gift sets, follows the shop | plastic-free packaging |

Dana serves the **younger cluster** and drafts: **Marisol, 31**, renter, hosts friends, buys gifts locally.

---

## Q1 — What data does the survey collect?

> A. Only demographic, because answers can be counted
> B. **Both** demographic (age + zip) **and** psychographic (what matters most)
> C. Only psychographic, because surveys record opinions
> D. Neither — a survey is a traditional tool, not a digital one

---

## Q1 — Answer: **B**

**Why:** age + zip = measurable (demographic); "what matters most" = values (psychographic).

**Trap A** — counting doesn't change the type. **Trap D** — the question, not the tool, decides the type.

---

## Q2 — Why serve the younger cluster?

> A. She can appeal to **anyone who might ever buy** soap
> B. Serving one segment **guarantees** the line outsells current products
> C. She can stop collecting data outside that segment
> D. Designing for the buyers **most likely to purchase** is **more effective and less costly** than a wide audience

---

## Q2 — Answer: **D**

**Why:** targeting one group usually converts better and costs less.

**Trap A** — the opposite strategy. **Trap B** — "guarantees": targeting improves odds, promises nothing.

---

## Q3 — What is the Marisol description?

> A. A **customer profile** — a fictional sample customer built from demographic + psychographic data
> B. A **market segment** — it represents many customers
> C. A record of Dana's single best **real** customer
> D. A subscriber-list entry

---

## Q3 — Answer: **A**

**Why:** a profile personifies the target — one invented person.

**Trap B** — one zoom level too far out: segment = group, profile = one member. **Trap C** — misses "fictional."

---

## Q4 — How does a rewards program raise profit?

> A. It cuts the **manufacturing cost** per unit
> B. It **guarantees** repeat purchases
> C. It builds **loyalty and repeat buying**, raising the **lifetime value** of each customer
> D. It removes any need to attract new customers

---

## Q4 — Answer: **C**

**Why:** relationship tactics raise LTV through repeat purchases; referrals also push CAC down.

**Trap B** — "guarantees" again. **Trap A** — borrows from operations; a punch card doesn't touch production cost.

---

## Q5 — Read the table

**Cinder Peak Cocoa** ran two campaigns:

| | Spring | Fall |
|---|---|---|
| Advertising | $6,000 | $6,000 |
| Other marketing & sales | $2,000 | $3,000 |
| New customers | 400 | 300 |
| Average LTV | $60 | $90 |

> Which conclusion is supported?

---

## Q5 — Answer: **Fall produced more value per customer**

$$\text{Spring CAC} = \frac{6000+2000}{400} = \$20 \qquad \text{Fall CAC} = \frac{6000+3000}{300} = \$30$$

| | Spring | Fall |
|---|---|---|
| CAC | $20 | $30 |
| LTV − CAC | $40 | **$60** |

**Trap:** "Spring CAC = $15" divides advertising alone. CAC uses **all** costs.

---

## Q6 — The gym's risk

> A gym advertising itself as a **trusted community space** stores card numbers and check-in histories in an **unsecured spreadsheet.** What risk does this create **for the gym?**

> A. None — a breach harms only customers
> B. A breach could **drive members away, violate stated values, damage reputation**
> C. **Any** collection of customer data is illegal
> D. The risk ends once members accept the terms of service

---

## Q6 — Answer: **B**

**Why:** unsecured data → breach → fraud/identity theft → the harm rebounds on the business.

**Trap A** — sends harm one way only. **Trap C** — collecting data is legal; the issue is **securing and disclosing** it.

---

## Wrap-up

- **Marketing ≠ advertising** — identify → promote → sell → deliver
- **Data → who / what / how**
- **Demographic** (census traits) vs. **psychographic** (contents of the mind)
- **Segmentation → target customer → profile** — targeting is more effective and less costly
- **Relationship tactics** → **LTV up, CAC down**
- **Data risks:** privacy · breach · identity theft · fraud → lost customers, values, reputation

> **Data is the whole game — handle it well, and handle it safely.**

# AP Business with Personal Finance
### Course Introduction

What we'll learn · how it's built · how you're assessed

> First-ever AP exam: **May 2027**

---

## What is this course?

An **introductory, college-level** course in **business + personal finance**.

- **College equivalent:** one semester of intro-to-business
- **Prerequisites:** none — just willingness to work individually and in teams
- A **Career Kickstart** AP course — built with colleges **and** industry employers
- Built straight from the official **Course and Exam Description (CED)**

You'll explore the business disciplines of **entrepreneurship, marketing, finance, accounting, and management** — **plus** personal finance.

---

## What makes it different

- **Career Kickstart:** developed with industry leaders, not just colleges → skills for real careers
- **Work-based learning:** you act like a founder — product development, customer research, marketing, financial documents
- **Two projects, not just tests:** the **Business Canvas Project** and the **Financial Advisor Project**
- **Personal finance is woven throughout** — not a separate afterthought

---

## The course framework = 2 components

| | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Course Skills** | Business + professional/leadership skills you develop all year (5 categories) |
| **Course Content** | 5 units across the key business disciplines + personal finance |

You build the skills **through** the content — by applying them to your own projects and to business cases.

---

## The 5 units at a glance

| Unit | Title | Exam weight | ~Periods |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Businesses, Competition, and New Ideas | 20–30% | 37 |

---

## Unit 1 — Businesses, Competition, and New Ideas
*20–30% · the entrepreneurial foundation*

1.1 What Is a Business · 1.2 Markets & Competitive Advantage · 1.3 PESTEL Factors & the Business Environment · 1.4 How Do Business Ideas Originate? · 1.5 Vision · 1.6 Business Ethics · 1.7 Organization, Roles & Responsibilities · 1.8 Supply Chains

> **Running story:** a student selling candy out of his backpack. By the end, a kid with a backpack is a business with values, partners, rules, and a supply chain.

---

## Unit 2 — Marketing
*20–30% · the customer question*

2.1 Marketing to Customers · 2.2 Consumer Behavior · 2.3 Market Research · 2.4 Product · 2.5 Price · 2.6 Place and Channels · 2.7 Promotion and Marketing

> **Running story:** Theo, 16, building **Trued Cycles** (used-bike refurbishing). Who is the customer? What do they want? What makes them say yes?

---

## Unit 3 — Personal Saving & Borrowing **+** Business Finance & Accounting
*25–35% (two parts)*

**Part 1 — Personal Saving and Borrowing:** 3.1 Saving for Future Purchases · 3.2 Borrowing, Credit, and Debt

**Part 2 — Business Finance and Accounting:** 3.3 Accounting & Financial Management · 3.4 Business Expenses · 3.5 Financial Capital · 3.6 The Income Statement · 3.7 The Balance Sheet and Net Worth · 3.8 The Cash Flow Statement · 3.9 Ethics and Financial Reporting

---

## Unit 4 — Management and Strategy
*15–20% · running and growing the business*

4.1 Management and Leadership · 4.2 Evaluating Performance · 4.3 Strategy and Decision Making · 4.4 Strategic Frameworks: Porter's Five Forces

---

## Unit 5 — Personal Finance
*not on the AP exam (taught before or after)*

5.1 Taxes, Net Income, and Budgeting · 5.2 Managing Personal Risk · 5.3 Saving and Investing for Education, Retirement, and Goals

> Culminates in the **Financial Advisor Project** — give financial recommendations to a fictional household (budget, college, home-buying, retirement, giving).

# The AP Exam
## 2 hours 40 minutes · 60 MCQ + 4 FRQ

---

## Section I — Multiple-choice
**60 questions · 60% · 70 minutes** (set-based)

**By unit:**
- Unit 1: 20–30% · Unit 2: 20–30% · Unit 3: 25–35% · Unit 4: 15–20%

**Personal finance is woven in:** 12–15 items across Units 1–4 = **20–25% of the exam**.

**By skill:** Concept Application 45–55% · Decision Making 25–35% · Entrepreneurship 5–15% · Communication 5–15%

---

## Section II — Free-response
**4 questions · 40% · 90 minutes**

| Part | Question | Weight | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **IIA** | **Q1** Business Canvas Project Exam-Day Validation | 15% | **25 min** |
| **IIB** | **Q2** Personal Finance | 5% | **65 min** |
|  | **Q3** Business Concept Application | 5% | *(shared)* |
|  | **Q4** Business Decision | 15% | *(shared)* |

> **IIA = Q1 only (25 min). IIB = Q2 + Q3 + Q4 (65 min).** FRQs assess Units 1–4 and Skills 1–4.

---

## FRQ 1 — Business Canvas Validation (deep-dive)
*15% · 25 minutes · Skills 1, 2, 4*

On exam day you respond in writing about **your own** Business Canvas Project:

1. **Pitch** your product idea
2. **Explain how you used hypothesis testing** to inform a decision
3. **Explain why a common entrepreneurial challenge** may threaten your product's **viability**

> The project itself isn't submitted — this FRQ is how your project work is scored. **Keep your notebook/test results all year.**

---

## How personal finance runs through everything

- Unit 3 Part 1 + Unit 5 are dedicated personal finance
- **But** 12–15 MC items weave personal finance into Units 1–4 (≈ 20–25% of the exam)
- Covers all **National Standards for Personal Financial Education** (Council for Economic Education + Jump$tart)
- Anchored by the **Financial Advisor Project** (Unit 5)

---

## How we'll work this year

- **Lesson videos** (MAMAKO) — built straight from the CED, in order, with original examples
- **Per-topic notes:** a **Summary** + a **Practice** set + these **Slides**
- **Running stories** per unit (candy seller → Theo → …) — concepts taught through one business
- **Cross-links everywhere** — each topic links forward and back

> Structure in the vault: `AP Business with Personal Finance/` → one Summary, Practice, and Slides file per topic.

---

## How to succeed

- **Learn the definitions cold** — then learn the *traps* (each topic's practice set lists them)
- **Follow the running story** — it's the connective tissue across the whole course
- **Keep your Business Canvas alive** all year — FRQ 1 depends on it
- **Match the method to the question** — most wrong answers are *right words in the wrong place*
- **Do every practice set** — the exam repeats the same trap patterns

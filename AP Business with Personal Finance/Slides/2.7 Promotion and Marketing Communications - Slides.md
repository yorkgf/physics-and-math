# Part 1: The Promotional Mix
### LO 2.7.A — Five tools to reach customers

---

## What is a marketing campaign?

A **marketing campaign** is a coordinated effort that uses multiple promotional tools to promote a product or brand to potential customers.

A campaign is not one ad or one post — it is a **strategic combination** of tools working together toward the same goal.

> **The goal of a marketing campaign: differentiate the product, build brand loyalty, and increase sales.**

---

## The promotional mix — 5 tools

| # | Tool | What It Does |
|---|------|-------------|
| 1 | **Media advertising** | Paid messages through mass channels (TV, radio, print, billboards) — reaches many people at once |
| 2 | **Personal selling** | One-on-one interaction — detailed info, demonstrations, relationship building |
| 3 | **Sales promotion** | Short-term incentives — discounts, coupons, buy-one-get-one — drives quick decisions |
| 4 | **Direct marketing** | Targeted messages sent to specific potential customers — flyers, brochures, email |
| 5 | **Public relations** | Activities that build a favorable public image — press releases, events, media coverage |

> **No single tool is "best" — the right mix depends on the product, the customer, and the goal.**

---

## Tool 1 — Media Advertising

**What it is:** Paid messages through mass-media channels that reach a large audience with the same message at the same time.

**Channels:** TV, radio, newspapers, magazines, billboards, online display ads

**Best for:**
- Routine purchases (groceries, beverages, clothing)
- Building broad brand awareness
- Reaching many people quickly with the same message

**Example:** A billboard in a busy shopping district showing a new lipstick shade

> **Media advertising = one message → many people → at the same time.**

---

## Tool 2 — Personal Selling

**What it is:** Direct, one-on-one interaction that provides detailed product information or a demonstration.

**Includes:**
- Face-to-face sales conversations
- **Sales pitch** — a short presentation conveying the product's value proposition
- Product demonstrations
- Livestream selling (modern form)

**Best for:**
- Consequential purchases (expensive, high-involvement decisions)
- Products that need explanation or demonstration
- Building trust and relationships

**Example:** A salesperson showing how a skincare product works on your skin

> **Personal selling = one message → one customer → detailed and personalized.**

---

## Tool 3 — Sales Promotion

**What it is:** Short-term incentives designed to encourage rapid purchasing decisions or reduce unsold inventory.

**Types:**
- Discounts and coupons
- Buy-one-get-one-free (BOGO)
- Free samples
- Limited-time offers
- Loyalty programs (short-term rewards)

**Best for:**
- Driving quick purchases
- Clearing unsold inventory
- Attracting new customers to try a product

**Example:** "50% off — this weekend only!"

> **Sales promotion = a temporary incentive that creates urgency.**

---

## Tool 4 — Direct Marketing

**What it is:** Sending targeted messages directly to a large number of potential customers.

**Channels:**
- Flyers and brochures
- Direct mail
- Email marketing
- SMS/text messages
- WeChat messages

**Best for:**
- Reaching many potential customers with a targeted message
- Delivering coupons, offers, or product information
- Measurable results (track who responds)

**Example:** A restaurant mailing discount flyers to every household in a neighborhood

> **Direct marketing = targeted message → many specific people → measurable response.**

---

## Tool 5 — Public Relations (PR)

**What it is:** Activities that create a favorable public image rather than advance a specific sale.

**Includes:**
- Press releases
- Media interviews
- Sponsorships
- Charity and community events
- Cultural storytelling
- Crisis management

**Best for:**
- Building long-term brand reputation
- Shaping public perception
- Managing the brand during a crisis

**Example:** A company sponsoring a cultural festival and issuing a press release about its community involvement

> **PR = shape the image, not close the sale.**

---

## Matching tools to purchase type

| Purchase Type | Best Promotional Tool | Why |
|--------------|----------------------|-----|
| **Consequential** (expensive, high-involvement) | **Personal selling** | Customer needs detailed info, demos, trust |
| **Routine** (everyday, low-involvement) | **Media advertising** or **Direct marketing** | Customer doesn't need a salesperson — just awareness |

**Examples:**
- Buying a **car** → consequential → personal selling (salesperson, test drive)
- Buying a **soda** → routine → media advertising (TV commercial, billboard)

> **The more consequential the purchase, the more personal the promotional tool should be.**

---

---

## Florasis — Using all five tools

Florasis (花西子) is a Chinese beauty brand founded in 2017. In five years, it grew from zero to one of China's top cosmetics brands — by using **every tool in the promotional mix**.

| Tool | How Florasis Uses It |
|------|---------------------|
| **Media advertising** | Billboard ads in premium locations (Times Square NYC, Shanghai landmarks) |
| **Personal selling** | Li Jiaqi (李佳琦) livestream selling on Taobao Live — one-on-one product demos to millions |
| **Sales promotion** | Limited-edition holiday gift sets, "gift with purchase" offers, Double 11 deals |
| **Direct marketing** | WeChat mini-program, personalized product recommendations, customer messages |
| **Public relations** | Cultural storytelling ("东方美学"), charity campaigns, international media coverage |

> **Florasis did not choose one tool — it built a coordinated campaign using all five.**

---

## Florasis — Li Jiaqi and personal selling

**Li Jiaqi (李佳琦)** is China's most famous livestream seller — known as the "Lipstick King."

**How it works as personal selling:**
- Li Jiaqi goes live on Taobao Live and demonstrates Florasis products in real time
- He applies lipstick, explains ingredients, compares shades — **detailed product demo**
- Viewers ask questions and get **immediate answers** — one-on-one feel at scale
- He says "Buy it now!" and thousands purchase within seconds — **the modern sales pitch**

**Why this is personal selling, not media advertising:**
- Media advertising = one message to many, no interaction
- Li Jiaqi's livestream = **interactive, detailed, personalized** — even though millions watch, each viewer feels spoken to directly

> **Livestream selling is personal selling at scale — detailed demos + real-time interaction + immediate purchase.**

---

## Florasis — PR through cultural storytelling

Florasis's PR strategy is built on **"东方美学" (Eastern aesthetics)**:

| PR Activity | What It Does |
|------------|-------------|
| Product design inspired by traditional Chinese art | Creates a story: "this is not just makeup, it is culture" |
| Packaging with Chinese motifs (lotus, phoenix, embroidery) | Media covers the design → free publicity |
| Partnerships with traditional Chinese artists and craftspeople | Positions the brand as a cultural ambassador, not just a cosmetics company |
| International expansion PR ("Chinese beauty goes global") | Generates media coverage in Japan, US, Southeast Asia |
| Charity campaigns (donating to cultural preservation) | Builds goodwill and favorable public image |

> **Florasis's PR does not say "buy our lipstick." It says "we represent Chinese beauty" — and the media tells that story for free.**

---

## Florasis — Sales promotion

Florasis uses sales promotions strategically:

| Promotion Type | Example |
|---------------|---------|
| **Limited-edition sets** | Chinese New Year gift boxes, Mid-Autumn Festival collections |
| **Gift with purchase** | Free miniature products with orders over a certain amount |
| **Double 11 (Singles' Day)** | Deep discounts during China's biggest shopping event |
| **First-purchase discount** | New customers get a special price on their first order |

> **Sales promotions create urgency — "buy now or miss out." Florasis uses cultural holidays as the timing for these promotions.**

---

## 🔑 Exam points to remember — Promotional Mix

| # | Point |
|---|-------|
| 1 | Five tools: **media advertising, personal selling, sales promotion, direct marketing, public relations** |
| 2 | **Consequential purchases** need personal selling; **routine purchases** respond to advertising or direct marketing |
| 3 | **PR** builds image; **advertising** delivers a message; **sales promotion** creates urgency |
| 4 | **Livestream selling** = modern personal selling (interactive, detailed, real-time) |
| 5 | The best campaigns use a **coordinated mix** of multiple tools |

# Part 2: Digital Marketing
### LO 2.7.B — How technology changed promotion forever

---

## What is digital marketing?

**Digital marketing** = using the internet and digital tools (websites, email, social media, mobile apps) to connect with and serve customers.

**Traditional marketing** = offline methods that existed before the internet (TV, radio, print, billboards).

> **Digital marketing did not replace traditional marketing — but it changed the rules of the game.**

---

## Traditional vs. Digital marketing

| Dimension | Traditional | Digital |
|-----------|------------|---------|
| **Channels** | TV, radio, newspapers, billboards | Websites, email, social media, apps |
| **Audience** | Broad, general | Global OR precisely targeted |
| **Personalization** | One-size-fits-all | Tailored to individual customer data |
| **Cost** | High production and placement costs | Generally lower, especially for small businesses |
| **Data collection** | Minimal — hard to track who saw the ad | Extensive — clicks, views, shares, purchases tracked |
| **Measurability** | Difficult (how many saw the billboard?) | Highly measurable (clicks, conversions, engagement) |
| **Speed** | Slow — weeks to assess results | Fast — real-time performance data |
| **Communication** | One-way (business → customer) | Two-way (customer can respond, share, engage) |

---

## How digital marketing changed business

**The three big shifts:**

### 1. From broadcast to conversation
- Traditional: business talks, customer listens
- Digital: customer talks back — reviews, comments, shares, creates content

### 2. From guessing to knowing
- Traditional: "we think 1 million people saw our billboard"
- Digital: "we know exactly who clicked, when, and what they bought"

### 3. From expensive to accessible
- Traditional: only big companies could afford TV ads
- Digital: a small brand can reach millions through social media for almost no cost

> **Digital marketing leveled the playing field — small brands can now compete with giants.**

---

## Big data in marketing

Digital tools enable businesses to collect **big data** — large volumes of information about how customers respond to marketing.

**What businesses can track:**
- Which ads customers click on
- How long they spend on a product page
- What they buy, when, and how often
- What they share on social media
- What makes them more likely to buy

**Why this matters:**
- Businesses can **personalize** messages to individual customers
- Businesses can **optimize** campaigns in real time
- Businesses can **predict** what customers want before they know it

> **Traditional marketing could not collect this data. Digital marketing makes it automatic.**

---

## Florasis — Digital marketing strategy

| Platform | How Florasis Uses It | What It Tracks |
|----------|---------------------|---------------|
| **Xiaohongshu (小红书)** | User reviews, "种草" (planting seed) content, KOL/KOC posts | Which products get shared most, customer sentiment |
| **Douyin (抖音)** | Short videos, livestream selling, influencer partnerships | View counts, engagement, conversion rates |
| **Weibo** | Brand announcements, PR campaigns, customer interaction | Follower growth, campaign reach |
| **WeChat** | Mini-program store, personalized recommendations, customer service | Purchase data, repeat rates, customer preferences |
| **Taobao/Tmall** | E-commerce store, Double 11 campaigns | Sales data, customer demographics, buying patterns |

> **Florasis uses each platform for a different purpose — and collects data from all of them.**

---

## Florasis — KOL and KOC strategy

Florasis uses two types of influencers:

| Type | What They Are | How Florasis Uses Them |
|------|-------------|----------------------|
| **KOL** (Key Opinion Leader) | Major influencers with millions of followers (e.g., Li Jiaqi) | Big campaigns, product launches, brand awareness |
| **KOC** (Key Opinion Consumer) | Everyday customers with smaller followings who share genuine reviews | Organic word-of-mouth, authenticity, trust |

**The strategy:**
- KOLs create **awareness** → millions see the product
- KOCs create **trust** → real people share real experiences
- Together, they create a **funnel**: awareness → interest → purchase

> **KOLs are the megaphone. KOCs are the friend who says "I tried it and it's great." Florasis uses both.**

---

## Florasis — The 79-yuan eyebrow pencil crisis

In September 2023, Li Jiaqi responded to a viewer who said Florasis's eyebrow pencil was too expensive (79 yuan):

**Li Jiaqi said:** "Have you worked hard enough? Have your wages gone up?"

**What happened:**
- Massive **backlash** on social media — customers felt shamed for questioning the price
- Florasis's brand image was damaged — the PR relationship with Li Jiaqi became a liability
- Customers questioned: "Is Florasis a Chinese cultural brand, or just another expensive product?"

**The PR lesson:**
- A brand's image can be damaged in seconds by a spokesperson's words
- **PR crisis management** requires quick, genuine response
- Florasis had to rebuild trust — separating its brand identity from Li Jiaqi's personal brand

> **When your personal selling partner becomes a PR liability, the entire promotional mix is affected.**

## Your turn — Digital vs. Traditional

> A new local restaurant wants to promote its grand opening. It has a budget of $2,000.

**Should it use traditional marketing (newspaper ad, flyers) or digital marketing (social media, local food bloggers)? Or both? Justify your answer using the comparison framework.**

---

## Your turn — Digital vs. Traditional answer

**Best answer: Primarily digital, with some traditional.**

**Digital advantages for this scenario:**
- **Cost:** $2,000 can buy significant social media reach; a newspaper ad might consume the entire budget
- **Targeting:** Social media can target people within the restaurant's delivery area
- **Measurability:** The restaurant can track exactly how many people saw, clicked, and reserved
- **Speed:** Results are immediate — the restaurant can adjust the campaign on day one
- **Interaction:** Customers can comment, share, and tag friends

**Traditional addition:**
- **Flyers** in the neighborhood (direct marketing) can reach older residents who may not use social media
- **Local newspaper** ad can build credibility with the community

> **For a $2,000 budget, digital marketing delivers more reach, targeting, and measurability per dollar.**

## 🔑 Exam points to remember — Digital Marketing

| # | Point |
|---|-------|

| 2 | Digital enables **personalization**, **data collection** (big data), and **two-way communication** |
| 3 | Traditional marketing is **one-way** and **hard to measure**; digital is **two-way** and **highly measurable** |
| 4 | Digital marketing is generally **lower cost** and more **accessible** to small businesses |
| 5 | **KOL** = major influencer (awareness); **KOC** = everyday customer (trust) |
| 6 | A brand's promotional strategy can be damaged by a spokesperson's words — **PR crisis management** matters |

## Practice — Stimulus

**Greenwave** is a company that makes reusable water bottles from recycled ocean plastic.

They launched a marketing campaign with the following elements:
- A **TV commercial** showing ocean plastic being collected and turned into bottles
- A **partnership with a popular YouTuber** who reviews the bottle on camera and demonstrates how it's made
- A **"20% off your first bottle"** coupon emailed to people who signed up on their website
- A **press release** announcing that Greenwave has removed 1 million plastic bottles from the ocean
- **Flyers** distributed at a local marathon with a QR code to the company website

---

## Practice — Q1

> The TV commercial showing ocean plastic being collected and turned into bottles is an example of:

> A. Personal selling
>
> B. Media advertising
>
> C. Public relations
>
> D. Sales promotion

---

## Practice — Q1 answer

**Answer: B.**

A TV commercial is a paid message delivered through a mass-media channel (TV) to a large audience at the same time. This is the definition of media advertising.

**Traps:**
- **C** — The content of the ad (ocean plastic story) has PR-like qualities, but the medium (paid TV commercial) makes it advertising, not PR. PR would be if a news station chose to cover the story on its own.
- **A** — Personal selling is one-on-one interaction; a TV commercial reaches millions simultaneously

> **The tool is defined by the medium and method, not just the message content.**

---

## Practice — Q2

> The YouTuber who reviews the bottle and demonstrates how it's made is performing which promotional tool?

> A. Media advertising — the YouTuber is paid to promote the product
>
> B. Direct marketing — the video is sent to specific customers
>
> C. Personal selling — the YouTuber provides detailed information and a demonstration
>
> D. Public relations — the video builds the brand's image

---

## Practice — Q2 answer

**Answer: C.**

The YouTuber provides **detailed product information** and a **demonstration** (showing how the bottle is made). Even though it reaches many viewers, the format — detailed explanation, visual demonstration, personal review — is functionally personal selling in a digital format.

**Traps:**
- **A** — Media advertising delivers the same generic message to a mass audience; a product review is personalized and detailed
- **D** — The primary purpose is to inform and persuade about the specific product, not to build a general brand image

> **Modern personal selling includes influencer reviews and livestream demonstrations — not just face-to-face sales.**

---

## Practice — Q3

> The press release announcing that Greenwave has removed 1 million plastic bottles from the ocean is an example of:

> A. Sales promotion
>
> B. Media advertising
>
> C. Public relations
>
> D. Digital marketing

---

## Practice — Q3 answer

**Answer: C.**

A press release is a classic public relations tool. Its purpose is to create a favorable public image (environmental impact) rather than to advance a specific sale. The press release invites media coverage — which is earned, not paid.

**Traps:**
- **B** — Media advertising is paid placement; a press release is distributed to media outlets for them to cover voluntarily
- **D** — Digital marketing is a channel category, not a promotional tool; the press release could be distributed digitally, but its function is PR

> **PR = building image through earned media. Advertising = building awareness through paid media.**

---

## Practice — Q4

> The "20% off your first bottle" coupon emailed to website sign-ups is an example of:

> A. Direct marketing
>
> B. Sales promotion
>
> C. Both direct marketing and sales promotion
>
> D. Public relations

---

## Practice — Q4 answer

**Answer: C.**

This combines two tools: **Direct marketing** (targeted email sent to specific people who signed up) AND **Sales promotion** (20% discount = short-term incentive to drive quick purchasing decision). The email is the channel (direct marketing); the discount is the offer (sales promotion).

**Traps:**
- **A alone** — Direct marketing describes the delivery method, but misses the discount incentive
- **B alone** — Sales promotion describes the discount, but misses the targeted email delivery
- **D** — This has nothing to do with public image; it is a direct sales offer

> **When a discount is sent via targeted email, it is BOTH direct marketing AND sales promotion.**

---

## Practice — Q5

> If Greenwave wanted to reach the most customers at the lowest cost per person, which tool would be most effective?

> A. Personal selling — one-on-one conversations with each potential customer
>
> B. Media advertising — a single TV or billboard ad reaching thousands
>
> C. Digital marketing — social media content shared by influencers and followers
>
> D. Direct marketing — mailing flyers to every household in the country

---

## Practice — Q5 answer

**Answer: C.**

Digital marketing (social media + influencer content) offers the lowest cost per impression and the highest potential reach. A single viral post can reach millions for almost no cost. TV advertising reaches many but is expensive. Personal selling reaches one person at a time. Mailing flyers to every household is extremely costly.

**Traps:**
- **B** — TV advertising reaches many, but production and placement costs are very high
- **A** — Personal selling is the most expensive per-person tool — it is designed for consequential purchases, not mass reach

> **Digital marketing = lowest cost per person reached + highest potential virality.**

---

## Practice — Q6

> A student says: "Florasis should stop using Li Jiaqi after the 79-yuan controversy and switch entirely to digital advertising." Evaluate this recommendation.

> A. Correct — Li Jiaqi damaged the brand, so all personal selling should be replaced with media advertising
>
> B. Incorrect — Florasis should use only personal selling and stop all advertising
>
> C. Incorrect — the promotional mix should be diversified, not replaced entirely; over-reliance on one tool (whether Li Jiaqi or advertising) creates risk
>
> D. Correct — the controversy proves that personal selling is always more risky than media advertising

---

## Practice — Q6 answer

**Answer: C.**

The lesson of the 79-yuan crisis is not that personal selling is bad — it is that **over-reliance on one tool** creates risk. Florasis's brand became too closely tied to Li Jiaqi. The solution is to **diversify** the promotional mix — use more KOCs, invest in owned media (WeChat, brand app), and build PR independently of any single spokesperson.

**Traps:**
- **A** — "Replace entirely" is an overcorrection; personal selling through livestreams is still highly effective
- **B** — "Use only personal selling" would make the same mistake in reverse
- **D** — Personal selling is not "always more risky" — the risk came from over-dependence on one person, not from the tool itself

> **The promotional mix should be diversified — no single tool should carry the entire brand.**

---

## Trap patterns to watch

| # | Trap type | Where it appears |
|---|-----------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Tool = medium, not message** — a paid TV ad is advertising even if the content feels like PR | Q1 |
| 2 | **Livestream/review = personal selling** — detailed demo + interaction, even at scale | Q2 |
| 3 | **Press release = PR** — earned media, not paid placement | Q3 |
| 4 | **Discount via email = BOTH direct marketing AND sales promotion** | Q4 |
| 5 | **Digital = lowest cost per reach** — not personal selling or TV | Q5 |
| 6 | **Diversify the mix** — don't replace one tool entirely with another | Q6 |

## Wrap-up — 2.7 Promotion and Marketing Communications

**What we covered:**
- **Marketing campaign** = coordinated effort using multiple promotional tools
- **Five tools**: media advertising, personal selling, sales promotion, direct marketing, public relations
- **Consequential purchases** → personal selling; **routine purchases** → advertising or direct marketing
- **Livestream selling** = modern personal selling (Li Jiaqi + Florasis)
- **Digital marketing** enables personalization, big data collection, two-way communication, and lower costs
- **KOL** (major influencer) vs. **KOC** (everyday customer) — both build the marketing funnel
- The 79-yuan crisis teaches: **diversify the promotional mix; never depend on one tool**
- **Florasis** used all five tools + digital marketing to grow from zero to billions in five years

**That completes Unit 2 — Marketing. You now know the full marketing mix: Product, Price, Place, Promotion.**

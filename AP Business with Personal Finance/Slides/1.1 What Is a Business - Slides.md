## Meet the candy seller

- Shows up Monday with a backpack **half full of candy**
- Bought in bulk over the weekend; sells bars for about **3× what he paid**
- By Friday the bag is empty → he restocks

> He meets the course's **complete definition of a business** — and we'll prove it today.

<!--
Ask: is he a business? Most students say "no, too small." That's the misconception this topic fixes.

---

## The candy seller — our running example

Every concept today — and across all of Unit 1 — maps onto **one business**.

**The operation**
- **Source:** buys a 20-bar box at the supermarket on weekends (bulk price)
- **Sell:** between classes, in the hallway, one bar at a time
- **Price:** about **3× what he paid** (≈ \$1.50 a bar)
- **Restock:** by Friday the bag is empty → the weekend run repeats

---

## Why he has customers — and why he's a real business

**Why he has customers:** students want a snack mid-day, the vending machines are empty or off-limits, and leaving campus is against the rules → he solves a real problem **at the moment it hits**.

**He is a full business:** he makes the decisions, keeps the profit, and carries the risk — we'll even name his legal form in **1.7**.

> Watch for him all period. Every definition lands on his backpack.

---

## What is a business?

A business is an organization or entity that **produces and distributes products**.

- products = **goods, services, or both**

Notice what the definition **leaves out**: size · buildings · employees · legal paperwork.

> A business can be **any size**, face-to-face or virtual.

🤔 *Think first: by this definition, are Bombas, 凡客, and the candy seller all businesses?*

---

## What is a business? — in the examples

| | Produces? | Distributes? | Business? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bombas** | socks (goods) | online + stores | ✅ |
| **凡客 (VANCL)** | apparel (goods) | online store | ✅ |
| **Candy seller** | — (resells) | buys bulk, sells one bar at a time | ✅ (a tiny retailer) |

> **Producing / distributing products** is what counts — not size, buildings, or paperwork.

---

## Scale doesn't change the definition

| Example | Scale |
|---|---|
| The backpack kid | Smallest |
| One ice cream shop | Small |
| Mixue (蜜雪冰城) — ice cream under \$1, tens of thousands of stores | Huge |

All three **produce and distribute products** → all three are businesses.

**凡客 (VANCL)** once sold tens of millions of items a year; **Bombas** grew from a startup to a national brand.

> **Scale changes the numbers, never the definition.** ← the exam tests this.

---

## Goods vs. services

- **Goods** = tangible items (a candy bar, a phone, a desk)
- **Services** = work performed for compensation (a haircut, a tutoring session, a flight)

> Classify by **what the customer is primarily paying for** in the scenario.

🤔 *Think first: does Bombas sell goods, services, or both? What about 凡客?*

---

## Goods vs. services — in the examples

- **Bombas:** socks = **goods**; customer support + easy returns = a **service** → sells **both**
- **凡客 (VANCL):** the apparel = **goods** (its site + customer service = a **service**)
- **Candy seller:** the bar = a **good** (the convenience of hallway availability is the service wrapped around it)

---

## Two money terms (used all course)

- **Revenue** = money earned from selling, **before** expenses
- **Profit** = what remains after **all** expenses are subtracted

### **Profit = Revenue − Total Cost**

🤔 *Think first: how do 凡客 and Bombas each turn revenue into profit?*

---

## Revenue & Profit — in the examples

**Two models, same formula:**
- **凡客 (VANCL):** low price × high volume (fast fashion) — until over-expansion broke the model
- **Bombas:** premium price × fewer units (makes **2 pairs per sale** — one sold, one donated)
- **Candy seller:** revenue = everything collected this week; profit = what's left after the supermarket run + restocking

---

## Customer vs. consumer

- **Customer** = the individual or business that **purchases**
- **Consumer** = the individual who **uses** it (whether or not they bought it)

Buy your own lunch → you're **both** in one transaction.

🤔 *Think first: where is the buyer NOT the user? (Hint: a donated pair, or the candy seller's \$3/two-bar sale.)*

<!--
The single most-tested pair in Topic 1.1. Drill the one-line rule on the next slide.
-->

---

## Customer vs. consumer — in the examples

- **Bombas buy-one-give-one:** the buyer = **customer**; the donated pair is used by a shelter guest who never bought it = a different **consumer**
- **Candy seller:** see the **\$3 / two-bar split** (next slide) ↓

> **Purchase defines the customer; use defines the consumer.**

---

## The \$3 / two-bar split

A student hands the candy seller **\$3 for two bars** — one for himself, one for a friend in the next classroom.

- The buyer = **customer for both bars**
- The friend **consumes a bar he never bought** → he's the **consumer**

> **Purchase defines the customer; use defines the consumer.** *(Same split as Bombas's donated pair.)*

---

## Exam tip: who paid vs. who used

**Underline who PAID and underline who USED.**
If they're different people → that difference is exactly what the question is testing.

**Bombas:** who paid? the buyer. Who used? the wearer — *or the shelter guest*.

The customer can be a **business** too: a school buys software → school = **customer**, students = **consumers**.

---

## Needs, wants, market opportunities

- **Need** = essential for survival (food, water, shelter, basic healthcare)
- **Want** = improves life, not essential
- **Market opportunity** = a customer problem / need / want a business *might* address → **every business starts at one of these**

🤔 *Think first: is a pair of socks a need or a want? What about a flavored drink?*

---

## Needs, wants, opportunities — in the examples

- **Socks** are a **need** (basic clothing) — but a **\$12 seamless** pair is a **want**
- **Clothing** is a need — **凡客's fast fashion** is a **want**
- **Candy seller's opportunity:** students want a snack mid-day → he addresses it

> A **market opportunity** = a need/want a business *might* address. Every business starts at one.

---

## Problem-solution fit

The **match** between a customer problem and a product built to solve it.

🤔 *Think first: what problem does Bombas solve? 凡客? The candy seller?*

---

## Problem-solution fit — in the examples

- **Bombas:** problem = socks that hurt + shelters short on socks → a better sock **and** a donated pair
- **凡客 (VANCL):** problem = affordable, decent-quality fashion → "人民快时尚"
- **Candy seller:** snack want + empty/off-limits vending machines → candy in the hallway the moment it hits
- **Uber / DiDi:** unpredictable taxis + unclear pricing → match riders to drivers with an upfront price

> **Strong fit → the solution is worth more** to the person with the problem.

---

## "You can't serve everyone"

A business **cannot** satisfy all potential customers → it must **select.**

**Selection questions:** severity? · how many share it? · can we solve it better? · will they pay more than it costs? · can we reach them?

🤔 *Think first: which customers did Bombas choose? What did 凡客 do instead?*

> ⚠️ An answer choice that says **"serve everyone"** = the **wrong answer.**

---

## Selection — in the examples

**Won by selecting:**
- **Bombas** chose **premium, mission-minded** buyers — *not* the cheapest-sock market
- **Candy seller** works only **his own grade's hallway** — a deliberate, narrow selection

**Lost by trying to serve everyone:**
- **凡客 (VANCL)** — started with shirts, then sold *everything*; inventory blew up and the brand thinned out → collapsed

---

## Value

**Value** = the worth or benefit of a product to customers = **the highest price they'd willingly pay.**

> Value **rises** as problem-solution fit improves.

🤔 *Think first: how much would a Bombas buyer willingly pay? A 凡客 buyer?*

---

## Value — in the examples

- **Bombas** buyers willingly pay ~**\$12** (comfort + mission = high perceived value)
- **凡客 (VANCL)** targeted price-sensitive buyers → value delivered via **affordability**
- **Candy seller:** the same bar is worth more during **3rd period** than next to a supermarket checkout

---

## Value creation vs. value capture

| | Value **creation** | Value **capture** |
|---|---|---|
| Is about | the **customer** | the **business** |
| Means | provides a product that responds to a problem/need/want | charges a price **higher than cost** |
| Flows | benefit **to** the customer | profit **to** the business |

**Both required, in order:** create first, **then** capture.

🤔 *Think first: how does Bombas create AND capture? How did 凡客?*

---

## Creation vs. capture — in the examples

- **Bombas:** creates value (comfort + mission) and captures it (**premium price**)
- **凡客 (VANCL):** created value (affordable fashion) and captured it — until over-expansion eroded both
- **Candy seller:** creates (snack at the moment of want) and captures (**\$1.50 − \$0.50 = \$1.00/bar**)
- **MoviePass:** created value but captured **none** → shut down (coming up)

<!--
The topic is named for this pair. The most common exam trap = putting the capture definition inside a creation question (we'll see it in Q3).
-->

---

## The candy numbers

He buys a 20-bar box for **\$10** → cost = **\$0.50 / bar**. Sells each for **\$1.50**.

### **Value captured = Price − Cost = \$1.50 − \$0.50 = \$1.00 / bar**

**Customer side:** a student who'd have paid **\$2** pays \$1.50 → **keeps \$0.50 of benefit**.
→ **Both sides end up better off** → that's why the trade happens voluntarily.

> *(Bombas's capture = price − the cost of making **2** pairs — one sold, one donated.)*

---

## When you create but don't capture — MoviePass

**MoviePass (US):**
- ~**\$10 / month** to see a movie in theaters **every day**
- MoviePass paid theaters **near full price** for the tickets members used
- A member watching 4 movies/month cost the company **several × \$10**
- Price − cost was **negative** → shut down **2019**

> **Value creation** (people loved it) ✅ · **Value capture** (price > cost) ❌

---

## When you create but don't capture — ofo

**ofo 小黄车 (China) — same failure, different country:**
- Bikes on every corner, dirt cheap; users loved it
- But each bike's wear + upkeep cost more than the revenue → burned through billions, collapsed 2018–19
  ▶ https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV19V41177rV

> ⚠️ **Popularity + price below cost = value creation WITHOUT value capture.**

---

## Value proposition

A clear statement of how a product solves a problem and why choose it over alternatives.

**Template:** For **[target customer]** who has **[problem]**, our product delivers **[benefit]**. Unlike **[the alternative]**…

🤔 *Think first: fill in the template for Bombas. Then for the candy seller.*

---

## Value proposition — in the examples

**Bombas:** *"For people who want comfortable socks, a premium seamless pair — and for every pair you buy, we donate a pair to someone in need."*

**Candy seller:** *"For students stuck on campus who want a snack now, a candy bar in the hallway for \$1.50. Unlike an empty vending machine or a store they can't go to."*

> One sentence naming the **customer, the problem, the solution, and the edge.**

---

## Exam points to remember

- Business = **what it does**, not its size
- Classify goods/services by **what's primarily paid for**
- Customer vs consumer → **underline paid vs used**
- Value capture = **Price − Cost**
- **"Serve everyone"** = wrong answer
- Popularity + price below cost = **creation without capture**

# Practice — 6 questions

**3 on one stimulus (LumiDesk)** · **3 standalone**

Read → pause → **commit to an answer** → then reveal.

---

## Stimulus: LumiDesk

LumiDesk makes height-adjustable study desks for students in **small apartments**.

- Cost to manufacture: **\$90**
- Customers value it at: **\$260** (sits ↔ stands, folds flat)
- Sells for: **\$180**
- Tutoring centers now buy desks → students use them in lessons

**Three numbers:** C = \$90 · value = \$260 · P = \$180

---

## Q1 — Who is the consumer?

For desks bought by **tutoring centers**, which best identifies the **consumer**?

- A. the tutoring centers
- B. LumiDesk
- C. the students who use the desks during lessons
- D. the parents who pay tuition to the tutoring centers

<!--
Pause. Ask students to point at "who paid" and "who used" in the scenario before answering.
-->

---

## Q1 — Answer: **C**

**Why:** the consumer is the one who **uses** it. The centers **bought** (customer); the students **use** them (consumers).

**Traps:**
- **A** = the **customer** placed in a consumer question
- **D** = a **real but different transaction** (parents paying tuition)

> Answer the transaction the question points at.

---

## Q2 — Compute value captured

What is the **value captured** by LumiDesk per desk at the current price?

- A. \$80
- B. \$90
- C. \$170
- D. \$260

---

## Q2 — Answer: **B (\$90)**

### **Value captured = Price − Cost = \$180 − \$90 = \$90**

**Trap: A (\$80)** = \$260 − \$180 (value − price) → that's the **customer's** kept benefit, built from the **same three numbers** — which is exactly why it's there.

> In a capture question, **write price − cost before you look at the choices.**

---

## Q3 — Why is it value creation?

Which best explains why LumiDesk demonstrates **value creation**?

- A. sold at a price higher than manufacturing cost
- B. responds to the space problem faced by students in small apartments
- C. generates revenue from individuals and tutoring centers
- D. manufacturing cost is low relative to competitors

---

## Q3 — Answer: **B**

**Why:** value creation = responds to a customer's **problem / need / want**. The small-apartment space problem is what the product was built for.

**Trap: A** = the **value-capture definition** placed inside a creation question — **the most common trap in this topic.**

> Creation → the **customer's problem**; capture → **price & cost.**

---

## Q4 — Problem-solution fit

A snack company sees gym members want **high-protein food right after workouts**, and develops a **refrigerated protein snack in gym vending machines**. Which concept does this best illustrate?

- A. value capture
- B. barrier to entry
- C. problem-solution fit
- D. a need converted into a want

---

## Q4 — Answer: **C**

**Why:** A want was identified, a matching product built, placed at the point the want occurs.

**Trap: B** — a real **Topic 1.2** term used too early. *Recognizing a word isn't enough — it must match the scenario.*

---

## Q5 — Goods vs. services

HomeGuard: a one-time price for a **camera + doorbell** plus a monthly fee for **24-hour monitoring** through an app. Which best describes its products?

- A. goods only
- B. services only
- C. both goods and services
- D. neither

---

## Q5 — Answer: **C**

**Why:** Camera = good; monitoring = service.

**Trap: D** mixes two vocabulary sets. *Needs/wants = why buy; goods/services = what's sold.*

---

## Q6 — Choosing customers

A new meal-delivery startup has limited funding. Which best reflects how businesses address market opportunities?

- A. serve every possible customer in the city
- B. select a specific group whose problem you can solve better than competitors
- C. target the customers with the mildest problems
- D. delay choosing customers until the product is perfected

---

## Q6 — Answer: **B**

**Why:** A business **cannot satisfy all** customers → it selects.

**Trap: A** — "serve everyone" reads as ambition but is the **wrong answer** (and *will* appear on the real exam).

---

## Trap patterns to watch

| # | Trap | Where |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Customer ↔ consumer** swap | Q1 |
| 2 | **Value − price** (\$80) vs **price − cost** (\$90) | Q2 |
| 3 | **Capture definition inside a creation question** ← most common | Q3 |
| 4 | A real term from a **neighboring topic** as a distractor | Q4 |
| 5 | **Needs/wants** vs **goods/services** | Q5 |
| 6 | **"Serve everyone"** as the wrong answer | Q6 |

## Wrap-up

- A business **produces & distributes** products — **any size**
- **Customer** pays · **Consumer** uses
- **Create** value (solve a real problem), **then capture** it (price > cost)
- **You can't serve everyone** — select

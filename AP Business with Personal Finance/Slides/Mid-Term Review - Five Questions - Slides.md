## The five questions the class missed most

**1**　A tutoring company has no factory and no warehouse, yet it still has a supply chain. Which combination makes up its **service** supply chain?
**A.** Suppliers, distributors, retailers　**B.** Raw materials, manufacturing, inventory　**C.** Advertising, market research, pricing　**D.** Employees, resources, delivery systems

**2**　A company's logo is recognized and trusted, so customers choose it over identical products. The added value created by that recognition and trust is:
**A.** Product messaging　**B.** A value proposition　**C.** Brand equity　**D.** Market saturation

**3**　A business spends ¥12,000 on marketing and acquires 400 new customers, who will spend ¥150 each over three years. Which statement is correct?
**A.** CAC ¥12,000, LTV ¥150 → cut marketing
**B.** CAC ¥30, LTV ¥150, but judge CAC alone since LTV cannot be estimated
**C.** CAC ¥150, LTV ¥30 → raise advertising
**D.** CAC ¥30, LTV ¥150 → building strong relationships is profitable

**4**　A fitness app groups users by age, income and location, and by whether they value convenience, then writes: "Lin, 24, a busy graduate student who exercises before work." Lin's description is best called:
**A.** A market segment　**B.** A purchasing pattern　**C.** A customer profile　**D.** Customer data

**5**　A founder wants to know how many students would pay ¥30 a month for a planner app, and why others would not. Which research design fits both questions?
**A.** An experiment for both questions　**B.** Observation of existing students only
**C.** A focus group for both questions　**D.** A survey for "how many" + interviews for "why"

## B1 — what the paper asked

<div class="q"><b>B1. Explain how your business produces its product and delivers it to your customer.</b><br>
(a) Describe your production process (artisan or mass production), and explain which customer priorities and which core competencies led your team to choose it. (3)<br>
(b) Name your key suppliers and describe each stage of your supply chain, from raw materials (or the people, resources and delivery systems for a service) to the final customer. (3)<br>
(c) Choose one of the five factors a business weighs when choosing a supplier and explain which one mattered most. Then identify one risk in your supply chain and how your business would manage it. (3)</div>

**Problem — 80% average, but the marks lost are all structural.** Papers describe production in one paragraph, never answer (a) with a named process, and in (c) pick something that is not one of the five factors.

**Real answers from this class:**
> "**Our production is a snack shop near the school, which provide food for students. The customer priorities is food health... So that is a core competencies.**"
> → Never says **artisan or mass production** — (a) loses 2 of 3 marks before anything else is read.

> "**The political factors effect me the most because when it is in inflation, they may increase the price...**"
> → "Political factors" is PESTEL, not one of the five supplier factors (cost, quality, efficiency, convenience, risk).

**Correct form:**
> Prefer **mass production** because our customers buy on price and our competency is a cheap supply partner. → Suppliers: Tyson (chicken) and a local bakery. → Farm → our kitchen → chilled packing → own delivery → student. → Factor: **cost** — 60% of our unit cost is raw material. → Risk: **cold-chain failure** → buy insulated boxes and cap delivery radius at 3 km.

**Key concepts:** artisan (skill, small batches, customisation) vs mass (machinery, assembly line, scale) · chain order: raw materials → manufacturing → warehouse → distribution/retail → customer · five supplier factors: cost, quality, efficiency, convenience, risk — **every risk needs a named response**.

## B2 — what the paper asked

<div class="q"><b>B2. Explain how your business understands its customers and the data behind them.</b><br>
(a) Describe the customer data you collect, name two tools or methods you use to collect it, and explain how demographic and psychographic data each help you decide which customers to serve. (4)<br>
(b) Explain how you used market segmentation to identify your target customers, and describe your customer profile using at least two demographic and two psychographic characteristics. (3)<br>
(c) Identify one privacy, identity-theft or fraud risk your business creates for its customers, and explain one step your business will take to reduce it. (2)</div>

**Problem — 15 papers have no profile at all**, 6 never separate demographic from psychographic use, and 6 answer (c) with a product or supply-chain risk.

**Real answers from this class:**
> "**Our target customers are the people who wanna go to see it or people who are poor but also wanna go to protein.**"
> → A vague group with no name, age or lifestyle: this is a market, **not a profile** — (b) loses 2 of 3.

> "**The customer data I collect is that 80 percent of our customers are students, and we use primary-source and secondary-source research.**"
> → A number, but **no tool named** and no demographic / psychographic split.

> ✓ "**Our customer profile 'Chen Yu' is focused more on budget and price transparency; he is a Shanghai working-age person. He also wants private space and convenience.**"
> → Demographic (Shanghai, working age) + psychographic (price transparency, private space, convenience).

**Correct form:** *Lin, 24, a graduate student in Shanghai (age, occupation) who commutes 70 minutes a day (situation) and values speed over comfort (values) — willing to pay ¥30/month for 20-minute guided sessions.*

**Key concepts:** demographic data decides **who to serve and what to charge**; psychographic data decides **what to build and how to promote it**; profile = 2 + 2, ideally one named person · privacy question: undisclosed collection and resale = **privacy violation**; stolen by outsiders = **data breach**.

## B3 — what the paper asked

<div class="q"><b>B3. Explain how your customers actually decide to buy, and the tactic you will use to reach them.</b><br>
(a) When your target customer buys a product like yours, is the decision most likely rational, habitual, or impulsive? Explain using at least three of these factors: personal, psychological, social and cultural, situational. (5)<br>
(b) Design ONE sales tactic for your business based on at least two of Cialdini's principles of influence. Name each principle you use and explain exactly how your tactic applies it. (4)</div>

**Problem — 24 of 37 papers lose most of (b).** They describe an action ("we give a discount", "we hire actors") but never name the principle, so the examiner cannot tell which of the six it is meant to be.

**Real answers from this class:**
> "**Reciprocity. I think people feel obligated to give something back after receiving something of value.**"
> → Right concept, right definition, **but no tactic**: nothing in the business actually gives the customer a favour.

> "**If buying two products by buying single product with coffee beans you need to pay 100¥, but if you buy product with fix service, only need 150¥ and can free to fix in one year.**"
> → A real tactic (bundle + free repairs), **but no principle named**.

**Correct form — name it, do it, explain the mechanism:**
> **Reciprocity + scarcity.** *Tactic:* every machine comes with a free one-year repair plan, and we stock only 30 starter kits a month. *Mechanism:* the free plan creates a felt debt, the limited stock creates urgency. *Result:* repeat purchase on beans without cutting price.

**Key concepts:** reciprocity · consistency · consensus · liking · authority · scarcity. One tactic must carry **two named** principles — name → action → mechanism.

## B4 — what the paper asked

<div class="q"><b>B4. Explain the market research your team carried out and how the findings shaped your decisions.</b><br>
(a) State one business hypothesis you tested, in the form of a testable assumption about a customer, product, or market. (2)<br>
(b) Describe the primary-source research you conducted: which method(s) and why they match the data needed, the size and the composition of your sample, and two ways you made sure your questions and your sample were unbiased. (4)<br>
(c) Which type of data visualization best presents your most important finding? Explain the insight it gives you about your customer. (2)</div>

**Problem — 14 papers never name a chart, 12 give no sample size, 11 write something that is not a testable hypothesis, 4 leave it blank.**

**Real answers from this class:**
> "**Can you get a good service?**" (as the hypothesis)
> → A survey question, not an assumption about customers that can be proved true or false.

> "**We send out a sample of our product and let them taste the convenience.**"
> → An action you plan to take, not a prediction you can test.

> "**We use survey because we need to gather big amount of information. Most of our sample is composed by different price ranges that customers prefer.**"
> → Method and reason are there, **but no number of respondents and no composition**.

**Correct form — five elements, five lines:**
> ① **Hypothesis:** at least 40% of Grade 11 students would pay ¥30/month if a shared calendar were included.
> ② **Method + why:** a survey, because we need a countable share; interviews for the "why".
> ③ **Sample:** 86 students in Grades 10–12 (52% female) + 14 parents.
> ④ **Two bias controls:** neutral wording and anonymous forms (question design); random sampling by grade across classes (sampling).
> ⑤ **Chart + insight:** a stacked bar chart by grade showed Grade 11 chose the ¥25 bundle most → we priced it at ¥25.

**Key concepts:** method must match the data (survey = how many; interview = why; experiment = actual behaviour; secondary data = market size) · bias controls must cover **question design and sampling** · chart must match the data (trend = line, share = pie, comparison = bar).

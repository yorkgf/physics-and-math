## Course Description

AP Computer Science A is an introductory, college-level computer science course. Students learn to design, develop, and analyze programs written in the Java programming language to solve problems and accomplish tasks. The course emphasizes object-oriented programming and design, the use of data structures to organize large sets of data, the development and implementation of algorithms to process data, the analysis of potential solutions, and the ethical and social implications of computing systems.

This course is designed to be the equivalent of a one-semester, introductory college course in computer science. A minimum of 20 hours of hands-on, structured lab experiences are integrated throughout the year to engage students in individual and group problem-solving.

---

## Course Objectives

Upon completion of this course, students will be able to:

1. **Design code** - Determine an appropriate program design and develop algorithms to solve problems
2. **Develop code** - Write and implement program code involving data abstractions and procedural abstractions in Java
3. **Analyze code** - Determine the output or result of given program code, and explain why code may not compile or work as intended
4. **Document code and computing systems** - Describe the behavior and initial conditions that produce specified results in a program
5. **Use computers responsibly** - Understand the ethical and social implications of computer use, including privacy, bias, and intellectual property

## Course Policies

### Attendance
- Regular attendance is essential, as programming concepts build sequentially
- Students are responsible for obtaining notes and completing assignments when absent
- More than 3 unexcused absences may result in grade deductions

### Late Work
- Assignments submitted late will receive a 10% deduction per day
- Assignments more than 3 days late will receive a maximum of 50%
- Exceptions made for excused absences and emergencies

### Academic Integrity
- All work must be your own
- Collaboration is encouraged for labs, but individual submissions must be original
- Plagiarism or cheating (including unauthorized AI-generated code submission) will result in a zero and possible course failure
- AI tools may be used as learning aids outside of assessments but never on graded work

### Professional Conduct
- Students are expected to demonstrate respect, collaboration, and responsible computing practices
- Constructive code review and peer feedback are required during group activities
- Ethical use of data and respect for privacy are emphasized throughout the course

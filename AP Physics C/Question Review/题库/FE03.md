# Final Q03 - Average force during embedding

## Original Question
A projectile of mass $m_1$ is traveling with a horizontal speed $v_i$ to the right toward a block of mass $m_2$ that is at rest on a horizontal surface, as shown. The projectile becomes embedded in the block, and the block and projectile move together to the right with speed $v_f$. There is negligible friction between the block and the surface.
The duration of the collision between the projectile and the block is $\Delta t$. Which of the following expressions is equal to the average force exerted on the block by the projectile during the collision?
(A) $\frac{m_1 v_i}{2\Delta t}$
(B) $\frac{m_2 v_i}{2\Delta t}$
(C) $\frac{m_1 v_f}{m_2 \Delta t}$
(D) $\frac{m_1 v_f}{\Delta t}$
(E) $\frac{m_2 v_f}{\Delta t}$

## Answer Key: C

**Explanation:**
Based on Newton's second law, this force exerted by the projectile on the block equals $m_2 a$, where $a$ is the acceleration of the block. The acceleration is given by the change in velocity of the box. Since the block was not initially moving, this becomes $a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \frac{v_f - 0}{\Delta t} = \frac{v_f}{\Delta t}$. The average net force or the average force on the block by the projectile is $m_2 v_f / \Delta t$.

### Key Concepts
- Impulse-Momentum: $\vec{F}_{avg}\Delta t = \Delta \vec{p}$
- Embedding = perfectly inelastic collision (objects stick together)
- $F_{avg} = \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}$

### Common Mistakes
- Didn't account for direction/sign
- Used wrong initial/final velocities
- Confused with energy instead of momentum

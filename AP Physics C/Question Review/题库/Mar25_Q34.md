# Mar25_Q34 - New tension when rope is moved

## Question Topic
绳子张力与平衡 (Statics and Tension)

## Original Question
A horizontal uniform plank is supported by ropes I and II at points $P$ and $Q$, respectively, as shown above. The two ropes have negligible mass. The tension in rope I is $150\text{ N}$. The point at which rope II is attached to the plank is now moved to point $R$ halfway between point $Q$ and point $C$, the center of the plank. The plank remains horizontal. Which of the following are most nearly the new tensions in the two ropes?

(A) Tension in I: 75 N, Tension in II: 225 N
(B) Tension in I: 100 N, Tension in II: 200 N
(C) Tension in I: 112.5 N, Tension in II: 112.5 N
(D) Tension in I: 112.5 N, Tension in II: 187.5 N
(E) Tension in I: 150 N, Tension in II: 300 N

## Answer Key
**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:**
Initially, with rope II at $Q$, the tensions in both ropes are equal by symmetry, so the plank's weight is $2 \times 150\text{ N} = 300\text{ N}$.
When rope II is moved to $R$ (halfway between $C$ and $Q$), its distance from $P$ is $\frac{3}{4}L$ where $L$ is the length of the plank. Taking torques about $P$:
$mg(L/2) = T_{II}(\frac{3}{4}L)$
$300(1/2) = T_{II}(3/4) \implies 150 = \frac{3}{4} T_{II} \implies T_{II} = 200\text{ N}$.
The total upward force is $T_I + T_{II} = 300\text{ N}$, so $T_I = 100\text{ N}$.

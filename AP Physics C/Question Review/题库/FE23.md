# Final Q23 - Tension in the rope that is holding a plank

## Original Question
A $12 \text{ kg}$ plank, which is $4.0 \text{ m}$ long, is attached to a wall by a hinge on its lower end. A rope, attached to the plank a distance of $1.0 \text{ m}$ from its free end, is perpendicular to the plank and holds the plank at an angle of $37^\circ$ with the wall, as shown in the diagram above. The tension in the rope is most nearly
(A) $48 \text{ N}$
(B) $64 \text{ N}$
(C) $72 \text{ N}$
(D) $120 \text{ N}$
(E) $144 \text{ N}$

## Answer Key: A

**Explanation:**
The net torque on the plank is zero, since it is in static equilibrium. Thus, the clockwise torque produced by the gravitational force exerted at the center of the plank is equal in magnitude to the counterclockwise torque exerted by the rope on the plank. $\tau_{cw} = \tau_{ccw}$. $\tau = R_\perp F$. $(2.0 \text{ m})mg \sin 37^\circ = (3.0 \text{ m})T$. $(2.0 \text{ m})(12 \text{ kg})(10 \text{ m/s}^2)(\sin 37^\circ) = (3.0 \text{ m})T$. $T = 48 \text{ N}$.

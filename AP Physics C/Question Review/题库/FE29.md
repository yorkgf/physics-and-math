# Final Q29 - Sled pulled by snow dogs

## Original Question
A sled that weighs $5.0 \times 10^3 \text{ N}$ is pulled across level snow by dogs exerting a horizontal force on it. The coefficient of kinetic friction between the sled and snow is $0.050$. How much work is done by the dogs on the sled when the sled moves $1.0 \times 10^3 \text{ m}$ at constant speed?
(A) Zero
(B) $2.5 \times 10^5 \text{ J}$
(C) $5.0 \times 10^5 \text{ J}$
(D) $2.5 \times 10^6 \text{ J}$
(E) $5.0 \times 10^6 \text{ J}$

## Answer Key: A

**Explanation:**
Since the force exerted by the dogs is horizontal, the normal force must equal the weight of the sled because there is no motion in the $y$-direction and the $y$-components of all forces must add to zero. As a result, the normal force is $F_N = 5.0 \times 10^3 \text{ N}$. The frictional force is equal to the normal force times the coefficient of friction: $0.050(5.0 \times 10^3 \text{ N}) = 0.25 \times 10^3 \text{ N}$. Since the horizontal acceleration is zero, the force exerted by the dogs must be equal to the frictional force but directed opposite the direction of friction. The work done by the dogs over a distance $d$ is $W = d f \cos 0^\circ = (0.25 \times 10^3 \text{ N})(1.0 \times 10^3 \text{ m}) = 2.5 \times 10^5 \text{ J}$.

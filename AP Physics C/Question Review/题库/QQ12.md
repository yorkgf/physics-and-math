# Q12 - Falling sphere with resistive force

## Question Topic
有阻力落球 (Falling Sphere with Resistive Force)

## Original Question
A sphere of mass m is dropped from the top of a building and reaches the ground before achieving terminal velocity. The force of air resistance that acts on the sphere as it falls is given by F = -kv, where k is a positive constant and v is the velocity of the sphere. What happens to the magnitude of the sphere’s velocity and acceleration, and to the distance it falls during each second, as the sphere approaches the ground?
(A) Velocity: Increases, Acceleration: Increases, Distance: Increases
(B) Velocity: Increases, Acceleration: Decreases, Distance: Increases
(C) Velocity: Increases, Acceleration: Decreases, Distance: Decreases
(D) Velocity: Decreases, Acceleration: Increases, Distance: Decreases
(E) Velocity: Decreases, Acceleration: Decreases, Distance: Increases

## Answer Key

**Explanation:**
If the sphere never reaches terminal velocity, the resistive force will always be less than the force of gravity; thus, the net force and the acceleration will always be downward. Therefore, the magnitude of the sphere’s velocity will always be increasing. If the magnitude of the sphere’s velocity is always increasing, the distance fallen each second will be increasing. Also, if the sphere is speeding up, the resistive force increases, and the net force and acceleration decrease as the stone gets closer to the ground.
**Correct Answer: C**

## Analysis

This problem involves drag force and terminal velocity concepts.

### Resistive Force Models
**Linear drag** (low velocity): $\vec{F}_{drag} = -b\vec{v}$

**Quadratic drag** (high velocity): $\vec{F}_{drag} = -\frac{1}{2}\rho C_D A v^2 \hat{v}$

### Terminal Velocity
When drag equals weight:
$$mg = bv_T$$ (linear drag)
$$mg = \frac{1}{2}\rho C_D A v_T^2$$ (quadratic drag)

### Key Concepts
- Drag force increases with velocity
- Net force decreases as drag builds up
- Terminal velocity: constant velocity when forces balance

### Common Mistakes
- **Answer A**: Confused direction of drag (should oppose motion)
- **Answer B/D**: Wrong relationship between forces at terminal velocity
- Students choosing other answers likely didn't properly apply force balance

# Final Q04 - Center of mass - pyramidal wedge and cylinder

## Original Question
A wedge of mass $M$ is balanced about its center with a small cylinder of mass $m$ attached to its narrowest end, as shown. The linear mass per length of the wedge can be modeled by an equation of the form: $\lambda = b x^2$, where $b$ is a constant with appropriate units and $x$ is the distance from the narrowest end.
What is the ratio $\frac{M}{m}$ of the masses of the two objects?
(A) $\frac{2}{3}$
(B) 1
(C) 2
(D) 3

## Answer Key: D

**Explanation:**
The center of mass of the wedge is determined by $x_{CM} = \frac{\int_0^L x dm}{\int_0^L dm} = \frac{\int_0^L x b x^2 dx}{\int_0^L b x^2 dx} = \left. \frac{3}{4} x \right|_{x=0}^{x=L} = \frac{3}{4} L$ to the left of the narrowest end. This is $\frac{L}{4}$ to the left of the point about which the wedge is balanced. The ratio of the masses can then be found using rotational equilibrium: $Mg\frac{L}{4} = mg\frac{L}{2}$. $\frac{M}{m} = 2$.

# Mar25_Q31 - Force vs time graph for a wheel

## Question Topic
转动动能图像分析 (Rotational Kinematics and Torque)

## Original Question
A motor drives a shaft of radius $R/8$ that is attached to the center of a wheel of radius $R$. The motor is turned off, and the force that the motor exerts on the shaft, $F_{\text{motor}}$, varies with time, as shown. There is also a constant friction force of $0.4\text{ N}$ applied to the rim of the wheel in the opposite direction of the motion. During which time interval does the rotational kinetic energy increase and then decrease?

(A) From $t=0\text{ s}$ to $t=1\text{ s}$
(B) From $t=1\text{ s}$ to $t=3\text{ s}$
(C) From $t=3\text{ s}$ to $t=5\text{ s}$
(D) From $t=5\text{ s}$ to $t=7\text{ s}$
(E) From $t=7\text{ s}$ to $t=8\text{ s}$

## Answer Key
**Correct Answer: C**

**Explanation:**
The rotational kinetic energy is increasing when the torque applied by the motor is larger than the torque applied by the friction. The torque applied by the friction is $\tau_{\text{friction}} = R \cdot 0.4\text{ N}$, and $4.8\text{ s}$ is where the torque applied by the shaft is also equivalent to that value. $\frac{R}{8} \cdot F_{\text{motor}} = R \cdot 0.4\text{ N}$ -> $F_{\text{motor}} = 3.2\text{ N}$.
The graph equation for force is $F_{\text{motor}} = 8\text{ N} - 1t$.
Setting $8\text{ N} - 1t = 3.2\text{ N}$ gives $t = 4.8\text{ s}$. The interval $t=3\text{ s}$ to $t=5\text{ s}$ contains this turning point.

## Course Description

AP Physics C: Mechanics is a calculus-based, college-level introductory physics course. Students cultivate deep understanding of classical mechanics by building physical models through inquiry-based investigations. The course is equivalent to the first semester of an introductory college calculus-based physics sequence.

Students develop understanding across seven domains: kinematics, forces and translational dynamics, work/energy/power, linear momentum, torque and rotational dynamics, energy and momentum of rotating systems, and oscillations. Throughout, students practice three science practices — creating representations, applying mathematical routines, and scientific argumentation — by designing experiments, deriving expressions, and justifying claims with evidence.

**Laboratory Requirement:** A minimum of 25% of instructional time is dedicated to hands-on, inquiry-based laboratory work. Students maintain a laboratory notebook throughout the year; colleges may request lab materials as part of the credit-granting process.

**Prerequisites:** Students should have taken, or be concurrently enrolled in, calculus. Comfort with derivatives and integrals is essential by mid-year.

---

## Course Objectives

Upon completion of this course, students will be able to:

1. **Create Representations** — Construct free-body diagrams, motion graphs, energy bar charts, and other physical representations
2. **Apply Mathematical Routines** — Derive symbolic expressions, calculate numerical results with appropriate units, compare quantities across scenarios, and predict functional dependencies
3. **Design and Argue** — Plan experimental procedures, apply physical laws to make claims, and justify those claims using evidence and reasoning
4. **Solve Across Domains** — Connect and apply concepts from kinematics, dynamics, energy, momentum, rotation, and oscillations to complex multi-step problems
5. **Conduct Lab Investigations** — Design, execute, and write up inquiry-based experiments; identify sources of experimental uncertainty

**Lab 6 – Rotational Inertia (2–3 periods)**
A disk, ring, and solid sphere are released from rest at the top of an inclined plane. (a) Predict which reaches the bottom first using energy methods (no slipping assumed); (b) measure times down the ramp and compare. Alternatively, attach a mass to a string wound around a pulley of known radius; measure α and compute I. Compare to theoretical I = ½MR² (disk) or I = MR² (ring). Lab report includes derivation of working equation from Στ = Iα.

|------|-------|--------|----------------|--------------|
| 18 | Mar 1–5 | 6.4 Conservation of Angular Momentum; 6.5 Rolling; Lab 7 | Lab 7: Angular Momentum Conservation | L is conserved when Σ_ext τ = 0; rolling without slipping: v_cm = rω; KE_total = ½mv² + ½Iω² |
| 19 | Mar 8–12 | 6.6 Motion of Orbiting Satellites; Unit 6 Review & Assessment | Orbital mechanics problem set | Gravitational force provides centripetal acceleration; circular orbits: v = √(GM/r); Kepler's third law derived; **Unit 6 Assessment** |

**Lab 7 – Angular Momentum Conservation (2 periods)**
A student sits on a freely rotating platform holding masses at arm's length. Masses are pulled in; ω increases. Measure initial and final ω with a rotary sensor; compare L_i and L_f. Alternatively, a disk falling onto a spinning disk: measure ω before and after using video analysis. Lab report: derive expected final ω from conservation, compare to measured, discuss systematic error.

**Lab 8 – Rolling Objects Race (1 period)**
Hollow cylinder, solid cylinder, and solid sphere of same mass and radius are released simultaneously from rest at the top of a ramp. Predict and measure finishing order. Derive speed at the bottom v = √(2gh / (1 + I/(MR²))) from energy conservation. Compare predicted speeds to measured. Discuss what happens on a frictionless surface.

**Lab 9 – Spring-Mass SHM (2 periods)**
Mount a mass on a spring suspended vertically. (a) Measure period T for 5 different masses; plot T² vs. m and extract k from the slope (k = 4π²/slope); compare to k measured statically. (b) Record x(t) with motion sensor; fit to A cos(ωt + φ); verify v_max = Aω. (c) Compute and plot total mechanical energy vs. time to confirm conservation.

**Lab 10 – Pendulum (1–2 periods)**
(a) Measure T for 6 different lengths L; plot T² vs. L and determine g from slope. Compare to accepted value; discuss sources of error. (b) Use an irregular physical pendulum (e.g., a rod with hanging mass); predict T from I and d; measure and compare. Lab report in FRQ-format experimental design write-up.

## Course Policies

### Attendance
- Physics concepts build sequentially and labs cannot be made up individually — attendance is critical
- Students absent for a lab must complete a written alternative analysis assignment
- More than 3 unexcused absences may result in grade deductions

### Late Work
- 10% deduction per day late; maximum 50% after 3 days late
- Lab reports submitted more than one week late receive a maximum of 40%
- Exceptions for excused absences and emergencies

### Lab Notebook
- Students maintain a bound lab notebook throughout the course
- Each entry must include: date, question/hypothesis, procedure, data table, analysis, conclusion, and error discussion
- Notebooks are checked during lab write-up submissions and at end of year
- Colleges may request lab materials as evidence for credit — keep all materials

### Calculator Policy
- A graphing or scientific calculator is required for this course and permitted on all assessments
- The College Board permits four-function, scientific, and graphing calculators on both sections of the AP Exam
- No CAS (Computer Algebra System) calculators permitted on AP Exam day

### Academic Integrity
- All submitted work — problem sets, lab reports, FRQs — must be your own
- Collaboration on lab data collection is allowed; all analysis and write-ups must be completed individually
- Derivations copied from peers or AI tools will receive zero credit; understanding must be demonstrated in person if questioned
- AI tools may be used to check understanding or explore concepts, but never to generate solutions for graded work

## 3. Knowledge points by topic

### Topic 1 — Motion, forces and energy

#### 1.1 Physical quantities and measurement techniques

- Use rulers to measure length; use measuring cylinders to measure volume
- Measure time intervals with clocks and digital timers
- **Multiple measurements**: measure $n$ values and divide by $n$ (e.g. period of a pendulum)
- ⭐ Distinguish scalar (magnitude only) from vector (magnitude and direction) quantities
- ⭐ Scalars: distance, speed, time, mass, energy, temperature
- ⭐ Vectors: force, weight, velocity, acceleration, momentum, electric field strength, gravitational field strength
- ⭐ Resultant of two perpendicular vectors — by calculation or scale drawing (forces or velocities only)

#### 1.2 Motion

- $v = \dfrac{s}{t}$; average speed $= \dfrac{\text{total distance}}{\text{total time}}$; velocity = speed in a given direction
- Sketch, plot and interpret distance–time and speed–time graphs; identify at rest, constant speed, accelerating, decelerating
- Gradient of s–t graph = speed; area under v–t graph = distance travelled
- Near the Earth's surface $g \approx 9.8\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$, approximately constant
- ⭐ $a = \dfrac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}$; acceleration = change in velocity per unit time
- ⭐ Gradient of v–t graph = acceleration; distinguish constant from changing acceleration
- ⭐ Deceleration is negative acceleration; use in calculations
- ⭐ Falling in a uniform field with and without air/liquid resistance → **terminal velocity**

#### 1.3 Mass and weight

- Mass = quantity of matter in an object at rest relative to the observer
- Weight = gravitational force on an object with mass
- $g = \dfrac{W}{m}$, unit N/kg; equal to the acceleration of free fall
- Balance compares masses; spring/electronic balance measures weight
- ⭐ Weight as the effect of a gravitational field on a mass

#### 1.4 Density

- $\rho = \dfrac{m}{V}$
- Determine density of a liquid (measuring cylinder), a regular solid (dimensions) and an irregular solid (displacement)
- Decide whether an object floats from density data
- ⭐ Compare densities of two immiscible liquids to decide which floats on top

#### 1.5 Forces

**1.5.1 Effects of forces**

- Forces change the size and shape of an object; change speed or direction of motion
- No resultant force → object at rest or moving in a straight line at constant speed (Newton's first law)
- Resultant of forces along the same straight line: add in same direction, subtract in opposite
- Sketch/interpret load–extension graphs; describe the experimental procedure
- ⭐ $k = \dfrac{F}{x}$; spring constant = force per unit extension
- ⭐ Identify the **limit of proportionality** on a load–extension graph
- ⭐ $F = ma$; force and acceleration in the same direction
- ⭐ Circular motion: force perpendicular to motion; F↑ → v↑ or r↓; greater mass needs greater force ( $mv^2/r$ not required)
- Solid friction opposes motion and produces heating; drag in liquids; air resistance in gases

**1.5.2 Turning effect of forces**

- Moment = force × perpendicular distance from the pivot
- Principle of moments: one force each side → ⭐ more than one force each side
- No resultant force and no resultant moment → equilibrium
- ⭐ Describe an experiment showing zero resultant moment in equilibrium

**1.5.3 Centre of gravity**

- Define centre of gravity
- Experiment to locate the centre of gravity of an irregular lamina (plumb line)
- Effect of centre-of-gravity position on stability

#### 1.6 Momentum (all ⭐)

- $p = mv$
- Impulse $F\Delta t = \Delta(mv)$
- Conservation of momentum (one dimension)
- $F = \dfrac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}$ (resultant force = rate of change of momentum)

#### 1.7 Energy, work and power

**1.7.1 Energy**

- Stores: kinetic, gravitational potential, chemical, elasti力 × 支点到力作用线的垂直距离c (strain), nuclear, electrostatic, internal (thermal)
- Transfers: mechanical work, electrical work, heating, electromagnetic/sound waves
- Conservation of energy; simple flow diagrams → ⭐ Sankey diagrams and multi-stage processes
- ⭐ $E_k = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$
- ⭐ $\Delta E_p = mg\Delta h$

**1.7.2 Work**

- Work done = energy transferred; $W = Fd = \Delta E$

**1.7.3 Energy resources**

- Seven sources: fossil fuels, biofuels, water (waves/tides/hydroelectric), geothermal, nuclear fuel, solar cells, solar panels + wind
- Advantages/disadvantages: renewability, availability, reliability, scale, environmental impact
- Boiler → turbine → generator
- ⭐ Sun's radiation is the main source of all resources except geothermal, nuclear and tidal
- ⭐ Nuclear fusion in the Sun; research into fusion power generation
- Efficiency (qualitative); ⭐ $\eta = \dfrac{\text{useful output}}{\text{total input}} \times 100\%$ (energy or power form)

**1.7.4 Power**

- $P = \dfrac{W}{t} = \dfrac{\Delta E}{t}$

#### 1.8 Pressure

- $p = \dfrac{F}{A}$; everyday examples of increasing/decreasing pressure
- Pressure beneath a liquid surface increases with depth and density (qualitative)
- ⭐ $\Delta p = \rho g \Delta h$

### Topic 2 — Thermal physics

#### 2.1.1 States of matter

- Distinguishing properties of solids, liquids and gases
- Names of changes of state: melting, freezing, boiling/evaporation, condensation (solid↔gas not required)

#### 2.1.2 Particle model

- Particle arrangement, separation and motion in the three states; draw simple particle diagrams
- Particle motion and temperature; **absolute zero = −273 °C**, minimum kinetic energy
- Gas pressure = collisions of particles with a surface
- Brownian motion: random motion of microscopic particles in suspension — evidence for the model
- Explanation: random collisions with gas/liquid molecules
- ⭐ Forces and distances between particles determine properties of the three states
- ⭐ Pressure as force per unit area created by collisions
- ⭐ Distinguish atoms/molecules from microscopic particles

#### 2.1.3 Gases and the absolute scale of temperature

- Fixed mass of gas: constant volume + rising temperature → pressure rises; constant temperature + reduced volume → pressure rises (in terms of particles)
- $T(\mathrm{K}) = \theta(°C) + 273$
- ⭐ $pV = \text{constant}$ (fixed mass, constant temperature), including graphical representation

#### 2.2.1 Thermal expansion

- Thermal expansion of solids, liquids and gases at constant pressure; applications and consequences
- ⭐ Explain relative expansion in terms of particle motion and arrangement: gas > liquid > solid

#### 2.2.2 Specific heat capacity

- Rise in temperature increases internal energy
- ⭐ Temperature rise = increase in average kinetic energy of all particles
- ⭐ $c = \dfrac{\Delta E}{m\Delta\theta}$; define specific heat capacity
- ⭐ Experiments to measure the specific heat capacity of a solid and a liquid

#### 2.2.3 Melting, boiling and evaporation

- Melting and boiling absorb energy without a change in temperature
- Melting/boiling points of water at standard atmospheric pressure: 0 °C and 100 °C
- Condensation and solidification in terms of particles
- Evaporation: escape of more-energetic particles from the surface; causes cooling
- ⭐ Differences between boiling and evaporation
- ⭐ Effect of temperature, surface area and air movement on evaporation
- ⭐ Explain cooling of an object in contact with an evaporating liquid

#### 2.3.1 Conduction

- Experiments with good and bad thermal conductors (insulators)
- ⭐ Conduction in metals: lattice vibrations + free (delocalised) electrons
- ⭐ Why conduction is poor in gases and most liquids
- ⭐ Hierarchy: good conductors > poorer conductors > insulators

#### 2.3.2 Convection

- Convection is an important transfer method in liquids and gases
- Explain convection by density changes; describe experiments

#### 2.3.3 Radiation

- Thermal radiation is infrared; all objects emit it
- Radiation requires no medium
- Surface colour (black/white) and texture (dull/shiny) affect emission, absorption and reflection
- ⭐ Constant temperature ⇔ rate of absorption = rate of emission
- ⭐ Consequences when absorption > emission or emission > absorption
- ⭐ Earth's temperature and the balance of incoming/outgoing radiation
- ⭐ Experiments to compare good/bad emitters; good/bad absorbers
- ⭐ Emission rate depends on surface temperature and surface area

#### 2.3.4 Consequences of thermal energy transfer

- Everyday applications: heating pans, heating a room by convection
- ⭐ Complex situations with more than one transfer type: burning wood/coal, car radiator

### Topic 3 — Waves

#### 3.1 General properties of waves

- Waves transfer energy without transferring matter
- Features: wavefront, wavelength, frequency, crest, trough, amplitude, wave speed
- $v = f\lambda$
- Transverse: vibration ⊥ propagation (EM waves, water waves, S-waves)
- Longitudinal: vibration ∥ propagation (sound, P-waves)
- Reflection at a plane surface; refraction due to change of speed; diffraction through a narrow gap
- Ripple tank: reflection, refraction (depth change), diffraction at a gap, diffraction at an edge
- ⭐ Effect of wavelength and gap size on diffraction
- ⭐ Effect of wavelength on diffraction at an edge

#### 3.2.1 Reflection of light

- Normal, angle of incidence, angle of reflection
- $i = r$
- Image in a plane mirror: same size, same distance, virtual
- ⭐ Constructions, measurements and calculations for plane mirrors

#### 3.2.2 Refraction of light

- Normal, angle of incidence, angle of refraction
- Experiment with transparent blocks; passage of light through a transparent material (two media only)
- Critical angle; total internal reflection (experimental and everyday examples)
- ⭐ Refractive index as the ratio of wave speeds in two regions
- ⭐ $n = \dfrac{\sin i}{\sin r}$
- ⭐ $n = \dfrac{1}{\sin c}$
- ⭐ Optical fibres in telecommunications

#### 3.2.3 Thin lenses

- Action of converging and diverging lenses on a parallel beam
- Focal length, principal axis, principal focus
- Ray diagrams for a real image formed by a converging lens
- Image characteristics: enlarged/same size/diminished, upright/inverted, real/virtual
- Virtual image: diverging rays extrapolated backwards; cannot be projected on a screen
- ⭐ Ray diagram for a virtual image formed by a converging lens
- ⭐ Single lens as a magnifying glass
- ⭐ Converging/diverging lenses to correct long/short sight

#### 3.2.4 Dispersion of light

- Dispersion of white light by a prism
- Seven colours in order of frequency and of wavelength
- ⭐ Light of a single frequency is monochromatic

#### 3.3 Electromagnetic spectrum

- Seven regions in order of frequency and wavelength
- All travel at the same speed in a vacuum
- ⭐ $3.0\times10^8\ \mathrm{m/s}$ in vacuum, approximately the same in air
- Uses: radio (broadcasting, astronomy, RFID), microwaves (satellite TV, mobile phones, ovens), infrared (remote controls, thermal imaging, optical fibres), visible (vision, photography, illumination), UV (security marking, detecting fake notes, sterilising water), X-rays (medical imaging, security scanners), gamma (sterilising food/equipment, cancer detection and treatment)
- Hazards: microwaves (internal heating), infrared (skin burns), UV (skin cancer, eye damage), X-rays/gamma (mutation, cell damage)
- Satellite communication mainly by microwaves; low-orbit vs geostationary satellites
- ⭐ Mobile phones/Wi-Fi use microwaves (penetrate walls, short aerial); Bluetooth uses radio waves; optical fibres use visible/IR
- ⭐ Difference between digital and analogue signals
- ⭐ Sound can be transmitted as digital or analogue
- ⭐ Benefits of digital signalling: higher data rate, accurate regeneration, greater range

#### 3.4 Sound

- Sound produced by vibrating sources; longitudinal wave
- Audible range 20 Hz – 20 kHz
- A medium is needed; speed in air ≈ 330–350 m/s
- Determine speed of sound from distance and time
- Amplitude → loudness; frequency → pitch
- Echo = reflection of sound
- Ultrasound: frequency > 20 kHz
- ⭐ Compression and rarefaction
- ⭐ Sound travels faster in solids than liquids, faster in liquids than gases
- ⭐ Ultrasound uses: non-destructive testing, medical scanning, sonar (with depth calculation)

### Topic 4 — Electricity and magnetism

#### 4.1 Simple phenomena of magnetism

- Forces between poles and between magnets and magnetic materials; N/S pole, attraction/repulsion, magnetised/unmagnetised
- Induced magnetism
- Temporary magnets (soft iron) vs permanent magnets (steel)
- Magnetic vs non-magnetic materials
- Magnetic field as a region where a pole experiences a force; field pattern of a bar magnet
- Field direction = direction of force on an N pole
- Plotting field lines with a compass or iron filings
- Uses of permanent magnets and electromagnets
- ⭐ Magnetic forces arise from interactions between magnetic fields
- ⭐ Spacing of field lines represents field strength

#### 4.2.1 Electric charge

- Positive and negative charges; like charges repel, unlike attract
- Experiments producing electrostatic charge by friction; detecting charge
- Charging by friction transfers electrons only
- Conductors vs insulators: experiment and electron model
- ⭐ Charge measured in coulombs (C)
- ⭐ Electric field: region where a charge experiences a force; direction = force on a positive charge
- ⭐ Field patterns: point charge, charged conducting sphere, two oppositely charged parallel plates (no end effects)

#### 4.2.2 Electric current

- Current related to flow of charge
- Ammeters (analogue and digital) with different ranges
- Conduction in metals: movement of free electrons
- Direct current (d.c.) vs alternating current (a.c.)
- ⭐ $I = \dfrac{Q}{t}$
- ⭐ Conventional current + → −; electron flow − → +

#### 4.2.3 Electromotive force and potential difference

- e.m.f.: electrical work done by a source in moving unit charge around a complete circuit; unit V
- p.d.: work done by unit charge passing through a component; unit V
- Voltmeters (analogue and digital) with different ranges
- ⭐ $E = \dfrac{W}{Q}$
- ⭐ $V = \dfrac{W}{Q}$

#### 4.2.4 Resistance

- $R = \dfrac{V}{I}$
- Experiment to determine resistance using a voltmeter and ammeter
- Resistance of a metallic wire vs length and cross-sectional area (qualitative)
- ⭐ I–V graphs: fixed resistor, filament lamp, diode
- ⭐ Resistance ∝ length; resistance ∝ 1/cross-sectional area

#### 4.2.5 Electrical energy and electrical power

- Circuits transfer energy from source to components to surroundings
- $P = IV$
- $E = IVt$
- Define the kilowatt-hour (kW h) and calculate the cost of electricity

#### 4.3.1 Circuit diagrams and circuit components

- Draw/interpret circuit diagrams: cells, batteries, power supplies, generators, potential dividers, switches, fixed/variable resistors, heaters, thermistors (NTC), LDRs, lamps, motors, bells, ammeters, voltmeters, magnetising coils, transformers, fuses, relays
- ⭐ Diodes and LEDs, and their behaviour in circuits

#### 4.3.2 Series and parallel circuits

- Current is the same at every point in a series circuit
- Construct and use series and parallel circuits
- Combined e.m.f. of sources in series
- Combined resistance of resistors in series
- In parallel, source current is larger than each branch current
- Combined resistance of two parallel resistors is less than either alone
- Advantages of connecting lamps in parallel
- ⭐ Current into a junction = current out; p.d.s add in series; p.d. is the same across parallel branches
- ⭐ Calculate the combined resistance of two resistors in parallel

#### 4.3.3 Action and use of circuit components

- At constant current, greater resistance gives greater p.d.
- ⭐ Action of a variable potential divider
- ⭐ $\dfrac{R_1}{R_2} = \dfrac{V_1}{V_2}$

#### 4.4 Electrical safety

- Hazards: damaged insulation, overheating cables, damp conditions, overloading of plugs/extension leads/sockets
- Live, neutral and earth wires; switch must be on the live wire
- Trip switches and fuses; choose appropriate ratings
- Casing must be non-conducting (double-insulated) or earthed
- A fuse without an earth wire protects the circuit and cabling of a double-insulated appliance

#### 4.5.1 Electromagnetic induction

- Conductor moving across a magnetic field, or a changing field, induces an e.m.f.
- Experiment demonstrating induction; factors affecting magnitude
- ⭐ Direction of induced e.m.f. opposes the change causing it (Lenz's law)
- ⭐ Relative directions of force, field and induced current

#### 4.5.2 The a.c. generator (all ⭐)

- Simple a.c. generator (rotating coil or magnet); slip rings and brushes
- e.m.f.–time graph; relate coil position to peaks, troughs and zeros

#### 4.5.3 Magnetic effect of a current

- Field pattern and direction for a straight wire and a solenoid
- Experiment to identify the field pattern (including direction)
- Uses in relays and loudspeakers
- ⭐ Field strength varies with current magnitude
- ⭐ Reversing current reverses the field direction

#### 4.5.4 Force on a current-carrying conductor

- Experiment showing the force; effect of reversing current or field
- ⭐ Relative directions of force, field and current (Fleming's left-hand rule)
- ⭐ Direction of force on beams of charged particles in a magnetic field

#### 4.5.5 The d.c. motor

- Turning effect on a current-carrying coil; increased by more turns, larger current, stronger field
- ⭐ Split-ring commutator and brushes

#### 4.5.6 The transformer

- Construction: soft-iron core; step-up/step-down; primary and secondary
- $\dfrac{V_p}{V_s} = \dfrac{N_p}{N_s}$
- Use in high-voltage transmission; advantages of high voltage
- ⭐ Principle of operation
- ⭐ $I_pV_p = I_sV_s$ (100% efficiency)
- ⭐ $P = I^2R$ to explain reduced cable losses at high voltage

### Topic 5 — Nuclear physics

#### 5.1.1 The atom

- Structure: positively charged nucleus + orbiting electrons
- Positive ions by losing electrons; negative ions by gaining electrons
- ⭐ α-particle scattering supports the nuclear model: tiny nucleus, most mass, positive charge

#### 5.1.2 The nucleus

- Nucleus composed of protons and neutrons
- Relative charges: proton +1, neutron 0, electron −1
- Proton number Z, nucleon number A; neutrons = A − Z
- Nuclide notation $^{A}_{Z}\mathrm{X}$
- Isotopes; an element may have several isotopes
- ⭐ Fission and fusion: nuclide equations, qualitative mass–energy changes
- ⭐ Proton number ↔ nuclear charge; nucleon number ↔ relative mass

#### 5.2.1 Detection of radioactivity

- Background radiation; sources: radon gas, rocks and buildings, food and drink, cosmic rays
- Detector + counter; count rate (counts/s or counts/min)
- ⭐ Use background measurements to find corrected count rate

#### 5.2.2 The three types of nuclear emission

- Emission is spontaneous and random in direction
- α, β, γ: nature, relative ionising effect, relative penetrating power
- ⭐ Deflection in electric and magnetic fields
- ⭐ Explain ionising differences by kinetic energy and charge

#### 5.2.3 Radioactive decay

- Decay = change in an unstable nucleus, emitting α, β and/or γ; spontaneous and random
- α- and β-decay change the nucleus to a different element
- ⭐ Radioactivity due to excess neutrons or an overly heavy nucleus
- ⭐ Effect on the nucleus: greater stability, fewer excess neutrons; β-decay: n → p + e
- ⭐ Decay equations using nuclide notation

#### 5.2.4 Half-life

- Definition; simple calculations from tables or decay curves (no background)
- ⭐ Find half-life from a curve without background subtracted
- ⭐ Choose isotopes by radiation type and half-life: smoke alarms, food irradiation, sterilisation, thickness gauging, cancer diagnosis/treatment

#### 5.2.5 Safety precautions

- Effects on living things: cell death, mutation, cancer
- Safe transport, use and storage
- ⭐ Protection: reduce exposure time, increase distance, use shielding

### Topic 6 — Space physics

#### 6.1.1 The Earth

- Earth rotates on a tilted axis once in ~24 h → day/night, apparent motion of the Sun
- Orbits the Sun once in ~365 days → seasons
- Moon orbits the Earth in ~1 month → phases
- ⭐ $v = \dfrac{2\pi r}{T}$

#### 6.1.2 The Solar System

- Contents: Sun, eight named planets in order, minor planets/dwarf planets and asteroids, moons, comets and other small bodies
- Inner four rocky and small, outer four gaseous and large; accretion model (gravity, interstellar cloud, accretion disc)
- Gravitational field strength: greater with planet mass; decreases with distance
- Calculate light travel times
- Sun holds most of the mass → planets orbit it
- Gravitational attraction provides the centripetal force
- ⭐ Elliptical orbits; Sun not at the centre unless nearly circular
- ⭐ Analyse planetary data: orbital distance, period, density, surface temperature, surface g
- ⭐ Sun's field strength and orbital speeds decrease with distance
- ⭐ Object in elliptical orbit moves faster when closer to the Sun (energy conservation)

#### 6.2.1 The Sun as a star

- Medium-sized star, mostly hydrogen and helium; radiates mainly in IR, visible and UV
- ⭐ Stars powered by nuclear fusion; stable stars fuse hydrogen into helium

#### 6.2.2 Stars

- Galaxies contain billions of stars; Sun is in the Milky Way; other stars are much farther
- Define light-year
- ⭐ $1\ \text{ly} = 9.5\times10^{15}\ \mathrm{m}$
- ⭐ Life cycle: interstellar cloud → protostar → main sequence → red giant/supergiant → planetary nebula + white dwarf / supernova → neutron star or black hole → new stars and planets

#### 6.2.3 The Universe

- Milky Way is one of billions of galaxies; diameter ~100 000 light-years
- Redshift: increase in observed wavelength from receding stars/galaxies
- Light from distant galaxies is redshifted compared with Earth sources
- Redshift → expanding Universe → supports the Big Bang
- ⭐ Cosmic microwave background radiation (CMBR) observed in all directions
- ⭐ CMBR produced shortly after the Universe formed, redshifted into microwaves
- ⭐ Find recession speed from redshift
- ⭐ Find distance of a far galaxy from supernova brightness
- ⭐ Hubble constant $H_0 = \dfrac{v}{d}$; current estimate $2.2\times10^{-18}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$
- ⭐ $\dfrac{d}{v} = \dfrac{1}{H_0}$ estimates the age of the Universe

## 6. Common exam pitfalls

- Always give units; do not round too early in multi-step calculations
- Graph questions: gradient needs units; area under v–t is distance; gradient of s–t is speed
- The "distance" in a moment is the **perpendicular** distance, not along the force
- $g$ has units N/kg, equivalent to m/s² — be able to use both
- Wave speed depends on the medium; frequency depends on the source; on refraction frequency stays constant, wavelength and speed change
- Refractive index is a ratio of speeds, not of angles; both angles are measured from the normal in their own media
- Series → same current; parallel → same voltage; identify the circuit before choosing a formula
- A transformer changes voltage and current, **not power** (ideal case)
- α-decay: A − 4, Z − 2; β-decay: A unchanged, Z + 1
- Half-life is "half remains", not "time to lose half"; remember background radiation
- Definition questions: use the "quantity per unit …" form, avoid circular wording
- Explanation questions: phenomenon → cause → consequence — missing one step loses marks
- Command words: state (1 mark, no reason needed), describe (what happens), explain (why, using physics), suggest (apply to unfamiliar context), calculate (show working + unit), determine (may need graph/data)
